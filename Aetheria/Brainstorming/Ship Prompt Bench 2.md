# Ship Prompt Bench 2

> **Status: brainstorming bench, Imagination pass of 2026-10-04.** Not canon and not adopted. It replaces nothing: [[Brainstorming/Ship Prompt Bench|Ship Prompt Bench]] stays as the record of the first round. Prompts here are the third block of the composition order in [[Brainstorming/Ship Design Language|Ship Design Language]], written to follow the global block and the faction block. Roles come from [[Game Design/Faction Play|Faction Play]], craft rules from guidelines 1 to 12 in [[Brainstorming/Faction Ship Concept Prompts|Faction Ship Concept Prompts]], and the vacuum stance from [[Game Design/Design Pillars|Design Pillars]].

Operator brief, 2026-10-04: "I'd like a new batch of bench prompts, too." She had tested twelve first-bench prompts alone, without the global or faction blocks, on GPT Image 2.5. The household objects fell flat, Vasuki and Bison stood out, Squatter was the one animal she liked, and her husband loved the cute animals: Raft, Shellback, and above all the Gentle Giant. Her register ruling: "Some factions definitely have more space for whimsy. Corriedales is the obvious one to host these mascot ships like Raft and Shellback," and "DME is definitely more serious and reserved where the Pirates are wild and maximalist." The diagnosis behind this bench is that 86 first-bench prompts said "Its whole body follows a ___", which made every ship a costume.

## For the Operator

These are readings the docs did not settle. Each one is a choice I made so the bench could be written; overrule any of them.

1. **Corriedales roles are invented.** Faction Play lists Corriedales as a brand without a flag, a quirk or roles; its weapons ride on cheap Lucent hulls. Its three roles here (the fan's first fighter, a merch hauler, a companion escort) come from the first bench's budget fighter and from the brand note: toys, companion organisms, and surveillance that feels like affection. Lucent's own section keeps a holding ship and gains a headliner duelist in place of the budget fighter that moved.
2. **Where the hits went.** Raft, Shellback and the Gentle Giant were Cetacean and Lightsail prompts. Under the register table, those factions are now "whimsy turned up": an animal may lend its mass and its face, not its costume. So their successors here are faces and masses, and the whole-animal versions of the same delight live in Corriedales. If you would rather keep a whole-animal hull in Cetacean or Lightsail, that is a register change to Ship Design Language, not a bench edit.
3. **What counts as a household object.** I read it as the domestic, hand-sized things: appliances, furniture, containers, utensils, office supplies, hand tools. Vehicles and buildings are not household objects here; they are the terrestrial affordances Design Pillars allows (Cat's taxi), so they appear in the whimsical and spectacle registers. Candy counts as the high-whimsy register's "candy", not as a household object.
4. **The faction blocks still carry household similes.** NiteLife's block says espresso machine and smart speaker, Zhestokost's cast-iron stove, Cryonix's vacuum flask, Aya's lidded clay cooking pot, Sol Dominion's official stamp. Guideline 10 allows them as proportion only, and they enter every composed prompt. If composed renders drift toward costume, cut there first.
5. **Two analogies per composed prompt.** Most faction blocks already name a proportion simile, so an analogy attempt here adds a second, against guideline 1. The ship prompt is last and wins, so its analogy should govern the mass. The **Function** attempts carry no analogy at all and test whether the block's grammar is enough by itself. If an analogy attempt muddles where its Function sibling reads cleanly, trim the block's simile.
6. **A control pair is worth one render.** These prompts are not meant to be rendered alone; without their blocks they have no palette and no register. Rendering Vasuki and Bison composed, against their first-round renders, would show what the blocks add before you spend a batch.
7. **Lucent's spectacle register is still unconfirmed** in Ship Design Language. The Lucent attempts here take camera liberties (a stage deck, an up) on that reading.
8. **DME's burnout raider** was built around deep space burnout thrusters, and thrusters are yours to place. Its attempts carry the overheating as a cell bank with cut lines instead.

## How to Use This Bench

- **Compose before rendering.** Paste the global Aetheria block, then the faction's block, then one prompt from here, with a blank line between each. The prompts here do not restate a faction's palette or register; the faction block supplies both. A prompt assigns a colour only where a part is unique to that ship, such as a pirate crew's accent colour.
- **Each attempt names its mode**, so the bench tests the ways an analogy can enter a hull:
  - **Mass:** an animal or vehicle lends its proportion and the way its weight sits, named once. Vasuki's hood and Bison's hump are the model.
  - **Story:** the hull shows a history: what it was, what was done to it, what it has survived. Squatter's stolen freight pod is the model.
  - **Function:** no analogy. The job and the faction's grammar place the parts.
  - **Mass and face:** an animal lends its mass and its friendly face, not its body. The "whimsy turned up" registers: Lightsail, Ewan Hart, Cetacean Navigators.
  - **Mascot:** a whole, cute, plump animal. High-whimsy registers only: Corriedales, Miss Terri's, Framgång.
  - **Candy:** a confection's large form as the hull. Miss Terri's only.
  - **Terrestrial:** a building's or a vehicle's affordances, such as a door, a porch, a kerb or an up. Whimsical and spectacle registers only.
- **Safe bet** or **Moonshot**, as in the first bench. A moonshot says what it risks.
- Each faction says which first-round lessons it carries.
- **Thrusters, attitude ports and radiators are not named.** Ship Design Language leaves them to you. The Cryonix emitter scales are the one exception that block already names.
- Every prompt ends with the standard frame clause. Station prompts end with the station frame.

## Riskiest for Image-to-3D

- *Pouchling (Corriedales, kangaroo):* the pouch is a concavity, and a joey's head peeking from it is a small separate form. The prompt fills the pouch with a rounded cargo lid.
- *Piggyback (Corriedales, opossum with four young on her back):* four small bodies on one back may separate or merge. Fused, half-sunk humps are the hope.
- *Antler Rack (AU, moose):* palmate antlers are broad slabs, but the image model knows them as branching tines.
- *Still Water (Cryonix, standing heron):* the neck-and-bill barrel is long and thin by nature.
- *Swan Song Retreat (Framgång, swan boat):* the swan's neck is an arch in waiting. The prompt makes it a thick, upright prow.
- *Green Room (Lucent station):* buildings hung beneath a plaza are separate volumes with gaps between them, on purpose.
- *Free Sample (Miss Terri's, ice-cream van):* the awning wants to be a thin sheet; the prompt makes it a thick slab.
- *Last Mile (NiteLife):* three empty cartridge cradles are deep concavities Tripo may fill.
- *Every Pirate attempt:* the additions sit on the camera side only, so the far side is guesswork. Supply a matching rear view or accept the guess.
- *Frosting (Miss Terri's, cupcake):* the frosting swirl is fine relief that reconstruction may smear into noise.
- *Remora (Sol Dominion):* the docking disc is a large shallow concavity on the dorsal surface.

## Zhestokost

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Zhestokost|Zhestokost]] · [[Game Design/Faction Play#Zhestokost|doctrine]] · register: serious

**Aesthetic thesis.** The arsenal that learned to fly, with ammunition as architecture. An analogy may tell the hull where its weight sits and nothing more.
**Carries:** Bison's lesson that weight placed high and forward reads as power, and Vasuki's that hardpoints belong on the mass the analogy creates. The first bench's armadillo and walrus asked for whole animals; their mass survives here without their bodies.

### Role: Ammunition tender

**Pitch:** Two crew keep a column firing. The hull has built-in ammunition-transfer collars and deep magazines, two small point-defense hardpoints and one ventral utility hardpoint for a cargo grip. It is slow and carries nothing else to fight with, and the whole column's fight depends on it.

**A · Quartermaster** · Function · Safe bet
Design the Quartermaster, a two-person Zhestokost ammunition tender. The magazines are the ship. Four deep magazine sections stand in a row down the spine, each one a stepped rectangular mass taller than the cockpit, each with one square loading hatch on its crest and a stencilled calibre code on its flank. The cockpit sits low at the front in a thick armoured block, a narrow slit under a heavy brow, dwarfed by the magazines behind it. One squat ammunition-transfer collar projects from each flank beside the second magazine section, ringed with towing lugs. One small point-defence turret sits on each front corner of the cockpit block. A folded cargo grip lies flat under the belly between two heavy skids. One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.

**B · Babushka** · Story · Moonshot. The tender that outlived three columns. It tests whether a history can read inside one cast body; the image may split it into modules.
Design the Babushka, a two-person Zhestokost ammunition tender that has been lengthened twice over three generations of foundry pattern. Its forward third is the original casting, rounder and older, with softened edges and many welded patches; its middle third is a later pattern with crisper steps; its rear third is the newest, sharp-cornered and freshly enamelled. All three are cast into one continuous hull with thick weld fillets at the joins. Each third carries one deep magazine bay with a square hatch on top. A transfer collar sits on each flank of the middle third. Two small point-defence turrets sit on the oldest section, either side of a narrow cockpit slit. Three columns' unit codes are painted on the flank in a row, the first two crossed out with a single neat stencilled line. One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.

### Role: Flak gunboat (column line ship)

**Pitch:** One pilot flies a cheaper sibling of the Foundry Bell. It has one centerline medium ballistic hardpoint and two small flak hardpoints that shred incoming missiles, deep feeds and replaceable armor plates. It is slow to turn and nearly blind beyond visual range.

**A · Morzh** · Mass · Safe bet
Design the Morzh, a single-pilot Zhestokost flak gunboat. It carries its mass the way a bull walrus does: nearly all of it in one huge, folded, forward-heavy shoulder, tapering to a small low hindquarter. The shoulder is stacked from three thick overlapping armour folds, each a replaceable plate with a row of bolt heads along its edge. One medium cannon runs straight out of the front face of the shoulder on the centreline, its breech buried in the mass. Two short, thick flak barrels angle forward and down from beneath the front of the shoulder, one on each side. The cockpit is a narrow slit across the top of the front face, above the cannon. Ammunition feed hatches run in a row along the top of the shoulder. One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.

**B · Hammer Price** · Function · Safe bet
Design the Hammer Price, a single-pilot Zhestokost flak gunboat built outward from its gun. Start with one medium cannon laid along the centreline, its long recoil cradle and its feed path visible on the dorsal surface as a raised armoured channel running back from the breech to a deep magazine block behind the cockpit. Around the gun, the hull steps outward in three thick layers of bolted armour, each layer lower and broader than the one inside it, so the cannon sits at the top of a stepped pyramid. One small flak turret sits on each outer shoulder of the lowest layer. The cockpit is a narrow slit in the forward face of the middle layer, beside the barrel root. One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.
