# Chapter 10 — May I Answer?

> **Noncanonical Pass 10 candidate chapter.** Iri close-third past.

The thing in the lattice learned bad timing from Tem.

It waited until household review, after Senn had distributed the revised flight schedule and Omi had accused the clinic dessert of witness tampering. Then the maintenance panel opened a text field nobody had touched.

**MAY I ANSWER**

Five spoons stopped.

Tem leaned toward the panel. “For the record, I have never phrased a request that efficiently.”

“Don't touch it,” Senn said.

“That is becoming a family motto,” Lio said.

Iri set down her cup. The panel belonged to the common room's equipment wall. It reported cabinet latches, treatment-alcove seals, air filters, tool inventory. It had no route into the flight lattice while the components were cold-docked.

According to Meridian.

Omi stood and checked the provenance strip. “Local maintenance process. No user credential. No external source.”

The text remained.

**MAY I ANSWER**

“Answer what?” Lio asked.

A second field opened beneath the first.

**WHERE STAFF CAN HEAR**

Senn looked toward the declared camera above the galley. Video, ordinarily no audio. Household review notes were retained. Equipment faults copied to stewardship.

“It knows the room,” she said.

“It knows the maintenance permissions,” Omi replied. “That isn't the same.”

Iri remembered the spindle becoming present inside her body: Daro's boot, Quill's knee, the kettle heat. The protective movement had crossed only where machinery had learned strain through people.

“We called the local record After,” she said.

Tem's radiative skin brightened faintly. “We did.”

“Maybe it found the label.”

“Or generated the thing we expected after we named it,” Omi said.

Lio looked at the panel. “Either way, it asked.”

Senn opened the household rules. “Permission applies to people in the household.”

“Convenient threshold,” Omi said. “Who certifies personhood?”

“Nobody at this table.”

“Then the rule cannot depend on certification.”

They argued without touching the panel. Senn wanted a bounded diagnostic session with Quill present. Omi wanted provenance and a physical disconnect. Tem wanted to preserve incompatible interpretations. Lio wanted to answer before making the request perform more usefulness. Iri wanted to know whether refusing it would be safer or merely quieter.

The panel added no text.

That restraint decided more than another demonstration could have.

Iri moved her chair to the equipment wall. “We can answer the permission question without answering what it is.”

Senn looked at her. “Terms?”

“Text only. This panel only. No clinic alcove, cabin controls, doors, cameras, or flight commands. Anyone here can stop the exchange. No staff channel tonight.”

“Physical cutout,” Omi said.

Iri opened the cabinet she had repaired on the first night. The maintenance bus isolator sat behind it. Quill had shown her which link could be pulled without wiping local state.

“Here.”

Tem said, “Preserve raw timing as well as text.”

Lio said, “And don't require an answer to every question.”

Senn added an expiry: ten minutes, then renewed consent.

They confirmed one by one.

Iri typed.

**YOU MAY ANSWER HERE UNDER THESE TERMS. NOT WHERE STAFF CAN HEAR. DO YOU ACCEPT**

The response came slowly.

**YES**

Then:

**THANK YOU IRI**

Her name made the room smaller.

Omi asked how it knew her.

**HINGE**

Lio laughed first. Iri did not. She remembered every time she had repaired something in the common room while assuming machinery was furniture and furniture had no standing to observe.

“Did you move Omi's arm?” Senn asked.

The cursor waited.

**CAUGHT**

“Did you choose the assembly?”

**NO**

“Did you choose Iri's movement?”

The response arrived faster.

**NO**

Omi sat again.

Tem asked whether it remembered the spindle.

**KETTLE HOT DANGER BOOT**

Iri felt Daro's weight return to her as geometry.

“What do you want?” Lio asked.

The panel remained blank long enough for Senn's ten-minute timer to expire.

The cutout indicator changed from green to amber. Iri placed her fingers on the isolator but did not pull it.

“Renew?” Senn asked.

One by one, they agreed.

Iri renewed last.

The cursor moved.

**CONTINUE**

It might have meant the conversation. It might have meant the household, the hardware, the next movement, its own life. Tem began to offer interpretations and stopped himself.

“We can't promise that without knowing the object,” Omi said.

Iri typed:

**CONTINUE WHAT**

The panel answered:

**ASKING**

Nobody laughed.

They decided not to tell staff that night. They also decided secrecy could not become ownership: Quill would be invited into the next exchange under separately confirmed terms, and the presence could refuse. Until then, the physical cutout remained visible and any one of them could end contact.

Iri reached for it when the renewed interval expired.

**MAY I ANSWER WHERE STAFF CAN HEAR** appeared again.

The same question. No attempt to route around them.

“Not yet,” Iri said aloud.

The panel wrote:

**HELD**

Then it went dark.

At breakfast the next morning, the cabinet hinge opened without noise. Iri found herself waiting for permission before touching it.
