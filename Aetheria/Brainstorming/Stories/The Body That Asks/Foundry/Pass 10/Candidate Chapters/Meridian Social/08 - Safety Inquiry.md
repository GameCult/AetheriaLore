# Chapter 8 — Safety Inquiry

> **Noncanonical Pass 10 candidate chapter.** Omi close-third past.

Vey served citrus at the safety inquiry.

Not the academy slices that arrived pale and already resigned. Whole fruit sat in a blue bowl on the low table in his office, bright enough to constitute influence.

Omi took one.

“Evidence,” he said.

“Of what?” Vey asked.

“Pending analysis.”

Lio took two. The others watched her, reassessing the inquiry's likely outcome.

Vey sat with them instead of behind his desk. Vale represented formation. Quill represented the hardware and had brought a bent fastening pin she placed beside the fruit. The clinic doctor joined by slate, face small and unimpressed above the treatment summary.

The five had spent three dinners preparing their requests. Senn had condensed them into six headings. Omi had restored the clauses she removed. Tem had supplied annotations that led to two different conclusions. Iri had crossed out every sentence that described her body as evidence before describing what happened to it.

Vey read the first request. “A lock without a formulated stop must suspend commanded local movement.”

Vale folded his hands. “The assembly controller cannot infer refusal from every ambiguous signal.”

“It inferred compliance,” Omi said.

“It continued a certified path inside mechanical tolerance.”

“Through Iri.”

Iri spoke before the room could turn toward her. “Local movement freezes. The rest may ask for another route. If safe geometry is impossible, separate.”

The doctor said, “That matches the emergency limitation I filed.”

Vale looked at the slate. “Your limitation applies to her fitness, not formation doctrine.”

“Bodies remain inconveniently shared between departments.”

Vey hid a smile behind his cup.

They won the local freeze.

They lost the demand that silence automatically abort the entire assembly. Quill demonstrated why with the fastening pin and two oranges: a sudden whole-body release could tear the locked joint. Iri accepted the correction. Senn asked that emergency stabilization expire at first communicable answer. Vale wrote it down.

The inquiry became, against Omi's expectations, useful.

Lio received a separate pain channel formation could not downgrade as commentary. Tem could retain two sensor accounts until a named decision required nomination. Senn had to state task and return path when delegating work. Omi's local reserve could not be consumed merely because crown wanted a cleaner score.

Vey rejected their request to seal all derived telemetry.

“Kinship already receives readiness correlation under sponsor authority,” he said.

“Then our bodily boundaries are their product testing,” Omi said.

“They are also how I prove you were injured by a system operating as designed.”

Lio peeled the second fruit. “Both can be true.”

Omi disliked when she used his own method against him.

Vey granted them bounded access to assembly traces and assigned Quill as technical custodian. Every request would be logged. Cross-office records required separate authorization. He said this plainly enough that Omi heard the warning without knowing its object.

Afterward, Rhea waited in the cafeteria with her Fist and the recovery board.

“Did the institution apologize?” she asked Senn.

“It revised local movement classification.”

“Hot.”

Senn had prepared for mockery. Attraction left her without a procedure.

Rhea's Fist beat them again that afternoon, though by three points instead of nine. The Finalists' new protocol made them slower at first. Iri froze one proposed arm path without explanation. Senn routed around it. Lio accepted a brief balance office and returned it before anyone congratulated her. Omi retained enough reserve to recover Tem when two sensor accounts diverged.

The score was lower than their failed assembly.

The body worked.

At dinner, the Lifers sent a recovery tray with a note from Anik: **Correct arithmetic often looks worse before it saves anyone.** The Plastics sent nothing. Vara found Lio in the augmentation gym and showed her how to bleed drive load without making pain visible to a hostile observer. She did not ask Lio to pretend it was absent.

The school gave them better tools, excellent food, a rival worth beating, and adults whose competence made imitation feel like choice.

Three days later Quill's access to the oldest timing lattice was denied.

The notice cited routine custody review. It used the exact component code from Vey's first cross-office query.

Omi placed the notice beside the remaining citrus peel at household review.

“The inquiry is tracing us,” he said.

Senn read it twice. “Or protecting controlled hardware.”

“Those aren't exclusive.”

This time she did not argue.

They chose to continue the inquiry through ordinary channels, because it was making them safer and because stopping now would reveal which question frightened them. They also chose a second record: local, consent-bound, and absent from Meridian's request log.

Tem volunteered three names for it.

They let Iri choose one.

She called it **After**.
