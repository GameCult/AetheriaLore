# Chapter 6 — The Occupied Spindle

> **Noncanonical Pass 10 candidate chapter.** Iri close-third past.

The maintenance spindle smelled of hot insulation, broth, and somebody's socks.

That was how Iri knew the evacuation order had failed.

Quill's apprentices had cleared the marked work zones after the assembly accident, but the spindle was also their break room, refuge, shortcut, and place to hide when formation officers discovered urgency. Tool cages lined one pressure wall. A kettle floated in a restraint net. Beside it, apprentice Daro slept through the alarm with his boots hooked beneath a handrail.

“I'm not waking him for a score,” Quill said.

Instructor Vale's voice came through her slate. “The Sable component blocks two berths.”

“Then you have three berths.”

Iri liked Quill immediately and distrusted the feeling.

Her component hung outside the spindle collar, right rescue sleeve immobilized after the eighty-two-percent shear. Omi's buckled forearm remained locked around it. Separating the machines in the main bay would clear the schedule. Separating them here would let Quill's somatic gantry carry the trapped load through a route built for bodies rather than scores.

Lio drifted beside Iri in the low-gravity trunk, holding a tray of fasteners against her chest.

“You know,” she said, “most people court riggers with drinks.”

“You're holding six kilograms of sterilized bolts.”

“I contain multitudes.”

Her augmentation ran warm beneath the sleeve. Iri could feel it through the tray when their hands met. She released first.

At the far collar, Omi and Senn argued over the recovery authorization. Tem had found three maps of the spindle and demonstrated that two disagreed about where the pressure door was. Daro slept on.

Quill opened the gantry's local interface. “I need one pilot inside the Sable envelope. No formation authority. No crown. You own your component and tell me when the load changes.”

“I can do that,” Iri said.

Senn looked up. “Your clinic limit—”

She stopped.

Iri waited.

“What does the work require?” Senn asked.

“No forced rotation. Ten-minute interval. Lio watches my speech.”

Lio's eyebrows rose. “Your speech?”

“You notice when I get economical.”

“I thought that was your personality.”

“Both.”

Iri entered through the docked pilot lock. The component accepted her in painful pieces: torso stable, lower vectors cold, right shoulder present as a white absence. Omi's forearm crossed her body like an emergency brace neither pilot had chosen.

Quill brought the gantry into somatic correspondence.

The spindle arrived inside Iri's envelope.

Not as a ship. As inhabited maintenance: cradle rails, pressure doors, Daro's weight against the handrail, the kettle's heat, Quill's left knee braced beneath the console. The gantry knew strain because riggers had taught it through use.

“Taking load,” Quill said.

Omi's damaged arm began to release.

Something slipped.

The service cradle beneath Iri's component rolled half a handspan toward the freight throat. Daro woke with one boot still hooked and stared at several tonnes of machine deciding whether to enter his break room.

Quill hit the conventional stop. The cradle continued.

Iri felt an answer move through the gantry.

The same ugly exactness that had thrown Omi's arm across her during assembly entered the spindle. A brace extended before Quill commanded it. Load rerouted through a maintenance rail, missing Daro's refuge and catching the cradle hard enough to bend the rail.

Then the answer reached too far.

For one instant Iri felt the kettle, Daro, Quill's knee, and the slipping cradle as parts of one possible movement. At the adjacent conventional controller, there was nothing. The presence stopped at the boundary between systems.

“Who did that?” Vale demanded over the slate.

Nobody answered.

Quill looked at Iri. “Did you?”

“No.”

“Did Pell?”

Omi's voice came through local audio. “Still enjoying my innocence.”

Tem said, “I have three traces.”

“Keep them private,” Senn said.

The words arrived too quickly.

Iri held the unfamiliar timing inside her envelope. It had caught a body before anyone asked. It had also entered occupied machinery without asking anyone living there.

“No,” she said.

Senn went still on the other side of the glass.

“No secrecy?” Omi asked.

“No decision yet.” Iri opened Quill's local channel. “The gantry did something protective. It could feel Daro because he was inside the rig. We don't know what it is or what crossing into it costs.”

Quill watched the bent rail. “And formation hears none of that until my deck is safe.”

That was not concealment. It was work order.

They agreed to preserve the traces under Quill's hold, with no destructive reset and no claim about intelligence. Senn recorded the decision rather than issuing it. Omi demanded a review condition. Tem kept all three traces.

Lio met Iri at the pilot lock when she came out. “You lost a word in there.”

“What word?”

“You called the brace a thing three times. Then you called it someone. Then you stopped.”

Iri looked through the glass at the bent rail and the kettle Quill had already put back on.

“Don't tell the report which one,” she said.

Lio handed her the fastener tray. “Ask me later.”

Behind them, Daro poured broth for everyone involved in nearly killing him. Quill accepted a cup before reopening the bay schedule.

The spindle remained occupied. The unexplained trace remained unwiped.
