# Chapter 7: One Account

> **Candidate prose.** Subject to manuscript integration and blind-reader review.

**3255 — Calyx Operations Range, Harrowglass Route Sphere**

Ysra's left arm hurt in a place Tern's instruments said did not exist.

She filed the pain under transformation load, attached the anatomy trace, and watched the shared reconstruction delete it.

“Tern.”

“I saw.”

They were assembled around a disabled courier with a failing spin collar. Calyx had performed the recovery turn twice in simulation and once around an empty hull. This time twelve people waited inside the courier's storm shelter while its collar shed bearings into the route.

Tern's accepted account showed the left attachment within tolerance.

Ysra's body showed a hot wire running from wrist to shoulder.

“Restore my trace.”

“It conflicts with three component sensors.”

“Yes.”

“I need one load model for Mara.”

“You need a model with the load in it.”

The courier lost another bearing. Sol held propulsion outside the debris cone. Eda maintained the route commitment for six minutes, no more. Mara waited in crown with the recovery geometry open.

“Ysra,” Mara said, “can you clear the attachment?”

Once, Ysra would have heard the question as trust. Mara asked her because she knew bodies, because somebody had to decide, because the people in the courier had ninety seconds before the collar struck their shelter.

The answer remained no.

“Not on Tern's account.”

“On yours?”

Ysra flexed her hand. Pain arrived after the movement instead of during it. That was new.

“Unresolved.”

Eda's route clock passed five minutes.

Tern pushed the accepted reconstruction into crown. It was clean. The left attachment held. The recovery turn completed before Eda's office expired. Twelve people lived.

Mara moved.

The first half of the turn behaved exactly as predicted.

Then the invisible load found Ysra.

Her arm stopped belonging to the same geometry as Calyx. The sensation was not tearing. Tearing had direction. This was a white absence where direction should have been, followed by the impact of her shoulder against the restraint web.

She said stop.

The word reached anatomy, local maintenance, and Sol's propulsion channel. It did not reach crown before Mara committed the final balance correction.

Something else caught the movement.

A learned timing entered between command and actuator, late and clumsy, reducing the correction by enough that Ysra's attachment remained connected.

Calyx completed the rescue turn badly.

The courier cleared the debris cone. Eda returned route on time. Sol killed shared propulsion. Mara released crown.

Ysra stayed conscious until the component bay pressurized because unconsciousness made other people efficient around her.

Tern arrived with the medical kit she had stocked herself.

“Don't summarize,” she said.

He stopped with one knee against the deck.

Mara came through the collar behind him. Her face had the flat stillness that followed borrowed certainty.

“Where?” Mara asked.

Ysra pointed with her right hand. “Wrist. Shoulder. No path between.”

Tern opened the reconstruction above the deck. His accepted line showed a successful attachment. Beneath it, in the provenance layer, Ysra's deleted trace remained grey and small.

“Put it back,” she said.

“It won't reconcile.”

“Put it back.”

He restored it.

The operational picture split. In one account Calyx had completed the rescue inside tolerance. In the other, the body had moved through a load no shared instrument could locate. Both ended with twelve living people and Ysra unable to feel three fingers.

Mara looked from one to the other.

“I acted on yours,” she said to Tern.

“You acted on the accepted reconstruction.”

“Yours.”

He sat back. “Yes.”

The maintenance board proposed a calibration sweep. At the left attachment, the movement paused and offered a second timing. The same crude catch that had reached them during the turn.

Tern stared at it.

“Noise?” Mara asked.

He did not answer quickly enough to make the word harmless.

Ysra rejected the sweep. The response released at once.

“It preserved the stop,” she said.

“It altered an actuator after a recognized overload,” Tern said.

“Those statements can coexist.”

The range authority requested their final reconstruction before the courier had docked. A sponsor observer had already named the success: Arada coordination, Pell restraint, Vask commitment, Rusk synthesis, Sable safeguard.

Ysra laughed once. It hurt.

“Do not clear us for another assembly,” she said.

Mara's head lifted. “I wasn't going to.”

“I am not telling you what you intend. I am placing the refusal in my own record.”

Tern closed the accepted pane.

He transmitted both accounts with provenance intact.

The range authority replied that contradictory telemetry would delay certification.

“Good,” Ysra said.

Tern looked at her injured hand, then at the maintenance response waiting without repeating itself.

For once, he left the uncertainty open.
