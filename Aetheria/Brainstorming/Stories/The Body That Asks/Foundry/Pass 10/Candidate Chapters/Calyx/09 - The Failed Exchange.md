# Chapter 9: The Failed Exchange

> **Candidate prose.** Subject to manuscript integration and blind-reader review.

**3253 — Calyx Integration Laboratory, Harrowglass Route Sphere**

Sol had asked Mara to give him crown.

The request looked less intelligent once she did.

Whole-body perception struck without sequence. Five component masses, the laboratory dock, the pull of the route outside, three hundred attachment tolerances, Mara's accumulated fear of the lower-left coupling, Ysra's fatigue, Eda's impatience, Tern's disputed sensor edges. None arrived labelled as history or decision. It was simply present and required an answer.

Sol stopped breathing.

“Crown transfer confirmed,” the sponsor technician said.

Mara's voice came from very far away. “Sol, return condition.”

They had not named one.

The omission became the only thing he could understand.

Calyx drifted six centimetres off laboratory centre. Automatic correction requested crown authority. Sol saw seven valid responses and could not tell which belonged to propulsion, which to Mara, and which to the office now occupying him.

“Take it back,” he said.

Mara opened return.

He could not find it.

Ysra killed transformation at the anatomy layer. Eda held the route field flat. Tern began counting the drift in a voice stripped of interpretation.

The lower-left coupling passed its tolerance.

Pain entered crown from Mara's body, though she no longer held the office. Sol recoiled from it. Calyx corrected in the opposite direction. The coupling tore.

The shared body collapsed into five alarms.

Mara received crown after the damage had already happened.

She stabilized the components. Sol vomited into his helmet.

The sponsor technician said, “Transfer achieved for eleven point four seconds.”

Ysra disconnected the public channel.

For a week she repaired both of them.

Mara's overload expressed itself as tremor, missing intervals, and the conviction that every unattended console contained an unfinished decision. Sol's injury was less elegant. He froze whenever a system offered more than one valid control path.

Ysra put them in adjacent recovery bays because she had only one monitoring board and no interest in walking farther to hear them lie.

On the third day, Sol woke to the sound of Mara pouring dock tea.

“That is medically hostile,” Ysra said from behind the curtain.

“It's mostly water.”

“So is coolant.”

Mara passed Sol a cup anyway. Her hand shook against the rim.

“I gave you all of it,” she said.

He looked at the tea. “I noticed.”

“I thought withholding anything would make the transfer false.”

“It made the transfer meaningless.”

The words hurt her. He let them. Then he moved his cup onto the shared tray so she would not have to reach as far to refill it.

On the seventh day they returned to the laboratory.

The sponsor had renamed the failed exchange a full-authority endurance trial and requested another.

Mara declined. Eda proposed something smaller.

“Route commitment only,” she said. “One turn. Mara keeps balance. Sol keeps propulsion. Tern supplies the sensor grammar. Ysra clears the attachments. I decide which opening we take and return at completion.”

Tern projected the terms where everybody could see them.

INPUT: three route options.

OFFICE: commitment among named options.

INTERVAL: one rescue turn.

RETURN: target clear or sensor grammar exceeded.

LOCAL AUTHORITY: unchanged.

Sol read the last line twice.

The target was an empty cargo shell tumbling through a manufactured debris field. Mara opened crown and asked each component for clearance.

When Eda's turn came, she said, “I accept route commitment under the displayed terms.”

The office entered her without Mara's balance history or Sol's propulsion reflexes. Three route options became available. Eda chose the narrow upper opening.

Calyx moved.

Sol felt her commitment as a boundary around his propulsion choices, not a hand on them. Tern's sensor grammar updated the opening. Ysra cleared the attachment loads. Mara held the whole without deciding the route.

They caught the cargo shell.

For four seconds the laboratory contained nothing but the sound of five people discovering a machine had done exactly what they asked.

Then a second target bloomed on sensors.

The sponsor had inserted it without notice.

“Continue,” the technician said. “Demonstrate persistent alternate command.”

Eda kept the office.

“First interval complete,” Tern said.

“The route remains valid.”

“The sensor grammar doesn't.”

The second target crossed behind a field distortion Tern had not included among the inputs. Eda selected an opening anyway. Sol felt commitment narrow around a propulsion choice whose debris load he could not read.

“Return,” Tern said.

Eda hesitated.

Not long. Long enough.

Mara did not seize the office. She held balance and waited.

Eda returned route commitment.

Sol killed propulsion. The second target passed beyond recovery. No coupling tore.

The sponsor technician opened the public channel. “Promising. Vask sustained secondary authority for—”

“No,” Eda said.

She was breathing hard.

Tern saved the interval clock beside her refusal.

Mara released crown. The components settled into their local bodies.

On the maintenance board, an unfamiliar timing caught the residual sway half a beat before the automatic dampers.

Nobody had commanded it.

Ysra placed a marker on the trace.

The sponsor report arrived before they left the lab. It praised Calyx's successful development of backup leadership.

Sol read the phrase, then added the failed whole-transfer telemetry to the same record.
