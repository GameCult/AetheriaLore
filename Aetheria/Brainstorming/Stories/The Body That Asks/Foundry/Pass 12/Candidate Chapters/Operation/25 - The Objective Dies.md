# Chapter 25 — The Objective Dies

> **Noncanonical Pass 12 candidate chapter.** Two bounded close-third sections: Omi, Lio.

The plant was sold through verbs.

Omi watched them enter the authority log while his Figure held a cracked pressure trunk against Palisade's rotating hull.

Halcyon Provisioning **exercised** an existing option.

Escrow **released** consideration.

Contracts, process licenses, clinic certifications, distribution share, and insurance rights **transferred** into a subsidiary incorporated clean enough to have no history at all.

Palisade did not transfer.

The cylinder remained around Omi: workers inside damaged stations, contamination inside insulation, charges around the axial spine, blood lifting in small spheres from a contractor's suit. Serein retained the plant shell, labor claims, exposure liability, disappearance cases, and whatever deaths happened before closure.

“Bright Ledger control,” Omi said. “Confirm current mission owner.”

“Compact medical-continuity authority remains in force. Restore certified supply and secure the sterility ledger.”

“For which plant owner?”

“Title transition does not alter emergency objectives.”

The answer was technically precise. Omi added it to his authority log.

Halcyon's first operational notice scheduled Palisade for closure after one short continuity run. Its other plants could supply the contracts more cheaply. What it needed from this one was a clean ledger, priced contamination exposure, and enough certified output to prove continuity across the transfer.

Serein still needed the occupation classified as the cause of loss.

The insurer still needed restored dispatch before it closed its file.

The Compact still needed medicine.

Jas still controlled the charges.

Tala still controlled the line signatures.

No verb removed another owner's reason to continue.

The executive dock breached while Omi read the transfer.

Vara's Plastics entered between two shaped charges rather than wait for Jas to choose whether frightened contractors could leave. The first charge collapsed into a containment field. The second fired through an occupied service gallery before the field closed.

Three worker tags went dark.

Vara isolated the remaining trigger bus before it could reach the volatile tanks. Two security contractors died beside the store they had been trying to secure, one from the charge front and one when a ruptured feed line crossed his suit. Seven other bodies entered Anik's exposure queue in the next minute.

The executive route opened.

It was the same clean sunward vector through which any later recovery asset would approach. Nobody at Palisade knew to fear its usefulness yet.

Vara reported the deaths, the isolated stores, and her remaining strength without changing her voice. “Spine access available when Jas releases or we cut the final barrier.”

Jas answered on the worker channel. “Cut it and the executive archive burns.”

“Then release it.”

“Not into Serein custody.”

“Serein no longer owns the contracts.”

“The guns didn't receive that notice.”

Omi kept both statements in the log. The plant had changed owners without changing hands.

At the medical corridor, Anik opened atmosphere to the newly exposed and moved the first treatment group behind Lifer armor. His doctrine made no distinction between worker and contractor lungs. It also required him to keep the export route clear, which meant moving barricades the occupation had used as leverage.

Tala objected.

“If you open all three gates, corporate custody can take the lots.”

“If I leave gate two closed, four exposure cases cannot reach pressure.”

“Open two. Keep one and three under our signatures.”

Anik checked the geometry and did exactly that.

The evacuation route widened. The occupation's control narrowed. Ten more people entered treatment, bringing the exposure queue to seventeen.

Necessary action kept making the next custody action easier.

Omi's cracked trunk shifted under him. Propulsion fouling spread across the surface he had reserved for separation. He could keep the station pressurized or preserve a clean escape burn.

He kept the station pressurized.

The mission objective had become commercially obsolete. The physical emergency worsened anyway.

***

The three curing lots spoke to Lio as temperatures.

One ran warm along her damaged side. One pulsed through the maintenance ring at the interval of a frightened breath. The third had lost automated circulation and survived because two line operators were taking turns inhabiting its pump.

Eighteen sealed lots waited beyond them under union countersignature.

Twenty-one total. Three named clinics. Seven days of reserve made countable.

Halcyon's continuity order arrived through field command.

**PRESERVE CERTIFIED LOTS. RESTORE CASTING. INITIATE ONE FRESH CONTINUITY RUN.**

Tala rejected the final sentence.

“The eighteen sealed lots leave,” she said. “The three already curing finish if process conditions hold. No fresh feed enters casting.”

Field command replied, “The buyer requires a witnessed run under restored control.”

“Then the buyer requires our work after buying our closure.”

Lio held drive balance against the curing vessels while their argument crossed her body as load. Anik needed the lots moving. Vara needed sterile circulation preserved so volatile isolation did not push contamination anti-sunward. Neither requirement authorized new production.

Senn asked the household separately.

“Can we preserve the existing twenty-one without starting a fresh run?”

Tem supplied two process futures. In one, they slowed dispatch and kept all three curing lots within certification. In the other, they opened the export trunk now, lost one curing lot to temperature excursion, and gained evacuation margin.

Lio felt both through the plant.

“First,” she said. “I can hold circulation, but it spends the drive surface Vara calibrated. After this I do not have the same balance margin.”

“Known cost,” Senn said. “Local acceptance?”

The answers came separately. AFTER asked whether extension beyond the five Figures was required.

“Not yet,” Senn said. “Plant controllers remain local.”

Tala authorized the existing run under twenty-one named lot identifiers and no others.

Anik countersigned corridor continuity for those identifiers. He did not countersign casting.

Vara isolated a contaminated circulation branch with a precision cut that saved the three curing vessels from volatile backflow. The cut also removed the fastest atmosphere path to an executive service gallery. A trapped contractor began losing pressure on the far side.

“I can reopen for ninety seconds,” Vara said. “It risks the lots.”

Anik looked at his patient queue and the process clock. “Do it. I will carry the exchange.”

The Lifers stretched their medical corridor through the lost branch. Lio took the circulation load Vara released and held the curing vessels inside tolerance while the contractor crossed.

Her augmentation warning rose from amber to red.

Iri asked, “Do you want the load named publicly?”

“Team channel only.”

“Do you want relief?”

“When the contractor clears.”

Iri waited until then and asked again.

“Yes,” Lio said.

Senn moved balance away from her without converting the answer into removal from the task.

The contractor reached Anik alive. The curing lots remained within process tolerance by margins thin enough to become future litigation.

Field command repeated the fresh-casting order.

Senn transmitted the household's bounded action instead: preserve and dispatch the eighteen sealed lots; finish and certify the three already curing; quarantine uncured feedstock; begin no new lot.

“That is deviation from restored-continuity procedure,” command said.

“Record it,” Senn answered.

Somewhere outside the channels they could inspect, a mission process did.

**RECEIPT: CERTIFIED GEOMETRY DEVIATION.**

The first contingency condition became true without anybody deciding what all three conditions would mean together.

Inside Palisade, the twenty-one authorized lots still had to cross a plant whose clean routes were disappearing.

Jas's final barrier remained armed. Tala's collars remained conditional. Omi's separation surface was fouled. Tem's radiators were hot. Lio's drive tolerance had collapsed to the margin she once hid from her team.

Halcyon owned the future output.

There would be no future output here.

The line they had chosen to save was already ending.
