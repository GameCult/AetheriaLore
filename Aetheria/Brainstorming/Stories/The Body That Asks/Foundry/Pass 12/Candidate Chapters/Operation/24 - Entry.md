# Chapter 24 — Entry

> **Noncanonical Pass 12 candidate chapter.** Multiple bounded close-third sections: Senn, Iri, Tem.

“Collar eight may open for rescue and twenty-one named clinic lots,” she said. “Not production. Not surrender. Not sale. Not archive custody. Not a future use because you were useful once.”

**HELD**

Tala's station appeared through the collar as pressure against Iri's ribs. Membrane web tension pulled along one side. Sterile-flow valves opened and closed like a second breath. Three workers occupied the pressurized segment: Tala at the somatic rig, an exposure technician binding a burn on his own wrist, and a young operator holding a manual stop because the powered one had been shot away.

The line was still running.

“Why?” Iri asked.

Tala nodded toward eighteen sealed lots stacked in the export queue and three curing vessels whose process clocks could not be paused without ruining them.

“Because hospitals don't become less real when owners do,” she said. “Those twenty-one leave under our signatures. Nothing fresh enters casting.”

Anik's voice crossed from the medical corridor. “I have receiver confirmation from three clinics. I can hold sterile exchange if the export gates open in lot order.”

“You can hold the corridor,” Tala said. “You do not assign the lots.”

“Agreed.”

The exchange made Iri trust both of them less simply than the mission brief had requested.

A pressure fault struck collar nine. One of Senn's other components reported a worker station losing atmosphere through a cracked inspection trunk. Iri started to turn toward it.

Tala locked her collar.

The stop entered Iri's Figure as a local absence. No command crossed it. The membrane station remained present without becoming available.

“My line stays here,” Tala said. “Send rescue around us.”

Iri passed the condition to Senn. Senn routed Omi's component to the pressure fault without asking Tala to yield. Their radial approach became slower and physically discontinuous.

Field command marked the path inefficient.

At minute forty-five, the corporate continuity office requested its first custody-and-dispatch attestation.

Vara had reached the executive dock. She had not reached the spine. Jas Orra's charges made the clean axial route a chamber nobody could enter twice.

No authorized office could sign restored custody.

The first attestation expired.

Tem received three accounts of the contamination and believed none of them enough to discard the others.

The corporate process feed showed sterility loss beginning eleven minutes after the occupation seized the line. Worker instruments showed exposure excursions over six prior shifts. Palisade's damaged sensing showed both events as one spreading thermal smear because two buses had been physically bridged after executive telemetry failed.

“Which sensor is lying?” field command asked.

“Possibly none,” Tem said.

“Nominate an operational account.”

“Corporate feed for current automated valve state. Worker instruments for historical exposure. Plant sensing for present heat where it still has continuity.”

“We require one contamination boundary.”

“Then you require a worse plant.”

He opened his component's active apertures.

The radial ring resolved in expensive detail. Heat climbed through his radiative skin as each pulse returned from the cylinder. He could see where volatile vapor had entered insulation, where occupation barricades interrupted circulation, where a corporate repair had placed a clean sensor downstream of a dirty seal.

The active picture cost him six percent of the radiator margin reserved for escape.

Jas answered from the axial archive station.

“I will release a safety index,” they said. “Lot signatures, exposure times, sensor replacement dates, current charge exclusions. No names. No organizer files. No archive keys.”

Field command demanded the full sterility ledger.

“What do you need?” they asked.

“Signatures sufficient to compare the three sensor histories.”

“And who receives your comparison?”

“My local record first. Mission action receives the bounded result. Publication is a separate decision.”

Jas released the index.

Tem joined it without merging it. The first worker exposure signatures predated the occupation by nineteen days. Corporate maintenance had replaced two sensors after the initial excursions and before the terrorism filing. The plant's current contamination still worsened after workers armed the line and cut executive circulation.

All three facts remained.

“Contamination predates occupation,” Tem told the household. “The present spread does not.”

Omi asked, “Does the index show why Serein filed when it did?”

“It shows what the filing made administratively possible. Not motive.”

“Keep the distinction.”

Tem did.

The second continuity request arrived at minute ninety.

Vara's feed showed the executive dock partially secure and the spine still denied. The radial ring showed five separate components holding rescue routes that did not add up to corporate custody.

Tem could have supplied process continuity. He could not supply dispatch control.

The second attestation expired.

An insurer process with no body at Palisade compared two absences and issued a signed fact.
