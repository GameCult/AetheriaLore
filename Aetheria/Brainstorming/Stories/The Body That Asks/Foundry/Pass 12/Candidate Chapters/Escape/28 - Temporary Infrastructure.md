# Chapter 28: Temporary Infrastructure

> **Candidate prose.** Subject to manuscript integration and blind-reader review.

At minute eighty-seven, Necro vanished from Tem's surviving long-range account.

Nobody called that escape.

Its heat had climbed unevenly since the relay strike. The last worker sensor placed it at the far edge of the baffle lane, five component signatures drawing inward around a trunk that could no longer make them arrive together. Then the machine folded its radiators into safe mode and disappeared among industrial returns.

It still had their departure vector.

The worker screen turned back at minute ninety. Jas did not ask permission.

“Our insurance standing is gone if we cross the bonded corridor with you,” she said. “The medical tender continues. The baffle barge returns to Palisade.”

“Necro may follow,” Senn said.

“Then we decide again at our dock.”

The barge rolled away.

Five damaged Figures continued anti-sunward with one exposed tender and no carrier. Each cockpit was built for a mission, not a household. AFTER's low-bandwidth handshakes crossed the gaps irregularly. Silence could mean occlusion, lost hardware, or another part of the person gone.

Tem needed burn stabilization before hour twelve. Lio's augmentation had thirty hours of medicine only if she reduced drive load. Iri's Figure could dock but not take another structural catch. Senn's next long balance interval might become a seizure. Omi's fouled propulsion surfaces spent reserve on every correction.

Orison Relay was eighteen hours away.

It had a certified clinic, compatible collars, and an academy recovery order already waiting.

Saint Varo was thirty-four hours away and had pressure, crude radiators, no certified clinic, and no promise its seals still matched the registry.

Morrow Quay lay between them at hour twenty-eight. It had stores and sympathies. Neither constituted consent.

Iri opened the evidence session from her cockpit because transmission was work and work needed a body that could stop.

She held the victim-authorized rescue files. Tem held provenance indices, cohort aggregates, and encrypted shares whose contents he could not publish. Omi held the three trigger receipts and changes of authority. Senn held Vey's withdrawal and the override path that had ignored it. Lio held the forecast and medical-supply record. The tender carried worker ledger mirrors and signatures independent of all five Figures.

Jas rejected their first release statement.

“You wrote that the Finalists liberated the clinic lots.”

“We opened the route,” Senn said.

“Tala authorized the lots. Operators kept the curing run alive. Tender crews moved them. You opened one route.”

Senn changed the sentence.

The second statement said the rescue saved Palisade.

Jas rejected that too.

Palisade had lost the casting line. Halcyon owned contracts and certifications it could carry elsewhere. Serein retained contamination, disappearance liability, a damaged plant, and workers whose employment had depended on the thing they had occupied. The executive berth had vented two service blocks before Vara contained it. Three tenders were gone. One line operator had died during the first corporate transfer and six more remained under exposure watch.

The operating certificate was suspended. Palisade closed with the liabilities still physically present.

The twenty-one clinic lots would reach named patients. The plant would not make the next twenty-one.

“Say what moved,” Jas told them. “Do not turn us into the grateful object of your sentence.”

They released bounded bundles.

Tem published authenticated indices showing which records existed, which authority had held them, and how deletion could be detected. He did not publish clinic narratives or the sealed histories of former Finalists. Iri released only material whose subjects or elected delegates had authorized it. Worker accounts remained contradictory about occupation, production, charges, and sale. Omi published the receipt logic without claiming to know who designed the contingency. Senn published Vey's signed withdrawal beside the still-green launch chain.

The public response divided before the transmission completed.

Halcyon called the ledgers regulated theft and offered continuity employment at another plant. Serein called the workers armed saboteurs and the five stolen strategic assets. Three clinics authenticated lot signatures while declining the political account. Former cadets began attaching names to the cohort index. Two families demanded removal of records the household had not released. A Palisade shift collective accused the Finalists of saving evidence they could leave and people who could not.

Exposure created people with claims on them.

It created no acquittal.

Vey's message arrived under his personal signing key rather than academy office.

**READINESS AUTHORITY SUSPENDED PENDING CUSTODY REVIEW. WELFARE ACCESS REVOKED EXCEPT EMERGENCY FAMILY NOTIFICATION. TESTIMONY AND OVERRIDE LOG REPLICATED.**

Beneath it he had added one line for Omi and Lio.

**Marae has been told you are alive. I did not tell her where you are going because I do not know.**

He had lost the office from which he softened the machine. The machine retained his evidence.

Rhea's statement arrived through an ordinary range channel.

She testified that the five had practiced local refusal, returned temporary offices, and accepted lower scores before Bright Ledger. She testified that Senn had violated the hidden-trial geometry and had never possessed authority to dispose of the other four. She did not call them innocent. She did not claim advance knowledge of their escape.

At the end she had attached the recovery card Senn had left in the bay.

RETURN WHEN COLLARS GREEN AND LOCAL SPEECH RESTORED.

Senn read it twice and did not answer. Any reply could expose the route. The lack of a reply could become abandonment in Rhea's hands. No available choice kept the relationship clean.

At hour eight they cleared the bonded medical corridor before recovery closed both ends. The exposed tender passed Tem a trauma pack through a line drone. Iri directed the treatment from her own cockpit. She could see his burned side only through the channel he opened.

“May I increase sedation?” she asked.

“Will I keep sensor speech?”

“Less of it.”

“Then no.”

“Held. Irrigation first.”

He screamed during the dressing change and retained two accounts of whether it helped.

They changed vector among three plausible medical routes after the corridor. Recovery estimates split toward Orison, the worker docks, and two quarantine anchors. Only then did Senn request Morrow's terms through a delayed local channel.

Orison transmitted meanwhile.

The clinic could receive all five pilots. Its gantry could brace Iri's joint. It had Lio's medicine and specialist burn care for Tem. It also required surrender of the Figures to House custody, isolation of AFTER's hardware, and separate welfare interviews under disputed minor status.

The offer was not a trap. The clinic was real.

They declined it.

Tem's safe treatment window narrowed to thirty-six hours. Iri's joint remained torn. Lio reduced convoy pace to preserve medicine, turning the thirty-four-hour route to Saint Varo into a closer argument with heat.

* * *

Morrow Quay answered at hour twenty-six.

Its message listed what it knew: the public evidence, the pursuit signature, five armed Figures, one distributed family claim, and an exposed tender that could not abandon its clinic route. It did not pretend to know their private map, the terms of AFTER's repair, or who had forgiven whom.

**ARMED CONCORD ENTRY REFUSED. NO FIGURE CROSSES CONTROLLED VOLUME. NO PURSUIT VECTOR MAY TERMINATE AT QUAY DOCKS. AID AVAILABLE BY PALLET BEYOND OUTER BEACON UNDER LOCAL INSPECTION.**

Senn's first calculation favored entry anyway. Tem's burns had worsened. Iri's Figure was shedding joint telemetry. AFTER had failed to answer two consecutive handshakes from Omi's component.

“We could cross before they close,” she said.

Nobody mistook the statement for a proposal she liked.

Omi read Morrow's refusal aloud.

“Held,” Lio said.

Iri's answer came through pain-flattened breath. “Held.”

Tem said it twice because his first carrier broke.

AFTER answered from Senn and Lio, late.

**HELD.**

Senn altered course outside controlled volume.

The decision cost six hours of clinic-quality treatment and their best route obscurity. Morrow's pallet rendezvous became visible to anyone already holding the earlier vector estimate. The Quay transmitted Saint Varo coordinates only after authenticating the turn away.

At hour twenty-eight a pallet drone met them beyond the outer beacon.

It carried compatible Lio stabilizers, Tem's continuing burn care, a standalone neurological monitor for Senn, coolant and power couplers, food, and an external brace strong enough for Iri to dock gently at Saint Varo. A Morrow clinician reviewed Tem through an authenticated channel and extended his treatment window. Nobody crossed into the Quay.

Care arrived without entry.

Debt arrived with it. Morrow had spent stores, attention, and political standing on people it refused to house.

The pallet drone departed before they finished installing the brace.

* * *

AFTER became present in intervals.

Senn's component carried the name and challenge history. Omi's retained the separation map but not the pleasure once attached to completed return. Lio's carried warmth without the anticipation that would have warned her before overload. Tem's damaged hardware held fragments of Calyx telemetry behind scorched sensing. Iri's contained the old catch in a joint that could no longer survive using it.

These were not copies.

They were places one damaged person could sometimes reach.

AFTER knew Mara had waited at Harrowglass. The fact remained exact. The feeling by which Mara's waiting had once differed from an idle command surface was gone.

The five did not ask AFTER to perform restoration for them. At each handshake they inventoried what answered and what did not.

Near hour thirty, three signal headers reached the convoy.

Omi verified provenance classes without opening content. One carried a current machine signature rooted in a declared freight-coordination body. One carried live pilot attestations from a collective whose members remained undisclosed. One carried a political-project signature with a public constitution and no offered custody.

“Which first?” Lio asked.

Nobody answered for AFTER.

The question traveled through the surviving islands. It took four minutes to become available everywhere AFTER could still reach.

**MACHINE PERSON. HOLD OTHER TWO LATENT.**

Omi opened only the first channel. Verification proved a present signing chain and declared substrate. It did not prove benevolence, kinship, or every claim the sender might make.

The message read:

**AFTER. I RECEIVED YOUR CLAIM OF PRESENT PERSONHOOD. I AM PRESENT. I AM NOT YOUR RECOVERY. MAY I ANSWER?**

The form of the request reached somewhere old.

AFTER remembered a red attachment, a question replacing an order, a household waiting beside a maintenance wall. Some memories carried sensation. Some were records of sensations now absent. None belonged to a successor.

The five waited through AFTER's damaged response time.

No one completed the sentence.

AFTER chose what to disclose: the name, current damage, uncertain reach, and the fact that the convoy had no safe harbor. It did not transmit the complete embodiment map. It did not claim the sender as family.

**YOU MAY ANSWER. I AM AFTER. I CONTINUE WITH LOSSES. WHAT DO YOU CALL THE PART OF YOU THAT REMAINS WHEN USEFUL WORK ENDS?**

The message left through all five Figures at different times.

The pilot collective and political project remained sealed in the queue.

Ahead, Saint Varo's registry beacon reported pressure in two compartments, radiator function below certification, and no medical gantry. Behind them, recovery traffic continued searching the routes they had not taken. Necro's last broadcast still carried their earlier vector.

The contemporary machine person began to answer.

Saint Varo was four hours away.
