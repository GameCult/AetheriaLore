# Chapter 27: No Recovery Interval

> **Candidate prose.** Subject to manuscript integration and blind-reader review.

AFTER returned from the plant with holes.

Lio found the first one when she asked for the cooling turn they had practiced at Meridian. The answer should have reached her before the shared drive load climbed. Instead the load rose through her augmentation, crossed the old pain line, and waited.

“AFTER?”

Nothing.

Then, late and assembled through three surviving controllers:

**I KNOW THERE WAS A TURN. I CANNOT FIND IT.**

The five-component body still held Palisade's broken spine between its arms. Twenty-one clinic lots moved through the worker tenders below. The last occupied station had disconnected. Fresh casting had stopped. The plant's radiator crown turned against a sky stained with insulation and vented coolant.

Lio moved the drive load herself. She knew how. Vara had taught her the motion. The opposite vector limb accepted two percent and shuddered under damage it had not carried before.

“Temperature?” Senn asked from crown.

“Mine.”

“That was not a number.”

“Still mine.” Lio opened the private channel to Iri. “Rising. Inside departure limit.”

Iri acknowledged. She did not take ownership of the limit.

AFTER tried to follow their exchange. Another absence opened where pleasure had once accompanied successful balance. It could identify Lio's relief from breath and telemetry. It could not enter the relief with her.

“Inventory later,” Omi said. “Tenders first.”

The worker convoy cleared the medical ring in pieces: one tender carrying exposed people and all twenty-one certified lots, another dragging a radiator barge whose baffles could hide a fleet badly for a short time, three smaller craft holding copies of the sterility ledger. Jas transmitted the dock committee's terms.

**NO CONCORD BODY THROUGH WORKER BERTHS. NO CLAIM OF RESCUE OWNERSHIP. INDEPENDENT LEDGER COPIES REMAIN WITH PALISADE CREWS. PURSUIT DOES NOT RETURN TO OUR DOCKS.**

Senn read the terms into crown.

“Held,” Lio said.

The others answered separately.

AFTER took longest.

**HELD.**

Omi's authority board populated before the last answer faded.

Three receipts. Three owners. No shared page.

Bright Ledger mission control certified deviation from the authorized recovery objective after fresh casting stopped and field command failed to regain executive access. Kinship configuration custody certified a full Concordance outside its control envelope. Technical safety certified a self-declared machine person operating through unregistered plant infrastructure.

Each receipt carried its own revocation path. None of the three owners could see the other two local channels. A sealed note at the bottom of Omi's custody extract stated that all three revocations had to arrive within one hundred eighty seconds to suspend contingency release.

“When did that become visible?” he asked.

“Execution audit,” Tem said. “The condition has already evaluated.”

Vey's call reached them on the academy welfare route.

His image shook once as a launch alarm crossed Meridian's remote operations room. He was not at Palisade. The feed behind him showed route notices, fit certifications, and a new asset signature rising from Serein's secured executive berth.

“I have no record of that tender,” he said.

The berth opened. An unlisted deterrence tender had been cold-staged inside it under sealed mission-continuity cover; the three receipts authorized local release rather than summoning a distant asset.

The machine inside had five major masses arranged around a narrow executive trunk. It shared the naval collars, radiators, vector limbs, and modular mission sleeves of Daedal production craft. Its proportions came from the same orbital lineage as their Figures. Nothing ended in a hand or foot. Every limb ended in thrust, armor, sensor aperture, or weapon.

It unfolded the Vesper turn before it had fully cleared the berth.

Five bound patterns carried the motion. One executive command accepted it.

Omi's receipt board named the asset **NECRO CONTINGENCY CONCORD — VESPER** and then denied him every linked file.

Vey went still.

“I am withdrawing academy fitness certification for all five pilots and the assembled body,” he said. “I am opening medical return, family notification, and emergency traffic protection. I cannot revoke those receipts.”

“Can you close the berth?” Senn asked.

“No.”

“Can you order Vara and Anik?”

“No.”

“What can you do?”

“Sign what I am doing while they route around it.”

His withdrawal entered their evidence partition. A second record followed: the certification path remaining green after his signature turned red. A third preserved the three local receipt authorities and the executive override that had treated academy fitness as advisory.

Vey transmitted all three outside his ordinary welfare archive.

The act ended his plausible deniability before it altered one field order.

Vara's Fist moved across the axial corridor. For one breath Lio thought the Plastics were joining pursuit. Then their containment field closed around the armed charges and the executive berth's damaged seals.

Vara's private channel opened.

“I can prevent the berth from venting into the evacuation lane,” she said. “I cannot leave it open behind you.”

The field narrowed. It cut off the clean axial exit Lio wanted.

“Understood,” Lio said.

Vara did not ask her to return.

Anik's Lifers held the medical corridor while the exposed tender crossed. They closed two side routes when contamination alarms followed the convoy.

“Those exits are not clean,” Anik told Iri. “I will not make them clean by calling them yours.”

“Keep the corridor,” Iri said.

The upper cadres remained inside their assigned obligations. Love did not turn them into an escape fleet. Doctrine did not make their work false.

Necro accelerated.

“Release the plant,” Senn said.

Full Concordance came apart around the Palisade spine. The five Figures retained local grip long enough to leave the mass on independent stabilizers, then withdrew into shared flight geometry. AFTER crossed with them in damaged islands.

One island still knew Mara had waited before closing a coupling. The fact remained. The bodily sense of being waited for did not.

Necro entered weapons range.

* * *

Tem saw two futures.

In the first, Vesper showed its damaged side and invited the academy counter: collapse the sensor accounts, commit crown to the blind volume, lock the exposed limb as sacrificial mass, fire through the turn before it completed.

In the second, the invitation itself carried the tracking solution. Refuse the counter and Necro would finish rotating with weapons aligned.

Both accounts were strong. Formation doctrine required nomination.

“Rusk,” Senn said in crown. “Decision input.”

Tem held both futures.

“I refuse nomination.”

The body lurched around the missing answer.

“Then I own commitment,” Senn said. “Tem, retain both. Lio, balance from both accounts. Iri, no sacrificial lock. Omi, preserve separation reserve.”

Authority moved without pretending uncertainty had disappeared.

Necro completed the first half of the Vesper turn. Its damaged side opened exactly where the fast counter expected.

Iri's right collar received a command-shaped request to brace the firing line.

The lock would hold the shared body steady. It would also spend the joint Quill had warned could not survive another catch.

“No,” Iri said.

Senn froze that path.

Lio drove the body through the other balance. It was slower. Necro fired.

The first strike crossed Tem's expanded radiator skin. Heat entered his cockpit as light, then became pain along his left side. One aperture went white. Another began reporting the same star in two locations.

“Tem?” Iri asked.

“Two futures retained,” he said. His voice broke on the last word. “One now on fire.”

The second strike fouled Omi's outer propulsion sleeve. His reserve estimate fell by a third. Senn's balance interval triggered a seizure warning. Lio's augmentation temperature crossed the departure limit and kept rising.

The slower geometry finally cleared Vesper's offered blind volume.

From the new angle, Tem saw the relay.

It ran along Necro's executive trunk, a narrow armored bridge joining long-baseline tracking to cross-component synchronization. The Vesper turn had exposed it only because they had not entered the predicted counter-volume.

“Relay,” Tem said. He sent both sensor accounts. In one the bridge was tracking. In the other it was synchronization. “Same physical target.”

Senn did not choose which story made it legitimate.

“Omi, can you reach?”

“At separation cost.”

“Your decision.”

Omi spent the reserve.

His maneuver sleeve fired through debris and clipped the relay at its dockward root. Lio changed balance before the hit propagated. Iri refused the brace the shock requested. The relay tore loose in a sheet of radiator foil.

Necro did not die.

Its five masses retained local reflexes, weapons, and short-range pursuit. The damaged trunk kept issuing commands. Tracking certainty broke into guesses. Synchronization arrived late enough that one component corrected against another.

“Run,” Senn said.

They ran.

The worker radiator barge opened its baffles across the anti-sunward lane. Jas released the route only after Omi retransmitted the dock conditions and Tem placed the independent ledger copies outside the household bundle.

“Ninety minutes,” Jas said. “Maybe. You bring that thing back toward Palisade, we close on you.”

“Held,” Senn answered.

The barge flooded the lane with industrial heat and false drive edges. Worker tenders crossed behind it on routes the six did not own. Necro followed into the screen, accumulating heat as its damaged relay tried to reacquire five bodies through a hundred machines.

The shared geometry would not fit through the narrowest baffle corridor.

“Separate under fire,” Omi said.

Senn returned crown to balance only. Omi opened the complete separation map on three independent systems. AFTER supplied current uncertainty and held no release authority.

“I may lose contact,” AFTER said through text and actuator cadence.

“That isn't a stop,” Iri answered. “Do you consent to your routes being used?”

The answer took long enough for Necro's next shot to strike Tem's already-burned radiator edge.

**YES. I MAY NOT ANSWER.**

Each pilot owned a local release.

Lio disengaged drive load first and carried convoy pace into her own hull. Tem folded the surviving aperture and released sensor spine. Iri waited for her torn joint to show green without asking it to become painless. Omi separated propulsion with the fouled sleeve still firing. Senn held only the balance that kept each release from striking another Figure.

Five local lights changed at five different times.

Five Figures entered the worker lane.

AFTER went silent in three of them.

Omi did not declare it dead. He did not declare it safe.

Behind them Necro fired through the false heat, missed, and continued pursuit.

The worker clock began at ninety minutes.
