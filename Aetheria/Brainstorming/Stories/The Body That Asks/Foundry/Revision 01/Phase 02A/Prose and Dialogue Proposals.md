# Prose and Dialogue Proposals

## Proposal Authority

These proposals lower the accepted Phase 02 calendar, academic life, mystery half-lives, and protected material into scene-level prose and dialogue work. They do not replace the calendar, decide chapter boundaries, or authorize whole-chapter drafting.

The baseline voice remains the foundation: dry wit, exact bodies, administrative language made material, and emotion carried by bounded action. Revision should vary that voice across a school year rather than replacing it.

## Governing Prose Rules

1. Full procedural language appears when a form is first built, altered, violated, or contested by a new constituency. Familiar use contracts into shorthand and silent competence.
2. Each scene receives one dominant verbal register. Background mysteries may pressure diction but do not take over every conversation.
3. Characters may understand urgent safety facts quickly. They do not produce finished emotional or political interpretations on demand.
4. Institutional language remains attached to a body, object, schedule, grade, route, meal, medicine, or room. Abstract explanation alone does not carry a scene.
5. No two adjacent scene endings use the same rhetorical device.
6. Protected lines receive clear space. Do not surround them with rival aphorisms or explanatory paraphrase.
7. Palisade is quarantined until Bright Ledger assignment. No school-year scene names or hints at Palisade, Serein, Halcyon, Tala, Jas, the strike, the plant geometry, twenty-one lots, fresh casting, or the specific political conflict. Curriculum remains broad and non-prophetic.

## Adolescent Voice Lock

The five are technically elite teenagers. They may speak with frightening precision about trained domains: load, sensor confidence, route geometry, component fit, pain location, evidence handling, and the exact rule on a form. They do not share mature language for attachment, trauma, authority, consent, or institutional reproduction. Baseline lines that sound like finished room analysis must be cut, reassigned, delayed, or converted into action.

### What teen precision may do

- name a rising joint load to the degree;
- identify who owns a tool, channel, route, or submitted record;
- quote a rule because the rule is presently hurting them;
- argue tactics and grades with domain vocabulary;
- notice a contradiction without knowing why it matters emotionally;
- invent shorthand after repeated practice.

### What teen precision may not do by default

- diagnose another person's care as a stable authority pathology in the moment;
- explain how fear, gratitude, class, or reporting shaped an entire relationship;
- distinguish trust, belief, forgiveness, repair, and obligation in polished parallel clauses every time;
- summarize the room's distributed institutional authority while emotionally implicated;
- state the novel's constitutional lesson immediately after living it;
- speak like five psychology graduates who all attended the same unusually militant seminar.

### Replacement behaviors

Room-level understanding moves into the gap among:

- bad accusations that contain one true edge;
- jokes used to dodge a frightening answer;
- status games about scores, competence, mentors, bodies, clothes, and who gets watched;
- silence and leaving;
- task language stretched beyond its proper use;
- bodily avoidance, changed seats, locked channels, unfinished food, overtraining, and volunteered work;
- cruelty followed by shame without immediate apology;
- insight arriving weeks late and remaining incomplete;
- adult records that classify too neatly;
- older Calyx language that is more formal but still professionally and personally distinct.

### Concrete baseline cuts and conversions

These examples set direction; later line editing must inspect the whole manuscript for the same fault.

1. Baseline teen synthesis: “Fear explains why it took our choice. It doesn't return it.”

   Convert to Iri's action and narrower speech: “You hid the route.” Iri turns the map so all five copies are visible. “Send it.”

2. Baseline teen synthesis: “Care does not become a lien because you are afraid.”

   Convert to Lio's angry accusation: “You don't get to make us owe you for being scared.” It is less exact, more personal, and may be partly unfair.

3. Baseline teen synthesis: “I isolated you without asking. I called reversibility mine to judge.”

   Senn cannot formulate the completed diagnosis immediately. Use: “I thought I could put it back.” After interruption or silence: “I didn't ask.” The larger authority reading belongs to the reader and later record.

4. Baseline teen synthesis: “An undisclosed term is still a term. I made your body legible by hurting it. Both belong in the record.”

   Omi under shame becomes procedural and evasive: “Log the second checksum. Log my hand. Don't summarize it.” His recognition of what he did matures later.

5. Baseline teen synthesis: “You used my refusal as load advice.”

   Keep for adult Sol, whose professional history earns it. Do not give an equivalent polished formulation to sophomore Omi immediately after injury.

6. Baseline teen synthesis: “You presented absence instead of uncertainty.”

   Tem may say this only as evidence critique after cooling down. In the live confrontation he should be uglier or more personal: “You knew the blank wasn't blank.”

7. Baseline teen synthesis: “I needed to know whether being close to you meant becoming material in a report you thought would save me.”

   Convert Iri/Lio confrontation into partial speech: “You wrote Mika down.” / “Mika was hurting us.” / “Would you write me down?” Let the twenty-three-minute presence answer more than the dialogue can.

8. Baseline room analysis: “The initial restriction was clinically supportable... narrow authorities performing their permitted acts.”

   Keep the separate documents and Vey's bounded answers. Cut Tem's or narration's immediate complete systems summary. The reader sees welfare, clinic, formation, and handling fail to join.

9. Baseline teen synthesis: “Care does not become a lien because you are afraid” and similar balanced maxims around AFTER repair compete with `YOU CUT WHERE I AM`.

   Delete the competing maxims. Let the machine person's simpler accusation own the sequence.

10. Baseline emergency completeness: “I accept collar load for evacuation and rescue, not production custody.”

    Live call: “Rescue load. No casting.” Complete terms exist in the signed trace.

### Adolescent scene rule

For every Meridian scene, the packet must distinguish:

- what the POV can technically identify;
- what the POV wants other teenagers to think;
- what the POV cannot yet admit;
- what action or body behavior reveals the gap;
- what room-level conclusion remains available only to the reader.

No adolescent POV scene may close with a complete diagnosis the character has only just earned the evidence to begin misunderstanding.

## Idiolect Anchors and Replay Tests

- **Anchor:** labels, ownership verbs, missing signatures, boundary-testing jokes. Legal thought comes from ward experience and social vigilance.

## Risks and Countermeasures

### Added school scenes imitate the baseline liturgy

**Risk:** the book gets longer while every new scene still argues procedure.

**Countermeasure:** each added scene packet names its non-doctrinal verbal register, comic owner, ending type, and whether any mystery column advances. If none advances, do not retrofit one.

### Idiolect becomes gimmick

**Risk:** Omi always jokes legally, Tem always offers two answers, Iri always says no, Senn always rehearses, Lio always performs.

**Countermeasure:** every anchor gets at least one reversal. Omi must nominate; Tem must choose badly; Iri must want something petty; Senn must speak desire; Lio must be honest without an audience.

### Seasonal texture becomes decorative montage

**Risk:** food and weather-equivalent details assert time without changing life.

**Countermeasure:** recurring objects change ownership, wear, access, or emotional meaning. Establish recurrence in scenes before compressing it.

### Humor flattens stakes or distributes one wit

**Risk:** everyone sounds like the narrator and horror becomes quip delivery.

**Countermeasure:** assign joke ownership and social audience. Let jokes fail, wound, or become unavailable. Maintenance, ward, peer, sponsor, and AFTER humor use different nouns.

### Protected lines become a greatest-hits parade

**Risk:** relocation preserves wording without earning it.

**Countermeasure:** preserve prerequisites first. If a line cannot survive honestly, flag it for coordinator adjudication rather than forcing it.

### Palisade leaks backward

**Risk:** courses, projects, or industrial examples conveniently rehearse the exact operation.

**Countermeasure:** run a lexical and causal quarantine check across `A0–F2`. Broad rescue and industrial education is allowed; Palisade actors, geometry, title problem, production dispute, and lot logic are not.

### Emergency prose remains too polished

**Risk:** Palisade and Necro read like constitutional minutes under fire.

**Countermeasure:** draft live channels first in fragments, then build separate post-event records. Audit every complete tactical sentence: keep it only if standing, authentication, or a new term requires completeness.
