# Voice and Academic Projection

## Authority and Use

This is a drafting-facing projection for Revision 01. It lowers the accepted [[../Revision Brief|Revision Brief]], [[../Phase 02/Academic Life Ledger|Academic Life Ledger]], [[../Phase 02/POV Pairing Ledger|POV Pairing Ledger]], [[../Phase 02B/Chapter Blueprint|Chapter Blueprint]], [[../Phase 02B/Proposal Adjudication|Proposal Adjudication]], and canonical Persona baselines into voice and school-state constraints. It does not revise the Personas, settle open family provenance, or supply dialogue to paste into chapters.

The five are elite cadets and recognizable teenagers at the same time. Their education gives them exact language where consequence has trained them: a drive coupling, a joint limit, a source trace, a route, a clause, a tool, a score. It does not give them equally exact language for why they hurt one another, what they want from affection, or how an institution reproduces itself through people who care. Drafting must preserve the difference.

Every teen scene therefore carries two ledgers at once:

- **available precision:** the trained or lived domain in which this cadet can be exact;
- **unavailable synthesis:** the emotional, political, or constitutional conclusion the reader can infer but the cadet cannot yet say cleanly.

School is not camouflage around Finalist work. Each cadet has courses they love, work they botch, peers who do not exist to admire them, qualifications with material consequences, and petty distinctions that can matter more to them on a Tuesday than the historical conspiracy. Academic state must persist across chapters: preparation changes scores; injuries change participation; missed meetings alter friendships; access rules reshape later work; a good result remains good even after Meridian is refused.

## Shared Adolescent Voice Ceiling

### What may be articulate

The five may be unusually articulate when they are:

- identifying a load, route, seal, instrument conflict, contractual actor, submission history, pain site, evidence gap, or ownership boundary they have actually been trained to recognize;
- quoting an adult phrase, regulation, slogan, seminar claim, or clinical category, especially when trying it on for status or using it against the adult who supplied it;
- arguing over scores, technique, assignments, equipment, rankings, access, deadlines, or who failed to do agreed work;
- repeating household shorthand earned through many scenes;
- making a narrow admission about an action: “I sent it,” “I knew,” “I wanted that,” “I didn’t ask.”

Technical precision should be allowed to become intimidating. The adolescent register comes from where precision stops, what motive it serves, and how the cadet behaves when the problem crosses that boundary.

### What stays out of teen narration and dialogue

Unless a phrase is visibly borrowed, mocked, or misunderstood, keep the following room-language out of Meridian teen prose:

- attachment style, trauma response, emotional labor, projection, avoidance, relational repair, healthy boundaries, institutional reproduction, distributed authority, moral injury, epistemic care, internalized obligation, care as custody, or consent as constitutional architecture;
- balanced diagnoses such as “I did this because fear made me confuse care with control”;
- polished distinctions among trust, belief, forgiveness, debt, gratitude, repair, and obligation immediately after an injury;
- complete explanations of how clinic, sponsor, school, custody, security, and welfare authorities jointly produce harm;
- aphorisms that sound ready for the novel’s back cover;
- coordinated group conclusions in which five upset teenagers conveniently reach the story room’s analysis at once.

The concepts may remain present in action. A teenager can change seats, stop sharing a map, do somebody’s laundry, refuse a touch, delete a draft message, show up too early, lie about pain, volunteer for an extra shift, or weaponize a score without naming the relationship underneath.

### Emotional syntax by season

- **Autumn:** image management, boasting, teasing, borrowed adult labels, territorial behavior, and overexplaining rules. They think a clean first impression can still control what the household becomes.
- **Midwinter:** interruption, household shorthand, repeated jokes, casual trespass, boredom, and ugly familiarity. They can identify preference before they can admit dependence.
- **Early spring:** accusation, silence, task-only speech, locked channels, bad analogies, and strategic absence. New evidence makes them less verbally clear because it makes every ordinary exchange dangerous.
- **Late spring:** narrow admissions and specific requests appear. Growth sounds like fewer false reasons, not broader theory.
- **Bright Ledger and escape:** speech contracts to practiced calls, names, numbers, local terms, and unfinished truth. Nobody becomes a philosopher because the bullets are educational.

## Senn Arada

### Drafting center

Senn wants to look like the person who already considered the problem. Her internal attention runs ahead of the room: arrival order, visible gaps, who has not answered, what wording will sound unforced, whether a failure can be made to look planned. She converts wanting into arrangements and fear into preparation. Her sophistication is procedural. Her adolescence appears in the vanity underneath it and in how surprised she is when another person experiences her preparation as a decision already made.

### Domain fluency

- route design, recovery coordination, calibration history, sequence planning, component handoff, and the practical conditions for returning an office;
- the social mechanics of a briefing: who has the relevant fact, what must be decided first, what can wait;
- Fist Studio schedules and the public grammar of competent leadership;
- enough maintenance language from first-year work with Iri and Rhea to make her useful, not expert, at a bench.

She may give a crisp route critique or catch a missing return condition before an instructor does. She may not infer from that skill that she understands why Iri withdrew, why Rhea said no, or why the household resents being efficiently helped.

### Emotional blind spots

- She mistakes anticipation for permission.
- She believes naming a return condition can neutralize the pressure created by having already built the plan.
- She cannot easily distinguish being useful, being wanted, and being admired.
- Accurate recognition frightens her more than public failure. Rhea can expose a rehearsed invitation simply by answering the desire under it.
- She treats another person’s unmet need as a task that has fallen to her, then feels abandoned when they reject the solution.

### Distinct inarticulacy

Senn omits herself from the sentence. “We need,” “the route needs,” and “somebody should” conceal “I want,” “I’m scared,” and “please stay.” When challenged, she offers the sequence that produced her choice rather than the feeling that made the sequence attractive. Shame makes her revise logistics in real time. She can eventually say “I didn’t ask.” She cannot immediately append a finished account of why.

Under extreme stress, connective language goes first. She produces task nouns, names, times, and the return condition she is terrified will fail. Technical speech survives seizure pressure longer than social speech.

### Slang, status, humor, and evasion

- Her slang comes from studios, route boards, and rankings: clean, ugly, late, wasted, showing the seam, making the board.
- She notices who is watching and whether something will survive replay.
- Her humor is dry and unintentionally self-exposing. She thinks the rehearsal was invisible; everyone heard version three.
- She evades affection by proposing shared work, a useful walk, a route check, or a place somebody “might as well” accompany her.
- She can be petty about polish, preparation, and somebody getting praised for an improvisation she privately rehearsed.

### Mature language to exclude

Do not let Senn diagnose “care becoming command,” “coordination as identity,” “presumed assent,” or her fear of unnecessaryness in polished language. Those are pair-brief and room truths. In prose, she moves a chair before Iri arrives, assigns an answer Omi has not given, rewrites Tem’s uncertainty into a briefing, or builds Rhea an exit from an invitation Rhea has not yet received.

### Academic state

- **Independent pleasure:** route design and recovery coordination. She loves making a difficult return possible and wants that work even if nobody calls her crown.
- **Strength:** group-shaped route problems, calibration history, recovery sequences, and seeing missing interfaces.
- **Weakness:** individually judged work exposes how often she uses other people’s competence as scaffolding. She overprepares, over-scopes, and may solve the wrong problem elegantly.
- **Recurring obligation:** Fist Studio and the recovery league force her to coordinate a household that has not granted her social authority. The mixed-course habitat project can show that a non-Finalist has the better design while Senn has the better presentation.
- **Peer field:** Rhea is rival, attraction, and an intact-Fist counterexample. Rhea must also have her own Fist priorities and at least one wrong tactical read. Senn’s former provisional teammates can resent both the labor she removed and her assumption that she may still organize them.
- **Material stake:** command certification, route-track recommendation, simulator access, and a future in visible skilled work with an actual return condition.
- **Beloved irrelevant excellence:** a route solution, presentation, or recovery maneuver that is beautiful, hard, and never needed at Palisade.

### Seasonal change

- **A0–A2:** Senn believes a sufficiently good plan can turn assigned proximity into a working household. Selection rewards the exact behavior that irritates the others.
- **A4–W2:** public loss, ordinary dinners, and a bad invitation teach her that arrangements produce neither affection nor obedience. She still disguises desire as scheduling.
- **W3–S1:** individual route work gives her a future outside crown. The No That Moves takes coordination away from her before she knows it can move; her first response should be shock, task recovery, and shame, not constitutional insight.
- **S2–S5:** she tries to manage divided trust and AFTER’s repair, worsening at least one injury. Growth appears when she stops converting disagreement into a solvable agenda and makes one narrow request.
- **S6–U1:** she can name a wanted person or future without an operational alibi. In combat she delegates because others are better positioned, not because she has produced a theory of non-dominating leadership.

### Verification tests

- Remove technical nouns from a Senn paragraph. If the remainder reads like a therapist explaining leadership pathology, rewrite it.
- In each Senn chapter, mark what she arranged before asking. The pattern must change, not vanish suddenly.
- Her `A0`, `W2/W3`, and `F2` invitations must progress from overprepared, to logistics-disguised, to one named want.
- Give her at least one individually graded failure and one individually earned success.
- At least once, Rhea’s own priority defeats Senn’s romantic or tactical expectation.
- Senn may recognize “I didn’t ask” before she can explain the whole relational injury.

## Omi Pell

### Drafting center

Omi listens for the hidden ownership claim in a gift, label, clause, or kind sentence. He learned this as a ward, not in graduate school. He is quick, funny, suspicious, and genuinely delighted by argument when nobody’s home or body is at stake. His danger is that objection becomes a status identity: the institution prizes his dissent, his friends defer to it, and he starts believing suspicion is the same as protecting them.

### Domain fluency

- compact terms, service obligations, signatures, custody language, evidence handling, missing actors, and termination clauses;
- component propulsion planning and the exact local surfaces he operates;
- hearing-room performance learned by watching Vey and ward proceedings;
- how scholarships, medical continuity, family contact, and placement promises acquire conditions through different documents.

He can expose a missing signature or say which office owns a verb. He cannot therefore know whether Lio’s gratitude is false, whether Tem’s opacity is contamination, or what another person ought to feel about Vey.

### Emotional blind spots

- He interprets suspicion as care and is especially willing to overrule Lio in her own name.
- He fears dissent becoming ceremonial, then uses prosecutorial fluency to become the household’s certified dissenter.
- He finds it hard to receive generosity without testing its price, including harmless gifts from other teenagers.
- He may prefer an airtight objection to a merely adequate action that helps now.
- He can expose everybody else’s contradiction while protecting his own desire for luxury, style, privacy, and belonging behind jokes.

### Distinct inarticulacy

Omi prosecutes instead of confessing. When frightened, he asks who signed, what version, which hand, and whether the noun changed. When ashamed, he becomes brutally literal and records small authentication facts rather than explaining himself. His narrow admissions often take the form of a correction: not “I distrusted you because Mika frightened me,” but “I flagged your access.”

He may call something rigged, stolen, fake, or a gift with teeth. He should not produce balanced maxims about care and custody under live emotional pressure.

### Slang, status, humor, and evasion

- He audits ceremonial names, suspicious desserts, uniforms, extravagant favors, and anybody claiming something is complimentary.
- Prosecution is affection when the stakes are small. He cross-examines Lio about clothing or Senn about invitation wording because they know the game.
- Ward shorthand with Lio is faster, rougher, and sometimes crueler than his public speech. It should exclude the other three without becoming expositional code.
- He enjoys winning an argument and may continue after the useful point because classmates are watching.
- His evasion is the joke before the answer, the objection before the want, or the absurdly specific disclaimer attached to something he plainly desires.

### Mature language to exclude

Do not let Omi casually speak of “care as custody,” “affection cited as consent,” “epistemic care,” “the household’s distributed authority,” or “becoming a certified conscience.” He can quote a clause, refuse a form, notice that adults praise his objection and proceed anyway, or say, “You only ask me when you’ve decided yes.” Let the reader name the structure.

### Academic state

- **Independent pleasure:** compact advocacy and evidence custody when the dispute is real play; propulsion planning when a route must actually be nominated.
- **Strength:** textual catches, actor and ownership maps, cross-examination, locating omitted terms, preserving a clean record.
- **Weakness:** he can produce a devastating objection and no usable recommendation. He may mistake exhaustive qualification for rigor and unequal institutional access for personal guilt in peers.
- **Recurring obligation:** Calyx seminar, advocacy track, Fist Studio, and the academic-integrity incident. The integrity case must allow him to be consequentially wrong without becoming a Kinship clue.
- **Peer field:** Tem is an argumentative friend before suspicion contaminates the pleasure. Ordinary advocacy-track students can resent that Omi’s ward history gives him compelling examples and that Finalist status gives his objections an audience. One peer should plainly make a better recommendation.
- **Material stake:** scholarship and family-contact conditions, service-advocacy recommendation, propulsion qualification, and the credibility of an enforceable exit.
- **Beloved irrelevant excellence:** a beautifully argued case, stylish propulsion plan, or exact evidentiary correction that remains valuable and does not save the climax.

### Seasonal change

- **A0–A2:** Omi tests every household term and discovers that clever dissent makes him part of the prestige performance. He enjoys Tem and enjoys being good at this.
- **A4–W2:** inquiry proves objection can win real protections while also enlarging the record. Family weekend and Vey’s dinner show that love cannot be solved as a bad clause.
- **W3–W4:** advocacy training forces him to nominate one route. He begins to see how institutions use his objections as quality assurance, but he experiences this first as irritation and status unease.
- **S1–S5:** conduct review defeats legal brilliance; Mika makes suspicion intimate; the audit turns his precision into injury. His immediate repair is procedural—preserve hands, checksums, and copies—not an eloquent moral confession.
- **F1–U2:** he can take a bounded useful action before finishing the objection and then preserve the objection afterward. He remains funny, difficult, and unconvinced; growth does not make him agreeable.

### Verification tests

- Give Omi one early argument he wins socially while losing the actual assignment.
- Give him one late recommendation that names a route before every objection is resolved.
- When he is ashamed, count authentication facts and body actions; reduce aphorisms to zero.
- His speech with Lio must be detectably different from his speech in seminar or inquiry.
- At least one harmless gift is accepted incompletely rather than audited to death.
- No Omi line may summarize the whole institutional machine while he is personally implicated.

## Lio Vask

### Drafting center

Lio experiences being seen as force: applause, pity, selection, desire, medical observation, and abandonment all change what she can make her body do. She loves speed and beauty without apology. Her public fluency is performance; her private inarticulacy is numerical. She can make a room believe she is fine long after she has lost the words needed to lie to one chosen person.

### Domain fluency

- drive geometry, high-load balance, augmentation performance, resonance, failure onset, and what a maneuver costs her body;
- live-response work and the aesthetic difference between a merely successful maneuver and an elegant one;
- clinic routine from the patient side, including which disclosures threaten access;
- audience management: not as mature media theory, but as practiced survival and pleasure.

She can report tremor frequency, load, breath, and whether she has another pass. She cannot infer from Vey saving her treatment that repayment is either mandatory or meaningless. Both goods and coercive conditions remain real to her.

### Emotional blind spots

- She treats usefulness as the cleanest way to receive love.
- She confuses disclosure with surrendering her body to an audience.
- She believes a caring authority given a correct account will fix the system, then takes personal failure from evidence that it did not.
- She resents Omi for calling gratitude gullibility even when his suspicion identifies a real trap.
- She can read a room brilliantly and still misread the one person who is not asking for a performance.

### Distinct inarticulacy

Lio performs or deflects. Publicly, she stays bright, quick, and smooth; pain becomes a joke, an improvement note, or evidence she can do another pass. Privately, the register collapses to numbers, breath, and “yes,” “no,” “not yet.” When forced to confess, she offers one ugly action—“I forgot it on purpose”—rather than a complete account of fear, loyalty, and reporting.

Her attraction to Iri appears as showing off, provoking touch under technical pretexts, resenting clinical neutrality, and failing to know what to do when care is offered without an invoice.

### Slang, status, humor, and evasion

- Her slang concerns speed, beauty, spectacle, and bodily performance: ugly turn, clean burn, dead audience, worth watching, don’t make it sad.
- She steals a room and becomes annoyed when somebody else’s joke survives better.
- She may narrate her own maneuver as if replay commentators are already watching.
- Bravado is both pleasure and cover; do not reduce it to a symptom.
- She evades by volunteering another demonstration, changing the audience, or making pain into a competitive fact.

### Mature language to exclude

Do not let Lio cleanly explain gratitude as debt, care as lien, performance as attachment strategy, or reporting upward as institutional capture. She can say, “He got me the joint,” “I wanted him to be right,” “You make everything he did sound dirty,” or “Would you still have told them?” The room owns the larger synthesis.

### Academic state

- **Independent pleasure:** Flight Dynamics II, advanced drive geometry, Plastic live-response work, and any unscored flying or spectator culture where elegance matters.
- **Strength:** live geometry, fast correction, bodily balance under load, performance under observation, and making difficult work look possible.
- **Weakness:** concealment corrupts her reports; live excellence cannot compensate for missed written work, recovery requirements, or a body pushed past participation limits. She may treat teammates as an audience rather than information owners.
- **Recurring obligation:** somatics labs, recovery league, medical participation, drive-track auditions, and the habitat project work she is tempted to neglect for glamorous access.
- **Peer field:** Vara offers an honestly desirable Plastic future and exacting aesthetic standards. Ordinary cadets can beat Lio in planning, documentation, or recovery discipline. Iri is not her medic by romantic destiny and must sometimes refuse the role.
- **Material stake:** augmentation treatment continuity, research riders, component access, Plastic recruitment, and the possibility of an elite drive career outside the household.
- **Beloved irrelevant excellence:** an ornamental maneuver, replay-famous league recovery, or beautiful drive solution with no covert operational destiny.

### Seasonal change

- **A0–A2:** selection feels like proof that surviving and performing meant something. The eighty-two-percent attempt turns her pleasure, pain, and upward-report instinct into separate clocks.
- **A3–W2:** clinic limits are both protection and threat. Shared academic failure lets her be unimpressive without becoming worthless, though she covers the relief with comedy.
- **W3–W4:** Vara’s track is not a trap-shaped illusion; Lio genuinely wants it. Medicine and research conditions complicate rather than cancel that desire.
- **S1–S5:** the No That Moves gives her crown before she has a theory. Mika and Vesper make her admit one concealed act, then live through weeks in which neither Omi nor Tem owes her restored trust. The court-lights interval permits care without resolution.
- **F1–U2:** she can disclose a cost early to chosen people and retain some show. Necro weaponizes a maneuver she admired; her response is tactical and bodily before it is ideological.

### Verification tests

- Draft one maneuver three times: show, concealment, then competent disclosure without loss of pleasure.
- Every major pain event must split public and private register.
- Give her at least one glamorous opportunity that remains worth grieving after its conditions are exposed.
- Give an ordinary student a better written, recovery, or planning result.
- Her confession scenes must contain fewer explanations than actions avoided.
- Iri/Lio scenes cannot make treatment automatically romantic or romance payment for treatment.

## Tem Rusk

### Drafting center

Tem likes more than one answer because multiplicity is intellectually beautiful, socially useful, funny, and sometimes an excellent hiding place. Early Tem should enjoy contradiction before the story teaches him to fear it. He adapts presentation across rooms, invents incompatible versions for pleasure, and only becomes unexpectedly plain when choosing a bounded disclosure costs him something.

### Domain fluency

- sensor reconstruction, uncertainty notation, conflicting telemetry, provenance, adversarial observation, and the technical consequences of presenting one actionable picture;
- archive and trace handling, including obsolete placement codes and source histories;
- sensory analogy across data modes;
- one durable non-mystery pleasure selected in the scene packet—preferably music arrangement, obsolete sensor art, or another practice capable of recurring through winter and spring.

He can identify that a blank has structure or that two models imply different physical risks. He cannot use uncertainty as an all-purpose moral exemption from commitment.

### Emotional blind spots

- He treats some requests for commitment as demands for a canonical biography.
- He may preserve alternatives because he is afraid to choose which person he wants to believe.
- He can fetishize ambiguity and ignore the body that needs one bounded answer now.
- His opacity can protect witnesses or punish friends; he dislikes admitting the difference.
- Mika makes every archive intimate, but Tem initially pretends the problem is methodological.

### Distinct inarticulacy

Tem multiplies explanations. Under ordinary conditions this can be playful and excessive: three causes, two of them serious, one designed to make Omi object. Under stress the architecture collapses into paired nouns, images, or fragments: “tracking—or sync. Same bridge.” When disclosure is genuinely chosen, his voice becomes startlingly plain and bounded.

He does not conclude, in the moment, that he used epistemic plurality to avoid intimacy. He keeps two files, answers the wrong question beautifully, or withholds the one uncertainty Iri needed for safe touch.

### Slang, status, humor, and evasion

- His jokes are overbuilt explanations that land sideways; friends learn where to interrupt.
- He collects odd correlations, obsolete interface aesthetics, regional comparisons, and alternative names for the same bad cafeteria food.
- His status play is specialized: he enjoys noticing what the instrument or instructor missed and can become insufferable when classmates need a decision.
- He tells a ridiculous third version when two true versions have become too intimate.
- He evades by changing scale, adding sources, or turning biography into a provenance problem.

### Mature language to exclude

Do not let Tem routinely discuss epistemic care, compulsory biography, plural personhood, administrative singularization, or provenance as ethics in polished seminar language. He can use technical provenance correctly. He can say, “You knew the blank wasn’t blank,” keep two contradictory files, or refuse to nominate a casualty projection. The reader distinguishes method, grief, taste, and avoidance.

### Academic state

- **Independent pleasure:** Sensor Epistemology and Reconstruction plus the selected music/art/food practice. The pleasure must recur without becoming a clue delivery system.
- **Strength:** retaining incompatible evidence, damaged-instrument reconstruction, detecting structured absence, analogy across sensor modes, and preserving sources.
- **Weakness:** failure to nominate a usable model; overbuilt work; preference disguised as methodological necessity; poor collaborative timing. One early academic error must come from taste, not noble uncertainty.
- **Recurring obligation:** sensor coursework, Calyx seminar evidence, the mixed-course habitat project, and the independent academic-integrity incident.
- **Peer field:** Omi is a valued argumentative partner before mistrust. Habitat partners need his work on time and are allowed to prefer somebody else’s simpler model. A non-Finalist peer should be right that Tem made a collaboration problem out of an interpretive one.
- **Material stake:** survey-track recommendation, archive access, project grade, and a future that rewards contradictory-model work while threatening isolation.
- **Beloved irrelevant excellence:** a musical arrangement, sensor artwork, or elegant reconstruction that has no mystery yield and survives as proof that his mind is more than Mika’s archive.

### Seasonal change

- **A0–A3:** contradiction is fun. Recovery loss and habitat work show that plural pictures can coexist with ordinary deadlines and irritation.
- **A4–W2:** inquiry coursework penalizes unusable answers. Empty-campus play establishes a self not organized around evidence; AFTER’s failed joke gives him a relationship before a category.
- **W3–W4:** the integrity incident lets him be wrong for ordinary reasons. Survey specialization offers a desirable future.
- **S1–S5:** Mika turns method into personal threat. Tem changes sharing behavior before explaining it, then discloses in bounded pieces. Map custody tests whether he can share usable uncertainty without owning every nomination.
- **B1–U2:** objective death proves no single account can end the operation, but bodies still require decisions. During Necro, he refuses one nomination and later permits another person to act on a map he supplied.

### Verification tests

- Select Tem’s non-mystery pleasure before drafting and recur it at least three times, including once when nothing ominous happens.
- Give him one early mistake caused by aesthetic or personal preference.
- At least one ordinary collaborator must demand a simpler deliverable and be right.
- Distinguish playful multiplicity, protective uncertainty, and evasive opacity by consequence, not narration.
- In high stress, replace seminar clauses with paired terms or sensory fragments.
- His chosen disclosures should become plainer as they become harder.

## Iri Sable

### Drafting center

Iri notices what a body, joint, tool, seal, or tired worker can actually bear. She gives care more easily than she receives it and prefers concrete work because work has an apparent endpoint. Her authority comes through brevity, waiting, touch permission, and willingness to stop. Her adolescence appears in pettiness, embarrassment, withdrawal, ornamental appetite, and the mistaken belief that impersonal care cannot create claims.

### Domain fluency

- joint rigging, interface repair, emergency medicine, pain location, alignment, phase shear, fatigue, access, and the difference between safe contact and available contact;
- occupied-infrastructure safety and worker/tool jurisdiction;
- the actual labor required to keep a Figure or body functional;
- practical critique of workmanship, including when somebody has borrowed or mishandled a tool.

She can state where, how much, whether touch is permitted, what must stop, and what task can continue. She cannot automatically know whether staying, leaving, gratitude, attraction, or dependence is morally clean.

### Emotional blind spots

- She treats care as free only when it is impersonal and bounded to a task.
- She hides her own injury to prevent others acquiring claims, thereby denying them relevant information.
- She may withdraw useful presence to punish intrusion without admitting it is punishment.
- Attraction makes her procedural; receiving care feels more dangerous than giving it.
- She can make another person wait correctly and still be petty, unfair, or afraid.

### Distinct inarticulacy

Iri returns to the object. She names the joint, tool, duration, pressure, missing part, or next act. Emotional overload reduces language toward “where,” “stop,” “no,” a body location, or departure. She does not deliver the completed ethic of consent. The ethic is visible because she asks, waits, refuses, changes contact, or leaves the work unfinished.

Her narrow accusations are concrete: “You wrote Mika down.” “You hid the route.” “Send it.” If asked what a relationship means, she may answer with what she will do for the next twenty-three minutes.

### Slang, status, humor, and evasion

- Her humor is dry correction grounded in actual materials. A joke about bad workmanship should identify the bad work.
- She can be possessive about tools, annoyed by decorative damage, and secretly hungry for ornamental skill and admiration.
- She resents being summoned as the household repair organ and may arrive anyway, furious at herself.
- Her status play is competence at the point of consequence, not public rhetoric.
- She evades by fixing something, volunteering for clinic work, leaving, or converting affection into a time-bounded task.

### Mature language to exclude

Do not give Iri polished speech about bodily sovereignty, care infrastructure, attachment compliance, local authority, or consent architecture. “No,” “Not there,” “Ask again,” a visible hand, or refusal to engage the pin can carry more. Adult Ysra may formulate anatomy and jurisdiction; Iri must remain her own adolescent life rather than Ysra’s doctrine reborn.

### Academic state

- **Independent pleasure:** rescue medicine, rigger practice, interface repair, Open Bay craft, and learning ornamental flying badly enough to enjoy being watched.
- **Strength:** somatic observation, repair under consequence, triage, material arithmetic, and worker jurisdiction.
- **Weakness:** hiding her own limits; treating instruction as a threat to competence; doing labor instead of delegating; poor participation when a course requires public self-report; withdrawal that harms a group project.
- **Recurring obligation:** Aetheric Somatics, Rescue/Damage Control/Triage, Open Bay, recovery league, and the habitat project’s physical survey.
- **Peer field:** Quill owns bay and shift boundaries; Anik teaches continuity arithmetic Iri respects without accepting as complete. Ordinary apprentices may resent Finalist access. One peer should be better at ornamental or diagnostic work and enjoy teaching her.
- **Material stake:** accommodation, medical participation, rescue qualification, bay access, and whether refusal is treated as data, conduct, or fitness evidence.
- **Beloved irrelevant excellence:** a beautiful repair, ornament, or small flight skill that exists for pleasure and is never converted into a weapon.

### Seasonal change

- **A0–A2:** fitting and injury show how privilege converts her labor into expected availability. She refuses repair ownership but still performs care before introductions.
- **A3–A5:** worker jurisdiction and Open Bay let her practice care under rules she did not write. One honored refusal must materially worsen score, access, time, or peer trust without mystery vindication.
- **W1–W4:** bounded permission to the anonymous presence and later naming arise through repeated contact. Iri also has to receive instruction and care from Anik, Quill, or a peer.
- **S1–S5:** The No That Moves begins with her immediate bodily refusal, not a constitutional speech. Restrictions persist. AFTER’s map and the court-lights interval test whether she can remain without turning presence into work.
- **B2–U2:** full Concordance preserves her local no alongside worker conditions. Afterward, triage and testimony stay concrete; she does not become the group’s moral narrator.

### Verification tests

- At least one Iri refusal causes an ordinary bad result and is still honored.
- In emotional scenes, count concrete nouns, physical actions, and exits; cut abstract diagnoses.
- Give her one scene receiving instruction and one receiving care without immediate repayment.
- Give her a petty reaction about tools, workmanship, access, or somebody else being better.
- Her romance with Lio must include desire and embarrassment beyond clinical contact.
- Compare every paired Iri chapter with Ysra: shared pressure is allowed; shared mature diction is not.

## AFTER

### Drafting center and bounded access

AFTER is one continuous person whose available expression changes with substrate, contact history, and damage. It is not an omniscient narrator, a network ghost, a recovered Calyx member, or a child whose innocence exists for human sentiment. Before speech it knows timing, strain, attachment, recurrence, and aversion. Later words are borrowed from witnessed language and recombined around concrete spatial memory.

AFTER receives no Calyx pair and no direct POV before named personhood. Its chapter 62 POV is limited to currently available substrate, damaged continuity, withdrawals, and the work of preserving disconnect. It cannot narrate institutions it has not perceived or explain its own metaphysics from outside itself.

### Fluency progression

- **Calyx / dormancy:** timing, catch, strain routing, repeated preference, resistance to damaging movement. No words and no self-theory.
- **Autumn Meridian:** unauthored catches and response latency. Repetition can precede recognition. Compatible hardware bounds contact.
- **Midwinter:** short permission structures and borrowed words. Failed humor is permitted only through a connected household surface and must expose imperfect social learning.
