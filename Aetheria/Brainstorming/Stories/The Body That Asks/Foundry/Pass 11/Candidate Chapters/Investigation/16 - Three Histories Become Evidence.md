# Chapter 16 — Three Histories Become Evidence

> **Noncanonical Pass 11 candidate chapter.** Tem close-third past.

Mika existed in three incompatible histories.

In Lio's, Mika had protected a failing Fist until protection became command. In Tem's, Mika had been the only pilot willing to preserve more than one casualty future. In Meridian's, the obsolete placement code described a clinical discontinuity followed by successful reassignment.

For four days, Omi allowed Tem to read the joined receipt but not copy it into the household record.

He called the restriction provenance control. Tem called it being treated as contaminated evidence. They performed both interpretations politely enough to continue eating dinner.

On the fifth day Vey's welfare request returned.

It contained no location and no attestation from Mika. It did contain the same obsolete code attached to Tem's transfer out of the Fist. The clinic row said protective fixation. Formation said incompatibility under shared authority. Candidate handling said recomposition eligible.

“That phrase was not in my record when I transferred,” Tem said.

Senn had spread the documents across the common table without combining them. Five signed local summaries sat beside Vey's extract. No household account occupied the center.

“Can you prove that?” Omi asked.

Tem opened the student archive he had kept since first year. “My copy names a routine placement correction. Same authentication root, earlier issue time.”

Omi inspected both. The current archive had not replaced Tem's copy. It contradicted it while claiming the same event.

“Manufactured contradiction,” Tem said.

“Or corrected classification.”

“A correction with no correction receipt.”

Omi went still.

He removed Tem's access restriction himself.

The household record accepted Tem's archive without making it authoritative. The change restored his ability to search their shared provenance index. It did not repair the dinners in which Omi had watched him like a hostile port.

Tem used the restored access to join the obsolete code against public material first.

No private clinic texts. No sealed welfare narratives. Graduation programs, Finalist rosters, alumni announcements, competition footage, assignment indexes. The ordinary surfaces Meridian displayed because prestige required memory.

Mika appeared in a first-year formation photograph. The next term's program listed the same Fist with one Seat changed and no withdrawal notice. A later alumni reel praised three graduates and omitted two students who had appeared in every prior score board.

Senn said, “Absence isn't disposition.”

“Correct.” Tem added it as a separate account.

Lio leaned over the table without touching his controls. “Search the code family, not the names.”

Tem did.

Eleven earlier Finalist households emerged as gaps rather than files. A clinic continuity notice followed by Seat reassignment. A formation withdrawal followed by classified placement. A scholarship extension attached to a student who vanished from public rosters. An insurance appeal naming a training exercise no academy schedule admitted had occurred.

Each case alone had a lawful explanation. Together they repeated one operation: split a household after a safety or coordination finding, preserve selected members, recombine Seats, continue the program.

Omi built the candidate-index join. He refused Tem's first version because it treated every public disappearance as the same outcome. Tem refused Omi's because it excluded unresolved names. They kept both and marked the difference.

Iri counted the students rather than the rows.

“These are people,” she said. “Not eleven failed machines.”

Senn changed the display. Names first. Administrative operations beneath them.

Mika remained absent. No join revealed whether Mika was alive, in classified service, in treatment, or dead. Tem kept all four possibilities visible until Lio asked him to stop putting death beside Mika's name as if uncertainty made it gentle.

He moved the possibilities into a sealed note only she and he could open.

That was not forgiveness. It was a new permission.

The newest candidate row populated while they watched.

ARADA, SENN — crown retention anomaly; command-track suitability.

PELL, OMI — dissent persistence; reserve-control suitability.

VASK, LIO — emergency balance acquisition; propulsion adaptation.

RUSK, TEM — contradictory perception retention; synthesis instability.

SABLE, IRI — local refusal persistence; rescue-load tolerance.

Below the five names, a status field waited.

RECOMPOSITION REVIEW: DEFERRED BY WELFARE HOLD.

Vey's help had not removed them from the machine. It had made the current position of its teeth visible.

Senn closed the public network path. “Nobody searches for the other households through a school credential.”

“Agreed,” Omi said.

Tem looked at the eleven incomplete histories and Mika's missing one.

“Then we preserve the index and stop,” he said.

Lio asked, “For how long?”

The overhaul notice on the wall supplied the only honest answer.

Not long enough.
