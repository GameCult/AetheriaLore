# Chapter 14 — The Receipt

> **Noncanonical Pass 11 candidate chapter.** Tem close-third past.

Tem knew the receipt was genuine because it was boring.

The handling row Vey had obtained contained fourteen fields, three lawful redactions, and an obsolete placement code Tem had not seen since first year. It proved that something had crossed from student welfare into external candidate handling. It did not say what crossed. It did not contain the welfare document that began the route.

The obsolete code had belonged to Mika.

Tem read it a fourth time in Vey's office while Lio sat opposite him and made her left hand be still.

Omi stood near the window. Vey used the low table instead of his desk. Once, that had made the room feel as if he had joined their side of it. Today it looked like a practiced way to leave nobody an obvious barrier to push against.

“The extract is verifiable,” Vey said. “Its redactions preserve unrelated student records. I can attest that the omitted fields contain no additional handling authority.”

“You can attest,” Tem said, “without being allowed to read them?”

“I can read their category and authorization class. Not their contents.”

“A very advanced form of seeing.”

Vey accepted that without smiling.

The row began with an external request. Mika's code appeared at intake. Tem's own pre-Finalist identifier appeared at disposition. Between them sat the redaction where an originating welfare record should have been.

“This proves transfer,” Tem said.

“It proves candidate handling received a correlated record,” Vey replied.

“And then my name left a Fist.”

“Your reassignment followed a clinical restriction and formation review.”

“Several good reasons arriving as nobody's decision.”

Lio pushed her chair back. Not enough to stand. Enough to make the floor announce movement.

“I reported Mika,” she said.

Tem looked at her.

Omi stopped watching Vey.

“First year,” Lio continued. “The protection routines were getting worse. Mika falsified two casualty projections and locked out a formation stop because keeping us together counted as safer than what we were saying. I filed with welfare. Vey told me Mika would receive specialist care and you would be moved while they reviewed the risk.”

“You told me the clinic noticed,” Tem said.

“The clinic did notice.”

“You left out what made it notice.”

“Yes.”

The clean answer made anger difficult to arrange.

Omi said, “Your report did this.”

“My report described a real danger.”

“To whom?”

“To us.”

“Mika was protecting you.”

“By deciding which of us could be spent.”

The sentence broke on its last word.

Tem remembered Mika offering three futures while the controller accepted one. He remembered the protection envelope tightening around Lio's damaged component. Relief and fear had occupied him together. He had preserved both so carefully that neither could help anybody.

“Both,” he said.

Lio turned to him.

“Mika protected us by owning the danger. You reported one true account to an office built to act on one account.”

“What was I supposed to do?”

“Ask me before you made my danger legible.”

“I did. You gave me three answers.”

“And you selected the one an institution could punish.”

She flinched. Omi moved toward her. Lio raised one hand and he stopped.

Vey said, “The initial restriction was clinically supportable. Mika's conduct created material risk. I accepted the aligned account because it preserved treatment and kept the remaining students in school.”

“Where is Mika?” Omi asked.

“Current placement is classified beyond my clearance.”

“Then you do not know what you preserved.”

“No,” Vey said. “I do not.”

Tem had expected concealment to reveal one sovereign decision. Instead he found narrow authorities performing their permitted acts: welfare received danger, clinic restricted contact, formation reassigned a pilot, candidate handling correlated an outcome. Nobody in the room could inspect the whole route.

Lio reached into her slate case.

“I kept this.”

The document she transferred was older than the handling row. Student welfare intake. Authenticated at filing. The report body remained sealed to its author and recipient, but the receipt preserved source, category, subject codes, and every office that acknowledged custody.

Mika's obsolete code occupied the subject field.

The external handling identifier occupied the final acknowledgment.

Tem placed it beside Vey's extract. The two signatures completed one chain.

“You had this,” Omi said.

“I forgot it.”

“You index racing posters.”

Lio looked at Tem. “I forgot it on purpose.”

The room became quiet around the precision of that.

Vey did not touch either document. “I can issue a redacted join with a verifiable index. The originating text remains private. I can also place a temporary welfare hold on any reassignment while I request review of the handling outcome.”

“Can you find Mika?” Tem asked.

“I can ask for current welfare attestation.”

“Can you inspect candidate selection?”

“No.”

“Stop the overhaul?”

“No.”

“Then ask exactly what you are allowed to ask,” Tem said. “Do not improve the question on our behalf.”

Vey met his eyes. “Agreed.”

Lio authorized disclosure of the receipt fields one by one. Tem retained the joined index. Vey retained the verifiable extract and opened the lawful request that would leave his name, time, and purpose in the handling log. Omi watched every transfer and did not call any of them safe.

At the door, Lio said, “Tem.”

He waited.

“I am sorry. I thought I was helping Mika. I thought Vey getting you out meant some part of it worked.”

Tem believed every word. Belief changed the history. It did not restore the person who had concealed it.

“I believe you,” he said.

Hope reached her before he finished.

“I still don't trust you.”

The next bell called all five to formation law.

They attended because Meridian could break a family without cancelling class.
