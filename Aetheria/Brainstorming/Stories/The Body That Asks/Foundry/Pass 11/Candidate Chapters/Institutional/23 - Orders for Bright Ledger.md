# Chapter 23: Orders for Bright Ledger

> **Candidate prose.** Subject to manuscript integration and blind-reader review.

Senn signed nothing before breakfast.

The deployment assent waited on the household panel while five medication clocks, three family messages, one repair notice, and Tem's cafeteria poll competed for the same screen. Meridian had placed the operation above them in institutional gold. Omi dragged it beneath Iri's clinic window.

“Chronological correction,” he said.

“Political claim,” Senn said.

“Also breakfast.”

The house steward arrived at the sixth chair with a storage clerk and a pile of pressure-rated kit bags. Nobody had cleared the common table, so mission seals accumulated beside rice bowls and Lio's cooling pack. The clerk checked each bag against a pilot tab. The steward asked permission before entering cabins and looked faintly pleased when the rule slowed him down.

Bright Ledger offered three ways into Palisade.

The Plastics would take the axial executive corridor, isolate armed charges, and establish command access to the plant core. The Lifers would enter through the medical freight ring, preserve clinic lots, and maintain evacuation continuity. The sophomore Fist would use radial maintenance collars to stabilize occupied machinery and carry messages between routes whose authorities did not trust one another.

The brief called the last role integration support.

“It means we become the joint everybody else loads,” Iri said.

“Only within declared rescue scope,” Senn replied.

Omi read the sentence back to her.

She heard what it presumed. “No. That is the condition we would require.”

AFTER attended through the equipment wall. Its map overlaid all five Figures, complete known nodes in white, uncertain routes in grey, vulnerable continuities in red. No hidden geometry remained solely under its control.

Senn opened a fresh household decision rather than the academy assent.

“Proposed action: deploy for rescue, contamination control, evacuation, and preservation of existing clinic lots. No agreement to fresh production, executive custody, post-operation service, recomposition, or a return route not separately accepted.”

“Evidence?” Tem asked.

“Separate decision.”

“Good.”

They examined the operation by body rather than mission paragraph.

Iri owned whether her reinforced collars entered Palisade's somatic maintenance system. Lio owned her drive load and augmentation margin. Tem owned active sensing and which accounts he nominated for action. Omi owned local propulsion reserve and separation geometry. Senn could accept crown only for a named whole-body task with an expiry. AFTER could refuse extension beyond present hardware and had to state any known survival cost before asking.

Each accepted the rescue action separately.

Iri conditioned her assent on local collar authority remaining able to disconnect.

Tem required provenance on every plant feed and retained the right to carry incompatible accounts.

Omi refused any order that converted his objection into evidence the group had considered and accepted a cost.

Lio accepted the work and declined to preauthorize Vara's control if she became medically impaired.

Senn went last.

“I accept coordination for the declared deployment,” she said. “Return condition: completion or refusal of the bounded rescue action. This does not answer custody after return.”

The panel recorded five assents and AFTER's sixth.

**I ACCEPT THE RESCUE SCOPE. I DO NOT ACCEPT OVERHAUL OR UNDISCLOSED ROUTING.**

Only then did they open Meridian's form.

It had one button.

**HOUSEHOLD ACCEPTS DEPLOYMENT**

Omi stared at it with professional disgust.

Senn attached their decision record and pressed the button. The form accepted the attachment without promising anybody would read it.

The rest of the day remained offensively normal.

They attended two academic blocks. Tem delivered a reconstruction exercise in which every answer implicated a different sensor. Iri sat her rescue-medicine examination and emerged angry because Anik had made the questions fair. Lio completed Vara's live-geometry turn without hiding load from her team. Omi bought a strip of silver pressure tape from the student store because he wanted the inside of his pilot cell to look expensive during possible catastrophe.

At the long cafeteria interval, Rhea's Fist occupied the table the Finalists usually claimed. Rhea had spread maintenance cards over Senn's place.

“You leave for one corporate emergency and social order collapses,” Senn said.

“I began early.”

They ate from ordinary trays. No sponsor menu appeared. Anik sent extra broth to everyone holding medical clearance, including two freight apprentices at the next table. Vara stole Lio's untouched dessert and denied it while chewing.

Rhea returned Senn's calibration cards one at a time. She had annotated them in red.

“Your return conditions are too verbal,” she said. “Under load you shorten them until only the task survives.”

Senn looked at the third card. Rhea had replaced RETURN WHEN SAFE with RETURN WHEN COLLARS GREEN AND LOCAL SPEECH RESTORED.

“That's good,” Senn said.

“I know.”

“Come to the final practice.”

Rhea glanced toward her own Fist. “We have range.”

The answer landed without drama. Senn had asked. Rhea had somewhere else to be.

“After?” Rhea said.

“If the bay stays open.”

“Then maybe.”

The component court smelled of sterilizer and hot radiators when the five descended after lunch. Quill had given them one imperfect hour before departure inspection. She refused to extend it.

“Your operation does not make my night shift longer by divine law,” she said.

“Can we make it longer by work order?” Senn asked.

“Already tried. It lacked charm.”

They practiced offices cold-docked first.

Senn opened crown for whole-body balance and gave Tem the propulsion-vector office. He received route commitments, current mass, thermal limits, and expiry. He did not receive Omi's local vernier knowledge or Lio's drive tolerance.

“Vector office held,” Tem said. “Two accounts retained.”

Senn introduced a simulated collar refusal.

Tem nominated the slower route and returned the office at the named boundary. Omi complained that the slower route spent his reserve. Tem agreed without changing the account.

They reset.

Lio received crown for damaged-drive balance while Senn took rescue triage from Iri's task packet. Iri supplied patient order, disconnect limits, and the observations that changed touch. Senn did not receive Iri's hands, tolerance, or authority over a local stop.

The first run failed when Senn tried to carry three rescue priorities without returning one.

“You are hoarding casualties,” Iri said.

“That sentence is medically unhelpful.”

“Return the second route.”

Senn did. Lio balanced around the changed load and yielded crown when local speech returned.

On the third run, Omi owned separation without taking crown. Senn stabilized the whole and could not cancel a pilot's release. AFTER supplied the shared map but not the release decision. Five local indicators went green at five different times.

They separated cleanly.

Quill stopped the clock.

“Good enough to reveal a new category of mistake,” she said.

Rhea did not arrive. Her range block ran long. Senn checked the bay entrance once and returned to the evidence table.

No Figure would carry the whole record.

Tem held authenticated indices and contradictory plant feeds. Iri held victim-authorized medical and rescue material. Omi held authority changes, objection, and separation logs. Lio held drive and corporate transfer timing. Senn held office grants and returns. AFTER held its own embodiment and challenge history, split across all five components because that was where it lived.

Quill prepared sealed copies for three custodians who could verify deletion without reading private contents. Vey owned the readiness certificate and his emergency log. Rhea would receive an external timing witness only if she consented after return; they did not assign her courage in advance.

The academy brief listed Palisade's radial collars as ordinary somatic maintenance interfaces.

Iri enlarged the diagram.

“Compatible with our joint gantry,” she said.

Tem overlaid the manufacturer families. “Derived, not identical.”

“Enough for embodied access?” Senn asked.

“Enough to ask at the dock,” Iri said. “Not enough to promise.”

They added the uncertainty to the mission record.

Vey met them at the Finalist gate with the readiness certificate. He checked fit limits, bay release, range clearance, custody authorization, and pilot assent as separate lines. Four were green. Custody remained amber.

“Bright Ledger command has accepted your attached household terms as supplementary,” he said.

“Meaning?” Omi asked.

“They are part of the record.”

“Not the order.”

“Correct.”

Vey signed readiness anyway.

His readiness line turned green. A separate House custody token arrived twelve seconds later and cleared the final amber line.

Their recomposition notice moved from immediate review to post-return review. That was the extent of his victory. It was also the reason they could launch together.

The house steward brought down the kit bags they had packed that morning. Somebody in stores had added five cafeteria pastries under an emergency-calorie code. Omi audited the label and distributed them unequally according to preference.

Senn took the apricot one and ate it beside her cold-docked Figure while Quill's crews completed the handoff. Around them the academy changed shifts. Ordinary cadets came off range complaining about instructors. Laundry carts crossed the radial lift. Rhea's Fist appeared in the upper gallery, late and still in flight skins.

Rhea tapped two fingers against her wrist.

Time.

Senn returned the gesture.

Then she climbed into her pilot cell and closed the service lock on the school day behind her.
