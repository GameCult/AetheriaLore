# Chapter 15 — The School Continues

> **Noncanonical Pass 11 candidate chapter.** Lio close-third past.

The morning after Tem said he believed her and did not trust her, Lio had drive lab at eight.

Meridian did not cancel excellent equipment because somebody's understanding of herself had become unusable.

Vara waited in the gym beside the hostile-observer rig. “You're late.”

“Forty seconds.”

“Then recover forty seconds.”

Lio entered the cradle.

The task was the internship qualifier Vara had promised: carry a failing drive surface through a public approach without revealing which vector limb had lost tolerance. Her team channel remained private. The observer feed belonged to three Plastics and two sponsor evaluators.

Iri usually watched Lio's private load map.

Today Anik stood at that console.

“Sable withdrew,” he said.

Lio tightened the left restraint. “Did she say why?”

“No.”

The household rule made the answer sufficient. Lio hated it for working.

She launched the simulation.

Pain arrived where it always did, at the boundary between augmentation and the body's memory of what had once been there. Lio named the load on the private channel. Anik acknowledged without softening his voice. She bled drive through the opposite limb and kept the public silhouette clean.

At the first failure, the observer feed guessed wrong.

At the second, one Plastic identified the damaged side from her breathing.

Vara stopped the run. “You are concealing from your team.”

“I disclosed the load.”

“You disclosed the number. Your breath changed before the number.”

Lio looked at Anik.

He said, “I assumed the difference was pride and proceeded.”

That was the Lifer answer: preserve the work, carry the ugly interpretation, remain useful tomorrow.

“Again,” Vara said.

Lio told Anik what the breath meant. She hated the exposure less than she hated making him infer it. The third run finished with thermal margin intact. The sponsors offered the observation slot.

She accepted.

For ten clean seconds she wanted the future exactly as offered: classified approach work, adult equipment, people who treated pain as serious information rather than disqualification.

Then the lab clerk handed her the authorization slate.

The placement system populated her comparison cohort before signature. VASK appeared beside prior holders of the ceremonial Seat. One row carried Mika's obsolete handling code.

Lio stopped breathing correctly.

“Problem?” Vara asked.

“Wrong record join.”

The clerk reached for the slate. Lio moved it away.

She could report the error. Reporting was what responsible people did when a system put the wrong body in the wrong category. She could also photograph the code into the local record before the clerk corrected it.

Tem no longer trusted her with unresolved evidence.

That did not make the evidence hers to simplify.

Lio opened a provenance capture and preserved the whole screen: internship offer, comparison rule, Mika code, clerk identity, timestamp, every context that might weaken the interpretation she wanted.

She sent the capture to Tem without commentary.

The reply did not come.

At lunch, Iri sat with Quill's apprentices. Lio took a Plastics table because crossing the room would make an apology performative and because Vara's people had saved her a seat she genuinely wanted.

Omi placed one citrus fruit beside her tray and left without sitting.

Tem's reply arrived during dessert.

**RECEIVED. DO NOT REPORT YET. I HAVE TWO OTHER USES OF THE CODE.**

No forgiveness. No accusation. Work bounded tightly enough to survive them.

Lio accepted the internship slot and did not sign the data-correction request. The school continued around both choices: first-years traded simulator rumors, Rhea's Fist celebrated another recovery score, Anik ate standing because somebody needed his chair.

Across the cafeteria, Iri looked up once.

Lio did not wave. Iri did not look away.
