# Harrowglass

> **Noncanonical voice and structure test.** This excerpt is drafting evidence only. It does not establish manuscript prose or setting canon.

Sol's hair had become a constitutional problem.

“Hold still,” Ysra said.

“I am holding still.”

“You are delivering a speech with your shoulders.”

The component bay had been depressurized everywhere except the service blister nested into Mara's pilot cell, so the loose hair fell with ceremonial slowness. Sol caught a black tuft against his sleeve and looked at it as though Ysra had falsified an injury report. She pushed his chin down with two fingers and ran the clippers behind his ear.

Mara watched the seal diagnostic turn from amber to green.

That was the important part. The seal would hold through the first separation burn. Sol could object to the haircut at whatever length history required.

Beyond the blister glass, Calyx occupied the dock in five dark sections joined by bands of field light. Mara's integration section cupped the blister against its pilot cell; four pressurized transfer collars crossed the join to the others. Each section was large enough to have acquired its own weather during assembly. The sensor body carried a skin of silver frost. The drive fields bent the work lamps into pale hooks. Mara's hull monitor showed no visible strain at all.

Tern came through the inner hatch with a speaker under one arm.

Eda looked up from the field kettle. “If that contains singing, I am opening the hatch.”

“It contains five historically significant compositions.”

“So did the last one.”

“History has moved.”

Sol twisted to see the speaker. Ysra stopped the clippers a finger's width from his scalp.

“Speech with the shoulders,” she said.

He faced forward again.

Mara poured the dock tea. The kettle had been repaired so often that none of its original handle remained. Sol had replaced the last cracked ceramic section with a coupling brace intended for a civilian tug, an offence Eda had promised to prosecute once she found a government with the correct jurisdiction. The tea tasted of iron and burnt peel. It always had. Mara filled five cups, placed Ysra's beside the medical board, and left her own cooling by the integration console.

The sixth portion was not tea.

She opened the local assembly channel and let the service blister's small noises enter it: clippers, kettle, Tern testing the speaker at a volume calculated to provoke, Eda's palm striking the mute control. None of these signals were operationally necessary. AFTER liked beginnings it could distinguish from alarms.

The reply arrived as a slight redistribution of warmth beneath Mara's left hand. Not language yet. Not quite. A remembered pressure, placed where the old catch had first kept Calyx from tearing itself apart.

Mara rested her palm on the console.

“We're here,” she said.

AFTER held the warmth for one breath, then released it.

Ysra finished Sol's hair and passed him the broom.

“At last,” Eda said. “A transfer of office with adequate preparation.”

Sol began sweeping. “I am choosing not to dignify that.”

“Your dignity is in the dustpan.”

Tern rescued his speaker from her reach. “Can we disassemble the most consequential machine in the Route Sphere before you break my property?”

“Your chronology is self-serving.”

It was easier while they were irritating one another. Mara had known this for years and had never found a respectable way to put it in a paper.

Then the dock clock crossed the agreed mark.

Nobody became symbolic. They put their helmets on.

The blister emptied through the four transfer collars in the order the separation geometry required. Sol crossed into propulsion and coupling. Eda took the field component and the liaison core she would burn before release. Tern carried the contradictory archives to the sensor component, where the dispersal keys had already been divided beyond his recovery. Ysra crossed last into the anatomy component after checking every seal twice and her own once. Mara stayed with the tea and the integration controls. Ysra would remain in the envelope after the others had gone.

Mara hated that. Ysra knew. Neither converted the hatred into authority.

At the inner hatch, Ysra tapped Mara's untouched cup.

“Still planning to drink that?”

“After.”

Ysra looked at her.

Mara heard the word only when it was too late to take it back.

“Waste of tea,” Ysra said, and sealed the hatch behind her.

Peak Concordance arrived without drama. That had taken them most of a decade.

Calyx opened around Mara as five complete bodies refusing to become parts. Sol's coupling sense entered as a set of available pressures, none of them commands. Eda laid the route across the dock and marked the sections that belonged to her judgment. Tern admitted four incompatible pictures of the debris field and one embarrassing song. Ysra's anatomy map placed pain beside load rather than beneath it. AFTER moved among them as learned timing, old aversion, present attention.

Mara accepted crown.

“First boundary,” she said.

Sol answered with the coupling tolerances. He had already destroyed the derivations. The values existed now only as perception passing through the body, useful for one final act and unavailable to whoever came looking afterward.

“Held,” he said.

Eda opened the departure route and then closed every branch that led toward a petitioner. The liaison core ran hot in her component, eating names, private requests, coordinates, proofs that would have made her look better.

“Held.”

Tern's accounts disagreed over whether the outer sensor spine could be released before the field collar. He gave Mara the disagreement without recommendation.

“Both held.”

Ysra's answer came last. “My limit remains my limit.”

Mara felt its position in the shared body: not a brake applied to the whole, not a plea for her judgment, simply a place the movement could not pass through and remain theirs.

“Held,” Mara said.

Mara wanted a small room with one difficult machine in it.

This was not that room.

AFTER touched the old catch.

Its attention passed through the five of them, pausing where their histories had made different damage. The answer did not arrive as consensus. AFTER had given them its fears during the planning: intervals that might become years, routes waking without one another, memories lost in forms no archive knew how to name. It had also challenged every intact proposal placed before it.

Mara did not ask whether it understood.

“Do you choose dispersal?” she asked.

The dock transmitted a recall demand on three official bands. Eda muted them one at a time.

AFTER gathered itself slowly. Warmth moved under Mara's hand, not repeating the old catch now but turning it outward. The learned pattern separated into five incomplete continuations. Each reached toward a different component. Each left something behind.

Then AFTER answered.

Yes.

The word was rough, assembled from actuator noise and six years of being waited for. It entered Concordance without taking crown.

Mara set the first release in motion.

The sensor spine went dark inside Concordance.

Tern's music stopped halfway through the chorus. His component released dorsal and cold-fired toward the outer debris lane, local beacon green. For nine seconds his sensor accounts remained available as four separate skies. Then the shared field lost their edges.

Mara kept the office open.

Eda burned the liaison core. The heat flashed through the drive field as a taste of copper. Names vanished. Access vanished. Eda's route remained, held precisely until its return condition, and then she gave it back.

Her component went dark inside Concordance. Mara saw its field flare blue as Eda rolled clear on the anti-spinward vector, drive awake and liaison core gone.

Sol complained once, because the second separation load pinched his new helmet seal.

“You moved,” Ysra said over local audio.

“I breathed.”

“Reckless.”

He swept the final coupling tolerances from the live body. Propulsion ceased to be shareable. The absence struck Mara harder than the release itself: a set of motions Calyx had known and would never know again.

Sol's component went dark inside Concordance. Its coupling clamps retracted; attitude jets pushed it sternward between the dock spars, helmet telemetry steady on Mara's local board.

The remaining body lurched. AFTER caught it.

Not perfectly. Perfection would have required routing through Ysra's limit.

Mara let the lurch stand. Ysra separated the next nerve bundle by hand, each motion slow enough to feel like indecision from outside. Inside Concordance it was exact. Pain arrived bright and private. Ysra did not distribute it for efficiency. She stated its location. Mara altered balance. AFTER carried the timing. Nobody called the cost agreement.

Ysra's breathing changed.

“Return,” Mara said.

“Not yet.”

The office gave Mara the ability to coordinate the next movement. It did not give her Ysra's body.

She waited.

One by one, the dispersed traces of AFTER withdrew from the coupling envelope. Some went cleanly. Some tore. One small anticipatory rhythm remained caught between Mara and Ysra, repeating the beginning of a protective movement after there was no longer a whole body to protect.

Ysra freed it.

Her component went dark inside Concordance. It drifted medical-spinward under the last separation impulse, Ysra's local carrier still transmitting breath and pulse.

Mara was alone in crown.

For the first time since Calyx had learned to move, the office contained no body large enough to obey it.

The recall demands continued. Cut field bands recoiled through the empty dock cradle. Mara's integration component listed toward the inner spars until she fired its independent thrusters and cleared them.

Mara released crown.

Nothing received it.

On the console, her tea had formed a dark skin. The cup trembled as the last field band failed. Mara caught it before it slid from the ledge.

The machine had become small enough to hold.
