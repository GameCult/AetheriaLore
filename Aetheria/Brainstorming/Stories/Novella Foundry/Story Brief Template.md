## Setting Authority

List sources in descending authority. Separate established canon from story-local invention. Name the historical boundaries and technologies or social conditions that must not appear.

### Ambient Cultural Axes

Choose only axes that should exert ordinary pressure throughout this story. Examples include class, religion, region, Movement, Mega sovereignty, profession, species, embodiment, language, generation, or legal identity.

For each selected axis, specify how it becomes visible through judgments, habits, interfaces, equipment, institutions, dependencies, jokes, and prejudice. Do not require explanatory tours. Unselected axes may remain background texture.

### Material Provenance

When relevant, treat gear, medicine, cognition, architecture, doctrine, and professional standards as products of institutions rather than neutral objects. Record who designed or controls them, what assumptions they embed, how users stereotype their provenance, and what incompatibilities or dependencies follow.

## Cast Seed

For each required person or collective, provide role, contradiction, competence, desire, refusal threshold, and relationship to the premise. Identify who may receive POV and who must remain outside it.

## Social Body

Name the workers, dependants, victims, institutions, markets, communities, and distant decision-makers whose choices or exposure matter. State which may remain aggregate actors.

## Causal Domain

Describe any warfare, mystery, economics, law, science, magic, romance, or other domain whose rules constrain outcomes. Distinguish:

- **Established:** sourced or mechanically demonstrated.
- **Provisional:** plausible and narratively admissible.
- **Contested:** too uncertain to decide an outcome alone.

## Reader Contract

- What must be understandable without setting homework?
- What mysteries should remain unresolved?
- What emotional attachment should exist by 10%, 25%, and 50%?
- What should a reader remember about each principal character without naming that character's job?
- What kinds of comedy, fear, wonder, or tenderness should recur?
- What would make the opening fail despite being technically competent?
