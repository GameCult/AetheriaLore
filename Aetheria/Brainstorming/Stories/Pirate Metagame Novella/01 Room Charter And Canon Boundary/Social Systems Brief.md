# Social Systems Brief

## Patent Fortune

The family's wealth comes from certified thermal-management components and control geometry. Their products are ubiquitous because failure is lethal and interoperability matters. The same certification regime also creates replacement monopolies, activation tokens, repossession covenants, and recurring royalties on continued habitability.

This produces a materially honest family conflict. The parents believe their work saves lives and can prove it. The captain discovers that the system can save a crew and seize its ship through the same automated action.

## Capital Progression

The captain begins with a liquidated hobby trust worth roughly eight percent of his expected inheritance. The first ship is purchased cleanly. Later money reaches pirate yards through increasingly lossy chains:

`royalty distribution -> respectable broker -> overvalued salvage or distressed claim -> yard credit note -> labor and components`

Early shadow-market premiums are tolerable. Public attribution, ship loss, and insurer attention progressively make clean money less useful. Eventually rescue guarantees, captured stock, reciprocal labor, and trusted sponsors matter more than nominal wealth.

He remains biographically rich while becoming operationally poor.

## Social Credit

Pirate and Navigator-adjacent witnesses care about:

- when evacuation was ordered;
- which property was sacrificed;
- whether low-status crew received equal priority;
- whether the shipmind was treated as crew;
- whether the captain remained exposed until evacuation completed;
- whether injury and dependant obligations were paid before the next refit.

The captain begins with inherited credit that pirate space distrusts. He gradually earns credit the family office cannot manufacture.

## Conduct

The capture-and-corridor cell may rob, coerce, board, intrude, ransom, and disable. Its boundary is not innocence. Its boundary is dependency and personhood:

> Never create a dependency failure larger than the prize, and never sell a person as part of the hull.

The rule generates disputes because indirect effects are hard to measure and people embedded in cognition products may be deliberately difficult to recognize.

## Loyalty Progression

1. The crew joins for pay, equipment, or opportunity.
2. The first sacrifice appears sentimental or panicked.
3. Repetition reveals a command invariant.
4. Experts with better offers stay because his incompetence is bounded in a way competent predators may not be.
5. Trust in evacuation priority permits more candid tactical disagreement.
6. The captain learns to turn disagreement into command rather than treating it as resistance to vision.
