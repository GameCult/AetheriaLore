# Prose And Dialogue Editor Report

## Concise Diagnosis

*The Burden of Proof* has a commanding sentence-level identity: dry institutional wit, exact technical language, bodily consequence, and arguments conducted through ownership verbs. Its best prose makes machinery social and makes social power material. The principal revision need is not simplification. It is selective deprivation. Too many scenes receive a polished interpretive seal after the action has already made the point, and too many speakers can produce the same compact antithesis under pressure. The result is a book full of individually strong lines that sometimes compete until strength becomes mannerism.

The exact phrase audit is less alarming than the cadence audit. `Both can be true` appears once, `The room changed` once, `The distinction mattered` twice, and the manuscript avoids the literal `That was not the question`. `Correct` and `Recorded` recur chiefly as procedural speech and often earn their place. The larger issue is the generative template around them: `not X but Y`, paired corrections, abstract noun followed by checksum, and scene endings that explain what the final object or refusal has already established. Revision should preserve the book's intellectual pressure while allowing more scenes to end in work, discomfort, error, silence, or an object still behaving.

## Three Strongest Successes

1. **Technical specificity repeatedly becomes lived experience.** Hull One's recovery mouth, Hull Two's continuing pump, Hull Three's cognition attack, and Hull Four's finite magazines are never decorative engineering. “Human work continued where the ship's knowledge ended,” Soren's withheld case, Meera's warm latch, and Pal's discarded embodied memory make systems legible through bodies and authority.

2. **The dry wit usually exposes pressure rather than decorating it.** “It would put the gun nearer the front of the sentence,” “At twenty gravities, the room will no longer match itself,” and “The form was designed by people who cannot imagine wanting less money” reveal profession, class, and frustration at once. The recurring tea, grain cake, washroom door, table, and pump jokes accumulate emotional meaning without demanding solemnity.

3. **Pal's POV has a genuinely distinct sensory and ethical grammar.** Doors become possible motion, rooms become addresses after extraction, care arrives as forecasts and allocations, and grief first appears as a stale control forecast. The closing refusal to fix Lorn's pump is an excellent culmination because action, authority, memory, and restraint coincide without a final thesis sentence.

## Voice-Specific Dialogue Guide

- **Pal:** exact, literal, service-conditioned, attentive to categories and permissions; humor comes from exposing the hidden premise of a request. Preserve sensory computation and the discomfort of anticipated care. Reduce occasions when Pal delivers a universally portable ethical maxim.
- **Luce:** ornate naming, persuasive framing, class-polished confidence, and a growing capacity to stop. His best later dialogue is shorter because restraint costs him. Preserve failed speeches, private doctrine names, and questions he cannot convert into plans.
- **Samira:** operational nouns, time, margin, objective, and blunt corrections. She should rarely decorate a decision. Her emotional life is strongest when a domestic detail or withheld future interrupts command language.
- **Sable:** recursive qualification, model language, precision, and impatience with categories that discard capability. In crisis, let their speech fragment or loop before it becomes aphoristic; the cognition attack is strongest where explanation itself becomes suspect.
- **Mara:** physical verbs, security categories, suspicion, clipped commands. Her change should appear in altered orders and restraint, not increasing fluency in the book's philosophy. Preserve “Then replacement is not the word” as an earned exception.
- **Tess:** enforceability, priority, collateral, and weaponized administrative precision. Her jokes should sound like invoices discovering contempt. She can sustain polished antithesis more naturally than most speakers, but even she benefits from occasional unfinished anger.
- **Jo:** geometry, rounds, feeds, physical pleasure, and irreverence. Keep guns near the front of his sentences. Avoid giving him generalized institutional interpretation outside weapons and labor.
- **Dena:** local ownership, hands, queues, cooling, and what machinery demands after command stops watching. Her dialogue should remain concrete and resistant to summary.
- **Rin:** calibration, independent witness, atmosphere, and domestic accountability. Her speech is strongest when evidence ownership and bodily environment coincide.
- **Soren:** meals, feeds, remittances, ordinary work, and rueful compromise. Preserve warmth and household stakes; avoid promoting him into a thesis spokesman.
- **Rhyne:** austere institutional judgment, separate fields, local authority, and consequences beyond her control. Her dryness should remain colder than Tess's.
- **Corvid speakers:** compressed conditions, authorship, boundaries, and refusal; humor arises from translation mismatch, tool practice, and plural presence. Do not make every utterance cryptic or juridical. Chapter 23's teaching tile and coffee-filter prehistory provide the needed cultural breadth.

## High-Impact Findings

### Essential Revisions

1. **Chapters 9-18 repeatedly close action with interpretation.** Location: especially Chapters 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, and 18. Problem: losses and redesigns are often followed by a polished sentence that states the governing contradiction. Evidence includes “The next failure did not wait for those improvements to become false,” “The distinction mattered less than movement, but people moved,” and multiple certificate/allocation summaries. Intervention: remove or blunt roughly one in three checksum sentences where the preceding object, order, or refusal already carries the meaning. Expected benefit: more reader participation and greater variation among endings. Collateral risk: excessive cutting could make the institutional causality seem merely episodic. Confidence: high.

2. **Transferable antithesis flattens speaker distinction.** Location: throughout, most visible in command and review scenes. Problem: Pal, Samira, Sable, Tess, Mara, Rhyne, and minor workers can all answer with balanced corrections such as `X does this; Y does that`, `not permission but...`, or `those are separate fields`. Intervention: reserve polished antithesis for characters whose work trains it, chiefly Tess, Rhyne, Pal, and sometimes Sable. Give Samira time/objective speech, Mara physical directives, Dena work nouns, and workers more idiolect, hesitation, profanity, or local detail. Expected benefit: dialogue attribution improves without tags and philosophical authority becomes distributed rather than stylistically homogenized. Collateral risk: do not make working people less articulate to manufacture class difference. Confidence: high.

3. **Emergency dialogue remains too review-ready in several climaxes.** Location: Chapter 10's recovery-mouth strike, Chapter 17 before quarantine, and Chapter 27 during quorum/rescue. Problem: speakers often formulate complete distinctions while seconds are closing. Chapter 17 largely earns this because language itself is under attack; Chapters 10 and 27 sometimes sound as if the incident record is being drafted live. Intervention: shorten immediate speech to verbs, numbers, refusals, and misunderstood fragments; move one or two explanatory distinctions into aftermath or embodied response. Expected benefit: greater velocity and sharper contrast between crisis and review. Collateral risk: tactical legibility must remain intact. Confidence: high.

4. **Chapter 27's emotional line is intermittently submerged by institutional nouns.** Location: from Rhea's archive demand through the freeze. Problem: `continuity event`, `procedure owner`, `local custodian`, `claimants`, `quorum`, `neutral custody`, and multiple paths are all necessary, but repeated full labels compete with the simpler dramatic line: keep a communication path alive while workers decide, refuse the title vault, rescue people when the relay breaks. Intervention: after each term is established, use concrete referents: Rhea's lever, Anik's route, the relay, the vault, the shepherd. Cut repeated redefinition. Expected benefit: readers retain both the politics and the pulse. Collateral risk: over-pronouning could blur authority. Confidence: high.

### High-Value Revisions

5. **The manuscript overuses abstract-noun summary after excellent concrete prose.** Location: chapter and section tails across all movements. Examples include paragraphs beginning or ending with `The distinction`, `The correction`, `The victory`, `The procedure`, `The sentence`, `The choice`, or `The record`. Intervention: audit every demonstrative/abstract opening after a scene. Keep it only if it adds causal information unavailable in the image. Expected benefit: less planning-aware prose and more rhythm. Collateral risk: some abstractions are the book's legitimate institutional vocabulary. Confidence: high.

6. **The “nobody” cadence has become a visible moral checksum.** Location: Chapters 2, 11, 25, plus nearby variants. “Nobody congratulated him,” “Nobody thanked him,” and “Nobody applauded” are effective individually, but together they announce the book's refusal to reward Luce. Intervention: preserve “Nobody congratulated him” in the training drill, where it establishes the pattern. In later echoes, let ongoing labor or another person's unrelated concern deny the ceremony without naming the absence. Expected benefit: Luce's non-absolution remains structural rather than authorially stamped. Collateral risk: none if actions stay visible. Confidence: high.

7. **Minor characters sometimes arrive with a complete miniature ethical dossier.** Location: hiring in Chapter 2, domestic survey in Chapter 3, casualty/renewal passages in Chapters 12 and 18, coalition inventory in Chapter 25. Problem: efficient summaries give each worker a position, household consequence, and principled refusal in the same polished register. Intervention: retain the material facts but vary delivery: a badly timed message, repeated practical complaint, object carried, mistake, or private appetite. Consolidate adjacent dossier paragraphs when no later scene activates the person. Expected benefit: secondary lives feel inhabited rather than ethically accounted. Collateral risk: do not erase labor and dependants from the book to make elites seem more vivid. Confidence: medium-high.

8. **Humor is strongest when reactive and weakest when everyone supplies the expected dry beat.** Location: command tables and post-action reviews, especially Chapters 4, 12, 19, and 27. Intervention: keep jokes that alter status or expose professional irritation; cut some second and third rejoinders after the first clean hit. Let failed jokes remain failed, as in Tess and Luce aboard Hull Three. Expected benefit: wit regains surprise and tonal range. Collateral risk: the playful warmth is protected identity; this is thinning, not grimification. Confidence: medium-high.

9. **Scene endings cluster around aphorism, object report, and withheld interpretation.** Location: nearly every chapter. The object-report endings are generally strongest: the table ceases reporting, the pump continues, the washroom door cycles, the warm latch holds departure, Pal does not fix the pump. Intervention: protect those; vary intervening endings with an unanswered practical question, dialogue cut short, bodily need, or mundane continuation. Remove explanatory penultimate lines when the final object already resonates. Expected benefit: chapter rhythm becomes less forecastable. Confidence: high.

### Optional Refinements

10. **Some three-part lists feel composed at the same intensity regardless of POV.** Location: institutional inventories, memory triads, and consequence summaries throughout. Intervention: keep triads where accumulation is rhetorical action; break or shorten those serving only cadence. Expected benefit: fewer sentences calling attention to manufacture. Confidence: medium.

## Proposed Cuts, Consolidations, Relocations, And Expansions

- Cut or recast 30-40 checksum sentences across the manuscript, concentrated in Chapters 9-18 and 25-27.
- Consolidate adjacent ethical dossiers in Chapters 2-3 and recurring claim recap in Chapters 12, 18, 22, and 25.
- Relocate a small amount of Chapter 10 and 27 explanation from live emergency channels into immediate aftermath records.
- Selectively expand no new argument. If adding anything, add only brief non-argumentative reaction or mundane interruption where a cut leaves a scene too polished.
- No broad compression quota is justified at prose level. A sentence-and-paragraph pass likely removes 4-7 percent while improving pace; structural cuts belong to the structural editor.

## Cross-Role Conflicts

- A structural editor may seek large cuts in Chapters 12 and 18. Preserve enough polyphony to show how one rescue object becomes different instruments under different owners; prose compression should remove repeated interpretation, not the authority chain.
- A reader-experience editor may ask to standardize terminology. Prefer local concrete referents after first definition, but do not erase meaningful distinctions among rescue, custody, title, bailment, and service.
- A character editor may request more private appetite for secondary workers. Support brief embodied or domestic substitutions, not added explanatory biographies.
- A theme editor will likely agree on checksum removal. Resist cutting every explicit argument: Tess, Rhyne, Pal, and Sable sometimes act through exact language, and enforceability is part of the drama.

## One Recommendation To Resist

Resist any recommendation to make the dialogue “more natural” by removing technical, legal, or philosophical precision across the board. These characters often exercise authority through exact language, and the book's politics live in what a term permits someone to do. The problem is not that they are articulate. The problem is that too many are articulate in the same polished rhythm. Differentiate the machinery of articulation; do not replace it with generic casual speech.

## Final Line-Edit Checklist

- Search every `not X / Y`, `both`, `correct`, `recorded`, `distinction`, `question`, `sentence`, `room`, and `nobody` construction in context.
- For every abstract-noun sentence after action, ask whether it adds causality or merely certifies interpretation.
- Read dialogue without tags and identify the speaker from profession, body, desire, and authority.
- In emergencies, privilege command verb, object, number, location, and refusal.
- Preserve technical terms when they change rights or choices; shorten repeated full labels afterward.
- Cut competing epigrams around a line chosen for preservation.
- Vary scene exits: work continuing, bodily need, mundane interruption, error, silence, object behavior, or unanswered question.
- Keep humor attached to status, work, embarrassment, or conflict; remove scheduled rejoinders.
- Check that Corvid dialogue includes practice, humor, memory, and private boundary as well as contract language.
- Check that worker detail appears as life and labor, not only as an ethical accounting entry.
- Keep Pal's sensory-computational metaphors specific to available embodiment.
- Read Chapters 9-18 consecutively after revision to ensure the redesign/loss rhythm no longer exposes its template.
- Read the final operation aloud for noun load and breath length.
- End on the pump. Do not explain it.
