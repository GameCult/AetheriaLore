# Reader Experience Writer Proposals

## Governing Aim

These proposals treat hierarchy, not simplification, as the primary reader-experience repair. Technical and legal material remains when it changes a choice, body, livelihood, authority, or tactical objective. The preferred cut is the second certification after the reader has already understood the action.

The anchors below refer to the immutable baseline at `Aetheria/Fiction/The Burden of Proof.md`. Sample language is illustrative candidate prose, not a claim on final voice.

## Cross-Chapter Assembly Checks

1. **Entry audit:** On assembly, read only the first page of every chapter in sequence. Each should establish viewpoint, present desire, changed state, and concrete situation without turning those four items into a formula.
2. **Hull audit:** At Chapters 9, 13, 16, and 19, verify that the reader can state which hull is current, what survived transfer, what did not, and broad elapsed time.
3. **Cast-recall audit:** Search clusters of three or more secondary names. Add at most one relational, bodily, or object cue where absence makes recall genuinely uncertain; otherwise cut redundant introductions.
4. **Term hierarchy audit:** In each scene, identify the one active claim or procedural term that changes choice. Retain other terms only when they establish conflict, provenance, or later causality.
5. **Tactical-objective audit:** Before each operation, verify the reader can state the objective and protected limit in one sentence. During reversals, update only what changed.
6. **Afterlife audit:** After every major action, retain one unique aftermath job and two or three representative consequences. Preserve transmission paths; cut completeness theater.
7. **Modulation audit:** Protect meals, waiting, maintenance, messages, bodily care, refusal, and departure from being consumed by compression. They are the book's recovery intervals and often its emotional proof.
8. **Serial-continuation audit:** Prefer chapter exits that leave a changed pressure, unresolved desire, or concrete next danger. Remove polished conclusions that close both action and interpretation when the following chapter needs momentum.
