# Coordinator Synthesis

> **Status:** Accepted coordinator blueprint. This document adjudicates council evidence and governs candidate revision. It does not itself amend the manuscript or canonical story state.

## Evidence Frame

The immutable baseline is the 50,836-word, 28-chapter manuscript at `Aetheria/Fiction/The Burden of Proof.md`, Git blob `a7ccee35e3a34a601f041652b24f0cb6e6be0ba6`. The working hierarchy remains: setting canon governs boundaries; the accepted manuscript governs depicted events and language; coordinator-approved sideband governs latent facts and future constraints; unresolved disagreement stops the affected revision.

Two evidence classes must not be conflated:

- **Reader evidence:** the Reader Experience report is a manuscript-only agent cold read. Its confusion, fatigue, recall, emotional-center, and skimming observations are direct evidence about this reading experience, but not independent human-panel evidence. The most important findings are insufficient hierarchy inside abundance, weak reorientation around hull/cast transitions, competition between tactical and legal information, repetitive institutional afterlives, and a strong retained emotional center in Pal and Luce.
- **Primed council evidence:** the other six reports were written with the Revision Brief, specialist remits, and relevant sideband available. Their convergence is useful diagnosis, continuity analysis, and design reasoning, but it is correlated evidence from agents sharing model lineage and context. Claims such as the exact Corvid vote structure, Pal embodiment topology, chronology, authority ownership, and resource arithmetic are sideband-grounded constraints rather than cold-reader discoveries.

The strongest conclusion is supported by both classes: the book is not failing because it contains too much machinery. It is tiring because too many scenes complete their material argument and then certify it through interpretation, institutional propagation, or a second character's polished gloss.

## Core Promise

*The Burden of Proof* is a character-driven systems novel about whether care can become freely chosen after care has been trained, rewarded, owned, and made indispensable. Luce keeps building increasingly sophisticated answers to coercion; Pal, the crew, workers, captives, claimants, and the Twelvefold force those answers to become bounded institutions rather than extensions of his virtue. Four successive ships embody four arguments about recoverability, visible repair, cognition, and local authority. The climax succeeds because no sovereign mind owns the whole decision, and the ending preserves Pal's temporary presence without converting it into service, absolution, or permanence.

Revision must make that promise easier to feel, not easier to summarize.

## Top Five Problems

1. **Repeated completion.** Enacted proof is followed by moral checksum, claim propagation, renewal catalogue, or scene-tail interpretation. The concentration is highest in Chapters 9-18 and 25-28.
2. **Insufficient hierarchy inside abundance.** Hull state, chapter desire, tactical objective, legal claim, and emotional cost often arrive at equal intensity. Readers understand sentences locally while losing confidence about what must be retained.
3. **A shared dialect of correction.** Too many characters speak in polished distinctions, paired abstractions, and adjudicative epigrams. This weakens private texture and makes the cast sound like one distributed editorial intelligence.
4. **Uneven cultural and personal accumulation.** Supporting crew are often vivid by department; Corvid people are rigorous as authorities but receive too little early childhood, grief, humor, authorship, and interpersonal continuity. Samira's Nia cost and Sable's bodily/class dependence are underfed before late payoff.
5. **A few load-bearing ambiguities.** Corvid campaign-vote wording, Pal's 118 kg/28 kg/mobile-body topology, the continuity-freeze causal chain, movement elapsed time, and Jo's final hand strain need compact clarification. These are not plot failures, but compression could turn them into contradictions.

## Disagreements And Proposed Rulings

### Compression target

**Disagreement:** The initial plan entertained 12-18 percent. Structural analysis argues 8-12 percent, approximately 9 percent preferred. Other reports recommend many line and aftermath cuts without proving a larger total.

**Proposed ruling:** Treat **6-10 percent** as the first-pass hypothesis, with **8-9 percent** the likely landing zone. Do not set a quota. A second pass may reach 10-12 percent only if fresh-reader evidence still shows fatigue and no protected worker, Corvid, domestic, custody, or loss material has been thinned. Reject 12-18 percent as an advance target.

### Middle-cycle simplification

**Disagreement:** Reader and structural evidence identify Chapters 9-18 as repetitive; a tempting response is to merge failures or remove a ship. Theme, systems, and character reports argue the four ships make distinct propositions and losses.

**Proposed ruling:** Preserve every ship and loss chapter. Shorten setup and certification around them. Chapters 10, 11, 15, and 17 remain near current scale; Chapters 9, 12, 13, 16, and 18 become more asymmetrical and selective.

### Aftermath consolidation

**Disagreement:** Full aftermath maps establish systems consequence, but reader evidence shows predictable drag and primed reports warn that harmed people become validation nodes.

**Proposed ruling:** Give each aftermath one unique structural job and two or three representative consequences. Keep transmission paths required for later knowledge or claims. Delete secondary examples that prove an already legible proposition. Do not merge POVs in ways that grant forbidden knowledge.

### Character expansion versus compression

**Disagreement:** Supporting characters need private texture, while the manuscript needs fewer words and fewer names active at once.

**Proposed ruling:** Expand only by **replacement and relocation**. Trade checksum prose or redundant institutional examples for brief embodied recurrence: Samira/Nia, Sable's maintenance and clinic dependence, one Ilya/Ren beat, one Edda/Miko or self-concealment beat, and one Rhyne maintenance/Paz marker. Do not add new POVs for Ilya or Edda merely because sideband once proposed them.

### Corvid orientation versus opacity

**Disagreement:** Cold-reader evidence reports difficulty retaining individuals. Corvid design requires private identity and rejects a human-readable roster key.

**Proposed ruling:** Orient through stable public role-name, repeated individual action, object, relationship, and consequence. Seed the cracked teaching tile, cache loss, Tlak/Vek trust, Qerr's contested humor, and authorship earlier. Never publish private identity topology or collapse twelve declarations into consensus.

### Final-operation explanation

**Disagreement:** The climax needs exact legal and resource causality, but institutional nouns compete with its emotional line.

**Proposed ruling:** Move one compact failure-state explanation to Chapter 22 or 25: two witnessed local commits on enrolled assets are required for recognized continuity freeze. In Chapter 27, foreground the verb chain: **workers commit; custodians witness; the route transmits; enrolled institutions recognize**. Keep exact entrance, threshold, and exit resource states; cut intermediate accounting that does not change choice.

### Pal embodiment terminology

**Disagreement:** Sideband says 118 kg portable shell; Chapter 24 names a 28 kg cognition shell; Chapter 28 uses a small wheeled body while “the shell” remains berthed. The intended nesting is plausible but not explicit.

**Ruling:** No redesign. Pal's twenty-eight-kilogram cognition shell is the removable inner continuity body seated inside the 118-kilogram portable support shell, which supplies emergency power, cooling, networking, sensors, and carry structure. Emergency hull evacuation moves the complete 118-kilogram assembly. Controlled harbor transfer may remove the inner shell onto a shock trolley. The wheeled maintenance body is a local peripheral and contains neither continuity nor the portable support plant. Establish this topology once before Chapter 24 depends on it.

### Corvid contract terminology

**Ruling:** The Twelvefold is one contracted unit receiving one economic cut and two campaign-council votes. The parliament may split those two votes, and neither vote lends individual consent. Chapter 23's line claiming outsiders count one vote and Chapter 28's `one contractual vote` are continuity errors; revise them to preserve one cut, two votes, and twelve persons/rescue/medical states.

## Movement Foregrounds

### Movement I: Beautiful Answers

**Foreground:** Luce's desire to turn moral injury into a beautiful institution, and the crew's conversion of that institution into enforceable limits. Each operation should be read through one bodily dependency rather than through the completeness of its doctrine. **Foreground:** three increasingly voluntary and intimate losses strip property, embodiment, cognition, custody, and loyalty apart. **Revision pressure:** preserve loss chapters; shorten setup and certification; distinguish hulls at chapter entry; let grief remain active when the next ship arrives; vary aftermath scale; protect knowledge transmission and injury continuity.

### Movement III: The Uses Of An Honest Ship

**Foreground:** honest machinery creates usable limits but cannot choose the right objective. The final victory comes from bounded authorities preserving a route through which workers can decide. Pal's free choice occurs before victory and remains valid after the operation ends.

**Revision pressure:** let Chapter 21 carry the critique of Chapter 20; turn Chapters 22-26 into a tightening launch sequence; state the final failure condition early; keep one argument foregrounded at a time in Chapter 27; contract fully to embodied presence in Chapter 28.
