# Corvid Culture Writer Proposals

## Governing Position

These proposals preserve the Twelvefold as twelve private persons, one contracted unit, one economic cut, two campaign-council votes, and twelve independent bodily, medical, rescue, cache, and consent states. Human-facing `Huginn` and `Muninn` remain rotating public roles. No proposal supplies a roster key, stabilizes either role-name to a body, or gives a human POV access to true-name topology.

The revision need is distributive. Chapter 23 already proves that Corvid culture can carry parliament, humor, grief, inheritance, authorship, disagreement, and non-instrumental life. Earlier chapters too often employ Corvid people as correct operational limits. The strongest intervention is to establish a few recurring cultural objects and relationships before they become tactically useful, funding additions by cutting adjacent checksum prose.

## Ranked Proposals

### 1. Chapter 6, `Service Interval`: Seed inherited error as Corvid humor

- **Priority:** Essential.
- **Exact anchor:** After `Dena tried again. The result made two ravens leave and one steal her bread.` and before `Soren laughed despite the unanswered message.`
- **Action:** Add 90-140 words. Let the failed tool request provoke a second, primarily Corvid exchange around an old navigation error stored on a small layered ceramic object. Humans witness incompatible reactions but receive no translation. Do not yet call it a juvenile teaching tile or explain its permissions.
- **Subject rationale:** The chapter already gives Corvid language, play, privacy, and a scene in which nothing advances the campaign. It is the cleanest place to establish that jokes circulate among Corvid people, accrue contested age, and can become authored inheritance. The humor should not exist mainly to decorate human domesticity.
- **Sample insertion:**

  > A second raven answered with three notes too close together for Dena to separate. The others reacted unevenly: one struck the perch in delight; another turned their head away with elaborate offense. Muninn pulled a palm-sized ceramic leaf from beneath the table, touched a cracked-looking line near its edge, and repeated Dena's error back to the room. This produced more argument than laughter.
  >
  > "Was that better?" Dena asked.
  >
  > "Older," Huginn said.
  >
  > Nobody translated the distinction.

- **Grounding:** Chapter 23 establishes the layered ceramic teaching tile, its oldest navigation error, authored jokes, dead mentors' corrections, and unequal permission to revise. The Twelvefold Replay ties Kesh and Tik-tik to juvenile continuity, Qerr to authored humor, and Ek to control of administrative truth.
- **Benefit:** Chapter 23 becomes recognition rather than introduction. Corvid childhood and humor exist before the plot needs the tile as evidence.
- **Collateral risk:** Calling the object a schoolbook, explaining the joke, or making Dena's error the origin of the inherited joke would domesticate and cheapen it. Preserve ambiguity about whether this is the same physical tile until Chapter 23.

### 2. Chapter 12, `Terms of Rescue`: Embody cache loss and denied consolation

- **Priority:** Essential.
- **Exact anchor:** After `Eleven members of the Twelvefold voted to continue. Vek abstained until the lost cache received standing in the damage record.`
- **Action:** Add 150-220 words of behavior before the chapter closes. Vek inventories an unrecoverable absence; Tlak offers a duplicate or repair channel and is refused; another raven closes the human-facing liaison path around the exchange. Samira may observe boundaries and material actions, not interpret the relationship.
- **Subject rationale:** Human hull loss receives shirts, tools, a warped token, soot, and rooms. Corvid archive loss currently resolves chiefly as a vote condition. The cache must be felt as authored social continuity, not another asset class. Tlak/Vek's damaged trust gives the loss a recurring relationship without exposing private identity topology.
- **Sample insertion:**

  > At the far end of the species-care gallery, one raven placed three intact cache wafers beside an empty restraint sleeve. Another moved a duplicate toward the sleeve. The offer stopped halfway between them.
  >
  > "Not requested," said Muninn from the overhead rail.
  >
  > The duplicate withdrew. No one filled the empty sleeve. A third raven turned the clinic display so its inventory field faced the wall before the humans could decide whether the omission was damage, privacy, or both.
  >
  > Samira added `unrecovered private archive; title retained by claimant` to the loss record and left the contents blank.

- **Grounding:** Twelvefold Replay: Vek voluntarily yields an irreplaceable archive, grieves severely, and does not permit Tlak to console; Tlak later receives one entrusted duplicate shard. Qerr loses a private joke archive; Tik-tik loses a juvenile remittance route; Ek loses administrative identities. The report specifically calls for behavior-led aftermath rather than ledger explanation.
- **Benefit:** Vek's abstention becomes a particular judgment with relationship history. Later entrusted access can register as change.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not sentimentalize the scene, reveal cache contents, or let Samira narrate what the refusal means. Do not make Tlak's offer accepted here; that would erase the sideband's repair arc.

### 3. Chapter 13, `A Reasonable Doubt`: Establish Tlak/Vek trust through bounded access

- **Priority:** High-value.
- **Exact anchor:** Rewrite the three-sentence sequence beginning `The inspection found one hidden supplier path capable of locking Pal's cradle interface. Vek identified it.`
- **Action:** Rewrite in place, at equal or lower word count, so Tlak explicitly refrains from requesting Vek's cache route while producing the public-record bypass. Keep all work under bounded authority and opaque IDs.
- **Subject rationale:** The current passage already places Vek and Tlak beside one another but treats them as interchangeable technical contributors. A single refusal to presume access continues Chapter 12's relationship without decoding which public body is which.

Tlak did not request the private route behind that finding. Ek refused to trade inspection of the Corvid tool station for removal instructions; Tlak built the clean bypass from public maintenance records instead.

Existing Chapter 13 authority labels already permit Vek, Ek, and Tlak to be named without mapping them to bodies or role-names.
- **Benefit:** Trust accrues through restraint. The reader can follow a relationship shape while the private identity boundary remains intact.
- **Collateral risk:** Do not explain the older breach here. The restraint should carry its own pressure.

### 4. Chapter 15, `Abandonment`: Pay off entrusted duplicate custody

- **Priority:** High-value.
- **Exact anchor:** After `The Twelvefold moved private caches through declared routes and abandoned one volume whose contents remained theirs.`
- **Action:** Add 45-80 words. Vek assigns one duplicate shard to Tlak during evacuation; Tlak accepts without opening, indexing, or merging it. Keep the moment subordinate to abandonment tempo.
- **Subject rationale:** Chapter 12 establishes refused consolation and Chapter 13 establishes non-presumptive work. This third beat converts trust into a concrete, bounded action under pressure. It is not reconciliation by speech and not access to the primary archive.
- **Sample insertion:**

  > Vek passed one sealed duplicate to Tlak across the rotating spine. Tlak authenticated custody without opening the index and clipped it inside a separate rescue pouch. Neither role-name announced the transfer. The primary cache remained with Vek until the route closed.

- **Grounding:** Twelvefold Replay, Tlak Campaign V and Vek Campaign V: Vek allows Tlak to carry one duplicate shard during evacuation, beginning repair; the primary replacement cache is still abandoned.
- **Benefit:** A recurring relationship changes through action while loss remains loss. The duplicate does not compensate for the abandoned primary cache.
- **Collateral risk:** The exact campaign label in sideband associates this entrustment with the Hull Two evacuation. Preserve that placement. Do not imply Tlak receives live keys or full contents.

### 5. Chapter 8, `The Courtesy of an Open Door`: Give Ek authorship, not only authentication

- **Exact anchor:** Rewrite the exchange beginning `"Transmission authentic," Muninn said. "Passenger consent absent. We will not manufacture it for custody."`

- **Subject rationale:** Ek's present line makes the correct ethical check. The revision should also establish a Corvid author deciding the permissible meaning of a record. This prepares Chapter 23's demand that copied records retain author and claimant.
- **Sample replacement:**

- **Grounding:** Cast Bible and Twelvefold Replay: Ek specializes in identity and provenance, authenticates Kavi, refuses protective custody forgery, and claims authorship over administrative truth.
- **Benefit:** The final authorship mechanism develops from established cultural practice rather than appearing as a late capability.
- **Collateral risk:** Avoid a provenance lecture. Kavi's testimony remains Kavi's; Ek owns the integrity decision, not the testimony.

### 6. Chapter 10, `The Open Mouth`: Surface a real minority position

- **Priority:** High-value.
- **Exact anchor:** In the tactical decision immediately before the return-mouth catastrophe, at the first Corvid recommendation concerning deception or retrieval geometry.
- **Action:** Replace a single consensus-like liaison recommendation with two terse authorized statements: Qerr favors preserving a deception package or one more ambiguous interval; Kesh gives exact headcount/retrieval priority. Qerr complies after losing the task argument, without being made foolish.

- **Collateral risk:** Exact placement must not slow the catastrophe or imply a parliament vote can override an individual's unsafe assignment. Kesh owns the emergency priority for the named interval; Qerr retains dissent.
