## Scene-Shape Constraints

- No more than two consecutive chapters may end on a formal decision or signed document.
- Chapters 6, 13, 19, and 24 protect ordinary pleasure and inconvenience from plot compression.
- Hull-loss chapters contain an interval in which a person addresses the loss without immediately explaining it.
- Chapters 12 and 18 use documents differently: the first reinterprets a rescue; the second physically transfers custody. Chapter 16 privileges Pal's uninterrupted embodied silence over its allocation handoff.
- Chapters 22-27 express coalitional politics through veto, delay, refusal, signature, withheld access, and sacrificed value rather than constituency speeches.
- Chapter 27 presents one foreground question at a time: vote, temptation, casualty, movement, commit, disengagement, home.
