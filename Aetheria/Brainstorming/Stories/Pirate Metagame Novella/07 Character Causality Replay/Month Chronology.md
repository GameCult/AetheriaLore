## Solvency And Lead-Time Check

- Hull One operates for roughly twenty-two months after commissioning. Eight named months contain deployment, combat, or high-tempo recovery; fourteen are low-burn, yard, berth, escort, rescue, salvage, or ordinary work. At the accepted 290,000 SU active burn and 92,000 SU low-burn average, gross mundane income plus bounded Wake credit covers protected obligations without implying a spare refit fortune.
- Hull Two's conversion takes roughly twelve weeks, inside the accepted 10-16-week emergency range.
- Hull Three takes eight months from financing to delivery, inside its 7-9-month range, then receives four months of trials before first combat.
- Hull Four takes eight months, inside its 7-9-month range.
- Rin, Kesh, Trraak, Khraa, Tlak, Rruk, Sable, and Edda receive recovery or calibration time before later duties. No injury vanishes between adjacent scenes.
- Testimony and insurer doctrine receive at least weeks, usually months, to propagate.

## Evacuation Seating Check

Two eighteen-person boats carry the complete thirty-six-person complement: thirty-five biological persons and Pal's physical shell.

- **Hull One:** eighteen assigned seats per boat; Pal's shell occupies one reinforced seat after manual extraction.
- **Hull Two:** wave one puts twelve aboard boat A; wave two puts twelve aboard boat B; wave three moves the remaining eleven biological people plus Pal sequentially through the single usable interface, adding six seats to each boat. Final load is eighteen and eighteen.
- **Hull Three:** the two isolated mechanical routes terminate at separate boats. Seventeen biological people plus Pal use boat one; eighteen biological people use boat two. The shell reaches boat-one cooling handoff by minute twenty-three.

These allocations are planning truth, not a requirement to dramatize loading in prose.
