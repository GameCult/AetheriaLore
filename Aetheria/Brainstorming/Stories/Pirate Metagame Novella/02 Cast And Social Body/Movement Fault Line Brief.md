# Movement Fault Line Brief

Mara Kest and Sable Orison disagree about where authority and risk should live, not whether technology exists.

Mara's baseline politics correctly identify enhancement caste, proprietary medicine, labor displacement, and unequal mortality. Her anthropocentrism converts that analysis into exclusion. She favors legible plans, physical confirmation, local overrides, simple fire priorities, and human-scale abort conditions. Her prior Front organizing harmed Corvids and augmented workers.

Sable's Enhancement Liberal politics defend bodily autonomy and oppose licensing. Their premium Finch architecture remains a material privilege sustained by scarce specialist labor. They favor distributed sensing, revisable commitments, smart terminal inspection, recall authority, and bounded machine-speed delegation. Their temptation is to mistake superior processing for superior authority.

They do not reconcile through debate. Trust grows only when each can predict the other's refusal, sees it survive personal cost, and builds a plan that does not require moral surrender. Mara may act better without becoming forgiven. Sable may protect autonomy without ceasing to benefit from caste infrastructure.
