# Paradise Denied

**Status:** active story room; completed entry brief, pre-Pass 1.

An unnamed man in a failing late-Sol Bloom wants the terminal escape his friends chose, but every reward architecture offered to him produces unbearable contradiction instead of bliss. Clinics classify him as a treatment failure. He drinks, injures himself, and turns his despair outward as cruelty until the Rupture turns the dissonance inside his mind into a property of the world.

Crowds become torture. Dreamer repositories become the only places he can find that feel quiet. When one stored mind begins screaming through that quiet, he recognizes the condition every provider refused to understand and enters the vault after them. The novel follows refusal becoming care without pretending that suffering was necessary, ennobling, or harmless to the people forced to survive beside it.

## Current Invariants

- The protagonist's resistance to wireheading is an anomaly, not strength of character, purity, or chosen-one proof.
- His suffering explains his cruelty without making other people acceptable collateral.
- The Rupture worsens his condition before it makes him useful.
- Resonance tracks unresolved distinctions among possibilities, not pain or pleasure as such.
- Dreamers remain people. Their quiet does not make extraction, abandonment, or custody automatically legitimate.
- Care begins when another mind becomes real enough to alter behavior; it does not require absorption, control, or self-erasure.
- The ending may give suffering a use in the protagonist's later choices, never a retroactive reason.
