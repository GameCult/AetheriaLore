# Ship Prompt Bench

> **Status: brainstorming bench, Imagination pass of 2026-10-03.** Not canon and not adopted. These prompts extend [[Brainstorming/Faction Ship Concept Prompts|Faction Ship Concept Prompts]] past the release bar of 13 hulls (the 3 existing hulls and the 10 faction concepts) and 3 stations. Roles come from the faction doctrines in [[Game Design/Faction Play|Faction Play]]. Looks come from [[Brainstorming/Faction Flavor and Visual Identity|Faction Flavor and Visual Identity]]. The hardpoint vocabulary is from [[Game Design/Ship-shape and Up to Specs|Ship-shape and Up to Specs]].
>
> **Superseded for new renders by [[Brainstorming/Ship Prompt Bench 2|Ship Prompt Bench 2]] (2026-10-04).** The lessons this bench taught are guidelines 10 to 12 in [[Brainstorming/Faction Ship Concept Prompts|Faction Ship Concept Prompts]].

Operator brief, 2026-10-03: "a bunch of proposals for unique ship prompts since that'll enable me to spend my time generating content the next few days." And: "I'll want multiple attempts per role since a lot of them are bound to be duds. This is also an opportunity to explore faction aesthetics so tell it to go wild, I wanna see cool moonshot ideas with flavor".

## How to Use This Bench

- Each faction opens with an **aesthetic thesis**, the visual language its attempts explore. When an attempt is a dud, the thesis still says what it was trying to show.
- Each **role** has one procurement pitch with its hardpoints. Under it sit two or three **attempts**, each built on a different analogy and given its own hull name. Each attempt is labelled **Safe bet** or **Moonshot**. A moonshot states the guideline it bends and why it might still work.
- Every prompt is ready to paste. Each ends with the standard frame clause from the guidelines; station prompts swap "ship" for "station".
- The guidelines are treated as craft rather than as a cage. Every attempt still uses one analogy whose anatomy places the parts, and one mass.

## Riskiest for Image-to-3D

These are the attempts most likely to fail at the Tripo stage, as distinct from failing at the image stage.

- *Squatter (Pirate, hermit crab):* the claw and the borrowed shell form two objects in a relation, and the asymmetric claw has no hidden-side counterpart. Supply a matching rear view or accept a guessed far side.
- *Mainline Hit (DME, amp stack):* the four speaker cones are deep concavities, and Tripo may fill them or punch them through.
- *Clock Seed (Megiddo, dandelion head):* a radial seed head is thin-feature soup; it survives only if the image keeps the seeds as fat, fused nubs.
- *Stiletto (Alakrita, high-heel shoe):* the heel is a thin strut by nature, and the arch under the sole is a see-through gap.
- *Peacock Train (Alakrita):* the crest and the folded train want to become thin plumes.
- *Rime (Cryonix, pinecone):* dozens of overlapping scales are fine relief that reconstruction tends to smear into noise or turn into spikes.
- *Showroom (Framgång, travel trailer):* the trailer reads correctly only if it is shiny. The prompt asks for matte anodized metal, so the image may lose the identity or Tripo may misread the sheen.
- *Trophy (Lucent, trophy cup):* the cup's handles are arches by nature. The prompt fills them, but the image model may open them.
- *Skipping Stone (Adrasteia):* the pebble deliberately places no parts, so Tripo gets a clean mass and the game gets an unreadable one. It is a silhouette test, not a hull.
- *Vault Rock (Megiddo station):* an irregular rock has no symmetry for Tripo to infer the far side from. That is acceptable for a rock, but expect lumpy guesswork.
- *Gummy Bear (Miss Terri's):* the limbs may separate at the joins, and the gummy reference pulls toward translucency.

## Zhestokost

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Zhestokost|Zhestokost]] · [[Game Design/Faction Play#Zhestokost|doctrine]]

**Aesthetic thesis.** Every ship is a piece of the foundry that learned to fly: cast iron, poured masses, ammunition as architecture. These attempts push from armoured beasts toward foundry objects and industrial nostalgia. They test whether "arsenal" reads best as an animal's hide or as the machinery itself. Charcoal, iron gray and dull oxide red, with cream stencils, throughout.

### Role: Ammunition tender

**Pitch:** Two crew keep a column firing. The hull has built-in ammunition-transfer collars and deep magazines, two small point-defense hardpoints and one ventral utility hardpoint for a cargo grip. It is slow and carries nothing else to fight with, and the whole column's fight depends on it.

**A · Quartermaster (armadillo)** · Safe bet
Design the Quartermaster, a small two-person Zhestokost ammunition tender spacecraft. Its whole body follows an armadillo: one low, heavy, domed body covered in four broad overlapping armor bands from shoulders to hips. Each band is a magazine bay with one square hatch along its crest. The armadillo's blunt head is the cockpit, a narrow slit under a thick armored brow. One small point-defense turret sits on each shoulder. Where the hind legs would be, one thick, squat docking collar sits low on each flank for ammunition transfer. The tail is a short, thick, tapered drive housing ending in two square nozzles, with stubby radiator plates beside it. Charcoal armor bands, oxide-red magazine hatches, iron-gray head and belly, small cream serial numbers stenciled on every band. One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.

**B · Pantry (rifle cartridge)** · Moonshot. The ship is literally ammunition. It might read as a prop, so the cockpit slit and hatches must carry the scale. The cartridge has no hidden side, which Tripo will like.
Design the Pantry, a small two-person Zhestokost ammunition tender spacecraft. Its whole body follows one oversized rifle cartridge lying on its side: a long, thick cylindrical case, gently necked toward a rounded bullet nose. The bullet nose is the armored cockpit, with a narrow observation slit across its tip. Four thick raised bands ring the case at even intervals; each band is a magazine section with one square hatch on top. The cartridge rim at the stern is a thick flange carrying two square drive nozzles, with the round primer cap at its center as the reactor access port. One small point-defense turret sits on top of the flange at each side. One squat docking collar sits on each flank of the case. Dull matte brass case, charcoal bullet nose, oxide-red bands, cream headstamp lettering around the rim. One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.

### Role: Flak gunboat (column line ship)

**Pitch:** One pilot flies a cheaper sibling of the Foundry Bell. It has one centerline medium ballistic hardpoint and two small flak hardpoints that shred incoming missiles, deep feeds and replaceable armor plates. It is slow to turn and nearly blind beyond visual range.

**A · Anvil (blacksmith's anvil)** · Safe bet
Design the Anvil, a small single-pilot Zhestokost gunboat. Its whole body follows a blacksmith's anvil: one solid, heavy casting with a flat working face on top, a long tapering horn at the front and a square heel at the rear, standing on a broad, solid, waisted base. The horn is the barrel housing of one medium cannon, its muzzle at the horn's tip. The cockpit is a narrow slit in the front face of the body, just above the root of the horn. The flat working face is a deck of four bolted, replaceable armor plates. One small flak turret sits at each rear corner of the heel. An ammunition feed channel runs in a recessed groove from the heel to the horn's root. Two square drive nozzles sit in the rear face of the base. Charcoal casting, iron-gray armor plates, oxide-red feed channel, cream stencils on the base. One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.

**B · Morzh (walrus)** · Safe bet
Design the Morzh, a small single-pilot Zhestokost gunboat. Its whole body follows a walrus: one massive, thick, rounded body with heavy folded shoulders and a broad blunt head. The walrus's two tusks are two short, thick flak cannon barrels angled forward and down from beneath the muzzle. The cockpit is a narrow armored slit across the brow. One medium cannon sits on the hump of the shoulders, its barrel laid forward along the head. Two short, thick fore-flippers are armored sponsons holding ammunition feeds. The body tapers to a broad, thick tail fluke that carries two square drive nozzles and flat radiator plates. Deep folds in the hide are overlapping armor plates. Charcoal hide plates, iron-gray tusks and barrel, oxide-red feed hatches, cream unit numbers on the shoulders. One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.

**C · Smokebox (steam locomotive front)** · Moonshot. Industrial nostalgia as faction identity. The risk is wheels; the prompt seats the boiler on a skirted base so that none appear.
Design the Smokebox, a small single-pilot Zhestokost gunboat. Its whole body follows the front half of a heavy steam locomotive: one long, thick, cylindrical boiler resting on a solid skirted base, with a round smokebox face at the front. The round smokebox door is the gun port, with the muzzle of one medium cannon at its center. The two buffers on the front beam are two small flak turrets. The short, thick chimney on top of the boiler is a stubby sensor stack. The cockpit is a squared cab at the rear of the boiler with two small square windows facing forward. Ammunition hatches run in a row along the boiler's flank. Two square drive nozzles sit in the rear face of the cab. Charcoal boiler, oxide-red buffer beam and skirt, iron-gray smokebox face, cream numbers on the cab. One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.

## Miss Terri's Sugariffic Snack Company

[[Brainstorming/Faction Flavor and Visual Identity#Miss Terri's Sugariffic Snack Company|Faction Flavor]] · [[Game Design/Faction Play#Miss Terri's Sugariffic Snack Company|doctrine]]

**Aesthetic thesis.** Confectionery packaging that is secretly hazmat handling: twist caps, piping tips and squeeze nozzles that spray corrosives. These attempts push dessert objects that have dispensing anatomy, and one moonshot tries a mascot. The question is how far "cute" can go before it stops reading as a warship. Strawberry pink, mint, cream and cherry red, with opaque painted bands in place of jelly walls.

The cockpit is a small rounded canopy on the bottle's upper shoulder, just behind the cap. One small defensive turret sits on top near the rear. The flat base of the bottle is the stern, with two round drive nozzles. Strawberry-pink bottle, mint screw cap and nozzle, cream label band, cherry-red drive rims. One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.

**B · Piper (frosting piping bag)** · Safe bet

Mint bag, cream pleats, strawberry-pink star tip, cherry-red twist knot, small bubbly lettering on the flank. One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.

**C · Gummy (gummy bear)** · Moonshot. A mascot hull. It bends rule 6 because the gummy reference pulls toward translucency, so the prompt asks for a sugar-dusted, opaque matte. The limbs are fused stubs so that the joins hold.

One complete ship, centered with clear margin around it, three-quarter front view from slightly above showing the dorsal surface and one side; moderate lens; plain seamless light gray background; soft key light with gentle form shadows; matte finishes throughout.

### Role: Sample tender

**Pitch:** Two crew hand out free samples and resupply the swarm. The hull has a consumables hold, a built-in reservoir refill coupling, one small defensive hardpoint and one utility hardpoint. It is unarmored, everyone's friend, and carries corrosives.

## Finch Cybernetics

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Finch Cybernetics|Finch Cybernetics]] · [[Game Design/Faction Play#Finch Cybernetics|doctrine]]

**Aesthetic thesis.** Sense organs made into hulls: ears, eyes and joints rendered in pearl ceramic with prosthetic seams. The attempts test how close to anatomy Finch can go before it becomes body horror, which the faction note forbids. Pearl ceramic, satin titanium, smoky gray sensor membranes painted matte, and tiny celadon indicators.

### Role: Track relay shadow

**Pitch:** One pilot flies a shadow with a built-in ChirOptos passive array and a narrow-beam track uplink, plus one small low-signature hardpoint. It shares your position with everyone hostile to you, and it breaks under one hit.

**A · Pinna (bat with folded wings)** · Safe bet
Design the Pinna, a small single-pilot Finch Cybernetics tracking spacecraft. Its whole body follows a bat at rest with its wings folded tight: one compact, smooth, teardrop body wrapped in two thick, folded wing panels, and a small head with two very large, upright, rounded ears. The two ears are the passive sensor arrays, broad and thick, with their inner faces covered by matte smoky gray membranes. The cockpit is a small tapered canopy where the bat's face would be. One light low-signature weapon aperture sits under the chin. The folded wing panels run back along the flanks with narrow radiator seams, and the body tapers to two small drive nozzles. Pearl-ceramic body and wing panels, smoky gray ear membranes, satin titanium joint rings at the ear roots, tiny celadon indicator dots.
