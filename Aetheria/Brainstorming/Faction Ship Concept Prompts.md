# Faction Ship Concept Prompts

These are small, player-scale hull pitches. Each is designed for one pilot or a small crew. A hull's shape shows what it does: weapon arcs, sensor apertures, ammunition storage, radiators, thrusters, and service access have visible places. Its built-in advantage is a reason to buy that hull and accept its tradeoff. These designs use the faction identities in [[Brainstorming/Faction Flavor and Visual Identity|Faction Flavor and Visual Identity]] and the material combat choices in [[Game Design/Design Pillars|Design Pillars]], [[Game Design/Action RPG Layer|Action RPG Layer]], and [[Game Design/Ship-shape and Up to Specs|Ship-shape and Up to Specs]]. The pre-Elysium designs here use armor, heat management, and point defense rather than shields.

Every prompt requests one complete vessel in a single three-quarter front view, with the dorsal surface and one side visible. The ship is isolated on a plain light gray background, evenly lit, with no setting, other ships, people, typography, diagram panels, or alternate views. This keeps the silhouette and mounted equipment clear for image-to-3D work.

## Sol Dominion — Mandate

**High concept:** A small interdiction cutter that identifies a contact, records the encounter, and disables its drive before the contact reaches a controlled route.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot and a systems officer can enforce a corridor without bringing a patrol flotilla. A built-in sensor-fusion and evidence recorder improves target classification and lock persistence. Two forward medium hardpoints favor precise disabling weapons; two small flank mounts take point-defense interceptors. Its broad dorsal sensor plane and protected recorder are visible parts of the hull. The mass and power reserved for certified sensing leave less room for cargo and acceleration.

**Tagline:** “The route is accounted for.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Mandate, a compact two-person Sol Dominion interdiction spacecraft, roughly the scale of a patrol boat rather than a capital ship. Single complete three-quarter front view showing its dorsal surface and one side. A short armored central spine carries a clearly bounded two-seat pressure cabin, a broad flush dorsal sensor plane, recessed antenna fields, a protected recorder block, two forward medium weapon mounts with unobstructed firing arcs, and two small flank point-defense mounts. Paired rear drive nozzles and compact radiator strips complete a functional small-ship layout. Pale gray ceramic, graphite structural bands, desaturated blue, tiny crimson authority marks, registry blocks, and precise repeated panel bands express standardized administration without multiplying the hull into a modular train. Plain light gray background, soft even studio light, entire ship visible, no port, planet, traffic, crew, lettering, inset, or second view.

## Lucent Media — Headliner

**High concept:** A mobile laser duelist that acquires targets aggressively and wins short, spectacular firing windows.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot operates a fast attack ship built around a pre-installed active sensor and twin forward laser hardpoints. An oversized capacitor gives its lasers a strong opening burst; exposed radiator shutters must open after the burst, making the ship conspicuous while it sheds heat. A small ventral utility mount accepts a decoy or defensive tool. The camera-ready shell also exposes the tactical bargain: prongs frame the guns and sensor, while colored side pods house power and cooling.

**Tagline:** “Make the first shot the headline.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Headliner, a compact single-pilot Lucent Media laser fighter. Single complete three-quarter front view showing dorsal surface and one side. Its white-and-gold pointed central hull has a small dark cockpit and twin forward laser emitters held between sweeping cobalt-blue prongs. Overlapping pale lavender shells, red-orange blade tips, and teal capacitor and radiator pods surround a visible dark navy chassis. Deep gaps reveal real mounts, service clearances, and radiator shutters; the shells retain both broad curves and sharp tips. Restrained seam lights accent the painted component color blocks. Two compact rear engines and visible attitude thrusters keep it a spacecraft. Plain light gray background, soft even studio light, entire ship visible, no stage, audience, planet, lettering, inset, or second view.

## Rossum & Douglas — Standard Model 42

**High concept:** An inexpensive missile picket that holds a target lock and launches from outside gun range.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot and an optional operator use a built-in guidance computer to improve guided-launcher lock stability. Two recessed medium missile hardpoints and a small defensive mount give it a narrow but useful job. Internal magazines preserve the plain wedge outline but reduce cargo space; the ship handles adequately and does nothing else with distinction. The procurement slide calls that specialization a platform strategy.

**Tagline:** “A measurable improvement.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Standard Model 42, a small one- or two-person Rossum & Douglas missile craft. Single complete three-quarter front view showing dorsal surface and one side. Make it an utterly ordinary broad, shallow blue-gray flying wedge with a blunt tapered nose, tiny recessed cockpit windows, two rectangular missile bay doors near the forward edges, one small defensive mount, flat panelled upper surfaces, repeated identical hatches, and a wide but compact rear engine bank. A flush guidance sensor and plain off-white stripe are its only other distinctions. The ship should look competently manufactured and boring, a minor variation on many other company wedges. Plain light gray background, soft even catalog lighting, entire ship visible, no scenery, lettering, inset, or second view.

## Aya Collective — Open Hand

**High concept:** A settlement escort that can defend a convoy and stay serviceable far from a manufacturer's yard.

**Procurement pitch:** A pilot and one maintainer operate an escort with four small point-defense hardpoints arranged for broad coverage, one forward medium conventional-gun mount, and a compact ammunition locker. Its built-in fault-isolation and standardized feed interfaces make damaged mounts easy to replace using locally held parts. The cost is modest thrust and a smaller main gun than a dedicated attacker. Independent suppliers provide the ordnance; Aya crews own configuration, servicing, and firing decisions.

**Tagline:** “Keep one another moving.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Open Hand, a small two-person Aya Collective escort spacecraft. Single complete three-quarter front view showing dorsal surface and one side. A rounded protective frame surrounds a compact pressure hull and four clearly placed small point-defense turrets with broad firing arcs. One forward medium gun, removable feed boxes, accessible controller housings, replaceable barrel collars, branching coolant lines, and a visible service hatch show how local crews maintain it. Terracotta, warm cream, deep green, charcoal, restrained copper, and a few patterned removable panels; cleanly fitted repairs and local workshop marks. Paired rear drives and small radiator panels remain visible. Plain light gray background, soft even studio light, entire ship visible, no settlement, people, text, inset, or second view.

## Cryonix — Stillwater

**High concept:** A cold-running scout that buys time for passive observation and one carefully chosen shot.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot gains a built-in thermal reservoir and signature-managed emitter skin that extend quiet running while sensors gather a track. A single centerline precision hardpoint rewards the patient first attack; one small defensive mount covers retreat. The ship must unfold its emitter planes and dump stored heat after commitment, becoming easier to detect. Its delicate premium surfaces require specialist inspection.

**Tagline:** “The first trace is yours to find.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Stillwater, a compact single-pilot Cryonix scout spacecraft. Single complete three-quarter front view showing dorsal surface and one side. A narrow protected cockpit and central core sit within thin exact blue-black and silver-blue shells. Folded but visibly broad emitter planes flank the hull, with continuous thermal paths, lattice apertures, a flush passive sensor face, one centerline precision weapon aperture, one small defensive mount, paired compact drives, and tiny vernier ports. Icy cyan and faint violet iridescence appear only at grazing angles; markings are sparse inspection seals and alignment grids. The thin surfaces are engineered radiators, not decorative wings. Plain light gray background, soft even studio light, entire ship visible, no lunar facility, workers, text, inset, or second view.

## Zhestokost — Foundry Bell

**High concept:** A close-range ballistic bruiser that keeps firing after its armor and gun assemblies take hits.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot and a loader or systems partner operate a hull with one oversized centerline cannon, two small side gun hardpoints, deep ammunition storage, and reinforced feed paths. Built-in recoil bracing and easy access to replaceable armor plates support sustained fire and field repair. It is slow to turn, burns ammunition quickly, and has little space for subtle sensors or cargo.

**Tagline:** “Ring it again.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Foundry Bell, a small two-person Zhestokost heavy gunship. Single complete three-quarter front view showing dorsal surface and one side. A low broad body of stepped rectangular masses wraps thick armor around a clearly bounded cockpit. An immense centerline ballistic barrel, two smaller side gun mounts, visible ammunition feeds, recoil braces, replaceable armor slabs, towing points, heavy rear drives, and protected radiator grilles make the firing and repair logic unmistakable. Rough cast metal, dark steel, ceramic armor, charcoal, iron gray, dull oxide red, cream serial marks, and wear concentrated around feeds and hatches. It is dense and heavy, not a capital ship. Plain light gray background, soft even studio light, entire ship visible, no arsenal bay, workers, text, inset, or second view.

## Cetacean Navigators — Waykeeper

**High concept:** A rescue escort that keeps a convoy alive through uncertain contacts and damaged ships.

**Procurement pitch:** A small mixed-environment crew uses built-in route sensing, rescue transponders, and towing controls. Two small point-defense mounts protect the approach; one medium utility hardpoint accepts a tractor or recovery tool. Parallel aquatic and dry pressure spaces let the crew operate without treating either environment as an afterthought. It gives up heavy offensive armament and high acceleration for rescue equipment and life support.

**Tagline:** “No one crosses alone.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Waykeeper, a small Cetacean Navigator rescue escort for a mixed-environment crew. Single complete three-quarter front view showing dorsal surface and one side. Two streamlined blue-green aquatic pressure bodies run alongside a compact dry crew compartment, joined by short silver structural arches and explicit transfer locks. Tapered navigation booms, a forward recovery tool mount, two small point-defense mounts, tow fittings, rescue beacon housings, compact drives, and controlled radiator panels explain its escort role. Translucent sections reveal water as a substantial protected volume, not an aquarium display. Deep ocean blue, soft gray, pale sand, and small warm amber lock lights. Plain light gray background, soft even studio light, entire ship visible, no convoy, route scenery, text, inset, or second view.

## Finch Cybernetics — Quiet Sense

**High concept:** A sensor hunter that finds and tracks targets while staying out of their attention.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot flies a hull built around a pre-installed passive sensor and precision interface. Two forward light hardpoints favor low-signature weapons or specialized tools; a ventral utility hardpoint accepts a probe. The hull improves passive track quality and fine maneuver control, but flush fitted systems are costly to calibrate after damage and leave little room for heavy armor.

**Tagline:** “Notice first.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Quiet Sense, a small single-pilot Finch Cybernetics sensor craft. Single complete three-quarter front view showing dorsal surface and one side. A continuous tapered pearl-ceramic hull fits closely around a compact cockpit, with broad flush passive sensor membranes, two light forward weapon apertures, one ventral probe mount, fine maneuvering thrusters, modest rear drives, and narrow recessed radiator seams. Satin titanium joints and smoky translucent sensor surfaces flow into the shell with the precision of a fitted prosthetic. Tiny celadon indicators and technician-readable marks only. Sleek and organic in continuity, without exposed flesh or tentacles. Plain light gray background, soft even studio light, entire ship visible, no scenery, text, inset, or second view.

## Aeronautics Unlimited — Farrier

**High concept:** A frontier utility ship that mines, surveys, and survives a dangerous worksite with the same hull.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot and one workmate get a built-in survey package, a forward industrial-tool hardpoint, two light defensive hardpoints, and a modular cargo grip. The hull supports quick swaps between a mining head, salvage cutter, and other standard tools. It pays for this flexibility with middling combat performance and exposed tool fittings that need maintenance after a fight.

**Tagline:** “Get there. Get started.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Farrier, a compact two-person Aeronautics Unlimited work ship. Single complete three-quarter front view showing dorsal surface and one side. Its simple rounded-rectangular cabin, cylindrical equipment modules, and arched structural frame expose a forward interchangeable tool socket, one installed mining head, two small defensive weapon mounts, cargo grips, lifting points, numbered service bays, compact drives, attitude thrusters, and modest radiator panels. Chalk white, slate blue, bare aluminum, and restrained ochre safety marks, with light mineral abrasion. The layout is balanced and practical, with clear access to every module. Plain light gray background, soft even studio light, entire ship visible, no excavation, worker, text, inset, or second view.

## Pirate Coalition — Second Owner

**High concept:** A captured freighter turned ambush and salvage craft by one crew with uneven access to parts.

**Procurement pitch:** A pilot and two or three crew use a stolen freight hull with a mismatched sensor pod, an oversized industrial gun, a capture tool, and a replacement drive. It can identify valuable cargo, threaten a ship, and haul a prize, but the improvised power and cooling paths limit how long it can fight. Every useful modification has a visible mount and service route; the crew's ingenuity is the selling point, and compatibility is the bill that follows.

**Tagline:** “Possession has a maintenance schedule.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Second Owner, a small Pirate Coalition raider built from a captured freight spacecraft. Single complete three-quarter front view showing dorsal surface and one side. Keep the original compact rectangular cargo hull identifiable beneath an asymmetrical sleek sensor pod, one oversized industrial gun on a credible reinforced mount, a forward capture-tool hardpoint, a carefully braced replacement engine, exposed but organized cable and coolant routes, and practical radiator panels. Surviving manufacturer finishes show beneath primer, welds, crossed-out serials, and one repeated crew emblem. The additions make tactical sense even though they do not match. Plain light gray background, soft even studio light, entire ship visible, no dock, crew, text, inset, or second view.
