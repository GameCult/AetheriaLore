# Faction Ship Concept Prompts

These are small, player-scale hull pitches. Each is designed for one pilot or a small crew. A hull's shape shows what it does: weapon arcs, sensor apertures, ammunition storage, radiators, thrusters, and service access have visible places. Its built-in advantage is a reason to buy that hull and accept its tradeoff. These designs use the faction identities in [[Brainstorming/Faction Flavor and Visual Identity|Faction Flavor and Visual Identity]] and the material combat choices in [[Game Design/Design Pillars|Design Pillars]], [[Game Design/Action RPG Layer|Action RPG Layer]], and [[Game Design/Ship-shape and Up to Specs|Ship-shape and Up to Specs]]. The pre-Elysium designs here use armor, heat management, and point defense rather than shields.

The silhouette leads each design. A governing shape gives the eye a way to read the whole vessel; major forms then flow into its cabin, weapons, drives, and radiators. These comparisons guide construction rather than turning ships into literal animals or objects.

Every prompt requests one complete vessel in a single three-quarter front view, with the dorsal surface and one side visible. The ship is isolated on a plain light gray background, evenly lit, with no setting, other ships, people, poster text, diagram panels, or alternate views. This keeps the silhouette and mounted equipment clear for image-to-3D work.

## Sol Dominion — Mandate

**High concept:** A small interdiction cutter that identifies a contact, records the encounter, and disables its drive before the contact reaches a controlled route.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot and a systems officer can enforce a corridor without bringing a patrol flotilla. A built-in sensor-fusion and evidence recorder improves target classification and lock persistence. Two forward medium hardpoints favor precise disabling weapons; two small flank mounts take point-defense interceptors. Its broad dorsal sensor plane and protected recorder are visible parts of the hull. The mass and power reserved for certified sensing leave less room for cargo and acceleration.

**Tagline:** “The route is accounted for.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Mandate, a compact two-person Sol Dominion interdiction spacecraft. Its silhouette is a short, blunt spearhead: a narrow armored nose widens into two authoritative shoulders, then draws back to a clipped engine stern. The cockpit is sunk into the nose like a small observation slit. One continuous dorsal sensor plane runs from cockpit to shoulders; the shoulders house paired forward disabling-weapon mounts, with small point-defense turrets tucked just outside them. Graphite bands cross the hull like measured divisions on an instrument, and slim radiator strips continue those lines toward two rear nozzles. Keep the volumes joined into one purposeful patrol craft, not a train of modules. Pale gray ceramic, desaturated blue, tiny crimson authority marks, precise registry blocks. ## Lucent Media — Headliner

**High concept:** A mobile laser duelist that acquires targets aggressively and wins short, spectacular firing windows.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot operates a fast attack ship built around a pre-installed active sensor and twin forward laser hardpoints. An oversized capacitor gives its lasers a strong opening burst; exposed radiator shutters must open after the burst, making the ship conspicuous while it sheds heat. A small ventral utility mount accepts a decoy or defensive tool. The camera-ready shell also exposes the tactical bargain: prongs frame the guns and sensor, while colored side pods house power and cooling.

**Tagline:** “Make the first shot the headline.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Headliner, a compact single-pilot Lucent Media laser fighter. Its silhouette evokes an extravagant gaming mouse caught in a forward lunge: a pointed white-and-gold cockpit is the central prow, while two cobalt dorsal prongs sweep over it and converge toward the firing line. Broad pale-lavender side shells peel away from the center like adjustable palm rests, exposing a dark navy chassis in deep, deliberate gaps. Their rear edges rise into red-orange fins; teal capacitor pods nest beneath them and feed twin forward laser emitters. Radiator shutters are visible inside the side gaps, so opening them changes the ship's outline after a burst. Every layer has a mount and thickness; the curves flow into sharp tips rather than becoming a pile of blades. Restrained seam light, two compact rear drives. ## Rossum & Douglas — Standard Model 42

**High concept:** An inexpensive missile picket that holds a target lock and launches from outside gun range.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot and an optional operator use a built-in guidance computer to improve guided-launcher lock stability. Two recessed medium missile hardpoints and a small defensive mount give it a narrow but useful job. Internal magazines preserve the plain wedge outline but reduce cargo space; the ship handles adequately and does nothing else with distinction. The procurement slide calls that specialization a platform strategy.

**Tagline:** “A measurable improvement.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Standard Model 42, a small one- or two-person Rossum & Douglas missile craft. Its entire silhouette is a flying doorstop: one broad, shallow trapezoidal wedge tapers from a wide engine stern to a blunt nose. The upper surface stays almost flat, rising only enough at the center to enclose a tiny recessed cockpit. Two rectangular missile-bay doors lie flush along the sloped forward edges, mirroring each other with dreary precision; a small defensive mount and a flush guidance sensor barely disturb the plane. Repeated hatches and an off-white stripe follow the wedge's geometry rather than inventing a second design. Muted blue-gray industrial coating, ordinary seams and scuffs. Render this competent, boring production ship without rescue by sculptural fins or dramatic greebles. ## Aya Collective — Open Hand

**High concept:** A settlement escort that can defend a convoy and stay serviceable far from a manufacturer's yard.

**Procurement pitch:** A pilot and one maintainer operate an escort with four small point-defense hardpoints arranged for broad coverage, one forward medium conventional-gun mount, and a compact ammunition locker. Its built-in fault-isolation and standardized feed interfaces make damaged mounts easy to replace using locally held parts. The cost is modest thrust and a smaller main gun than a dedicated attacker. Independent suppliers provide the ordnance; Aya crews own configuration, servicing, and firing decisions.

**Tagline:** “Keep one another moving.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Open Hand, a small two-person Aya Collective escort spacecraft. Its silhouette evokes two cupped hands protecting a seed: paired rounded outriggers curve forward around a compact central cabin but leave the nose and weapon arcs open. A medium conventional gun extends from the protected center, while four small point-defense mounts sit at the outriggers' outer knuckles, able to cover the ship and its neighbors. The curves are sturdy replaceable frames, not decorative wings; removable feed boxes and controller covers fit into their inner faces where a maintainer can reach them. Coolant branches return from the mounts into the central hull, whose rear narrows into paired drives and modest radiators. Terracotta and warm cream shells, deep green recesses, restrained copper fittings, patterned removable panels, well-fitted repairs. ## Cryonix — Stillwater

**High concept:** A cold-running scout that buys time for passive observation and one carefully chosen shot.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot gains a built-in thermal reservoir and signature-managed emitter skin that extend quiet running while sensors gather a track. A single centerline precision hardpoint rewards the patient first attack; one small defensive mount covers retreat. The ship must unfold its emitter planes and dump stored heat after commitment, becoming easier to detect. Its delicate premium surfaces require specialist inspection.

**Tagline:** “The first trace is yours to find.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Stillwater, a compact single-pilot Cryonix scout spacecraft. Its silhouette is a narrow dark spindle held inside the folds of a precise metal fan. The protected cockpit and flush passive sensor form the tapered front of the spindle; a single centerline weapon aperture ends that line. Two broad silver-blue emitter planes fold back from the waist, close to the hull in quiet running, their shallow lattice surfaces visibly connected to the thermal reservoir beneath the cockpit. A small defensive mount sits at the rear hinge, where the folded planes cannot mask it. The spindle resolves into paired compact drives; the fan's edges remain thin and exact, shaped by heat flow rather than decorative feathers. Blue-black graphite, icy cyan, faint violet iridescence at grazing angles, sparse inspection marks. ## Zhestokost — Foundry Bell

**High concept:** A close-range ballistic bruiser that keeps firing after its armor and gun assemblies take hits.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot and a loader or systems partner operate a hull with one oversized centerline cannon, two small side gun hardpoints, deep ammunition storage, and reinforced feed paths. Built-in recoil bracing and easy access to replaceable armor plates support sustained fire and field repair. It is slow to turn, burns ammunition quickly, and has little space for subtle sensors or cargo.

**Tagline:** “Ring it again.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Foundry Bell, a small two-person Zhestokost heavy gunship. Its silhouette is an armored fist built around one enormous centerline cannon: square shoulders of stepped plating close around the barrel, then taper only slightly toward a low, heavy engine stern. A narrow cockpit slit sits above the gun breech, protected by the same armor brow. Two smaller gun mounts project from the outer shoulders. Deep ammunition feeds run back through visible recessed channels to a thick central magazine; recoil braces continue those channels into the hull, so the weapon feels structurally inseparable from the ship. Replaceable armor slabs overlap like industrial masonry, with protected radiator grilles and heavy drives at the rear. Rough dark steel, ceramic armor, dull oxide red, cream serial marks, wear at feeds and service hatches. Dense and brutal at small-crew scale. ## Cetacean Navigators — Waykeeper

**High concept:** A rescue escort that keeps a convoy alive through uncertain contacts and damaged ships.

**Procurement pitch:** A small mixed-environment crew uses built-in route sensing, rescue transponders, and towing controls. Two small point-defense mounts protect the approach; one medium utility hardpoint accepts a tractor or recovery tool. Parallel aquatic and dry pressure spaces let the crew operate without treating either environment as an afterthought. It gives up heavy offensive armament and high acceleration for rescue equipment and life support.

**Tagline:** “No one crosses alone.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Waykeeper, a small Cetacean Navigator rescue escort for a mixed-environment crew. Its silhouette evokes a pair of swimming bodies keeping pace around a slimmer companion: two long blue-green aquatic pressure hulls flank a dry central work cabin, flowing together at the bow under a shared navigation bridge and separating toward paired rear drives. Short silver arches and transfer locks make the three volumes one functional ship. A forward recovery tool projects from the central gap like an offered hand; two small point-defense mounts sit high on the outer hulls, clear of the tow approach. Rescue beacons and radiator panels follow the long sides without breaking their rhythm. Translucent sections reveal protected water volume rather than a display tank. Deep ocean blue, soft gray, pale sand, small amber lock lights. ## Finch Cybernetics — Quiet Sense

**High concept:** A sensor hunter that finds and tracks targets while staying out of their attention.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot flies a hull built around a pre-installed passive sensor and precision interface. Two forward light hardpoints favor low-signature weapons or specialized tools; a ventral utility hardpoint accepts a probe. The hull improves passive track quality and fine maneuver control, but flush fitted systems are costly to calibrate after damage and leave little room for heavy armor.

**Tagline:** “Notice first.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Quiet Sense, a small single-pilot Finch Cybernetics sensor craft. Its silhouette evokes a diving bird of prey held just before the strike: a beak-like tapered cockpit leads the form, and two swept radiator and sensor surfaces flow back from its shoulders like close-held wings. The pearl-ceramic shell blends smoothly into those surfaces with prosthetic precision; smoky passive-sensor membranes occupy their leading edges. Two light weapon apertures sit forward and below the cockpit like tucked talons, with a probe mount between them. Fine maneuvering ports follow the wing roots, while modest rear drives end the taper without a bulky engine block. Satin titanium joints, narrow radiator seams, tiny celadon indicators. The avian analogy governs the contour; it is a credible pressure hull and machine, not a literal bird. ## Aeronautics Unlimited — Farrier

**High concept:** A frontier utility ship that mines, surveys, and survives a dangerous worksite with the same hull.

**Procurement pitch:** One pilot and one workmate get a built-in survey package, a forward industrial-tool hardpoint, two light defensive hardpoints, and a modular cargo grip. The hull supports quick swaps between a mining head, salvage cutter, and other standard tools. It pays for this flexibility with middling combat performance and exposed tool fittings that need maintenance after a fight.

**Tagline:** “Get there. Get started.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Farrier, a compact two-person Aeronautics Unlimited work ship. Its silhouette resembles a sturdy pocket multitool opened for work: a rounded-rectangular central cabin forms the grip, while one short arched structural arm reaches around either side to carry swappable tools and cargo clamps. A mining head projects from the forward central socket; the arms shelter its feed lines but leave a clear removal path. Two small defensive mounts sit at the arm roots, close enough to service from the cabin. Cylindrical tanks and compact rear drives continue the plain geometry, with numbered bays, lifting points, and modest radiator panels arranged where work crews can reach them. Chalk white, slate blue, bare aluminum, restrained ochre safety marks, light mineral abrasion. The forms are simple and balanced, without deluxe bodywork. ## Pirate Coalition — Second Owner

**High concept:** A captured freighter turned ambush and salvage craft by one crew with uneven access to parts.

**Procurement pitch:** A pilot and two or three crew use a stolen freight hull with a mismatched sensor pod, an oversized industrial gun, a capture tool, and a replacement drive. It can identify valuable cargo, threaten a ship, and haul a prize, but the improvised power and cooling paths limit how long it can fight. Every useful modification has a visible mount and service route; the crew's ingenuity is the selling point, and compatibility is the bill that follows.

**Tagline:** “Possession has a maintenance schedule.”

**Image prompt:** Design the Second Owner, a small Pirate Coalition raider built from a captured freight spacecraft. The silhouette starts as a plain rectangular cargo shuttle, then bends visibly around one crew's ambush plan: a heavy industrial gun runs along its upper spine, a sleek stolen sensor pod leans out on one side to see past the gun, and a capture arm curls forward beneath the opposite side. The replacement engine is offset at the stern, held by substantial braces that feed its thrust into the original frame. Organized coolant and power runs bridge the mismatched parts, with radiators placed where gun and drive heat can reach them. The donor hull remains legible beneath surviving company paint, primer, bare welds, crossed-out serials, and one repeated crew emblem. It is lopsided with intent, not random junk.
