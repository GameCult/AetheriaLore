## Proposal for Canon

### Founding Logic: The Dose Holds Still

At the edge of [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Concepts/Pseudospace|pseudospace]] the maps drift, clocks argue and bodies respond oddly to familiar things. The first settlers out there found that almost nothing they brought behaved the same way twice, except a measured serving. A carefully specified dose of a carefully specified compound did the same thing to a person on Tuesday as it did on Monday. Miss Terri's original shop, a sweets counter on an outbound station, became the place where frontier settlers learned to tune their bodies, and later their ship minds, to a world that would not hold still.

So the faction's internal logic is: **when the world won't stay put, change yourself, one serving at a time.** Everything else follows from that. Adaptation through chemistry is faster than adaptation through institutions, which makes Miss Terri's the purest expression of [[Narrative Themes#Acceleration And Unequal Control Of Pace|acceleration]]: it gives people what they need before anyone has agreed it is safe.

The candy is not a disguise. It is how the frontier remembers that a dose is a treat, a comfort and a promise, and how taking a risky one comes to feel like a party instead of an amputation.

### Day to Day and Under Pressure

Miss Terri's people feed each other constantly. A shift starts with a shared tray; a dispute is settled over a meal both parties must finish. Feeding is the first act toward any stranger, which is why Faction Play's ships greet before anything else.

Under pressure they do not dig in. They apply something and leave. A Miss Terri's crew thinks of a fight the way a pharmacist thinks of a prescription: administer, step back, let it work. Staying to watch the corrosion is gauche, like hovering while someone tastes your cooking.

### How They Build

- **Every product has a serving.** Weapons are rated in servings, reactors in servings per shift, armor in servings it can take. A part without a serving size is unfinished.
- **Open by hand.** Twist caps, squeeze nozzles and pull tabs, because frontier hands fix things without tools. Every reservoir can be opened by one person in a glove.
- **Delayed release is a virtue.** Double Bubble Gum is the house philosophy: the best product keeps working after it leaves the counter. Design reviews ask "and then what does it do?"
- **The hazard collar is honest.** The cherry-red collar around every nozzle in [[Brainstorming/Ship Design Language#Miss Terri's Sugariffic Snack Company|Ship Design Language]] is not irony. Miss Terri's labels everything accurately; it simply lists the side effects after you accept.

### Values, Fears, Humor

They value **response**: a body that still reacts, a mind that still lights up. They fear **the plateau**: tolerance, the day nothing works on you any more. In a culture of escalating servings, tolerance is mortality, and people speak of a plateaued elder the way other cultures speak of the dying.

The funniest thing in the company is the ingredient list read aloud after the meal. The look on a customer's face at ingredient forty is the oldest joke they have.

### Rituals and Customs

- **The First Spoon.** Whoever designs a product eats, sprays or installs it first. The founder stories are mostly about first spoons that went wrong.
- **Batch names.** Ships are named like flavors with batch numbers: *Cherry Riot 41*, *Lemon Grief 7*.
- **The closed-eye mascot.** The mascot decal near every cockpit winks. A mascot painted with both eyes shut marks a ship that lost a taster.

### Outsiders and the Player

Friendly by default, and genuinely so. Refusing food is the one reliable way to offend them. A player who accepts samples is treated as family; a player who reads the label first is treated as a grown-up, which is respected but colder. Zhestokost is the cultural opposite: a supply tail and thick armor are the strategy of a people who expect the world to stay the same, and Miss Terri's finds that adorable.

### Internal Tension: The Kitchen and the Second Helping

**The Kitchen** is the pharma corporation the operator described: dosage chemists, batch records, a Mega-sized catalog, and the discipline of the measured serving.

**The Second Helping** is the insane cabal of the operator's candy-sector story: settlements that went further out and kept escalating, until the dose stopped being a way to live at the strange edge and became a way of becoming part of it. The Kitchen sells to them, fears them, and does not say their name at the counter. Every Miss Terri's child knows the line between a treat and a second helping, and every adult has a cousin who crossed it.

### Only If You'd Been There

- Hull plating near the nozzles is faintly tacky underfoot during EVA, and the whole station smells of burnt sugar and ozone.
- Prices are listed per serving, never per unit, and nobody will tell you how many servings are in a unit.
- Ask a Miss Terri's pilot how she is and she will tell you what she has taken today, in order.

## Tie-Back to Play

This note explains Faction Play's quirk instead of decorating it: the one pass and pull-away is a prescription, not cowardice. It suggests one cheap variant on existing levers: a **Second Helping swarm** uses the same hulls and palette but has `break_off_hull` and the timed flee turned off, so the tell is a pink swarm that does not leave after its pass, and the adjustment is to run. It gives the empty consumables item class (see [[Brainstorming/Corporate Roster and Item Wishlist|Corporate Roster]]) a cultural reason, and gives copy and barks a unit, the serving. For Ship Design Language, add "a serving-size panel on every reservoir" and the closed-eye mascot as a memorial variant. It does not need a tolerance mechanic; the plateau stays lore unless consumables later need diminishing returns.
