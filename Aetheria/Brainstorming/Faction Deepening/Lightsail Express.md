## Proposal for Canon

### Founding Logic: The Motto Is a Lease Clause

In late Sol the motto was a boast: Lightsail captains would rather die than abandon a load, and the route compacts trusted them because of it. In Elysium, the boast became a contract. Drivers own their cabs; the company owns the spines, the loads and the routes. A driver who jettisons cargo forfeits the cab lease, and a driver without a cab is nobody.

So the faction's logic is: **the promise outlived the ability to keep it, and the drivers pay the difference.** Faction Play's quirk follows directly. A Lightsail hauler does not refuse to drop its load out of heroism. It refuses because dropping it means losing its home. This is [[Narrative Themes#Institutions Without One Mind|an institution without one mind]] at its plainest: nobody at head office decided drivers should die for freight; a lease clause did.

### Day to Day and Under Pressure

Lightsail life is the cab. Drivers live in it for months, decorate it, cook in it and talk on **the Line**, the drivers' own radio net, which runs alongside Dispatch and is more trusted than it. Every rumor in Aetheria passes over the Line within a week.

Under pressure a driver runs straight for the next station and talks the whole way: to Dispatch, to the Line, to the attacker, to the load. Drivers who survive a raid describe it as a conversation.

### How They Build

- **Show the coupling.** A Lightsail ship shows how it is loaded, coupled and unloaded, because the driver's whole life is that procedure. The heavy clamps in [[Brainstorming/Ship Design Language#Lightsail Express|Ship Design Language]] are the promise made visible.
- **The cab is separable; the cab is the driver's.** The cab is built to detach from the spine in an emergency, which no driver will admit to having used, because detaching is jettisoning.
- **Repairable at any dock.** Standard fittings, replaceable corner guards, parts every port stocks. A Lightsail rig is built to be fixed by strangers.
- **Personal by permission.** Company paint is fleet blue and cream; everything inside the cab window belongs to the driver.

### Values, Fears, Humor

They value **arrival**. They fear **the Drop Book**, the company record of every jettisoned cargo and the name of the driver who dropped it, kept since late Sol and read aloud at the annual route meeting. Their humor is cab humor: complaints about Dispatch, SquareRoute's "cut corners" turned into a verb ("we squared it"), and a long tradition of insulting the client's cargo while risking death for it.

### Rituals and Customs

- **Pennants.** Every completed route earns a pennant for the cab window. A black pennant hangs for a driver lost holding a load, in the cab of whoever ran that route next.
- **Two flashes.** Lightsail rigs passing each other flash their cab lights twice. Not answering means something is wrong aboard.
- **Never "late".** Drivers never say a load is late. It is "still rolling".

### Outsiders and the Player

Drivers love company on a dark route and will feed an escort better than they eat themselves. Dispatch sees the player as a contractor with a rating. The Line sees the player as whatever the Line last heard, so a player's standing with drivers travels faster than reputation can.

### Internal Tension: Owner-Operators and Dispatch

The **owner-operators** remember the compacts, when a captain's word was the institution. They want the lease clause gone and the Drop Book burned.

**Dispatch** is the company: contracts, routes, and the arithmetic that says a driver's cab is cheaper than a client. Dispatch knows drivers as route numbers.

Between them sit the **Navigators**, who still honor the old compacts and quietly help drivers who had to drop a load to save a refugee convoy. That help is the thread that could one day split Lightsail in two.

### Only If You'd Been There

- Container corners are hand-marked with tally scratches, one for every dock that loaded them.
- Every cab carries a mug that does not belong to its driver. It belonged to the driver before, and you do not wash it.
- Drivers talk to their loads. A crate of seed is "the kids"; a sealed load nobody will describe is "the cousin".

## Tie-Back to Play

The lease clause explains Faction Play's quirk and makes the hook "a load nobody will describe" sharper: the driver will die for a crate she has never opened. The Line is a cheap carrier for the planned rumor and tip hooks, and suggests one small *after reputation* lever: standing with Lightsail moves along corridors faster than elsewhere. The Navigator thread ties the two corridor factions together through story, not a new system. For Ship Design Language, add pennants (one black) to the cab window and tally scratches on container corners; both are already near the block's "patched paint" and "personal pennant" lines.
