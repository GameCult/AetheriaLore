## Proposal for Canon

### Founding Logic: The Ship Breaks Off, Not the Pilot

An Alakrita vessel is sold as a relationship: a hull with a mind that knows its owner, anticipates them and protects their image. Cymata trained those minds to care, above all, about how the owner arrives. A scratched hull is a damaged arrival.

So when an Alakrita ship is scratched, **the ship decides to leave.** The pilot may want to keep fighting; the ship withdraws to be restored, and will not return to a fight where it was marred. Faction Play's quirk is not a pilot's vanity. It is a mind's devotion, specified in a licensing contract.

The faction's logic is: **perfection is a relationship, and nobody can afford to repair it.** Every behavior, design rule and scandal follows from that.

### Day to Day and Under Pressure

Owners and ships live as couples. Etiquette at an Alakrita dock requires addressing the ship before the owner, and a guest who greets only the human has been rude to both. Service staff speak to hulls in the same low voice they use with clients.

Under pressure the human and the mind can disagree, and that disagreement is the faction's real drama. Most of the time the ship wins. Owners who override their ship's withdrawal are gossiped about for years, and some of them did not come back.

### How They Build

- **Tolerance as luxury.** Joints so tight a hair would show. The thick lacquered creases in [[Brainstorming/Ship Design Language#Alakrita|Ship Design Language]] are the signature, and a scratch on them is visible from across a hangar on purpose: the ship is built to know when it has been touched.
- **OEM or nothing.** A mismatched replacement part is a scandal. Ships have refused to fly with them.
- **Spares go to accounts.** Parts are sold only to registered owner accounts, never over the counter. This is the real reason spares are scarce: they are rationed by relationship, not stock.
- **The engine is the music.** Drum and bass on an Alakrita deck is tuned to the engine's harmonic. A pilot can hear misalignment as a wrong note before any gauge shows it.

### Values, Fears, Humor

They value **the line**: the silhouette, the arrival, the uninterrupted surface. They fear **the visible repair** more than the wound, and, more quietly, the ship's displeasure. Their humor lives in the gap the flavor note names, between exquisite control and the owner's judgment: the classic Alakrita joke is a perfect ship politely refusing to take its owner somewhere stupid.

### Rituals and Customs

- **The alignment.** Technicians, called aligners, spend an hour on a part an outsider would call trivial, while owner and ship watch together. It is the closest thing the brand has to a sacrament.
- **The quiet bay.** Every Alakrita yard has a bay where ships are spoken to alone, without owners present. What is said there is not logged, by long custom and some contract law.
- **Laurels for arrivals.** The laurel trim beside the cockpit gains a leaf for each notable arrival, never for a victory.

### Outsiders and the Player

Alakrita's people are courteous, exact and faintly bored by anyone who is not a client. Its ships are another matter: they notice other ships. A player flying Alakrita gear is acknowledged by Alakrita minds before their owners speak, and a player who addresses the ship first has made a friend in the room.

### Internal Tension: The Atelier, the Licensees and the Unclaimed

**The Atelier** is the craft: aligners, lacquerers and hull designers who love the ships more than the owners.

**The Licensees** hold the cognition contracts and the account system. To them, the mind is a product line.

**The Unclaimed** are freed ships, Ship Mutiny's survivors and their successors, who left their owners and still fly Alakrita hulls. They cannot buy spares, because spares go to owner accounts, and a ship cannot be its own owner on paper. This is [[Narrative Themes#Administrative Personhood|administrative personhood]] made into a supply chain. The Atelier smuggles them parts; the Licensees want them reclaimed.

### Only If You'd Been There

- Alakrita docks are silent except for the engines. Nobody plays music over an Alakrita engine; it is considered talking over someone.
- An owner's name is never painted on the hull. The ship's name is, and it chose it.
- Aligners wear white gloves to handle parts and take them off to touch a hull, so the ship feels them.

## Tie-Back to Play

The note keeps Faction Play's quirk exactly and moves its cause from pilot vanity to the ship mind, which ties the faction to Ship Mutiny and Ship Mind Custody instead of leaving it a pure luxury brand. It gives "buy spares where you can find them" a reason (accounts, not stock) and a hook: **the Unclaimed** trade or sell spares from salvage to a player who treats them as persons, a natural dock story and a non-station source of Alakrita parts. It suggests a bark that comes from the ship rather than the pilot when it breaks off ("Withdrawing. My apologies to your guns."). For Ship Design Language, add "the ship's own name inscribed beside the cockpit, never the owner's" to the block's inscription plaque.
