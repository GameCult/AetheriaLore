# NiteLife Energy

> **Status: brainstorming, 2026-10-03.** Index: [[Brainstorming/Faction Deepening|Faction Deepening]]. Everything under **Proposal for canon** is invention awaiting the operator's ruling.

## Already Established

- [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Minor/NiteLife Energy|NiteLife Energy]] runs power networks, fusion contracts and distributed power in late Sol; dependence makes it strategically untouchable; monopoly practice behind youthful lifestyle branding.
- It joined the Colonists promising an alternative to Earth's overcharging utilities, intending to become the indispensable supplier itself. Malaysian influence; "We keep the lights on, so you can keep on"; upgrade language; the MoveOnPro muon-catalyst reactor needs a Cottage-pi meson reservoir sold only by NiteLife ([[Brainstorming/Faction Flavor and Visual Identity#NiteLife Energy|Faction Flavor]]).
- The operator: "I've changed it to 'Turn down for what?', and their music influence to Complextro so they can have wobble cannons" (2021-01-20).
- Faction Play: guard ships never leave their stations; the reservoir is the real export.

## Proposal for Canon

### Founding Logic: The Night Shift Became the Nightlife

Power is the one service nobody notices until it stops, and it stops at night: when demand shifts, when crews are thinnest, when a habitat is asleep and cannot fix itself. NiteLife began as the people who were awake for that. Its first workforce was the colony grid's night shift, and the company's whole culture grew out of a simple fact of that job: **if you are the one keeping the lights on, the lights are where the party is.**

So the faction's logic is: **uptime is a way of life, and life is something you subscribe to.** The first half is the engineers' honest pride. The second half is what the company built on top of it.

### Day to Day and Under Pressure

NiteLife stations run on a night schedule. The main shift is the late one; offices open when other habitats dim. The service counters, lounges and music are not marketing laid over the utility. They are the break room of a workforce that never sleeps when everyone else does, opened to customers.

Under pressure they hold the node. A NiteLife guard does not leave its station because the station *is* the grid; a guard chasing a raider leaves a habitat exposed to a blackout. Leaving the post to win a fight is, in NiteLife terms, exactly the wrong heroism. This is why Faction Play's tight leash is not timidity: in their culture, pursuit is negligence.

### How They Build

- **The cartridge is the product.** Everything is designed around a replaceable reservoir that docks visibly into the equipment, exactly as [[Brainstorming/Ship Design Language#NiteLife Energy|Ship Design Language]] says. The machine is the console; the cartridge is the game.
- **Swollen shell, honest channel.** Smooth appliance volumes for the customer, one deep service channel for the crew. The channel is where NiteLife engineers actually live, and they are proud that it is ugly.
- **Version everything.** Hardware, software, procedures and people all carry version numbers. A new revision is an event.
- **Wobble.** Weapons are tuned to modulate (the operator's wobble cannons): energy delivered in rhythmic surges rather than a steady beam, because a grid crew's instinct is to manage load, not just dump it.

### Values, Fears, Humor

They value **uptime** above almost anything. "The lights stayed on" is the highest praise a NiteLife worker can give another. They fear **the dark**: a habitat gone black on their watch. They also know, better than any customer, that they are the dependency, and the company's quiet fear is the day a customer learns to make its own mesons.

Their humor is upgrade language applied to everything. A divorced worker is "on Single 2.0"; a bad shift was "a beta"; a dead colleague is "deprecated", said with real grief.

### Rituals and Customs

- **The drop.** A reservoir swap is a performance. The coupling is timed to the beat, and the catch clicks home on the drop. Customers come to watch cartridge changes the way others watch a DJ.
- **Lights-on.** At shift change every light on the station flickers three times: the grid is handed over and still holding.
- **The count.** Every station displays the nights since its last outage, as large as a club's name. Resetting it is a public shame for the whole crew.

### Outsiders and the Player

Everyone is welcome in the lounge. The distinction is not friend or foe but **guest or subscriber**. A ship running a MoveOnPro is "on the list": recognized, upgraded, given priority at the counter and gently reminded that its reservoir is due. A ship on someone else's reactor is a guest, served politely and pitched constantly.

### Internal Tension: The Grid and the Subscription

**The Grid** is the engineers' NiteLife: the Colonist promise of a utility that would not overcharge, kept alive by night crews who would run a blackout cable to a rival's habitat for free if it were dark.

**The Subscription** is the marketing division that turned that pride into lock-in. It wrote the Cottage-pi contract and owns the cartridge patent. Grid crews call Subscription staff "day people", which is the worst insult the company has.

### Only If You'd Been There

- NiteLife stations never fully dim. Spacers navigate by them because they are the brightest thing in a zone, and NiteLife knows it.
- Cottage-pi reservoirs carry a "best before" stamp. Mesons do not keep, and NiteLife does not let you forget it.
- Grid crews wear their implants' firmware numbers on their collars, and compare them the way other trades compare scars.

## Tie-Back to Play

The note turns the tight leash from an arbitrary number into the faction's ethic, which helps the tell (stations ringed by contacts that never leave) read as character. The one proposal that would change a lever is the **best-before reservoir**: if Cottage-pi spoils in cargo over time, "carry spare reservoirs" becomes "carry spares, but not too many, and route through NiteLife space anyway", which strengthens the existing quirk's lock-in instead of adding a new one. It needs only a decay on the consumable, no new system; if that is too much, keep the stamp as flavor. The Cut List keeps burst-then-drain weapons off the table; the wobble modulation is flavor for copy and visuals, not a behavior quirk. For Ship Design Language, add "a large 'nights since last outage' counter on the station hull" to station prompts, and keep the cartridge as the silhouette.
