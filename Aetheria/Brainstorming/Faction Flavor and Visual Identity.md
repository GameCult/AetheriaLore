# Faction Flavor and Visual Identity

Seventeen manufacturers and factions, each with a distinct character, voice, equipment family, and visual identity. These are the people a shipowner buys from, depends on, shows off, or regrets trusting.

The visual descriptions cover silhouette, materials, color, markings, people, and spaces, with an image starting point for each faction. Product names and advertising are collected from [[Brainstorming/Corporate Roster and Item Wishlist|Corporate Roster and Item Wishlist]]; linked faction notes carry their fuller histories.

## Zhestokost

### Character and Voice

Heavy combat hulls and ballistic weapons for buyers who expect to take punishment and keep shooting. **No such thing as overkill**: Marxism-Leninism, industrial brutalism, and death metal. Its advertising treats finesse as an embarrassing substitute for sufficient ammunition.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Zhestokost|Zhestokost]] is a militarized Martian state. It provides dependable food, housing, and repairs while command decides where people work and live. Its heavy weapons depend on coordinated foundries, stockpiles, supply routes, and repair crews. That gives the boast substance: someone has to keep delivering the shells.

The public voice is blunt, declarative, and pleased with its own excessive force. The humor belongs in ammunition ads, workshop remarks, and implausibly narrow disclaimers. People living under command have more varied lives than the slogans allow.

### Equipment and Copy

- **EMPKM / the Emperor:** “Pure Newtonian destruction.”
- **Cannonball:** “All the fun of a medieval siege, none of the smell.”
- **DeathCluster**, **MFer**, **6k Shooter**, and **Trebuchet** carry the siege-and-shrapnel vocabulary. MFer's pitch: “Our shrapnel turns your enemies into scrapnel.”
- **Manhattan reactor:** “might is right and finesse is for fools”.
- **Noka MKI hull:** its disclaimer insists that it “does, in fact, have a functioning brake system”.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Low, broad hulls built from stepped rectangular masses. Thick armor overlaps around recessed machinery; barrels, ammunition feeds, towing points, and replaceable plates remain easy to identify. Repetition makes a ship resemble a moving section of arsenal. The apparent weight comes from deep seams and substantial supports, rather than surface clutter.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Rough cast metal, dark steel, ceramic armor, and baked industrial coatings. Charcoal, iron gray, and dull oxide red, with small cream serial numbers and clearly placed safety marks. Wear collects around access panels, feed mechanisms, and replacement plates. Large block lettering and production stamps suit the command economy; ornamental heraldry is secondary to unit and equipment identification.

**People and spaces:** Heavy work clothes, welding protection, harnesses, and visibly repaired uniforms. Foundry aisles, ammunition handling spaces, and maintenance bays carry the same square geometry as the ships. Show the loaders and mechanics who make sustained fire possible.

**Image starting point:** A squat heavy combat ship in three-quarter view inside an arsenal bay, a worker beside an enormous removable gun assembly for scale. Hard overhead light reveals thick armor edges, soot, and orderly maintenance access. Its identity should survive in silhouette without relying on red paint or political symbols.

## Lucent Media

### Character and Voice

Lasers and active sensors sold by a business that wants every event to have an audience. Attention economics, extravagant gamer styling, and articulated mechanical spectacle shape its products. Lucent's hardware looks good while being watched using it.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Lucent Media|Lucent Media]] controls distribution, advertising, reputation, celebrity management, and much of Venus's public life. Audience access can decide whether someone gets work. The spectacle also rests on editors, moderators, set crews, cleaners, and transmission technicians. Lucent controls Corriedales in this era; Alakrita is a prestige supplier, not simply another Lucent brand.

Its voice is boastful, excitable, and fluent in sponsorship. Product names sound ready for a launch stream. The joke is that a battle is also a content opportunity, with somebody responsible for making sure the sponsor stays in shot.

### Equipment and Copy

- **CShot RainbowLite Lazer:** “both combat and concert ready”.
- **Rave 'n' Ravage** pulse laser, **EyesOnME** ion laser, and **TaranisLG** lightning gun, including the latter's vape tie-in.
- **OK Disperser** heat pump: “built to withstand even the most aggressive of rage quits”.
- **Notorious** reactor.
- **Adrian the Sound Guy:** a soundboard supplying battle effects that “also plays smooth jazz”.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Sculptural mechanical excess: a compact central body surrounded by overlapping shells, swept prongs, raised fins, and detached-looking side plates held on visible supports. Broad curved surfaces taper into sharp tips. Long paired blades reach forward over the nose; smaller fins flare upward and outward at the rear. Deep openings between the shells expose a dense inner chassis. The outline has a strong forward sweep, while its many layers invite the eye to keep finding another component.

**Mechanical detail:** High-end gaming peripherals supply the construction vocabulary: separate palm-like covers, wing-shaped side rests, ribbed grips, recessed vents, knurled wheels, exposed screws, metal rails, and adjustment mechanisms. At ship scale these become overlapping hull covers, outrigger pods, intake grilles, articulated mounts, and visible machinery inside the gaps. Each layer has a clear edge, thickness, attachment, and clearance from the next. Curved fairings and sharp angular plates share the same object. The finish makes technical complexity something an owner wants to show off.

**Materials, color, and markings:** The ship palette uses large, solid areas of golden yellow and white or pale lavender, crossed by cobalt-blue blades and vivid red-orange tips. Dark navy machinery gives the openings depth; muted teal secondary housings separate smaller assemblies. Color follows individual components, so adjoining layers remain readable. Gloss enamel, satin composite shells, polished metal, dark rubberized inserts, and ribbed surfaces provide distinct finishes. Gaming-hardware variants use graphite and black with acid-yellow inserts or RGB strips recessed along seams and undercuts. Lighting accents the construction; the painted ship retains its full identity with every light switched off. Small technical labels and selective branding leave room for the shapes and color blocks to dominate.

**People and spaces:** Performance wear over practical crew equipment; fitted protective panels, bright inserts, and visible fasteners echo the hardware. Controllers and consoles carry the same layered, adjustable construction as the ships. Public display areas give equipment enough space and light to show its profile, while service bays reveal the chassis beneath the removable covers. Cameras and production crews are part of Lucent's world, but the machinery itself supplies the spectacle.

**Image starting point:** A compact Lucent spacecraft in a clear three-quarter front view, isolated against a neutral background under broad studio lighting. A pointed white-and-gold central body sits inside sweeping shell arches, with twin cobalt-blue dorsal prongs reaching toward the nose, red-orange forward blades and rear fins, pale segmented side covers, and teal equipment pods. Large gaps reveal dark blue machinery and the supports connecting the outer layers. Render it as a carefully assembled, extravagantly styled machine with a readable silhouette, crisp component boundaries, and restrained luminous accents. Preserve the curved surfaces as well as the sharp tips.

**Design reference:** The [original Aetheria Lucent ship concept](<D:/Google Drive/Gamecult/Aetheria/Concepts/Ships/lucent_media/transparent_03.png>) anchors the ship's silhouette, layered construction, and color blocking. The gaming-mouse references carry the closely related language of separated covers, exposed chassis, adjustable fittings, ribbed surfaces, and illuminated seams.

## NiteLife Energy

### Character and Voice

Power generation and energy weapons wrapped in a youthful lifestyle brand. **“We keep the lights on, so you can keep on.”** Malaysian influence, interrupted organic curves, and “Turn down for what?” energy shape its identity. It joined the Colonists promising an alternative to Earth's overcharging utilities, with ambitions to become the indispensable supplier itself.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Minor/NiteLife Energy|NiteLife Energy]] already supplies major power networks, fusion contracts, and distributed services in late Sol. Its cheerful image covers the leverage of controlling something every customer needs.

Its voice is upbeat, casual, and full of upgrade language: Pro, Plus, version numbers, and promises to keep going. The dependency is unusually concrete. A great reactor can become a continuing purchase agreement for the one consumable that keeps it running.

### Equipment and Copy

- **MoveOnPro:** the only muon-catalyst fusion reactor on the market; it needs a meson reservoir sold only by NiteLife.
- **Cottage-pi:** the meson reservoir in that proprietary power ecosystem.
- **ChargeBlast+-** and **ChargeBlast SG**.
- **RevvITup 2.0:** “Excellent thrust, appalling torque. Known colloquially as the Unstoppable Force.”
- **FastBlast+-:** “Do less damage, faster.”

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Full, flowing volumes interrupted by crisp slots, flat coupling collars, and abrupt cuts. A reactor might be a smooth swollen shell crossed by a deep service channel; a thruster carries the same curved body into a sharply terminated nozzle. Make proprietary replaceable reservoirs visibly dock into the larger equipment.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Satin midnight blue and petroleum teal, with lime accents and warm white illumination. Molded housings, brushed alloy collars, translucent status strips, and small bright release catches. Graphics use rounded lettering and bold version badges. The finish belongs to an expensive consumer appliance scaled up to infrastructure.

**People and spaces:** Branded utility crews and sociable customer spaces around thoroughly controlled industrial installations. Lightweight workwear, bright service counters, and nightlife advertising make the utility feel like part of the customer's social life.

**Image starting point:** A NiteLife reactor and its removable reservoir in a clean service bay. A broad curved shell is broken by one dark mechanical channel and bright coupling details. Confident lifestyle advertising occupies the wall beside the very ordinary job of changing the cartridge.

## Lightsail Express

### Character and Voice

Freight hulls, cargo bays, and docking equipment with a Southern US trucking influence. **“We'll lose our lives before we lose your cargo.”** Modular, blocky, and emphatically space trucking.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Minor/Lightsail Express|Lightsail Express]] built its late-Sol reputation on reliable delivery, closely associated with the Cetacean Navigators and agreements that kept routes usable. Its captains moved food, data, and refugees when other institutions could no longer keep their commitments.

In Elysium, the sales pitch still promises speed and reliability, but customers cannot count on either. With no postal service and the major freight contracts in its grip, Lightsail holds business that a better delivery record alone cannot take from it.

The brand speaks in freight promises, route names, and practical cab humor. Its crews give an enormous logistics business a human face: somebody still has to dock the load and explain the delay.

### Equipment and Copy

- **Store-All Plus:** “Take all your cargo wherever your shipgo.”
- **SquareRoute** navigation software: “Sometimes it's good to cut corners.”
- **ClearPath** machine gun and **True North** thruster.
- Freight hulls, cargo modules, and docking bays give the brand its practical center.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Long cargo spines, rectangular detachable loads, heavy docking collars, and a distinct powered cab or tug section. A ship's arrangement should reveal how it is loaded, coupled, and unloaded. Large equipment has rails, tie-downs, and replaceable corner protection.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Faded fleet blue, warm cream, and vermilion route stripes on enamel-coated metal. Scuffed container corners, polished handholds, patched paint, company lettering, cargo numbers, and inspection labels. Small cab lights and personal decorations make the working vessel feel occupied.

**People and spaces:** Work jackets, practical boots, cab curtains, mugs, meal counters, and handwritten route notes. Southern trucking flavor comes through the workplace and its social life; the entire crew need not share one accent or dress code.

**Image starting point:** A laden freight tug at a docking gantry, its long chain of blocky modules stretching behind a small inhabited cab. Warm cabin light and a mechanic at a coupling break up the industrial scale. It should look handled daily rather than staged for a military parade.

## Finch Cybernetics

### Character and Voice

Passive sensors and bodily upgrades associated with cybernetic transhumanism. Sleek organic forms express a technocratic faith in careful integration. Its promise is effortless capability: better perception, finer movement, a body or vessel that responds as though the equipment were always part of it.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Finch Cybernetics|Finch Cybernetics]] is a premium medical and augmentation company whose careful fitting, rehabilitation, and maintenance restore real abilities. Access depends on company-controlled clinics, coverage, compatible parts, and firmware. Its elegance is also a visible class distinction.

Its clinical voice promises dignity through precise fitting and dependable support. Its ship equipment advertising is more flippant, selling the pleasure of a machine that responds immediately to its pilot.

### Equipment and Copy

- **ChirOptos** sensor with patented **EchoLock:** “so you always fly under the radar”.
- **Galapagos** hull: “the highest quality and lowest cost*”; the qualification is “when compared to units of higher cost”.
- **Prokope** thruster: “67% fatal and 100% fun.”
- Passive sensing, implants, and interfaces supply the core identity across its equipment families.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Continuous, tapered forms with the fit and articulation of a joint or sensory organ. Soft transitions between modules, flush sensor apertures, and recessed seams. A hull should look composed around sensing and movement; an implant should fit the person's body rather than use the person as a display stand.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Pearl ceramic, satin titanium, translucent smoky membranes, and small celadon indicators. Restrained markings placed where a technician can read them. Organic describes the continuity of the forms, not exposed flesh or indiscriminate tentacles.

**People and spaces:** Comfortable clinical clothing, fitted prostheses, tactile calibration tools, and quiet rehabilitation rooms. Show someone performing a precise ordinary action: handling a cup, working a small fastener, or testing sensation.

**Image starting point:** A passive sensor assembly beside a Finch prosthetic hand on a calibration bench, both sharing smooth tapered shells and nearly invisible interfaces. Soft broad light makes the fit and finish clear. The distinction from NiteLife is continuous integration, where NiteLife deliberately interrupts its curves.

## Aeronautics Unlimited

### Character and Voice

All-rounder hulls, mining equipment, dumbfire weapons, and explosives for getting resources out of places other firms have not finished reaching. British influence and minimalist geometric curves shape AU's identity. Its hardware is the general-purpose kit around which a new operation can grow.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Aeronautics Unlimited|Aeronautics Unlimited]] finances and operates yards, extraction sites, habitats, and supply networks. Residents gain homes and work but depend on the company's decision to keep funding repairs and deliveries. Expansion brings opportunity and leaves someone responsible for living at the unfinished end of it.

Its voice can combine practical confidence, exploration references, and painfully pleased engineering jokes. The equipment should look usable by a mixed contractor crew, with a clear task and sensible places to attach other tools.

### Equipment and Copy

- **Leonid** dumbfire swarm: “A rain of pain that falls mainly on space planes.”
- **Ranger** active sensor: “practically haunted by all the ghosts it picks up”.
- **Lidenbrock** mining rig and **Vulcan** reactor.
- **Jason** hull and upgraded **Argo:** “because this ship is fire”.
- **Earp** six-shooter and **Talaria:** “Go forth, first.”

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Cylinders, rounded rectangles, simple arcs, and compact wedges combined into balanced working machines. Clear tool sockets, lifting points, trusses, and removable modules. Curves are drawn from straightforward geometry, with very little decorative flourish.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Chalk white, slate blue, bare aluminum, and restrained ochre safety panels. Matte finishes, numbered bays, survey grids, and concise technical lettering. Abrasion and mineral dust reveal use without turning every panel into scrap.

**People and spaces:** Survey equipment, contractor jackets, tool lockers, temporary offices, and half-finished construction shells. A tidy manufactured core surrounded by an expanding worksite expresses AU better than a pristine corporate headquarters.

**Image starting point:** A compact utility ship servicing an asteroid excavation, its cylindrical modules and rounded structural frame visible around a replaceable mining tool. Neutral work lights reveal attachment points and scale. Keep it simpler and less luxurious than Alakrita, with a silhouette driven by access and versatility.

## Alakrita

### Character and Voice

**“Speed. Elegance. Alakrita.”** Light thrusters and fast, fragile hulls for people who can afford performance and its consequences. Roman influence, maximalism, aerodynamic creases, and drum and bass define its identity. Spare parts are scarce; the perfectly aligned whole is failure-prone; exclusivity remains part of the sale.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Minor/Alakrita|Alakrita]] sells luxury spacecraft, exclusive interiors, and intelligent ship systems. It licenses cognition from suppliers rather than owning every part of the mind aboard. Treating those minds as luxury possessions helped produce conflicts around ship autonomy.

Its advertising is poised and economical with words, confident that the buyer already wants to be seen arriving. The joke belongs in the gap between exquisite control and the owner's judgment.

### Equipment and Copy

- **Victoire:** “the most responsive thruster on the market, for the most irresponsible of racing pilots”.
- **Spectra:** “Travelling light, for those travelling light.”
- **Arctica:** “Go blazingly fast, without burning up.”
- **Tachyon** hull: “The next best thing to light.”

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Long, sharply creased bodies, swept blades, thin layered fins, and tightly aligned engine elements. Maximalism comes from many deliberate folds and fine details held in a disciplined overall shape. Aerodynamic styling is a brand choice in vacuum, not a claim that the ship needs air to work.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Ivory lacquer, wine-red enamel, black glass, and narrow polished bronze or gold inlays. Recessed fasteners and very precise joints; small scratches or a mismatched replacement stand out painfully. Roman influence can inform ceremonial proportion, inscription panels, and restrained laurel-like trim without turning the hull into a literal chariot.

**People and spaces:** Tailored travel clothes, meticulous service uniforms, pale upholstery, sculpted handrails, and custom fittings. Let the care required to preserve perfection appear in a technician aligning an apparently trivial part.

**Image starting point:** A long racing hull suspended above a luxury service cradle, its ivory creases and metallic inlays catching narrow studio lights. One removed thruster reveals how little tolerance the elegant shell leaves. The shape should read as expensive even without visible branding.

## Adrasteia

### Character and Voice

**“Shhh.”** Stealth and negative-entropy technology sold by a supplier whose location is unknown. Its premise is **the best defense is not to need one**, with Indian influence, sleek angular facets, and noir jazz. Other manufacturers dislike how little they know about it.

[[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Technology/Running Cold|Running Cold]] gives the capability a price. Adrasteia's equipment uses proprietary charged strange matter to convert heat into high-energy photons. Staying cold spends supplies and wears machinery; the system can also remove heat its crew needs to live. Quiet operation has a consumable bill and a human limit.

Its voice is sparse, dry, and faintly unhelpful. Product names invoke absence and impossible physics. The mystery should concern who makes the machinery and how it works, while the image still shows a coherent object.

### Equipment and Copy

- **Enigma Device:** arrives “in an unmarked box from an unknown address, with no instructions and no receipt”. A UV smiley sticker was added after customers kept losing it when switched on.
- **DarkMatter X:** a negentropy reactor that “has engendered a number of accusations of witchcraft”.
- **CryoVoid** and **Heat Death:** the two halves of the **Santulanum Duality Thermal Dispersion System**.
- **Silent H** passive sensor and **Silent T** active sensor.
- **ColdFire:** a stealth laser that does less damage than competitors' weapons.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Low, asymmetrical wedges and broad shallow facets, with recessed apertures and tightly closed surfaces. Components appear to tuck into one another. Make the important shapes legible through grazing light and restrained edge contrast rather than covering them in glowing circuitry.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Matte graphite, black ceramic, dark indigo, and a very slight violet sheen at oblique angles. Nearly absent branding. The little UV smiley can be the one absurdly friendly detail visible under an inspection lamp. Cold equipment may collect frost in a suitable atmosphere; frost is not a universal vacuum effect.

**People and spaces:** Quiet service rooms, concealed couplings, careful handling, and crew thermal protection. Unbranded insulated workwear and narrow inspection lights keep attention on the operation rather than the supplier's identity.

**Image starting point:** An Enigma Device on a dark inspection bench, its faceted shell resolved by one grazing white light and a small UV lamp revealing the smiley. Keep the silhouette visible. For a ship image, show a cold, low-profile hull against brighter debris rather than using a luminous magical cloak.

## Ewan Hart Inc

### Character and Voice

Farming equipment with a Midwestern US, tractor, dieselpunk, and bluegrass identity. **Money does grow on trees**, provided someone keeps the agricultural machinery running. Its usefulness is easy to understand: people need dinner after the more glamorous suppliers have left.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Minor/Ewan Hart|Ewan Hart]] supplies seeds, microbial soil systems, closed-loop ecologies, and agricultural support. Its systems can help settlements feed themselves while also creating dependence on proprietary ecologies they cannot readily reproduce.

The voice is familiar, practical, and capable of delivering an entire sales pitch in one unimpressed sentence. Competence matters more than rustic decoration.

### Equipment and Copy

- **Space Tractor:** “It's a tractor. In space.”
- **Good Old Boy Mining Drill**.
- Farming and habitat food-production equipment provide the broader family around those two ideas.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Broad housings, rounded fenders around exposed working parts, prominent tool arms, heavy couplings, and easy-to-reach service covers. Carry tractor proportions into vacuum equipment through the power unit and implement arrangement; wheels belong only where the machine actually uses a surface.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Faded harvest gold, deep agricultural green, cream labels, black hoses, and greasy steel. Thick enamel chips around corners. Large readable gauges, tactile controls, replaceable guards, and maker plates suggest equipment serviced with work gloves on. Dieselpunk is a mechanical visual influence, not a requirement for an engine exhausting into a closed habitat.

**People and spaces:** Patched work clothes, seed bins, irrigation pipe, tool racks, and crowded maintenance sheds beside carefully tended crops. The plants should look like food someone depends on.

**Image starting point:** A compact agricultural machine being serviced at the edge of a pressurized growing bay, its blunt power housing supporting interchangeable tools. Warm crop light catches worn enamel while a mechanic reaches an accessible coupling. The machine can be homely and thoroughly advanced at the same time.

## Rossum & Douglas

### Character and Voice

AI, automation, compute, and guided launchers under a reassuring robot-company image. German influence, trapezoidal panels, and muzak establish an insistently ordinary corporate identity. R&D is boring. Its ships are boring. Its catalog offers another size of the same flying wedge with the confidence of a company announcing a substantial achievement. The dangerous part of the advertisement generally arrives after the asterisk.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Minor/Rossum & Douglas|Rossum & Douglas]] centers on engineering assessment and certification. It examines particular configurations and supplies evidence others can use to judge risk. It also supplies or certifies operating shells for AI. Its findings do not decide personhood or guarantee that nothing can go wrong.

Its voice is institutional reassurance whose precise scope matters more than the customer first realizes. Product advertising stretches a guarantee to comic absurdity; technical findings retain the exact conditions and limits on which an operator must rely. Rossum's own products and other manufacturers' certified systems are distinct.

### Equipment and Copy

- **GT 1K** guided missile: “guaranteed to hit the target!*”; the target is defined as whatever it strikes.
- **SafeStuff** cargo bays: valuables explicitly exclude “pets, children, cupcakes, friendship”.
- **MIRV** line: escalating missile jokes.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** The flying wedge. Broad, shallow triangular or trapezoidal hulls narrow toward a blunt nose and terminate in a wide engine-bearing rear face. Large flat dorsal planes, beveled perimeter edges, and a thicker central section supply almost the entire shape. Small recessed windows, flush hardpoints, regular vents, and rectangular access panels barely interrupt it. Across the range, the wedge gets longer, wider, or thicker while retaining the same basic outline. Equipment repeats the vocabulary in squat chamfered boxes and sloped housings.

**Surface detail:** Repetitive panel seams, shallow grilles, identical service hatches, and occasional inset machinery fill out the hull without giving it a new idea. Most mechanisms sit behind covers. Detail establishes scale and manufacture; it leaves the broad planes and ordinary silhouette intact. The dullness is deliberate, not missing work waiting to be rescued with sculptural fins, exposed mechanical intricacy, or an unexpectedly elegant profile.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Matte or satin industrial coatings in light gray, muted blue-gray, navy, and off-white. A broad stripe or plain geometric paint block provides the livery. Dark engine recesses, small amber indicators, standard serial numbers, and modest corporate lettering complete it. Wear appears as ordinary scuffs and replacement panels. Neither exotic materials nor elaborate weathering turns the ship into a more interesting object.

**People and spaces:** Standard workwear, diagnostic carts, repetitive maintenance bays, waiting-room furniture, and bland reception desks. Catalog photography uses even lighting and conventional views. Muzak accompanies a sales presentation in which a slightly larger cargo compartment receives several slides.

**Image starting point:** An R&D utility spacecraft in a conventional three-quarter view against a neutral background. A broad, shallow blue-gray wedge has a blunt tapered nose, flat paneled upper surfaces, beveled sides, tiny recessed cockpit windows, and a wide rear engine bank. Off-white geometric livery crosses the hull. Repeat rectangular hatches and shallow vents across otherwise uneventful planes. Use even lighting, restrained engine glow, and ordinary industrial finishes. The result is a competently rendered, thoroughly unremarkable production ship.

**Design reference:** R&D's repeated flying wedges are a deliberate visual jab at the wedge-heavy ship designs in *Elite Dangerous*. The sameness is the faction's identity: a lineup of different models should look like a procurement comparison among minor variations on the same design.

## Death Monkey Explosives

### Character and Voice

**“Turn it up, burn it down.”** Thermal machinery, incendiaries, and equipment for continuing past sensible limits. Australian influence, menacing spikes, and lyrical product copy accompany the sales pitch: “Some people just want to watch the world burn. We're here to help!”

[[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Factions/Death Monkey Explosives|Death Monkey Explosives]] is a network of workshops, armed crews, revolutionary cells, and performers sharing a brand that nobody centrally owns. Yara So is its disputed founder; cells circulate incompatible accounts of her life and teachings. Different workshops can sell incompatible equipment under the same product name.

Members attack coercive dependencies and disagree bitterly about the suffering they cause. Their cultivated communion with the void can carry destruction through connections that would normally contain it. Crews prepare physical severance, recovery supplies, and care for people returning from that state. The noise accompanies real technical discipline and consequences it cannot make safe.

The voice is loud, profane, musical, and intimate. Product lyrics, remembered comrades, workshop jokes, and warnings can share a surface. Care belongs visibly beside the threat.

### Equipment and Copy

- **cold like my heart:** “heat death in a bottle like a molotov cocktail”. A [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Factions/Death Monkey Explosives Products|high-performance heat pump]] that transfers heat elsewhere in the system.
- **scorched void policy**, **banana split**, **pretty pretty bang bang**, **rail against the machine**, and **watch me burn** carry the weapons voice.
- **deep space burnout** thrusters and **not if i see you first** sensor.
- **gamma raybans (make you incandescent):** protection for work in ionizing environments.
- **make bombs not war** power generation and **fuck your feelings** sensing equipment.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Exposed serviceable machinery, hard projecting fins, spikes, heavy braces, detachable feed sections, and conspicuous break points. Hazardous connections have physical cuts a crew can reach. The design looks willing to spend parts to preserve one more action; the remaining repair access still makes sense.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Blackened steel, heat-stained alloy, scratched enamel, scorched red, acid yellow, and uneven white lettering. Workshop marks, hand-painted names, lyric fragments, and replaced panels vary between cells. The polished, expensive **cold like my heart** carries the same aggressive branding on a cleaner, finely finished shell.

**People and spaces:** A workshop that can become a venue: sound equipment, suspended tools, marked recovery lockers, food, and personal names beside emergency supplies. Crew protection is practical even when its paintwork is confrontational. Spikes should not make every useful handle inaccessible.

**Image starting point:** A DME thruster and cooling assembly during repair, with a severed feed section laid out beside it and a crew member repainting a name on a recovery locker. Hard work lights reveal heat damage and deliberate access. The threat comes from credible machinery and its users, not from covering everything in skulls.

## Miss Terri's Sugariffic Snack Company

### Character and Voice

Corrosive weapons and consumables sold with the enthusiasm of a candy counter. **Candy for all! Unexpected experimental substances for some!** A cultural melting pot of flavors, kawaii styling, and future bass makes every product feel like a new treat. Miss Terri's makes it difficult to tell where that treat ends and a chemical intervention begins.

Its voice is sugary, excitable, and cheerfully specific about ingredients. The unpleasant implication follows from what the product actually does: a snack stimulates a machine mind; a sauce is hot in a sense the customer may have underestimated.

### Equipment and Copy

- **Fizz Fizz Bang:** patented sherbet-fountain technology; **Cherry on Top**.
- **SodaPop!** chemically stabilized reactor: “'Slightly' corrosive”.
- **Fries With That:** a laser whose copy compares use to throwing a frail body into boiling oil.
- **Double Bubble Gum:** a time-release melee weapon.
- **White-Hot Sauce:** core-fusion chiles and 613 herbs and spices.
- **BitsyBytes AI Hyperdrive Snackz:** “Amphetamine for your AI!”

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Capsule shapes, stacked confectionery layers, bulbous reservoirs, squeeze nozzles, twist caps, and rounded dispensing mechanisms. Candy packaging and industrial chemical handling share one silhouette. The feed lines and sealed chambers still connect sensibly.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Strawberry pink, mint, cream, cherry red, and translucent soda amber. Gloss enamel, jelly-like transparent reservoir walls, foil seals, and brightly colored corrosion-resistant plumbing. Bubbly lettering, ingredient panels, cute mascots, and serving-size graphics sit beside real pressure fittings.

**People and spaces:** Cheerful aprons or branded protective coveralls, tasting-counter displays, and sealed processing equipment. Make the packaging edible-looking; do not make every structural component literally food.

**Image starting point:** A sherbet-fountain weapon on a pristine candy-shop display, its glossy capsule reservoir feeding a clearly mechanical nozzle through sealed lines. Soft commercial lighting makes the colors inviting while tiny etched corrosion marks near the outlet reveal use. Corriedales sells beloved toys; Miss Terri's sells things to ingest, dispense, or chemically unleash.

## Aya Collective

### Character and Voice

Self-repair and life support with an Afrofuturist identity, drawing on West African influences, Akan languages, and Afrihili. Its equipment matters when someone needs clean air, a repaired body, or a working pump without surrendering their independence to a supplier.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Aya Collective|Aya Collective]] is a federation of communes, cooperatives, clinics, sanctuaries, and mutual-aid logistics. Local institutions retain authority; coordination keeps shared supplies and refuge routes working. Care requires skilled labor and difficult allocation choices. Neither a self-repairing machine nor a generous promise removes that work.

**Voice:** Direct, welcoming, and confident about what people can maintain together. Instructions and repair knowledge carry its identity as strongly as a slogan.

### Equipment

Life-support systems and self-repair equipment sit alongside the clinics, pumps, food systems, and repair supplies that keep a sanctuary usable. Easy inspection and local maintenance make the equipment part of shared practical knowledge.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Rounded protective frames around replaceable cells, branching fluid paths, accessible couplings, and modular parts sized for local handling. Show how a person opens, repairs, and closes the system. Repeated modules can form broad woven-looking arrangements without implying that decorative fabric carries pressure loads.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Terracotta, warm cream, deep green, charcoal, and restrained copper. Durable matte shells, washable soft surfaces where people touch them, and visible repairs that fit properly. Patterned removable panels and local workshop marks allow variation across the federation.

**People and spaces:** Clinics, kitchens, repair benches, plant rooms, and shared courtyards adapted to habitats. People with different bodies work and rest there. Locally made woven screens, patterned clothing, and decorated removable panels carry cultural expression alongside modern technical equipment. Use supplied language and specific cultural references for inscriptions and meaningful symbols.

**Image starting point:** Two residents replacing a life-support cartridge in a lived-in communal clinic. Rounded modular frames, terracotta panels, copper fittings, and one patterned removable cover give it a distinct material identity. The technology is advanced, and the people using it are visibly allowed to understand it.

## Corriedales

### Character and Voice

**Family-friendly warfare.** A Lebanese-influenced toy company whose small component solved a targeting problem, opening a weapons market. Bubbly forms, children's toys, and in-universe cartoon characters give superfans a reason to outfit an entire ship in matching equipment.

[[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Minor/Corriedales|Corriedales]] is Lucent's family-entertainment brand in late Sol, making toys, mascots, companion organisms, and supposedly safe spectacle. Its existing line is **“From dolls to death, we'll always be with you.”** Familiarity can make surveillance and behavioral control feel like affection.

Its voice addresses a customer who already knows and loves the characters. Buying the next weapon can feel like completing a collection begun in childhood.

### Equipment and Copy

- **Lightning Wand**.
- **Charmed Shot**.
- **Corvald Corwin's Hot Shot**.
- **Abra Cadabra Alakazap**.
- Character-themed weapon families and matching ship outfits are the central merchandising idea.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Rounded toy proportions, oversized friendly housings, wand-like barrels, scalloped protective shells, and deliberate character-face arrangements. A targeting head might suggest a mascot's eyes while retaining recognizable optics. Repeated attachments make a set feel collectible.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Enamel and molded shells in sky blue, butter yellow, coral, and lavender, with soft-looking bumpers and contrasting mechanical interiors. Character portraits, badges, collectible series numbers, and cheerful instructions. Edge wear should show the difference between a toy-like shell and the serious equipment beneath it.

**People and spaces:** Merchandising displays, familiar character murals, and adults with lifelong attachments to particular series. Matching collectibles occupy household shelves and cockpit consoles; a beloved character can follow someone from a nursery to a gunner's station.

**Image starting point:** A ship's weapon rack fitted out as a matching Corvald Corwin collection. Rounded colorful housings repeat a mascot's visual features around real mounts and emitters. Bright retail lighting makes the affectionate design clear without turning the weapon itself into a harmless toy.

## Odla Framgång

### Character and Voice

Knockoffs, pseudo-spiritual products, and multi-level affiliate marketing in a Swedish retrofuturist, populuxe package. Most of the catalog is dubious; occasional excellent equipment keeps people searching. The range runs from zero-gravity spoon-knives to an exceptional nullgen shield.

The same commercial appeal runs through late-Sol [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Framgång|Framgång]]: wellness, purpose, and advancement sold through franchises and recurring purchases. Relief can be real while continuing hardship becomes a reason to buy further treatment.

The voice shifts between radiant assurance and bewildering product names. Its offer is personal improvement through one more purchase, with enough genuine value to make refusing the next pitch harder.

### Equipment and Copy

- **Spifes:** spoon-knives for zero-gravity snacking.
- The **ultimate nullgen shield**.
- The **Onomatopoeia** line: **Pow!**, **Click Click BOOM**, **Krrrrrrrchkkachkka**, **ch-ch-ch-ch--ch...fwoosh!**, and **Shsha-Clunk-Ow**, with umlauts as a branding flourish.
- **eeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeee:** an active sensor with advice about keeping a potato in one's sock instead of useful product explanation.
- Weapons named for durations of silence, and **gnrrrr-gnrrr**, a continuous weapon.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** Domestic space-age curves, oval control faces, chrome fins, pedestal mounts, and exaggerated luxury trim. Familiar competitor mechanisms fit inside shells that promise a better lifestyle. An awkward adapter or overly deep casing can reveal where the imitation does not quite fit.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Avocado, peach, cream, turquoise, chrome, and champagne gold. Gloss plastic, faux wood inserts, mirrored badges, starbursts, and aspirational seals. Show differences in finish quality across a product range; the rare excellent unit should be allowed to look and work well.

**People and spaces:** Demonstration lounges, affiliate stalls, presentation cases, and domestic aspirations scaled to a habitat. The seller may sincerely believe in the product and badly need the commission.

**Image starting point:** A populuxe sales display with a beautiful shield unit beside a ridiculous spife set and a bulky imitation sensor. Warm showroom light catches peach lacquer and chrome. Its exaggerated domestic luxury distinguishes it from Alakrita's precise, expensive performance hardware.

## Pirates

### Character and Voice

Cobbled-together stolen technology, crew names that change from encounter to encounter, and products named in emoji. Junkyard improvisation gives each vessel its shape. What holds it together is the crew's available parts, skills, and willingness to live with the result.

Each crew has its own markings, habits, and history of acquisition.

The voice is rude, opportunistic, and intensely local. Repainted names, stolen slogans, and a joke only the crew understands can all share one hull. Humor changes with the people aboard and the trouble they have recently survived.

### Equipment and Copy

- **🔥** flamethrower: “Turns out we did start the fire after all.”
- Emoji names turn equipment identification into a crew vocabulary.
- Stolen and adapted equipment provides the range rather than an exclusive technology specialization.

### Visual Identity

**Silhouette and construction:** An identifiable donor hull with asymmetrical additions, cut-down modules, adapted hardpoints, exposed cable routes, and bolted salvage plates. Each major addition has a plausible mounting and supply path. The ship can be ugly while remaining comprehensible to the people repairing it.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Several surviving manufacturer finishes interrupted by primer, bare welds, heat discoloration, and a crew's chosen accent color. Crossed-out serials, overpainted corporate logos, and large emoji marks identify appropriation. Maintain a few repeated crew markings so the image has unity despite the mixed parts.

**People and spaces:** Improvised work clothes, salvaged safety equipment, personal possessions, and tools fitted to incompatible fasteners. A good repair beside a ridiculous cosmetic choice makes the crew more convincing than random clutter.

**Image starting point:** A stolen freight hull with one sleek sensor pod, an oversized industrial gun, and a carefully braced replacement engine. Old paint schemes remain visible under a new crew emblem. Work lights pick out the joints that explain how it was assembled. DME shares workshop traditions and ritual; this crew's identity comes from its particular history of acquisition.

## Megiddo

### Character and Voice

Defensive technology, Hebrew cultural expression, and choral sound define [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Factions/Megiddo|Megiddo]]: mobile communities that doubt whether Elysium is the same creation humanity inhabited before the Rupture. Most Jewish communities reject Megiddo's conclusion, and Megiddo does not represent them.

Families live, study, work, and celebrate inside defended fleets while their dead await burial in hidden cryogenic Vaults. The faction preserves bodies, archives, and witnesses until the event it expects to resolve reality's status. Its engineers assume outside supplies and information may fail. Other people buy that protection without sharing the faith.

**“If this is Israel, where is it?”** expresses its central question. Its public voice is deliberate, exact about custody, and unwilling to treat survival as proof of continuity. Ordinary warmth persists inside that uncertainty.

### Equipment

Shields express Megiddo's defensive identity alongside layered armor, redundant radiators, passive sensing, decoys, hardened communication, interception, evacuation systems, thermal reservoirs, and cryogenic preservation.

### Visual Identity

**Visual anchors:** Earth constellations recur on ceilings, hulls, and woven screens. They must remain distinguishable from maps of the present Elysian sky. A Vault is spherical, cold, and deliberately unremarkable; it is concealed across a dispersed fleet, not displayed at the center of a visible escort formation.

**Silhouette and construction:** Public defensive vessels have nested protective volumes, recessed habitable spaces, substantial shutters, and separate service sections. Interiors reveal multiple protected routes and independently maintainable systems. The hidden Vault is the exception to ceremonial display: a quiet, unlabeled sphere that can resemble an inert rock.

**Materials, color, and markings:** Fleet palette of blue-black, warm pale ceramic, aged metal, and small silver constellation inlays. Heavy textiles soften occupied interiors. Earth-star patterns belong in specific commemorative and ritual surfaces, while local navigation uses a visibly different diagram style. Accurate Hebrew inscriptions require supplied text; decorative invented lettering would not carry the intended meaning.

**People and spaces:** Families, teachers, maintainers, and priest-technicians in spaces built for long residence. A room can contain a meal, repair work, and an absent sky on the ceiling. The community's care and its constraints should both be visible through lived use rather than endless empty tomb corridors.

**Image starting point:** A family and a technician in a protected fleet interior beneath an Earth-constellation ceiling, warm domestic light set against deep armored recesses. For an exterior, choose either a recognizable defensive vessel or a concealed Vault; an illuminated ceremonial fortress surrounded by escorts would contradict the Vault's survival method.
