# Bloom Habitat Systems Walkthroughs

Status: brainstorming. This note tests whether the Bloom ontology can explain movement and failure without hiding behind component lists.

## Baseline Physical Model Under Test

A mature Bloom is a rotating manufactured cylinder with a broad pressurized interior. The inhabited shell rotates. The axial hub is despun or partially despun for docking, traffic control, and external alignment. The interface between the two is expensive: transfer collars, rotary seals, spoke cars, utility swivels, bearings, maglev guides, and emergency locks.

This hybrid model is not final canon yet, but it solves several problems at once:

- ships dock at a mostly stable hub instead of chasing a rotating city wall
- residents live at high radius where gravity is comfortable
- light collection and traffic control can remain aligned to external conditions
- spokes become meaningful infrastructure instead of decorative lines
- class power concentrates at spoke bases and transfer interfaces

If this model fails, the alternatives are a fully rotating spire with despun dock collars, or a fully despun axial system with non-rigid transfer spokes. Both are possible, but each needs explicit machinery.

## Walkthrough 1: A Passenger Arrives

A passenger vessel docks at the despun hub. The first spaces are not scenic; they are control spaces. The ship meets a docking collar, hard seals, contamination sniffers, traffic authority, and customs logic. People do not step straight into the Bloom's open air because the open interior is precious. The hub protects the commons from pathogens, pressure mistakes, firmware trouble, contraband, and social trouble.

The passenger path is:

1. docking collar
2. pressure confirmation
3. customs and quarantine
4. identity/corporate access check
5. baggage/cargo separation
6. spoke assignment
7. rotating transfer
8. spoke-base arrival
9. open habitat air

The spoke-base arrival matters emotionally. This is the first time the visitor sees the Bloom as a world instead of a machine: gravity settles, the open atmosphere becomes visible, light has a direction, sound changes, and the curved distance of the cylinder starts doing its quiet psychological work.

AU distortion:

Corporate visitors descend through clean, bright transfer halls. Contract labor goes through intake lanes, debt confirmation, equipment issue, and sometimes medical scan before entering the same air. The architecture tells both groups that they arrived in the same habitat. It is lying by route design.

## Walkthrough 2: A Worker Reaches Service Ring Kappa

A baseline rigger living in service housing does not commute through the prestige spoke plaza unless management wants the optics. The practical path is shorter and meaner:

1. service housing corridor
2. shift gate
3. timekeeping check
4. locker and equipment issue
5. muster corridor
6. industrial-yard edge
7. badge or foreman release into Service Ring Kappa

The worker path is designed for countability. AU needs to know who is on shift, what tools they carry, what claimshare bracket they are in, whether they accepted the waiver, and whether a stoppage can be blamed on them later.

The octopoid worker path is different:

1. service or specialist housing
2. support-rig prep room
3. humidity skin check
4. oxygenation loop check
5. pressure cuff fit
6. limiter or compliance interface confirmation
7. work-support station
8. crawl throat release

That path is medical, mechanical, and disciplinary at once. The body is being prepared as a tool. If the story does not show that preparation somewhere, the later crawl throat danger floats loose.

The superintendent path is cleaner:

1. spoke-base control deck
2. badge-only corridor
3. glassed Kappa operations gallery
4. raised supervisor catwalk
5. badge stair or lift into work ring

This is why Ilya can arrive quickly. It is not because she is narratively convenient. The yard was built so authority can observe from above and enter through a protected route.

## Walkthrough 3: One Breath Of Air

A breath of air in a Bloom is not just oxygen in a room. It is a chain of recovery, circulation, thermal exchange, and social permission.

Working chain:

1. Oxygen enters the civic atmosphere from biological production, stored reserves, or electrolysis.
2. Fans and pressure controls push supply air into occupied districts near the outward/ground side.
3. People, animals, farms, factories, and machines add carbon dioxide, humidity, heat, particulates, volatiles, and odor.
4. Warm contaminated air tends to move inward relative to effective gravity.
5. High/inward returns pull air toward return trunks before it forms uncontrolled layers.
6. Filters, condensers, catalytic processors, bioreactors, and trace-contaminant traps clean it.
7. Heat exchangers remove thermal load into coolant loops.
8. Air returns to district supply or reserve buffers.

Spaces created by this chain:

- low/outward supply runs
- high/inward return galleries
- district fan rooms
- smoke curtain housings
- contaminant trap rooms
- humidity recovery lines
- sensor nests
- access hatches for filters and shutters

Scene implication:

If a worker says a route is unsafe, the exact question matters: unsafe because of pressure, smoke, heat, contaminant, sensor lie, valve drift, or because a previous worker got pinned while the lung flexed? Air system words need verbs attached.

## Walkthrough 4: Water Becomes Waste Becomes Water

Water arrives at the hub as cargo ice, stored reserves, or recovered loop water. It is checked before entering the habitat economy because a closed world cannot afford casual contamination. After treatment, it splits into grades.

Potable water goes to homes, clinics, food preparation, and emergency reserves. Irrigation water goes to farms, algae walls, fungal digesters, soil mats, and hydroponic racks. Industrial water goes to cooling, cleaning, fabrication, washdown, and hazard suppression. Coolant may be chemically distinct from water but shares pumps, exchangers, and maintenance problems close enough to cause trouble.

Return paths:

- greywater from sinks, showers, laundries, and humidity recovery
- blackwater from sewage and medical systems
- nutrient water from farms
- industrial wash from yards and factories
- contaminated water from incidents
- coolant loop losses and exchanger leaks

The important point is not that every loop exists. The important point is that every crossing between loops is suspicious. A heat exchanger, membrane, valve, or human shortcut can turn one grade into another in the bad direction.

Spaces created:

- hub quarantine tanks
- district storage bladders
- pump rooms
- potable trunk galleries
- greywater return runs
- industrial washdown drains
- nutrient recovery processors
- sterilization and cracking plant
- contaminated-hold cells

AU distortion:

Better districts get faster taste correction, more reliable pressure, lower contaminant thresholds, and quicker repair. Poor districts are told the water is within tolerance. A habitat can become unequal by ppm.

## Walkthrough 5: Heat Leaves The World

Matter can cycle. Heat cannot. Every comfortable room is a temporary victory over entropy with a bill attached.

Heat sources:

- bodies
- lighting
- farms
- servers
- pumps
- fabrication
- reactors or power conversion
- traffic systems
- industrial processes

Working chain:

1. Local systems absorb heat into air, water, coolant, or thermal mass.
2. District exchangers move heat out of occupied spaces.
3. Coolant trunks carry heat through utility mats, spokes, or shell routes.
4. Heat is staged into radiator loops, thermal stores, industrial reuse, or external rejection.
5. Radiators dump heat to space.

Spaces created:

- exchanger closets
- coolant manifold rooms
- radiator trunk galleries
- thermal buffer tanks
- exterior radiator fields
- shield penetrations for high-grade heat routes
- emergency heat-sink cells

Failure modes:

- a slum gets hot first because airflow repair priority was sold elsewhere
- a farm gets cold because heat was diverted to keep a factory online
- a service ring becomes lethal during a coolant bypass fault
- radiator maintenance becomes a labor choke point

Pallas implication:

If Kappa mixes pressure, air, and coolant, the reason may be thermal. A seal-lung cluster handling pressure transients could be physically adjacent to heat exchangers because compressing, expanding, filtering, and moving air changes temperature. That adjacency makes maintenance cheaper and faults nastier.

## Walkthrough 6: The Kappa Fault As Engineering

Proposed Kappa function:

Kappa is a seal-lung and bypass cluster serving an AU industrial yard segment near a spoke base. It keeps the open industrial atmosphere stable when large equipment cycles, service hatches open, or pressure disturbances move through nearby utility layers. It also carries coolant and condensate interfaces because the air-handling equipment has to exchange heat and remove moisture.

Why the service ring exists:

The human-scale ring lets crews reach replaceable pumps, panels, filters, incident boards, valves, and equipment carts without entering the cramped shell arteries.

Why crawl throats exist:

The seal lungs and sensor nests are embedded outward in the structural shell where widening access would cost shielding mass, pressure integrity, and downtime. The crawl throat is a transition from human service ring to specialist artery.

Why the route is dangerous:

- the lung can flex during equalization
- a valve can shift under pressure load
- a coolant transient can flash heat into a small volume
- a sensor can report clear while a mechanical seam has moved
- support rigs can snag where a human-scale rescue cannot follow

Why AU uses octopoid labor:

Modified octopoid workers can fold, brace, manipulate, and operate in low/zero-g service arteries better than baseline humans. AU converts this into a cost-saving geometry: keep the artery small, modify the worker, and call the resulting discomfort specialization.

Why the Pallas strike starts there:

The technical chokepoint gives workers leverage because management cannot easily replace the knowledge. The personhood issue emerges because the refusal is not just emotional resistance. It is memory, route knowledge, pattern recognition, and hazard judgment.

## Walkthrough 7: What A Blueprint Must Show

A useful blueprint for the Pallas Kappa scene should show relationships, not just labels.

Minimum elements:

- radial arrows: inward/open atmosphere, outward/structural shell/shielding
- spinward/counterspinward direction along ring
- spoke-side direction toward control deck and transit
- Service Ring Kappa as a curved human-scale gallery
- Kappa operations gallery above/side-inward with catwalk and badge stair
- worker entry from shift/equipment side
- octopoid prep/work-support station near, but not inside, the throat
- multiple crawl throats branching outward into shell arteries
- manifold room where air/pressure/coolant/condensate loops cross
- Kappa seal-lung cluster beyond the crawl throats
- emergency isolation boundary between industrial yard air and service cell

If those elements are not present, the image prompt will keep producing dramatic rooms with no reason to exist.
