# Bloom Habitat Ontology Drafts

Status: brainstorming. This is the working ontology space for replacing the current list-heavy [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Technology/Bloom Habitat Anatomy]] article with something that behaves like engineering.

## Draft 0: Constraint-To-Space Pattern

For each habitat feature, use this pattern:

1. Constraint: the physical, biological, economic, or political problem.
2. Engineering response: what the habitat does to satisfy the constraint.
3. Resulting spaces: the actual rooms, decks, shafts, surfaces, or voids created.
4. Normal access: who uses it when nothing is wrong.
5. Failure access: who enters when the system is failing.
6. AU distortion: how AU makes it cheaper, harsher, more monitored, or more profitable.
7. Scene implication: what this creates for fiction, IF choices, and visual design.

Example:

- Constraint: the open interior needs continuous air circulation, contaminant removal, and smoke control.
- Engineering response: high/inward return trunks, low/outward supply systems, local fan cells, smoke curtains, sensor fields, district isolation shutters.
- Resulting spaces: return-air galleries near inward ceilings, utility mats under streets, fan rooms at spoke bases, service manifolds at zone boundaries, crawl arteries for sensor and shutter access.
- Normal access: air techs, habitat ecologists, cleaning drones.
- Failure access: emergency crews, contamination teams, specialist crawl workers.
- AU distortion: defer shutter replacement, use cheaper sensor packs, send octopoid workers into cramped return pockets instead of opening wall sections.
- Scene implication: smoke, odor, heat, fan noise, and access hatches can make social pressure visible.

## Draft 1: Radial Layer Ontology

This is ordered from axis inward/outward as experienced from open habitat air.

### A. Axial / near-axis system

Primary purpose:

- docking and traffic
- despun or partially despun logistics
- light collection/distribution
- communications
- command and sensor functions
- low-g manufacturing or temporary lodging

Engineering constraints:

- low/negligible gravity
- rotating/despun transfer
- Coriolis issues at interfaces
- high traffic control requirements
- major pressure penetrations and locks

Spaces:

- docking hub
- customs/quarantine halls
- cargo lock arrays
- traffic control rooms
- axial plant rooms
- light-pipe distributor
- spoke-head transfer collars
- short-stay suites and shift crash quarters
- hub slums in leftover low-g volume

Not default:

- desirable family housing
- parks meant for ordinary ground life

### B. Open interior volume

Primary purpose:

- shared pressurized civic atmosphere
- psychological and economic advantage over planetary pressure habitats
- light distribution
- urban legibility
- aerial or suspended transit if scale permits

Engineering constraints:

- air circulation and stratification
- fire/smoke control
- humidity and precipitation control if any
- acoustic propagation
- pest/pathogen control
- emergency isolation without destroying the open-air premise

Spaces:

- visible sky/vault volume
- light spine or illuminated axial feature
- air return zones
- aerial work routes or drones
- emergency smoke curtains and invisible airflow boundaries

Questions:

- Do mature Blooms have artificial cloud/weather, or is that a luxury/ecological variant?
- How do residents read day/night without a sky?

### C. Inner surface civic deck

Primary purpose:

- ordinary life under artificial gravity
- housing, streets, agriculture, industry, culture
- local distribution of utilities

Engineering constraints:

- maximum effective gravity
- load-bearing for buildings/soil/water
- local drainage follows outward/down
- utility access under occupied surfaces
- emergency routing and crowd movement

Spaces:

- spoke-base districts
- residential belts
- farms and ecological surfaces
- industrial flats
- street grids or terraced districts
- utility mats below civic surfaces

### D. Utility mat / service interlayer

Primary purpose:

- distribute air, water, power, data, coolant, nutrient, waste
- expose maintainable interfaces below civic life
- isolate faults before they reach structural shell

Engineering constraints:

- must be reachable without demolishing districts
- must support heavy traffic in industrial zones
- must separate incompatible flows
- must prevent one leak from becoming a cascading fault

Spaces:

- utility galleries
- access corridors
- valve rooms
- fan rooms
- district manifolds
- pump closets
- greywater runs
- sensor closets

### E. Pressure/structural shell

Primary purpose:

- contain the open atmosphere
- carry rotational and pressure loads
- support civic deck and utility mat
- integrate TCS and aggregate shielding

Engineering constraints:

- hoop stress
- pressure load
- thermal expansion
- micrometeoroid damage tolerance
- cracks must be detectable and containable
- penetrations must be expensive and guarded

Spaces:

- structural ribs
- pressure laminates
- inspection seams
- seal lungs
- service rings
- bypass galleries
- crack-monitoring cavities
- sacrificial voids

### F. Shielding / exterior service layer

Primary purpose:

- radiation shielding
- micrometeoroid defense
- heat rejection interfaces
- exterior maintenance
- sensor and antenna placement

Engineering constraints:

- mass dominates
- exterior work is hazardous
- radiator surfaces need line-of-sight and maintenance
- shield penetrations must be minimized

Spaces:

- aggregate shielding beds
- exterior plates
- radiator belts
- antenna farms
- exterior robot tracks
- shield inspection galleries
- emergency patch pockets

## Draft 2: Flow Ontology

Every space should be explained by at least one of these flows.

### People

Public resident path:

- home -> street -> spoke-base transit -> market/work/public service -> home

Worker path:

- service housing or transit -> shift gate -> locker/equipment issue -> muster corridor -> yard/service access -> washdown/debrief -> housing/transit

Supervisor path:

- spoke-base operations deck -> glassed operations gallery -> supervisor catwalk -> badge stair/lift -> work ring

Security path:

- quick-response shaft or badge corridor -> containment point -> worker-facing line

Visitor path:

- docking hub -> customs/quarantine -> spoke transit -> host district

### Cargo and feedstock

- dock -> quarantine -> hub store -> freight spoke -> district receiving -> local storage -> use -> packaging/scrap return

Key point: cargo routes avoid public streets when possible, but every shortcut becomes a class boundary.

### Air

- processors/biological production -> spoke trunks -> open civic atmosphere/supply zones -> occupied spaces -> inward/high returns -> contaminant traps -> heat exchange -> processors

Fault paths:

- smoke rises inward
- toxic release triggers local shutters and dump cell
- breach triggers sector isolation and pressure reserve

### Water

- cargo ice/recovered water -> hub treatment -> potable/industrial/irrigation/coolant split -> district use -> greywater/blackwater/industrial waste/coolant return -> local treatment -> central processing -> storage

Design warning:

- potable water, coolant, and industrial wash cannot be treated as one pipe color.

### Heat

- people/farms/factories/processors generate heat -> local exchangers -> coolant loops -> thermal trunks -> radiators/thermal stores -> space

Failure visibility:

- warm housing, cold farms, radiator strike, hot service galleries, emergency work pauses

### Waste and biomass

- source sorting -> local recovery -> digesters/sterilizers/crackers/material recovery -> nutrients/feedstock/fuel/residue -> return/export/quarantine

Political point:

- waste handlers know the habitat's real metabolism.

### Data and authority

- sensors -> local control -> district operations -> Ramp Administration / corporate logging -> incident classification -> security/maintenance action

Ghostlight point:

- authority has a route through space and data. It should not teleport into scenes.

## Draft 3: Maintenance Access Ontology

Maintenance access is not a category; it is a cost decision.

### Level 0: Resident-visible service points

Purpose:

- routine repairs, meters, filters, local shutoffs

Visible examples:

- utility closet
- street hatch
- wall panel
- service kiosk

### Level 1: Human service gallery

Purpose:

- carts, baseline crews, replaceable modules, emergency access

Why it exists:

- high-frequency maintenance or heavy parts justify human-sized volume

### Level 2: Manifold room

Purpose:

- where flows branch, cross boundaries, or need isolation

Why it exists:

- valves and exchangers need inspection and replacement

### Level 3: Service ring

Purpose:

- repeated access around a shell segment, pressure cluster, spoke base, or industrial cell

Why it exists:

- repeated components need a circuit rather than scattered dead-end rooms

### Level 4: Specialist crawl throat

Purpose:

- transition from human access to smaller service arteries

Why it exists:

- widening the route costs too much mass, shielding, pressure integrity, or downtime

### Level 5: Service artery

Purpose:

- reach sensor nests, seal lungs, membrane seams, bypass valves, thermal pockets

Why it exists:

- critical nodes need access but not human-scale comfort

### Level 6: Blind/sacrificial void

Purpose:

- expansion, crush, acoustic damping, thermal buffer, construction residue

Why it exists:

- not every void is a corridor; some are there to be empty

## Draft 4: Pallas Kappa Space Model

Working decision:

Kappa is not the end of a hallway. Kappa is a named maintenance subsystem in a Pallas AU yard.

### Spatial setup

- Above/inward: open industrial atmosphere of the yard surface and overhead volume.
- Main work lane: Service Ring Kappa, human-scale maintenance gallery following a shell segment near a spoke-base industrial district.
- Supervisor layer: glassed Kappa operations gallery above part of the ring, connected to spoke-base control deck.
- Worker entry: shift locks and equipment issue from service housing/spoke transit.
- Biodrone entry: handler corridor and restraint staging.
- Octopoid entry: support-rig prep room connected to ring-side work stations.
- Outward/deeper shell: Kappa seal-lung cluster, bypass valves, sensor nests, crawl arteries, exterior-adjacent inspection pockets.

### Why Kappa exists

The Kappa subsystem manages pressure/airflow/coolant equalization for an industrial yard segment. It is close enough to public/open atmosphere that its failure matters quickly, but embedded enough in the structural shell that full human access would be expensive.

### Why octopoid workers are used

Not because the tunnels are whimsical. Because the high-value nodes are cramped, irregular, and low/zero-g:

- membrane seam inspection pockets
- flexible pressure-lung valve nests
- sensor arrays behind curved ribs
- bypass cuffs around coolant/air crossings
- drain/condensate traps where biofilm and corrosion accumulate

A humane system might redesign these nodes or use better robots. AU uses modified octopoid bodies because BioElevate made that cheaper.

### Why Nara's refusal matters

If Nara is a biodrone or engineered seal tech refusing the blind crawl, then the refusal likely concerns a sensor/inspection route that management classifies as equipment behavior. If she remembers Rell, the memory attaches personhood and hazard expertise to the route.

### Why Ilya arrives fast

Ilya is already watching from the Kappa operations gallery. The catwalk and badge stair let her physically enter before workers can reframe the event. That geometry is authority embodied.

### What the image prompt must show

- A curved human-scale service ring, not a terminal corridor.
- At least two Kappa crawl throats branching off the ring, one active and one visible in background.
- The glassed operations gallery above or side-inward, with catwalk and badge stair.
- The octopoid work-support station near a throat, not blocking the entire room.
- Manifold wall as a specific crossing of air/coolant/pressure systems.
- Worker shift entry distinct from supervisor entry.
- Radial orientation labels if blueprint style: inward/open air, outward/shell/shielding, spinward/counterspinward, spoke-side.

## Draft 5: Article Rewrite Shape

The final article should not be a catalog. It should be a walk through constraints.

Proposed sections:

1. What a Bloom is not: not hollow rock, not a station stack, not a magic city in a jar.
2. Primary physical model: rotating manufactured shell, open atmosphere, axial systems, spokes, shielding.
3. Rotation and habitability: where people live, where they work, where they do not sleep unless poor or on duty.
4. Pressure and air: global envelope, open commons, zoning, smoke, breach, service cells.
5. The shell as infrastructure: utility mat, pressure laminate, TCS grades, shielding, inspection voids.
6. Resource metabolism: air, water, heat, waste, biomass, cargo as linked loops.
7. Access hierarchy: resident, worker, specialist, supervisor, security, illicit.
8. Maintenance access as cost: why galleries/rings/throats/arteries exist.
9. AU customization: throughput, claimshares, species labor, safety waivers.
10. Pallas Kappa worked example.
11. Design checklist for scenes and item/economy hooks.

Rewrite rule:

No bare lists of technical nouns unless each item has a causal role already established.
