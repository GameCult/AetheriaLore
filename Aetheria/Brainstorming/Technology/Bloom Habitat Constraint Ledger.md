# Bloom Habitat Constraint Ledger

Status: brainstorming. This ledger exists to keep assumptions visible while revising [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Technology/Bloom Habitat Anatomy]].

Each entry should answer: if this is true, what follows?

## C-001: The inhabited cylinder is one broad pressurized commons

Claim: A mature Bloom's central selling point is open habitat air inside a large rotating shell.

Why it matters:

- ordinary districts are not sealed cans
- civic life can feel expansive without planetary terraforming
- emergency partitions are normally-open fault controls, not default walls
- smoke, contamination, riots, quarantine, and breach response are major design drivers

Questions:

- How large can uninterrupted civic atmosphere be before fire/smoke management requires invisible zoning?
- Are some AU industrial yards normally isolated from the public commons because of contamination or liability?

Current answer:

- The open interior is one managed atmosphere, but airflow zones and emergency barriers can isolate sectors quickly.
- Hazardous industrial yards can be inside the open atmospheric envelope while still using local air curtains, contamination vestibules, pressure doors, and service cells.

## C-002: Artificial gravity is radial and uneven

Claim: Gravity increases with distance from the axis. Near-axis space is operationally valuable but bad ordinary housing.

Consequences:

- prime housing belongs near spoke bases and high-quality inner surface districts
- axial hubs are logistics, offices, labs, docking, short-stay, and plant
- hub slums are social residue near jobs, not desirable axial luxury
- vertical movement is radial and physiologically strange

Failure if ignored:

- spire-adjacent luxury housing appears without explaining why rich people bought nausea

## C-003: The shell is manufactured from rubble, not carved from stone

Claim: A Bloom shell begins as contained asteroid aggregate processed into structural/shielding layers.

Consequences:

- maintenance spaces are designed voids, cast voids, utility mats, galleries, and service arteries
- the shell can contain irregularity from asteroid feedstock, but its critical layers are engineered
- widening a crawl space has mass, shielding, pressure, and routing costs

Failure if ignored:

- tunnels start behaving like mine corridors and Kappa becomes a cave with a sci-fi nametag

## C-004: Radiation and micrometeoroid shielding are not optional

Claim: The shell's outer mass is primarily shielding and structural defense.

Consequences:

- exterior-adjacent service is expensive and dangerous
- shield penetrations are rare, named, monitored, and politically contested
- docking, radiator, antenna, sensor, and maintenance penetrations require labyrinths, shutters, sacrificial plates, and inspection routines
- workers may be sent where robots should go when cost pressure wins

Failure if ignored:

- exterior access appears cheap and casual
- service corridors cut straight through shielding without consequence

## C-005: Light is piped, not windowed

Claim: Bloom uses spire light pipes and collectors rather than huge longitudinal windows as the default mature design.

Consequences:

- day/night is software, optics, maintenance, and politics
- light quality varies by district and becomes class pressure
- the spire is both life support and symbolic control
- exterior shell remains mostly continuous for pressure and shielding

Questions:

- Are there luxury window districts with expensive shielded view galleries?
- Do AU industrial Blooms cheap out on light pipe quality near worker districts?

Working answer:

- Yes to view galleries as controlled luxury, no to giant default windows.
- AU likely sells light quality and repair priority as claimshare upgrades.

## C-006: Heat has to leave the habitat

Claim: Every person, farm, factory, pump, lamp, and processor becomes waste heat.

Consequences:

- coolant loops are as important as air and water
- radiators need exterior exposure and maintenance access
- industrial districts are placed by heat rejection routes as much as by freight convenience
- thermal faults create visible social pressure: hot barracks, cold farms, delayed shifts, radiator maintenance strikes

Questions:

- Where are Bloom radiators mounted relative to shielding and rotating shell?
- Does the spire carry radiator trunks to exterior nonrotating arrays, or does the rotating shell carry segmented radiators?

Working answer:

- Large Blooms likely use both: rotating shell radiator belts for distributed loads and axial/exterior radiator farms for high-grade industrial plant.

## C-007: Air circulation follows effective gravity

Claim: Warm air, smoke, and buoyant contaminants tend inward, while cooler dense air tends outward toward the ground.

Consequences:

- fire response must manage inward smoke plumes
- return-air trunks may live high/inward, while supply diffusers serve occupied surfaces
- the open interior is not automatically well-mixed
- large agricultural or industrial zones need local circulation cells

Failure if ignored:

- air systems become a generic list of filters instead of a spatial machine

## C-008: Water and waste are the same story at different times

Claim: Closed habitats do not throw things away; they defer, transform, store, export, or poison themselves.

Consequences:

- greywater, blackwater, industrial water, irrigation, coolant, and potable water are separate grades with controlled crossings
- waste processing belongs near cargo routes and biological processors
- leaks and contamination events are political events
- sanitation labor has strategic power

Failure if ignored:

- resource flow becomes a dock-to-district list with no return logic

## C-009: The hub is not the city center in the usual sense

Claim: The hub is central geometrically and operationally, but not comfortable residentially.

Consequences:

- hub property is valuable for logistics and command, not family life
- worker slums near the hub reflect constraint and exploitation
- visitors may experience the hub as impressive, temporary, expensive, and alien
- locals may orient status around spoke bases and inner-surface light rather than axial proximity

## C-010: Spokes are bottlenecks and class filters

Claim: Spokes carry traffic and utilities between axial systems and the rotating shell.

Consequences:

- spoke access controls commute, freight priority, emergency response, and status
- spoke bases become high-value urban centers
- separate passenger, freight, emergency, and utility paths reduce conflict but increase cost
- AU can use badge routes and supervisor catwalks to make authority arrive faster than workers can leave

Questions:

- Does a spoke rotate with the shell, or is it a transfer interface between rotating and despun systems?

Working answer:

- Treat mature Blooms as hybrid: rotating shell and spoke-root structures; despun or partially despun axial hub; transfer collars and spoke cars handle rotating/despun exchange.

## C-011: Maintenance hierarchy must be derived from access cost

Claim: Maintenance spaces exist because certain failures must be reached at certain costs.

Consequence chain:

1. A system requires inspection or replacement.
2. The system has a location driven by pressure, flow, load, heat, shielding, or safety.
3. Access must be sized for the cheapest acceptable worker/tool.
4. Access needs staging, isolation, lighting, communications, and rescue logic.
5. Corporate operators reduce size and redundancy until failure politics push back.

This produces:

- public hatches
- utility closets
- human service galleries
- manifold rooms
- service rings
- specialist crawl throats
- service arteries
- blind pockets
- exterior-adjacent inspection routes

It does not produce random sci-fi corridors.

## C-012: AU customization is optimization under moral failure

Claim: AU does not misunderstand habitat engineering. It understands it and then chooses throughput.

Consequences:

- AU spaces should be competent, legible, patched, and cruel
- labor control rides on access routes, shift gates, claimshare upgrades, safety waivers, and incident classification
- uplifted octopoid bodies are matched to service arteries because widening the arteries or automating them costs more
- the geometry should expose the ethics

## C-013: Pallas Kappa must be a subsystem, not a room

Claim: Kappa is a named service subsystem or route cluster inside a Pallas yard.

Working model:

- Service Ring Kappa: human-scale maintenance gallery around a seal-lung and bypass cluster
- Kappa operations gallery: glassed supervisor booth above part of the ring
- Kappa throat(s): access points into smaller specialist arteries
- Kappa seal lungs: pressure/equalization components embedded outward in shell layers
- Kappa bypass: emergency reroute for air/pressure/coolant around a failing seal path
- Kappa sensor nests: small pockets where biodrones or octopoid workers are sent

Open issue:

- Decide whether Kappa is tied to a particular industrial district, spoke base, or shell segment number.

## C-014: The current Bloom article contains stale geometry

Problem: The older article uses terms like equatorial plane and polar farmlands in ways that may fit a spherical or toroidal mental model better than a cylindrical Bloom.

Possible resolution:

- For a cylinder, use axial ends, spoke bases, inner surface belts, and hub zones.
- If polar farmland remains, define it as endcap/low-g agriculture or specialty axial agriculture, not a generic opposite pole.

Action:

- Do not patch immediately. Resolve in ontology first.

## C-015: The ontology needs verbs, not labels

Bad shape:

- air valves, coolant manifolds, condensate traps, sensor racks, pressure cells

Better shape:

- return air from district X rises inward, is pulled into high returns, passes through contaminant traps, exchanges heat into coolant loop Y, and returns through low/outward supply diffusers; manifold room Z exists because those loops cross a pressure isolation boundary and need human-accessible valve replacement.

Rule for rewrite:

Every major noun must earn itself by appearing in a flow, load, access, or failure chain.
