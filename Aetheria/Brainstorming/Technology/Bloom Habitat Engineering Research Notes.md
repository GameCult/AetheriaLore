# Bloom Habitat Engineering Research Notes

Status: brainstorming, not canon. This note exists because the first pass at [[Bloom Habitat Anatomy]] moved too fast and replaced engineering reasoning with lists of plausible nouns. Treat this as the bench notebook for rebuilding the anatomy properly.

## Purpose

The goal is to understand a mature [[Bloom]] habitat as an engineered organism:

- what problem each volume solves
- what loads and flows pass through it
- what breaks if that volume is missing
- who can access it normally, in an emergency, or through privilege
- how [[Aeronautics Unlimited]] modifies [[Orbital Forge]]-derived standards for throughput and labor control
- how a Pallas-style Kappa maintenance space can be drawn without cheating

## Immediate Lessons From Research

### 1. A Bloom is closer to a rotating pressure ecology than a station stack

The inhabited cylinder is a large pressurized commons. That is its civilizational draw. Emergency isolation exists, but ordinary life is open habitat air, not a chain of cans.

Consequence: the ontology must start with the primary pressure envelope and open interior volume, then derive local barriers from failure management. It must not start with districts as sealed cells.

### 2. The shell is not asteroid rock with corridors cut into it

Real asteroids are rubble piles more often than solid mountains. A Bloom begins with an aggregated, contained, processed, reinforced shell. The useful phrase is manufactured shell, not hollowed rock.

Consequence: maintenance volumes are cast, left, bored, printed, or retrofitted into an engineered shell. They follow ribs, membranes, utility trunks, thermal paths, and inspection needs. They do not wander like caves.

### 3. Rotation dominates habitability

The high-radius inner surface has comfortable artificial gravity. The axial region has low or negligible gravity and awkward Coriolis effects during transfers. Spire-adjacent offices make sense. Spire-adjacent homes mostly do not, except short-stay, adapted bodies, desperate workers, or specialist quarters.

Consequence: prestige housing belongs near spoke bases on the inner surface, not next to the spire. Axial zones are valuable because of access, not comfort.

### 4. Air movement is a design problem, not a sentence fragment

In a rotating habitat, effective gravity points outward. Warm air and smoke tend to rise inward, toward the axis, unless fans, return ducts, barriers, and thermal management control the flow. A large open interior needs circulation strategy, not just air processors.

Consequence: vents, returns, smoke routes, and fire isolation are key parts of the visible city. A maintenance scene involving life support should know whether it is touching supply, return, contaminant capture, pressure reserve, or emergency isolation.

### 5. Life support is politically central

Project Rho's recurring point is brutal and useful: control over life support creates a hydraulic state. In a Bloom, air, water, heat rejection, waste recovery, and maintenance budgets are governance systems.

Consequence: AU labor control should appear through access to filters, shifts, claimshare air-quality upgrades, maintenance deferment, and who gets sent into failing service arteries.

### 6. Shielding likely dominates mass and geography

Any long-term space habitat must protect against radiation and micrometeoroids. Bloom solves this by using compacted aggregate and TCS. That shielding is not just outside scenery; it shapes where maintenance can go, what routes are expensive to widen, and why some crawl spaces remain small.

Consequence: the Kappa crawl network should exist because widening it would compromise mass, shielding, thermal path, or pressure integrity, not because the plot wants an octopoid worker in danger.

### 7. Windows are not the Bloom default

Classic O'Neill cylinders use large windows and mirrors in many designs. Bloom lore already says light pipes in the spire distribute collected sunlight. That is probably better for Aetheria: fewer huge pressure-window/radiation compromises, more controllable day/night and class politics.

Consequence: the interior has open air but not conventional sky. People see a light spine, diffuse vault, projected weather, cloud/air systems, and the opposite landscape depending on scale. The light system is infrastructure and politics.

### 8. The current article's list shape is a smell

A list can summarize an ontology, but it cannot substitute for one. The current article repeatedly groups nouns by vibes: pressure, valves, manifolds, cells, trunks, galleries. The rewrite needs causal chains.

Better pattern:

1. constraint
2. engineering response
3. resulting spaces
4. normal users
5. failure mode
6. social distortion under AU

## Open Questions That Need Decisions

### Does the spire rotate?

Options:

- Mostly despun axial hub and spire, with rotating shell transfer systems. Good for docking, light pointing, and hub industry. Harder at spoke interfaces.
- Rotating spire with despun docking collars. Good for rigid spokes and resident legibility. Harder for docking and external alignment.
- Hybrid: despun docking hub and fixed external collectors, rotating internal light mast or transfer collars. Most plausible for mature Bloom, but needs clearer anatomy.

Working hypothesis: mature Blooms use a hybrid axial system. Docking and external traffic are despun. The inhabited shell rotates. Transfer collars, maglev spoke cars, and rotary utility joints handle exchange. The visible light spine may be a rotating internal distributor fed through despun collectors.

### What is a spoke structurally?

A spoke cannot be a casual elevator shaft. It is one of the most expensive interfaces in the habitat.

Possible functions:

- passenger and freight transfer
- power/data/coolant/air/water utility trunks
- emergency evacuation route
- structural stabilization or damping
- psychological landmark
- control point for class and security

Need distinguish rotating spoke structures from despun hub interfaces.

### Where does agriculture live?

The old Bloom article talks about polar farmlands, which may be inherited from a less cylindrical mental model. In a cylinder, agriculture can live on the inner surface like everything else, with light-pipe priority and water/nutrient access. Low-g agriculture near axial or endcap zones is possible for specialty crops but should not be casually called farmland without explaining containment, light, labor, and harvest logistics.

### What exactly is TCS doing?

Current lore makes TCS radiation shielding, structural hardening, biocompatible substrate, and terraforming medium. That can work, but the layers need roles:

- aggregate shielding mass
- TCS structural matrix
- pressure liner or pressure laminate
- service void layer
- civic/soil/building substrate
- self-healing or bioactive maintenance layer

Need decide whether TCS is a single material family with grades, not one universal miracle paste.

### How big is a typical AU/Pallas Bloom?

Scale determines everything: rotation rate, Coriolis comfort, district size, transit mode, air mixing, maintenance segment size, and whether skybuses make any sense. The Pallas fixture can avoid exact dimensions, but the anatomy should name scale classes.

Working scale classes to test:

- yard shell: industrial Bloom or partially built cavity, tens of thousands to low hundreds of thousands
- mature civic Bloom: hundreds of thousands
- megacity Bloom: millions to tens of millions
