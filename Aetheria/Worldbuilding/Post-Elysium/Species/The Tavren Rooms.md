# The Tavren Rooms

In Tavren, a good host gives you somewhere to be alone. A friend staying for a week receives a small room with its own light controls, a washable wall hanging, and a door that closes properly. Hosts enjoy finding the right blanket or lending a beloved lamp. The guest may then spend the afternoon inside without providing an account of what was so interesting in there.

Tavren is a residential quarter in a trading settlement in [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/index|post-Elysium]], several locally born generations after its founders' displacement. Most residents belong to one of the peoples outsiders call [[Ratfolk]]. They call themselves Tavren people when speaking about their home; the name includes people who have moved in. Their quarter consists of low, enclosed housing courts beside a larger-bodied port district. Food and household goods arrive through the port. Residents earn their living through provisioning, clothing repair, teaching, bookkeeping, and the ordinary services of a settled population.

The founders built clusters of separate rooms around shared kitchens and washing spaces. Compact private rooms let them house more people without making every meal, argument, or waking hour communal. Their descendants made a taste out of the arrangement. People admire a room that fits its inhabitant: a work surface exactly within reach, low shelves of polished fragments, warm cloth beneath the feet, or a severe emptiness that someone has spent considerable money achieving. Children learn to knock, to mend soft furnishings, and to ask before changing another person's light. They also learn that hearing an argument through a wall does not make it theirs to repeat.

Supper often continues across several kitchens. Someone brings a sharp condiment to taste, someone wants help choosing a wall colour, and someone arrives solely to borrow the pot everyone knows they will return tomorrow. A resident can live in the same court as a partner without sharing a room. Others join rooms for children, lovers, or an elderly friend who dislikes sleeping alone. Neither arrangement counts as a more advanced form of affection. Asking someone to give up their room does, however, sound rather more serious here than asking them to marry.

These habits survive because shared utilities make private space affordable, and because residents like being close enough to visit without having to perform constant availability. The rooms have faults. Laundry smells travel, old partitions carry sound, and a household with money can buy much better seclusion. Some residents keep their doors open most evenings. They object when enthusiasts of privacy describe every unsolicited conversation as a failure of upbringing.

## Making Room To Stay

Human friends usually visit in the port district or in Tavren's few taller entrance rooms. The inner courts have doors, controls, and escape routes sized for their smaller inhabitants. A comfortable chair at the entrance cannot make the rest of a home accessible.

Now several households want to replace an unused storeroom with a court where small and large residents could both live. They propose full-height shared spaces with smaller private rooms opening alongside larger ones. The households collectively own the storeroom and would pay for construction from their savings and future rents; they must also fund suitable evacuation routes and utilities. This would be a substantial piece of their next generation's housing.

Deren, a Tavren upholsterer, wants to live there with her human partner, Mikal. She is tired of being hosted by him whenever they want an ordinary evening together. Her aunt Ivrin has offered them her own entrance room. It is generous, carefully furnished, and still somewhere Mikal cannot walk beyond the first kitchen. Ivrin thinks the new court would consume space that could give several young residents their first independent rooms. She wants Tavren to remain a place where being small is enough to fit.

The disagreement reaches beyond this couple. Some residents imagine Tavren becoming known for homes that accommodate unlike bodies without requiring constant assistance. Others want its savings spent making excellent small homes available to more people, including newcomers from other Ratfolk communities. Both futures could attract neighbours. They would leave different children able to stay.

## Open Hooks

- Deren has been asked to furnish the new court's first large room before the owners have agreed to build it. Ivrin has offered the fabric.
- A young tenant wants the storeroom divided immediately into cheap small rooms; another year of planning means another year sleeping beside her parents' kitchen.
- Mikal has drawn a kitchen he could share with Deren. She has returned the drawing with two private rooms added.
