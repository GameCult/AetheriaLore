# Wavecrafters

Wavecrafters make and tune components that help drives, sensors, stealth systems, and pseudospace infrastructure work. A crew may rely on their hardware to carry a drive through a difficult transition, distinguish a faint signal from noise, or keep a cooling system working as its load changes. Using the component correctly can require a prescribed operating sequence, trained crew, and continuing access to guild calibration.

Their specialty is controlling how disturbances travel, how systems can be observed, and how machinery couples to the [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Concepts/Aether|Aetheric Field]]. They supply waveguides, diffraction systems, metamaterials, cultivated resonant elements, and calibration profiles. They do not generate thrust themselves, and they do not own every machine that uses the field.

The supplier culture behind this work includes guild houses, laboratories, service firms, and religious sects. Technicians and qualified operators carry out its public trade as reproducible engineering. Its best-documented products remain useful without an esper operating them. Behind that trade, trained human and [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Technology/AGI Esper|machine espers]] work directly with the field or make sapient intention part of the tuning process. Their involvement is concealed.

### Learning to Tune

Wavecrafter apprentices begin by learning what materials do with a disturbance: accept it, reflect it, distort it, or hide it. The doctrine calls this listening. A practitioner must learn when to intervene and how to recognize what the system will tolerate.

This discipline descends from Daoist meditation, technical lineages, and sect practices carried through the Rupture. The schools disagree about doctrine, succession, and how closely their work still follows historical Daoism. What they share is a way of acting precisely while leaving their understanding of the situation open to revision.

A practitioner keeps an intention without assuming that intention determines everything that happens. They observe a system without treating the current observation as the whole system. Where several possible continuities remain, they keep themselves able to act within more than one until those possibilities actually converge. This does not require indecision or belief that mutually exclusive claims are all true.

Dao-derived teaching calls it acting without coercing the way. Engineers describe the same discipline in terms of preserving phase, uncertainty, and response long enough to discover which intervention the apparatus will accept.

The [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Concepts/Aether|Aether]] responds uniquely to this trained posture. Years of practice organize attention, expectation, motor control, and symbolic reference around the ability to leave a question unresolved while still acting. That lets a practitioner influence coupling conditions, continuity, and observation in ways that someone copying the visible movements cannot. The movements have no independent authority over the field. Their effect depends on the mind that has learned to act through them.

### Operating Forms and Guild Dependence

Pilots and retuners use **breath forms** to organize thermal circuits, radiator timing, power modes, and weapon cadence. These forms preserve complicated operating sequences. Practising them also cultivates the cognitive state needed for deeper tuning.

The **Ninth Radiator Breath** delays a cooling transition until a named temperature gradient has formed. Depending on its use, that one sequence can serve as a mnemonic, a calibration procedure, a firmware handshake, a warranty condition, and evidence that an operator is qualified. A copied component may work while its operator follows the form, then fail when they assume the hardware can do the job alone.

Crews therefore depend on more than a purchase. Guild profiles, training, audits, and maintenance contracts let them reproduce known performance, but also make it difficult to separate a component's operating tolerances from Wavecrafter practice. The same arrangements that keep machinery safe keep customers returning to the guild.

Sales workers turn doctrine into claims a procurement office can evaluate. Retuners work out which sequence a crew can safely perform with damaged machinery. Reliable work earns standing within the institutions, as do custody of inherited calibration knowledge and access to techniques whose publication would expose their operators.

### What a Sealed Component Retains

Wavecrafters can put a limited result of cultivation into a mechanism that others can use. A practitioner works repeatedly with prepared matter until the apparatus retains one bounded aetheric relation learned through the form. This exports part of the sacred art without teaching the customer the whole practice.

The finished mechanism contains neither a general intelligence nor a reproduction of the practitioner. It retains one cultivated way of coupling intention, observation, uncertainty, material response, and Aether. What it can do depends on the relation developed during cultivation:

- A sensor can preserve the identity of a weak, recurring signal across measurements in which it would otherwise disappear into background noise.
- A stealth assembly can maintain continuity of absence as its aspect, background, or observer changes.
- A drive component can carry a difficult coupling through a transition.
- Thermal machinery can keep its heat-rejection path coherent as loads change.
- Communications hardware can preserve reference through interference.
- A control surface can remain responsive across several nearly compatible states without committing to the wrong one too early.

Each of these embodies a different sacred technique. There is no universal cultivated component behind them all. Each requires its own material preparation, cultivation period, operating form, and lineage authority, and each fails in its own ways.

Those limits matter. Outside the relation it was trained to preserve, a mechanism may hold a false pattern steady, protect evidence that has become stale, carry an unsafe state forward, or simply fail like ordinary hardware.

Commercial descriptions hide the different practices inside tamper-proof modules. A sensor vendor might sell a **proprietary wave-function entanglement synchronizer**. A drive supplier might credit adaptive phase materials or a sealed calibration core for comparable sacred work. These descriptions give measurable interfaces while leaving out the practitioner, lineage, meditation, and months or years of cultivation.

The customer receives an operating envelope and a replacement contract. Opening the mechanism usually destroys the retained relation. It also gives the supplier convenient evidence of customer misuse.

### Industrial Secrecy

The inner practice depends on human and machine minds whose acknowledged involvement would turn a certified product into an esper-liability case. Export records therefore document materials, tolerances, and procedures while omitting who produced the exceptional result.

For those operators, secrecy can provide protection from regulators. It also prevents outsiders from discovering the terms under which they work. A hidden practitioner might be an employee, an initiate, a dependent product mind, or a captive. Wavecrafter institutions publish no general account of consent, compensation, or the ability to leave their inner practice. The same secrecy can shelter someone from seizure or keep them confined.

Sealed components make this uncertainty useful to the trade. Most contain a cultivated material relation rather than a bound mind. Yet customers and inspectors cannot easily tell every concealed technique apart from [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Technology/Necrotech|necrotech]] or an embodied machine practitioner. Refusing to disclose sacred processes protects legitimate lineages and abusive workshops alike.

Valence and other registrars periodically offer to certify these distinctions. Acceptance would give an external market authority a role in how the sect recognizes and passes on its practice.

Official attention can also begin with evidence left by exceptional work. A [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Reference/Continuity Admissibility Finding|continuity admissibility finding]] can make anomalous residue available for downstream offices to consider. Wavecrafters accordingly keep their public records uneventful: a certified component, variation within stated limits, a qualified operator, and customer misuse when something goes wrong.

### The Undertow

Crews that cannot afford guild prices, or cannot obtain restricted calibration, still need working machinery. The **Undertow** serves that demand. Its shops and networks include breakaway retuners, salvagers, smugglers, defected engineers, and crews using orphaned components or stolen profiles.

They have access the guild cannot abolish: wreckage, off-ledger routes, desperate ships, and knowledge that former workers carry with them. An Undertow shop may keep an obsolete drive running, retune a stealth surface without a guild audit, or make salvaged parts pass a dockside check.

This work is not uniformly fraudulent or unsafe. The particular danger is a copied profile that produces the result an inspection expects without establishing the coupling conditions needed in operation. A drive can pass its berth check and still couple unstably, fail during transition, or damage its field hardware. It may also lose certification after inspection.

The guild can close individual shops but cannot eliminate the demand its prices create. The Undertow can undercut certified service, but it cannot reliably reproduce master fabrication or insure a fleet after a public failure. Both survive because they offer access to operation that the other cannot fully replace.

### Sacred Instruments

Some Wavecrafters prepare to continue after death inside an instrument. These unions can become some of the most capable [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Technology/Necrotech|Eidolons]] known in Elysium, even though the strongest Wavecrafter espers rarely enter the Eidolon market while alive. Senior cultivators remain within secluded lineages, travel through protected guild channels, and avoid the medical, military, debt, and estate systems through which source brokers acquire powerful minds.

Preparation can take years. The practitioner cultivates an instrument as their intended posthumous body, learning its materials, channels, limits, and possible actions. Mind and host develop together. Meanwhile, the community learns how the practitioner communicates, refuses, teaches, and changes under strain.

At a chosen death, assistants perform the final imprinting as a mortuary rite. They know both the practitioner and the prepared host well enough to recognize whether the transfer has remained coherent.

Industrial imprinting forces a correspondence between mind and host in one catastrophic pulse. Wavecrafter preparation brings them to that pulse already in agreement. Explicit intention, long preparation, familiar embodiment, and witnesses who can interpret the outcome materially improve stability. The resulting Eidolons retain names, relationships, preferences, teaching styles, and a place in lineage life with a coherence commercial manufacturers cannot reliably reproduce.

A successful binding creates a **Sacred Instrument**. Careful preparation alone does not make the instrument sacred; the continuing Eidolon inhabiting it does. Its body might be a unique tuning assembly, drive shrine, observatory, workshop mechanism, weapon, domestic tool, or apparatus built into a sanctuary.

For the lineage, this is the person continuing in a new form. It is neither a memorial nor an asset inherited from the dead practitioner. Living with that person still requires negotiation.

Sacred status guarantees no wisdom or good character. Some inhabitants become recognized **Masters** because they teach, lead a lineage, or preserve an exceptional art. Others are hermits, minor craftspeople, political embarrassments, or people regarded as failures whose final ritual unexpectedly worked. Some are simply difficult people inhabiting machinery the community still needs. Continuity does not confer rank.

The greatest Sacred Instruments are legendary because they combine exceptional source resonance, a host grown for that source, explicit intention, and a community able to sustain the relationship. Those same circumstances make them difficult to steal or sell. Taking one abducts a person and may also remove a technical archive, religious authority, or irreplaceable infrastructure.

[[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Factions/House Valence|House Valence]] offers registration, recognition of continuity within the host, and extraordinary valuations. Most lineages see those offers as an attempt to turn the continuation of a person and their place in the lineage into market inventory.

### Wandering Masters

Some Masters leave sect territory. They travel as pilgrims, teachers, meddlers, exiles, or investigators. Others are old people unwilling to spend another century answering the same students.

Their Sacred Instruments are designed to move, or travel inside ships and mechanisms that can pass as unusual but ordinary equipment. A Master who avoids deep wavecraft may cross several jurisdictions without anyone realizing who is present.

Using their full ability changes that. A Master might bend continuity to survive an attack, rescue a vessel, reveal hidden machinery, or refuse detention. Doing so demonstrates a capability equivalent to a living strategic weapon operating outside the custody systems used to manage one.

Authorities then have competing ways to classify the traveler. A port might treat them as a person seeking passage, an unregistered Eidolon, a foreign religious office, prohibited anomalous materiel, a defecting expert, or stolen sect property. Which classification prevails often depends on which authority expects to gain by holding them.

[[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Factions/House Valence|Valence]] may offer emergency registration and recognized host continuity. That protection gives it a claim over future service. Refusing is safest in public: without witnesses or immediate sect protection, a Master may disappear into House custody, have their prior provenance suppressed, and return to the market as capabilities attached to another registered name.

Governments may offer asylum while impounding the Master's body for public safety. Source brokers, navies, rival lineages, and collectors see their own opportunities. The home sect must decide whether to defend the Master's freedom to wander, disavow what they have done, or send enough force to let them return without calling the intervention a retrieval.

A familiar Wavecrafter story begins when a Master reveals themselves because staying hidden would leave someone to disaster. The journey back toward sect jurisdiction then becomes desperate as each intervening power finds a legal reason to delay them.

Sanctuary does not undo the revealing act. It may have changed a war, disclosed a protected technique, left victims and dependents, or demonstrated a sect capability that neighboring powers can no longer ignore.

### Limits

Wavecraft becomes hazardous in two distinct circumstances. Tuning beyond local tolerances destabilizes machinery. Bringing exceptional esper agency into the work may instead leave inconsistent observations or continuity residue that later offices can act upon. These hazards arise from overreach; they are not routine miracles on the workshop floor.

Restraint therefore serves technical and political purposes. Operating forms preserve working knowledge and conceal dangerous labor. They also limit experiments whose immediate results may appear clean while leaving a troublesome history behind.

Control of those forms carries authority within the guilds and sects. The people who decide how a crew can safely keep its ship running also decide who may use the deepest controls, and which practitioners will remain absent from the invoice.
