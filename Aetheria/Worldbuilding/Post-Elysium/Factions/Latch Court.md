# Latch Court

At Latch Court, a child can support the team that beat her mother without being sent to apologize. She may still have to help carry her mother's chair to the next match. Families keep scarves for opposing sides on the same hook, and a new lover's allegiance is usually discovered before anyone discusses moving in.

Latch Court is a residential deck in Hinge Yard's rotating habitation ring, among the [[Daedal Houses]] during the first generations after displacement into [[Welcome to Elysium|Elysium]]. Its households live from joint repair, calibration, salvage work, and the wages of the crews who operate the yard's ship-sized articulated machines, called Figures. The court's particular enthusiasm is railball: a game played on a long table, where partners flick wooden discs past movable gates into an opponent's goal. Its teams have become a second set of attachments running through families, work crews, and romances.

## A Different Side

The yard's surviving service families became much more dependent on one another after displacement. Work that once came through a larger company now had to sustain a House. At Hinge, several families pooled their equipment and savings; their children grew up hearing the same names at work, at home, and whenever someone explained whom they could trust.

Railball began in the residential common room while the yard was rebuilding its trade. The tables were cheap, repairable, and small enough to play around people resting between shifts. Later, households cut doorways between three adjacent rooms to make space for spectators. The court still shares the yard's ordinary air, power, and repair services. Residents pay for its tables through small subscriptions, and lend the rooms for other uses between matches.

Good players win through accuracy, deception, and knowing when a partner is about to attempt something foolish. A gate moved at the right moment can turn a losing shot into a goal. Spectators enjoy the pause before somebody tries it. Players sit at the tables; adjustable seats and reachable gate handles matter more here than height or strength. Familiarity with the [[Cognitive Interfacing|interfaces]] that let pilots feel enormous machines as bodies supplies no automatic mastery of a wooden disc.

People join teams by invitation, friendship, or a trial evening. Children often begin by backing a parent, then discover the pleasure of choosing somebody else. Adults change sides too, although changing immediately after a defeat will follow a person for years. The teams deliberately recruit across work crews. A coolant fitter can captain a famous pilot, and a pilot can be an excellent player without being appointed captain of everything in reach.

These attachments have practical weight. Teammates visit during illness, accompany a frightened member to a clinic, or take someone's children for an afternoon. They also offer company to people who have separated from partners but still work at the yard. Such help remains personal: joining a side does not buy a household's labor. Some residents play without taking on that closeness; others dislike the game and use the rooms for reading or conversation on quiet evenings.

## Whose Colours Travel

Sera Pell, a joint-rigger and formidable captain, wants to invite neighboring yards to a tournament. She has spent years making a name that does not require explaining which pilot she keeps alive. Her husband Olan, one of those pilots, wants Hinge to enter a single team under the House colours. He imagines their daughter carrying its banner in front of visitors. The yard has survived long enough to host people for pleasure. He wants everyone to see that.

Their daughter has already promised to carry the banner of Sera's oldest rivals. Olan calls this a private preference that can wait for an ordinary match. Sera objects, then catches herself assuming her daughter will eventually come over to her own side.

Several households favor Olan's plan. Playing together would let them celebrate the work that supports them all. Others want visitors to meet the court's quarrelling teams, including people who have never worn a pilot's interface. The tournament would make their small local loyalties visible beyond the deck. Nobody has yet agreed which invitation to send.

## Unresolved Hooks

- The daughter asks Olan to help paint the rival banner. He has already bought cloth in the House colours.
- A visiting yard offers a match against Hinge's best team; Sera can field it only by asking her least favorite rival to captain.
- A recently separated couple both want to remain on their old side. Their teammates have been quietly arranging evenings so they never meet.
