# Wonderland

*"Stay tuned."*

Wonderland is a small splinter of [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Lucent Media|Lucent Media]] living deep in [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Concepts/Pseudospace|pseudospace]]. Its people descend from Lucent's [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Movements/Reality Architects|Reality Architects]]: the editors, predictive dramaturges, and perception engineers who once shaped how audiences understood events. They took the name the core worlds gave the frontier and made it theirs. Outsiders who say "Wonderland" are rarely sure whether they mean the territory or the people in it, and the people prefer it that way.

They have Lucent's taste for spectacle. Everything else about them is louder.

### The Break

The Architects noticed what had changed sooner than almost anyone. Their trade before the Rupture was attention: what an audience takes for granted, and how to arrange it. Afterwards, Lucent's own behavioural data showed anomalies tracking what audiences expected, and the Architects understood what that meant for a profession built on framing.

Lucent itself never needed to say so. In the core worlds, framing what billions of people expect is its business, and if that also helps keep flat space flat, the company has no reason to examine it. The Architects who left wanted to examine it closely. In the settled worlds, a frame needs a vast audience to hold against everyone else's expectations. Out where consensus is thin, a small crew rehearsed into one tightly shared frame can hold local reality on its own. The crew becomes its own audience.

Wonderland regards Lucent with professional contempt. Lucent broadcasts reality. Wonderland lives inside the edit.

### The Show Must Go On

A Wonderland habitat holds together only while its people keep performing the frame they share. Shifts are staggered so someone is always on. Rest is scheduled the way a broadcaster schedules reruns. Dead air is treated as a hull breach, because it can become one.

Standing works the way it did on Lucent's platforms, except that the stakes are physical. Whoever the crew watches most has the most pull on what the habitat does. Status games are survival, and a coup is a ratings coup. A member who falls into disgrace finds their surroundings cooperating less: a door that hesitates to recognize them, a corridor's gravity that takes a moment longer to agree.

Old Lucent habits continue in stranger forms. The faction keeps the personas of dead performers on the air, because the attachment many people share to a familiar character steadies the region around it. Cancelling a long-running show can fray the space it held.

### Genre

Each habitat runs on a genre its people have rehearsed together, and the genre's conventions are the local rules. In a noir station it always rains in the corridors. In a sitcom habitat, injuries are gone by the next episode. At a thriller depot, every exchange has a twist whether anyone wants one or not.

The conventions are strict and they can be learned. That is the only reason outsiders survive. A habitat's weirdness has a grammar, and a visitor who reads it can predict what the place will do.

Some failures are as old as the format. A habitat can get stuck in its own opening credits, because starting the episode would mean committing to a plot, and plots have consequences. Its people stay in the safe part, smiling, while the cast keeps growing. When two habitats' frames touch, conventions bleed together: a sitcom kitchen lit like a crime drama, with a cartoon's physics, and nobody certain whose rules apply.

What a frame cannot cast, it cannot protect anyone from. An outsider with a strong enough frame of their own, an [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Technology/Necrotech|Eidolon]], or a [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Factions/Death Monkey Explosives|DME]] practitioner who has become unbeing can walk through every genre untouched. To the habitat, they are something in the background that the story has no part for.

### Visiting

Traders, salvagers, and the lost who deal with Wonderland learn its etiquette or do not come back. The rules have the shape of old stories about the fair folk:

- **Don't contradict the genre.** It will fight you.
- **Don't play along too well.** It will adopt you.
- **Don't give your name on camera.** Named, you get a title card, and a title card is how the show casts you.
- **Don't accept props.** A gift, an offered drink, or a line handed to you is a role, and accepting it is accepting the part.
- **Never name the show.** Asking aloud what genre this is contradicts the frame from inside it.
- **Hit your marks and don't improvise.** A flat bit part is safe. A brilliant ad-lib gets you renewed for another season.
- **Mind the episode clock.** A visitor can spend one episode in a habitat and leave to find a season has passed outside, with [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Concepts/Temporal Nonlinearity|continuity residue]] to show for it.

Staying too long ends the same way however careful the visitor has been. They wave at the camera one day without meaning to, their name comes up, and they are a recurring character. Leaving after that means being written out, and genres have opinions about how characters get written out.
