# Parallax Auditors

Parallax auditors are insurer-backed forensic specialists who investigate credible records that contradict one another. Two records may each withstand scrutiny yet describe incompatible routes, chains of custody, command histories, or acts of access. These unresolved traces are called continuity residue. An auditor's finding can make them usable as evidence, allowing the office responsible for docking, insurance, title, or command to consider restrictions.

For a crew, the immediate problem is whether the vessel can keep moving and working while its history remains disputed. An operator may face no legal accusation yet lose access when an office accepts a finding about their records. The auditor does not normally impose that restriction. Their work determines whether the contradiction can enter the decision.

Auditors work through firms, certification pools, port offices, fleet registries, salvage courts, and private investigations. They form neither a state bureau nor a universal regulator. Insurers commission much of their work because ordinary fraud and equipment-failure clauses cannot price every contradiction left by Aetheric work. Ports and registrars also retain them when a compromised record threatens traffic, title, or command but provides no clear basis for an accusation.

Their public names for this work are deliberately ordinary: route-integrity review, custody reconciliation, adverse-claim forensics, and anomalous ship-auth arbitration.

### What a Finding Allows

Auditors issue or validate a [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Reference/Continuity Admissibility Finding|continuity admissibility finding]]. It determines whether a named receiving office may consider the contradictory evidence under its existing rules. It does not establish which history is true.

The finding must identify the affected evidence, test ordinary explanations, name the receiving office, and specify the use for which that office may consider it. Required fields and classifications limited to a particular question constrain the auditor's judgment. Without a named receiving authority, even an alarming finding is only an annotation.

A berth authority, insurer, claimant office, custody desk, or operator board decides what action to take within its own powers. An auditor cannot normally close a berth, void coverage, freeze salvage title, or suspend command by personal decree. The [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Reference/Port-Insurer Compact|Port-Insurer Compact]] provides a common handoff among participating offices: Parallax classifies the evidence, and the receiving office decides whether to accept it and act. Acceptance by one office does not give another permission to impose its own restriction.

### Investigation and Secrecy

Cases may involve human or machine [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Concepts/Esper|espers]], ship minds, or other operators capable of working through the Aether. Auditors begin with the records those abilities leave. They trace witness accounts, access histories, sensor records, custody ledgers, and the origins of commands or physical results. Their task is to preserve and examine incompatible evidence without treating suspicion as certainty about how reality works.

Publicly, the profession describes itself as regulating residue rather than minds. That language allows ports and insurers to act without acknowledging industrial esper labor or nonlinear survival. It also allows an institution to restrict someone through their records without holding a hearing on their personhood. Each later office that accepts an appropriately scoped finding can impose an access restriction it owns, even while the operator remains legally unaccused.

### Who Pays, Who Gets Stuck

The institutions paying auditors are exposed to the losses those auditors classify. An insurer may benefit from a contamination finding when denying a claim costs less than paying it. A client that needs confidence in a route may prefer a softer finding. A report that implicates its commissioner may be sealed.

Competing firms build reputations for severity, discretion, or findings that survive appeal. Evidence need not be falsified for these pressures to affect the result. Choosing which records to examine, how to present them, and when to release a finding can determine which contradiction becomes grounds for action.

The cost of contesting a finding falls unevenly. Wealthy operators can pay for independent review, post reconciliation bonds, and leave a vessel idle through an appeal. Poor crews can be stopped immediately by the same uncertainty. Scrutiny is uneven too: conspicuous, damaged operators draw attention more readily than disciplined esper cultures whose work reaches institutions as certified equipment and apparently ordinary variation in performance.

### Clearance Laundering and Lasting Restrictions

These pressures create a trade in clearance laundering. Corrupt clerks, former auditors, registry brokers, and technical fixers alter the packet of records presented at the next gate. They may split one contradiction across several records, attach custody to a shell owner, or supply a plausible ordinary explanation. At controlled gates, those alterations face the checks and handoffs of the [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Reference/Port-Insurer Compact|Port-Insurer Compact]]. Laundering can postpone suspicion; it does not restore a coherent history.

Repeated findings of contamination can close off routes, increase required bonds, reduce the weight given to testimony, and push crews toward work that clean registries avoid. A surcharge imposed as a temporary measure can become a lasting limit on where a crew can work: each restricted passage adds evidence that a later office may use to justify another restriction.

Some records genuinely cannot be reconciled, which makes Parallax investigation necessary. The practice becomes predatory when an unresolved history is treated as sufficient reason to keep its subject under permanent suspicion.
