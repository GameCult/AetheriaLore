# Continuity Admissibility Finding

A **continuity admissibility finding** is a document that says whether a named office can consider credible but contradictory records as evidence for a specific decision. It may let a berth authority consider conflicting command records when deciding whether a ship can dock. The office must accept the finding under its existing rules before using it.

[[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Factions/Parallax Auditors|Parallax auditors]] issue or validate these findings when records are individually credible but describe incompatible routes, command histories, custody chains, acts of access, or sequences of observation. The finding classifies what use an office may make of that evidence. It does not establish which history occurred, identify a true branch, prove continuity, decide personhood or title, or order a custody action.

## How a Finding Is Used

Consider a ship whose local helm record and an external route beacon both authenticate, yet place the same command under incompatible authority chains. Ordinary clock drift and credential fraud remain possible, but the available evidence supports neither explanation.

An auditor issues a restricted finding to the berth authority for one question: may ordinary docking proceed before the records are reconciled? If the authority accepts it, the finding allows that office to consider both records. Any docking restriction must come from the berth authority's existing powers and the protocol governing the handoff.

The finding does not prove that the ship-self crossed between histories or decide who owns the hull. It gives no permission for an insurer or custody desk to act.

## Required Record

A usable finding must identify:

- **Subject and claim:** the vessel, person, operator, record, or transaction under review, and the assertion the evidence concerns.
- **Contradictory evidence:** the records or other sources of observation that remain credible despite giving incompatible accounts.
- **Ordinary alternatives:** known explanations involving fraud, clocks, sensors, maintenance, transcription, or command authentication, and whether the available record supports or excludes them.
- **Admissibility status:** non-actionable, commercially qualified, restricted, or another classification recognized by the receiving institution.
- **Scope:** the precise question for which the office may consider the evidence.
- **Receiving office:** the port, insurer, registrar, claimant, custody, fleet, or other office asked to accept the finding.
- **Permitted use:** the proceeding or decision in which the evidence may be used.
- **Explicit non-findings:** the questions the review does not decide.
- **Review and expiry:** the event, date, new evidence, or completed proceeding that requires reconsideration or ends permission to use the finding.

Jurisdictions and professional pools use different classification names; there is no universal ranking. The document must make its scope clear regardless of its label. Evidence suitable for pricing an insurance risk may be unusable in a custody hearing. Acceptance for a question about one interrupted helm duty does not make the evidence admissible for title, cargo access, or general command.

## Investigation and Challenge

Auditors examine where the records came from, their maintenance history, tool marks, sensor behavior, command authentication, and independent witnesses. They also consider the incentives of whoever produced or preserved the evidence.

A contradiction by itself is insufficient. The finding must explain which ordinary causes were tested, which remain possible, and what new evidence could change the result.

Affected parties may challenge the evidence's provenance, missing records, reviewer conflicts, the finding's scope, or the receiving office's proposed use. If a finding is corrected or superseded, the earlier document should remain available rather than be silently replaced. Expiry leaves the underlying records intact. It ends permission to rely on that finding without renewed review.

## Handoff Between Offices

Classifying evidence and acting on it are separate decisions. Parallax makes contradictory evidence usable by an office; the office must already have authority over the decision it proposes to make.

At ports and insurer-linked custody gates, this handoff commonly follows the [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Reference/Port-Insurer Compact|Port-Insurer Compact]]. Each office must accept a finding separately and use it only for the named purpose. A berth authority's acceptance cannot suspend insurance coverage, freeze title, or reduce the weight of testimony in another proceeding. Each of those consequences requires a decision by the responsible office, with its own authority, notice, and review.
