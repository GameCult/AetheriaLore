# Running Cold

A ship makes heat doing everything that matters: thrusting, shooting, sensing, thinking. It sheds that heat only by radiating it, which is slow, and which is the most visible thing about it. So every crew runs the same arithmetic all day without discussing it. Do the thing, get hot, be seen, and lose the margin you were keeping for later.

Heat is not three problems. It is one fact with three consequences: a hot ship is a seen ship, a fragile ship, and a ship that cannot keep doing whatever made it hot.

What happens at the top of that band is old and thoroughly mourned; [[Worldbuilding/Politics/Thermal Death Culture|Thermal Death Culture]] carries it. This note is about the other direction, which is new, and which almost nobody meets.

## The Override

The safeties cut the load before the load kills the machinery. A crew under fire that lets its weapons shut down has decided to be captured, and being captured is not reliably survivable, so the safeties get overridden and the compartment cooks with the crew inside it. Dying later beats dying now.

Experienced crews argue about the override the way other trades argue about ethics, because that is what it is. Nobody disputes that heat kills. The argument is how many seconds of function are worth how much of a person, and who is allowed to decide that for somebody else.

## Negent Aboard

[[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Concepts/Negative Entropy|Negent]] gear converts heat into high-energy photons, so a ship carrying it can run colder than its surroundings. A ship that can run cold can do things no other ship can sustain. It can fire without pausing. It can sit in a debris field radiating almost nothing and watch a search pass over it. Adrasteia sells the capability and keeps the process.

It is also a device whose function is removing the heat its crew is made of. Negent does not distinguish between a gun's waste heat and a person's. The first generation of negent crews learned this by dying of it, and the death gives none of the warnings the familiar one gives: no rising panic, no argument about the override, no smell. People report feeling clear-headed and unusually calm, and then they stop reporting.

Spacers find this obscene in a way the ancestral nightmare is not. Cooking is an accident, even when somebody's decision caused it. Freezing aboard a working ship, surrounded by equipment operating exactly as designed, reads as a transaction with the crew listed among the inputs.

## What It Costs To Stay Cold

Adrasteia's monopoly is the smallest part of the price. Negent gear consumes a charged configuration of strange matter that only Adrasteia produces, so sustained cold costs a rate, payable in something a ship must buy, carry, and run out of at the worst available moment. A crew's willingness to keep firing is in practice a question about its cargo manifest.

The equipment pays the rest. Machinery held far below its designed temperature, and dragged there quickly, wears at a rate that would be called a fault in any other context. Negent crews replace parts continuously and treat that schedule as part of the cost of shooting. A gun that never has to stop is still a gun being spent.

Hence the shape of a negent engagement. The negent ship is quiet and untouchable and does not have to pause, for exactly as long as its charges hold and its parts survive. After that it is a very cold ship full of worn equipment, crewed by people who feel fine.

## Reading Somebody Else's Heat

Because heat is what sensors see, thermal discipline is also how crews read each other. A contact running hot has been working recently. A contact running unusually cold is dead, drifting, or carrying something Adrasteian, and a crew that guesses wrong about which is about to have a bad afternoon. [[Worldbuilding/Politics/Thermal Signature Warfare|Thermal Signature Warfare]] covers the practice that grew out of this.

It is also why most crews value a good sensor operator over a good gunner. The gunner decides how an engagement ends. The operator decides whether it happens at all.
