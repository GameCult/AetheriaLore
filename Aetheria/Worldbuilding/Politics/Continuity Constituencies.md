# Continuity Constituencies

In PSC-aligned late Sol, an enemy can be more useful than a subsidiary. A corporation that can show independent clinics, unions, habitats, carriers, or utilities depending upon its continued operation can argue that sanctions, seizure, or exclusion would injure parties outside the dispute. Risk officers call those dependents **continuity constituencies**: not voters and not necessarily supporters, but populations whose foreseeable harm changes the price of coercing the provider.

The practice grows from the externality accounting described in [[Worldbuilding/Politics/Restrictions on Warfare|Restrictions on Warfare]]. The [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Pan-Solar Consortium|Pan-Solar Consortium]] needs to distinguish an ordinary corporate loss from a disruption that will close clinics, strand ships, interrupt air or power, or break obligations across several jurisdictions. A firm with a large, credible continuity constituency receives no general immunity. It does gain arguments for narrower sanctions, higher conflict bonds, protected service corridors, emergency clearing access, and more time before an enforcement action becomes commercially final.

## Adverse Reliance

No universal census decides who counts. A continuity claim is assembled for a particular permit, sanction, arbitration, insurance review, or custody dispute. Contracts, service topology, maintenance history, substitution time, stored reserves, and testimony must show that the dependent is real, materially exposed, and difficult to replace before the relevant deadline. A [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Minor/Rossum & Douglas|Rossum & Douglas]] finding can make part of that evidence portable; the receiving insurer, port, panel, or court still decides what consequence follows.

Corporate dependents are persuasive only up to a point. A wholly controlled subsidiary may be treated as an internal loss the owner chose to create. A hostile union clinic, unaffiliated habitat, independent carrier, or rival workshop supplies stronger evidence because its injury cannot be dismissed as damage confined to the claimant's own balance sheet. This gives firms an incentive to preserve opposition as a separate legal and political body while ensuring that body cannot leave cheaply.

The cleanest instrument is an **adverse-reliance covenant**. The provider supplies power, cooling, clearing, clinical maintenance, route access, certification, or data custody below the dependent's immediate replacement cost. The dependent acknowledges the service and the time required to replace it while expressly retaining the right to organize, criticize, sue, or bargain against the provider. Clauses barring the firm from representing the dependent as an affiliate are not concessions to dignity alone. They preserve the independence that makes the dependency valuable evidence.

The bargain can be materially good. A union gains a clinic, a habitat keeps a heat loop certified, or a marginal carrier receives clearing access it could not otherwise afford. The provider must keep the service working and cannot casually liquidate the dependent without weakening its own future claims. Some constituencies win real bargaining room because management needs them alive, visible, and adversarial enough to count.

That room remains a form of enclosure. The provider needs an opponent, not an equal capable of departure.

## The Hostage File

Every continuity constituency produces a **hostage file**: the records needed both to prove dependence and to end it safely. Configuration histories, patient mappings, calibration records, credential chains, payment standing, route exceptions, spares inventories, and substitution estimates show a regulator what would fail if service stopped. The same material tells a replacement provider what must be carried across.

Custody is usually divided badly on purpose. A company retains live service history, an insurer holds loss assumptions, a contractor holds encrypted escrow, and the dependent sees only the fields required for daily operation. Private security firms guard repositories and witness handoffs because a compromised file can alter conflict bonds or sanctions exposure without a shot being fired. Their mandate is contractual: they protect the admitted record and release conditions, not the people described inside it.

This makes data denial a quieter weapon than termination. A firm can continue supplying a union clinic while withholding the current calibration lineage needed for independent recertification. It can release patient records without the maintenance history of the systems keeping those patients alive, or provide a valid export too late for the receiving yard's inspection window. Nothing has been remotely disabled. The dependent simply cannot prove that leaving will remain safe and recognized.

Hostage files are valuable to rivals as well. A buyer can discover which obligations must be preserved during an acquisition. A raider can identify the cheapest dependency to sever. An insurer can learn that a supposedly independent constituency is a paper shell. A labor federation can learn which four small migrations would strip a major firm of the external-harm argument protecting its next contract.

## Unspooling

The labor answer is **unspooling**: coordinated removal of dependencies until a firm can no longer count the departing groups as political collateral. It is not a strike. Work may continue throughout. Organizers mirror records, qualify substitute parts, move accounts, train maintainers, establish emergency reserves, negotiate receiving capacity, and transfer one live service at a time. Only after a replacement survives operation do they disclose enough of the new topology to challenge the provider's continuity claim.

The [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Movements/Awakened Labor Front|Awakened Labor Front]] uses unspooling where a stoppage would make workers appear to prove management's claim that only the incumbent can keep the system alive. [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Activist Network|Activist Network]] nodes can mirror bounded records and arrange handoffs without owning the decision to leave. The smallest successful efforts are quieter: several households sharing travel and medicine while two workshops maintain duplicate spares, or three clinics taking turns carrying the unused capacity that lets a fourth change providers. These arrangements create no general sanctuary. They make one exit survivable.

Unspooling is expensive precisely because it attacks political power rather than only revenue. Workers carry parallel systems, expose trusted maintainers, and risk losing subsidies before the replacement is ready. A rushed departure can kill the constituency it meant to free and become evidence that the old provider was indispensable. A slow departure gives the firm time to bundle services, alter certification, offer selective concessions, or move the hostage file behind another contractual boundary.

Rivals finance unspooling campaigns when they want an opponent cheaper to sanction, acquire, or attack. Their assistance may be technically excellent and politically predatory. Once the transfer is complete, the rescued constituency can discover that it has merely become external-harm evidence for a new provider.

## Corporate Geometry

Continuity constituencies make corporate boundaries openly tactical. [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Aeronautics Unlimited|Aeronautics Unlimited]] can describe a ramp as an independent claimant when abandoning its liabilities, then count the same ramp as an external dependent when seeking corridor protection for the supply network still feeding it. [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Minor/SolEx|SolEx]] challenges those boundaries during consolidation, arguing that a constituency governed through one audit and freight chain is an internal asset regardless of its local council or separate contracts. PSC panels rarely settle the principle. They decide which description is sufficiently bankable for the action in front of them.

The result is an order in which opposition can be subsidized, independence can be an asset owned by somebody else, and a successful act of mutual aid can reduce the regulatory shield around the people who performed it. A firm may offer concessions because it needs a union to remain. The union may accept because the clinic must open tomorrow. Both know that the relationship's political value ends when the dependent can walk away without taking anyone's air, body, wages, or records with it.

## Open Pressures

- When does a PSC panel treat an adverse constituency as genuinely independent, and which bloc benefits from exposing a union as a managed shell?
- Can an unspooling campaign remove enough dependencies to weaken a firm without making its workers and habitats cheaper targets for the rivals funding their exit?
- What does a security contractor do when both the provider and the dependent satisfy different release conditions for the only intact hostage file?
