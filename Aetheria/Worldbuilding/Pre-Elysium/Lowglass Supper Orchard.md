# Lowglass Supper Orchard

In Lowglass, a small agricultural settlement in Mars's Hellas Basin, people arrange supper around which pears are ready. The communal orchard grows several varieties that ripen at different points in its controlled growing cycle. A household brings its ordinary meal to the trees, shares a few slices of fruit, and stays to argue about whether this picking is better than the last. Children learn the routes between tables before they learn the varieties' names.

Around 2460, Lowglass belongs to the [[Aya Collective]], the federation of locally governed cooperatives and sanctuaries. Its place among the [[Territories|Hellas agricultural communities]] connects it to the shared water and crop arrangements established by the [[Hellas Mutual Defense Congress]]. Its orchard is a local enthusiasm. Neighbors can admire it without wanting to live there.

## Room for Something Slow

Lowglass grows its staple crops in enclosed production houses. The supper orchard occupies a separate pressurized hall, with insulated walls, growing lights, water recovery, and a service corridor connecting it to the settlement. Nobody steps outside into Martian air to fetch dessert. Pear trees are trained along low frames beside movable tables; roots occupy contained beds, and walking lanes remain clear. The hall's temperature and lighting follow the trees' needs, including their dormant interval. During that interval, people hold their suppers elsewhere.

Residents pay for the orchard through a share of the cooperative's produce income and agreed work hours. They could use the space and energy for a faster crop. Keeping trees means accepting years of pruning and care before a replacement earns its place. Lowglass makes that choice because residents want somewhere beautiful to spend time and fruit worth waiting for. The orchard also draws occasional paying visitors, though most suppers are simply neighbors eating after work.

The custom is to announce a picking when the fruit is ready rather than hurry it for a fixed festival date. There are several small suppers across the harvest. A child may spend days checking a particular branch, hoping its fruit will be ready when a visiting cousin arrives. A shift worker can miss the first picking without missing the whole occasion.

## Learning a Taste

Children begin by carrying plates and comparing slices. Later they learn thinning, pruning, and hand pollination beside experienced growers. The admired skill is remembering what a tree needs without turning every conversation into instruction. Some excellent growers are still working on the second part.

Each variety has its partisans. One holds its shape when cooked; another bruises so easily that eating it beside the tree is half its appeal. People trade recipes, practice songs for the suppers, flirt over incompatible judgments of ripeness, and save a portion for someone who cannot come. Growing, cooking, music, and good company offer different ways to belong. Residents who dislike crowds often help with the trees when the hall is quiet. Others prefer their own kitchens altogether.

Young growers want room to try unfamiliar varieties. Older enthusiasts disagree among themselves: some want to protect familiar tastes, while others are impatient for a fruit they have never eaten. An established tree occupies a real bed. Replacing it removes a reliable harvest, and the new tree may disappoint for several cycles before anyone admits it. Grafting a trial branch can reduce the gamble, but it cannot create unlimited space.

## Unresolved Hooks

- A much-loved cooking pear is healthy but unpopular with younger diners. Its chief defender has offered to teach every recipe she knows; two apprentices want its bed for a new variety.
- A returning resident brings cuttings and wants to name the resulting fruit after a former partner who still lives in Lowglass. The partner likes the pear and dislikes the dedication.
- Visitors offer enough money for a larger harvest supper to fund new benches. The cooks want the benches; several regulars want an evening when nobody has to be interesting to strangers.
