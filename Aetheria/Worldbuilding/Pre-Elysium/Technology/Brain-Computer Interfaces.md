# Brain-Computer Interfaces

Before the [[FTL Trigger]], brain-computer interfaces ranged from clinical implants to full sensory and motor integration. They made cognition an infrastructure market: employers, states, and media firms could sell access to augmented work and social life while retaining leverage over the hardware through which users perceived it.

Unlike [[Mind Uploading]], an interface mediates an existing mind's access to a body or system rather than claiming to instantiate a copy.

## The Neural Market

The market followed class power. [[Finch Cybernetics]] sold prestigious, closely maintained implants to elites. [[AstroDyne]] supplied durable systems for frontier and industrial work. [[CogniSys]] served the [[Sol Dominion]]'s administrative and security apparatus, while [[SolEx]] sold monitored interfaces intended to keep workers employable.

Subscriptions extended corporate control beyond purchase. Firmware, sensory filters, memory tools, and compatibility services could be withdrawn when payments or employment ended. Moving between vendors risked lost function and inaccessible records. [[Cognitum]] pushed the model further by selling [[Neuromorphic Firmware]] derived from upload-grade scans as intuition and behavioral assistance.

This produced four broad conditions rather than stable castes: people whose premium interfaces expanded their available work and experience; professionals whose augmentation was adequate but employer-dependent; workers issued narrow, monitored systems; and unaugmented people excluded from environments designed around neural access. The boundaries shifted with jurisdiction, debt, maintenance, and firmware support.

## Control and Resistance

BCIs made thought and action available to institutional discipline. Employers measured attention, fatigue, affect, and compliance. Some systems adjusted mood or restricted motor action under safety and security provisions. [[Zhestokost]] and the [[Sol Dominion]] integrated such controls into labor and military command; less coercive firms framed similar functions as wellness, optimization, or corporate culture.

Resistance centered on ownership and legibility. The [[Corvid Collective]] developed counter-surveillance, removal practices, and interfaces that users could inspect. The [[Free Neural Network]] distributed open designs and maintained communities where neural privacy remained possible. States and vendors treated unauthorized firmware as both a safety hazard and a threat to contractual control.

## Mediated Culture

Direct sensory exchange supported art, entertainment, training, shared memory, and altered subjective time. [[Lucent Media]] built immersive experiences around these capacities, then used the same access to measure attention and tune emotion. The distinction between expression and manipulation depended less on signal type than on consent, exit, and control of the interface.

## Daedal Ergotechnics

Late in the Sol era, Daedal Ergotechnics applied premium motor interfaces to full-body industrial frames. Its systems continuously reconciled balance, actuator position, load, and distributed touch, letting a worker experience shipyard machinery as an enlarged body. The costly method survived where damaged structures, bespoke fabrication, or artisanal prestige still rewarded named human judgment at machine scale.

This lineage passed through the shunt into the [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Factions/Daedal Houses|Daedal Houses]]. In Elysium, an interface built to preserve bodily continuity became a means of coupling intention to machinery through the [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Concepts/Aether|Aether]].
