Uplift uses technology to expand the cognitive abilities of a nonhuman biological lineage and its ability to participate in shared institutions. It can involve genetic changes passed to descendants, neural interfaces, or environments arranged to support learning, communication, and action. These supporting environments are known as scaffolds.

An uplifted person's abilities did not determine how they would be treated. Even demonstrated personhood could go unrecognized by a court, employer, or registry. Depending on their contract and jurisdiction, an uplift might be treated as property, kept in managed dependency, or recognized as a political participant.

The category excludes [[Biodrone|biodrones]], which begin as biological organisms grown specifically for labor. It also excludes uploaded and machine persons such as [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Concepts/Ship Mind Custody|ship minds]].

### The First Recognized Lineage

The first recognized uplift lineage was the [[Portia Line]]. In the 2268 Veyra Incident, a municipal quarantine team entered an illegal spider habitat equipped with cognitive scaffolds. Investigators found that the spiders had maintained the laboratory and operated its access systems. The inhabitants answered them with route diagrams.

An attempted seizure of laboratory assets consequently became a dispute over whether the inhabitants were persons. The incident also denied later firms the claim that nonhuman intelligence was necessarily a corporate-designed product.

### Commercial Uplift and Worker Dependence

[[BioElevate]], [[GeneSys]], and [[NeuroSyn]] subsequently developed commercial uplift lines for mining, navigation, reconnaissance, logistics, and orbital maintenance. Their programs commonly modified a lineage for a particular labor environment while keeping the habitats and interfaces that supported its workers tied to an employer.

That dependence shaped what happened when workers refused. Employers could classify refusal as a defect in the product, a change in how its supporting scaffold functioned—“scaffold drift”—or worker misconduct. Each classification let an institution treat the refusal according to its own rules, even while personhood remained disputed.

These programs produced populations whose descendants included the [[Cetacean Navigators]], [[Corvid Collective]], [[Cephalopod Syndicate]], and [[Elephant Enclave]]. Their political responses differed. Some sought recognition through existing courts and markets; others built autonomous habitats, labor organizations, or networks that helped people leave. Those choices grew from different institutions and histories, rather than from biology.

### Organizing Across Labor Categories

During the [[Pallas Species Strikes]] of 2719–2724, workers coordinated despite the different categories suppliers had assigned them. Uplift workers, baseline crews, and biodrones took control of technical chokepoints in Belt yards.

The strikes strengthened ties between [[Cephalopod Syndicate]] operators, human labor networks, and the future [[Awakened Labor Front]]. On Pallas, sentient labor could no longer be handled solely as a question of whether suppliers were liable for their products. Workers acting together had made it a struggle over coercive labor itself; containing the issue required ending that coercion.

### Late-Sol Infrastructure

Uplift cognition also contributed to late-Sol infrastructure. Models of cetacean cognition informed the routing logic of the 3025 FTL assembly, and uploaded and machine intermediaries stabilized that logic.

Uplifts neither invented FTL nor supplied its only possible means of working. The [[FTL Trigger]] displaced them into Elysium with the rest of Sol.
