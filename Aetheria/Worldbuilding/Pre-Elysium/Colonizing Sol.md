### Colonizing Sol

Humanity’s settlement of the solar system began through subcontracting, rather than a shared project governed on behalf of the species. During the 21st century, space agencies, sovereign wealth funds, insurers, platform monopolies, and extractive firms brought launch systems, orbital infrastructure, and mineral rights under private control. They continued to speak of development and human progress long after the work plainly served commercial control and extraction.

The first lasting off-world settlements were workplaces before they were civic communities. Colonists arrived owing money for transport, then paid habitat rent and oxygen fees while using employer-issued scrip. The company claiming to feed and protect them also controlled the necessities they had to pay for. A worker could formally resign and still be unable to leave the habitat that the job had financed. The right to quit meant little without a way out.

Earth remained important, but its governments increasingly lost the ability to dictate terms. Climate stress, fiscal crisis, and the departure of elites made them dependent on the firms they were supposed to regulate. Treaties survived chiefly to recognize leases, patents, and security arrangements. By the time the first Belt habitats could sustain themselves, ownership of docks, reactors, and life-support software had become the basis of sovereignty: those who controlled the systems that kept a settlement alive held governing power.

Late Sol combined posthuman technological change with dependencies that resembled feudal rule. Colonies were advertised as evidence of progress, while residents lived with debts that bound them to work, discipline based on predictions of their behavior, and necessities supplied by monopolies. Technological advances did not free people from the owners of their workplaces and homes.

Every later conflict in the setting grows from this foundation. [[SolEx]] inherits the bureaucracy that administers extraction. [[Framgång]] makes a business of the anxiety caused by permanent instability. [[Aya Collective]] rejects the company-town model and builds ways for people to sustain one another outside it. The [[Pan-Solar Consortium]] arrives later to restrain the conflicts threatening corporate rule’s survival, while preserving that rule itself.
