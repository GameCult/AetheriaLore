## March 17, 3025

On March 17, 3025, [[Sol Dominion]] activated the first true faster-than-light (FTL) assembly at the Mars Arete complex. The drive triggered a response that displaced whole civilizations from Sol into Elysium. The discontinuity experienced by those caught in the event is commonly called the **Rupture**.

The launch was an irreversible gamble by the late-Sol order. [[Quantum Dynamics]] supplied the field geometry, [[NiteLife Energy]] supported the power grid, and [[Cryonix]] supplied materials. The prototype vessel, built by [[Alakrita]], was designed to withstand loads no passenger craft was ever meant to face.

These visible contributions were only part of what the project depended on. Its routing logic used models of cetacean cognition. Uploaded minds and machine intermediaries helped stabilize the system. Several of its deepest prediction layers had been tuned with black-budget [[Cognitum]] firmware and conditioned cognition capable of sustaining nonlocal abstractions—concepts that ordinary human reasoning could not hold for long. The project reached into more systems and forms of cognition than its sponsors' claims of containment admitted.

[[Zhestokost]] guarded the site. Everyone involved understood that the launch was both a scientific milestone and a political coup: it committed the late-Sol order to an attempted escape rather than a negotiated response to decline.

When the drive crossed its operating threshold, it made contact with structures outside ordinary human physics and activated a quarantine response that already existed. The displacement did not neatly separate humans from nonhumans, biological lives from digital ones, or those belonging to sovereign powers from the stateless. Entire civilizations moved together, including their populations, institutions, and supporting systems, because the late-Sol order had already made them materially interdependent.

Those arriving in Elysium therefore brought more than the results of a propulsion experiment. They carried the relationships, dependencies, and compromises through which Sol had developed. The Rupture made that shared history impossible to separate: technological escape had moved the civilization, without freeing it from what it had become.
