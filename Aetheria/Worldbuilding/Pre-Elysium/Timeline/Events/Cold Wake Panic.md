### Cold Wake Panic

**Date:** 2241-2248
**Location:** inner-system test corridors, lunar thermal yards, early PSC insurer tribunals
**Primary actors:** PSC insurers, [[Cryonix]], Cinderlace researchers, [[Rossum & Douglas]] precursor teams, privateer cells, corridor security buyers

[[Cold Wake Panic]] was the moment early PSC corridor law admitted that thermal stealth had become a strategic problem rather than a boutique engineering trick. A run of near-miss interceptions, ghost tracks, delayed heat dumps, and misclassified quiet-running vessels convinced insurers that existing permit categories no longer described the real battlespace. Too many ships could remain cold long enough to make first detection politically explosive.

The panic mattered because it formalized a new vocabulary. PSC insurers began pricing thermal posture directly, [[Cryonix]]'s signature-managed skins moved from premium industrial specialty into overt strategic infrastructure, and the research programs that later fed [[Rossum & Douglas]] quiet-envelope certification gained political urgency. After Cold Wake, no serious power could pretend that waste heat was separate from warfare.

It also marked the threshold between crude thermal stealth and true signature shaping. Before the panic, most operators could hide heat, delay it, dump it, or throw out obvious decoys. Cold wake metamaterials made release patterns programmable enough that a heat source could imitate another plausible class inside its envelope. That did not make ships invisible. It made the scene dishonest. A quiet hull could look like a smaller courier or legal dump event; a heat ferry could carry waste away and spend it later as service traffic, a radiator accident, or the false punctuation that made some other silence seem normal.

Cold Wake also made femto-manufacturing practical outside the largest institutional stacks. The same thermal microcontrol that let machinery hide heat, move heat, and defer heat without immediately cooking itself made basement-scale precision fabrication newly plausible for the obsessed, the wealthy, and the reckless. The [[Portia Line]] emerged from that opening two decades later: not as a ship-stealth project, but as one of the first social consequences of thermal miracles escaping the cleanroom.
