### Pallas Species Strikes

**Date:** 2719-2724  
**Location:** Pallas Bloom Cluster and associated Belt yards  
**Primary actors:** [[BioElevate]], [[VitaForge]] biodrone suppliers, uplift workers, [[Aeronautics Unlimited]], precursor labor cells, [[Cephalopod Syndicate]]

The [[Pallas Species Strikes]] began when uplifted and heavily modified workers stopped work in dangerous cavity-construction and maintenance zones. They refused routes that management still wanted staffed. [[Aeronautics Unlimited]] (AU) and its suppliers expected to replace them with [[VitaForge]] BioDrone Standard workers and contract humans. That plan failed as the strike spread to essential technical systems that only the uplift crews knew how to operate.

The yards depended on these workers partly because their bodies could reach places other crews could not safely enter. [[BioElevate]] had modified its industrial cephalopod lines to work in dry yards, fitting them with pliable mobile life-support harnesses, humidity skins, oxygenation loops, pressure cuffs, and neural compliance interfaces. These systems let them survive and work in human workspaces, zero-g, cramped crawlways, seal lungs, and service arteries where the baseline crews and biodrone lines assigned to those yards could not maneuver safely.

For the cephalopods, surviving a shift depended on equipment built to make them useful in those conditions. They were not living and working in aquatic habitats designed around their comfort. AU territory was not Navigator territory: AU paid for cephalopod uplift because it needed industrial labor, with no commitment to accommodate the aquatic lives their bodies had evolved for. The support systems kept workers alive and productive; the yards offered no sanctuary.

Several employers and suppliers shared those workplaces. VitaForge biodrones, BioElevate uplift lines, baseline riggers, AU supervisors, and local maintenance contractors all worked within the same seal and air systems. Yet AU and its suppliers treated a refusal to enter a dangerous route differently depending on who refused. A biodrone's refusal became “product variance.” An uplifted worker's became “scaffold drift” or “adaptation variance.” A baseline worker's became “worker noncompliance.” These classifications let each company handle the refusal as a problem with its product or workforce while keeping questions of liability separate.

The strike gained force when workers began coordinating across those divisions. Management could classify them separately, but it could not easily replace their knowledge of the systems the yards needed to function. Cooperation made that dependence a source of political leverage.

The strikes exposed how much AU's frontier abundance relied on sentient populations it continued to treat as tools to be managed. They strengthened connections among the labor cells that would contribute to the future [[Awakened Labor Front]], [[Cephalopod Syndicate]] operators, and human labor networks that had previously treated uplift politics as a secondary concern.

Pallas remained unequal after the strikes. What changed was the Belt's understanding of these workers' power: anyone taking its politics seriously now had to reckon with sentient labor's ability to organize and refuse.
