### Ganymede Route Compact

**Date:** 2478  
**Location:** Ganymede orbital docks  
**Primary actors:** proto-[[Cetacean Navigators]], [[Lightsail Express]], dock labor syndicates, Jovian convoy insurers

The [[Ganymede Route Compact]] transformed uplifted navigation labor into recognized corridor governance. Long-haul carriers and dock operators had become dependent on cetacean route cognition, but still treated navigators as specialized assets rather than political partners. After a season of catastrophic routing disputes and preventable convoy losses, that arrangement became untenable.

The Compact established rescue obligations, shared hazard ledgers, route-steward representation, and a new legal status for navigator councils in convoy arbitration. This was the moment the future [[Cetacean Navigators]] ceased being a labor caste and became a supraterritorial political force.

Many later sanctuary and rescue systems trace their institutional ancestry back to Compact clauses.

### Witnessed Route Voice

<!-- Authored elaboration: Ghostlight Charter pass, 2026-09-04. -->

The Compact did not let a carrier, owner, or human interpreter speak merely because an uplifted navigator worked through its ship or equipment. For each convoy interval, the affected navigator watch lodged a **route-voice mandate** in the shared hazard ledger. The mandate named one steward and a predeclared alternate, the crews and route segment represented, its expiry, and the acts the steward could perform: authenticate a hazard reading, accept or refuse a route commitment, invoke a rescue diversion, and open convoy arbitration. It could not waive wages, injury claims, habitat support, personhood claims, or the rights of an absent navigator council.

A mandate became usable when it arrived through a navigator-controlled communication channel and two clerks witnessed the same scope: one from a dock labor syndicate and one from a carrier or insurer that was not choosing the steward. Their signatures certified the channel, record, and declared interval, not the wisdom of the decision. Spoken translation could accompany the record, but the source signal and any dissent remained attached. This made a translator an accountable instrument rather than a substitute principal.

The alternate succeeded only by answering through the recorded channel after the steward withdrew, became unavailable, or reached expiry. Silence never transferred voice to an employer. Any affected navigator could issue an immediate safety hold when collision or life-support danger left no time to test the full mandate; that hold could stop departure or force one evasive change, but it expired after the immediate decision and could not settle the underlying claim.

A represented navigator, dock syndicate, carrier, or insurer could challenge coercion, mistranslation, expiry, or scope. The registry then replayed the source signal through an independent interface and kept the ship at berth until a valid voice answered or the parties accepted a narrower commitment. Hazard data remained usable as warning during the challenge, but disputed assent did not. Delay consumed berth time, fuel margin, wages, and bond capacity. Wealthy carriers could afford redundant interpreters and spare slots; small crews sometimes accepted an ugly narrow mandate because a procedurally perfect delay would strand them.

The arrangement bound unlike interests without merging them. Insurers priced convoy bonds against witnessed mandates, dock syndicates refused clearance without one, and [[Lightsail Express]] depended on decisions other carriers would honor. Navigator councils, in turn, still depended on human-built docks, funded registries, and accessible communication equipment. A failed channel could therefore become political disenfranchisement while every office claimed only a technical fault. Dock recorders began keeping duplicate mandate receipts in separate union custody after several employers tried to replace contested source signals with clean translations. It was a small defense, useful precisely because nobody had to call it liberation before copying the record.
