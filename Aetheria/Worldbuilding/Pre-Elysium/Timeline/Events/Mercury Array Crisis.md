### Mercury Array Crisis

**Date:** 2966-2969  
**Location:** Mercury north-array sectors and synchronous orbit platforms  
**Primary actors:** [[Sol Dominion]], [[NiteLife Energy]], [[Lucent Media]], local labor crews, PSC finance committees

The [[Mercury Array Crisis]] began when forecasts used to schedule solar power failed. Military readiness, confidence in trade, and faster-than-light research models all depended on those energy predictions. Stabilizing the arrays therefore mattered well beyond Mercury: whoever could restore dependable scheduling would gain influence over the other activities that relied on it.

The failure became a struggle over who would govern the inner system's most strategically important solar infrastructure. [[Sol Dominion]] stabilized the forecasting failure and extended its administrative control before rival powers could exploit the disruption. It emerged stronger because its ability to coordinate a technical response also gave it lasting political leverage. That success did not make its forecasts infallible.

[[NiteLife Energy]] protected its contracts, while the finance committees of the Pan-Solar Consortium protected their appearance of neutrality. The crisis made clear to everyone involved that power forecasting had become as politically consequential as decisions about food or permits for war. Whoever controlled the schedules could influence much more than the supply of electricity.
