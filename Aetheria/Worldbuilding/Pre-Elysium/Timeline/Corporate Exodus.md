## 2025-2100

The [[Corporate Exodus]] is the period when private companies turn off-world settlement into a form of government. They control launch services, mining, habitat management, and debt enforcement together. For residents, that means the companies supplying the systems that keep them alive also have the power to set the terms of their lives.

Off-world industry increasingly abandons its claim to serve a public mission. Colonies are organized around privately owned infrastructure, and dependence on that infrastructure gives its owners authority over the population.

### Transport, Mining, and Food

The early breakthrough is the [[Soliton Drive]]. Dr. Mara Varga and Kenji Sato turn research into compact helium-3 confinement into a practical basis for new shipping systems, which [[Horizon Ventures]] commercializes. Horizon provides the transport network; [[TerraCore Industries]] brings excavation operations and the bureaucracy that manages them. Their merger in 2095 creates [[SolEx]], the first extraction enterprise to operate on a truly pan-solar scale.

[[Ewan Hart]] follows these firms into habitats that have already accepted company law. It sells seed banks, closed-loop agriculture packages, and agronomic consulting. These supplies help sustain settlements, but dependence on a food supplier gives that supplier leverage over residents, just as dependence on fuel does. A colony cannot act independently if another company controls the supplies needed to keep it running.

### Implants Become Workplace Systems

In the 2070s and 2080s, early [[Brain-Computer Interfaces]] expand beyond therapeutic use into systems for managing workers. [[NeuroSyn]] leads this shift. It markets productivity implants as freedom from fatigue while quietly making the cognitive functions they support dependent on subscriptions.

Different markets produce different designs. Frontier operators such as [[AstroDyne]] develop harsher systems that are easier to repair. Premium design houses, some of which later feed into [[Finch Cybernetics]], make augmentation desirable as a mark of aspiration before it becomes universal.

The data produced by interfaces also becomes a commodity. Employers want more than increased physical capability: they want to measure attention and compliance, and to regulate workers' emotional states. Payroll software and occupational health contracts become early means of exercising this control. The conflicts that later surround neural authoritarianism begin in these workplace arrangements.

### Selling the Exodus

The public promise is escape from a damaged Earth. For most people, the experience is entry into settlements where companies control essential services and the terms on which residents can use them.

[[Lucent Media]] gives the “space exodus” its glamour. [[Framgång]] turns anxiety into a market for self-optimization, selling people ways to improve themselves as they navigate these conditions. Early abolitionist currents, which later contribute to [[Aya Collective]], challenge the promise of emancipation: they describe the colonies as company towns with better branding.

By 2100, the central political argument across Sol is established. Corporations claim that only they can build the future. Their critics call that future technically impressive and socially medieval. Corporate infrastructure makes off-world society possible, while its ownership leaves residents dependent on the firms that govern them.
