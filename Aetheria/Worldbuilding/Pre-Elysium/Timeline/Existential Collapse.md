## 2750-3025

The [[Existential Collapse]] is the period when Sol’s aging infrastructure and institutions can no longer sustain the civilization built around them. People depend on systems with too many overdue repairs, while governments and corporations struggle to justify their authority. Whole populations have had their lives organized around other people’s profits. What once seemed harsh but stable begins to break down, and competing powers fight to decide how—and for whom—civilization will survive.

### Wireheading As Social Breakdown

[[Wireheading]] gives people access to sustained pleasure through direct neural stimulation. During the Collapse, [[NeuroPulse]] turns it into an industry large enough to serve both a mass market and those seeking a weapon. Repeating cycles of bliss spread through neural systems already used for work, social status, and immersive entertainment.

Other powers adapt the technology to their own purposes. [[Lucent Media]] packages the experience of collapse for consumption. [[Cognitum]] uses the systems that generate reward in uploaded minds to act against them. [[Zhestokost]] adds controlled ecstasy to its methods for suppressing insurgency.

People respond by seeking pleasure, order, or ways to keep one another alive. [[Hedonists]] become both customers of the pleasure industries and casualties of them. [[Disciplinists]] attract followers by promising that strict obedience and clear roles can hold a failing society together. [[Preservationists]] and mutual-aid groups become emergency response networks in practice. Rescue is selective: people who are still needed as workers for resource extraction may be left inside the systems harming them.

Basic income and other universal provisions keep many people alive, but do not give them a credible part in public life. Permanent withdrawal into immersion becomes an ordinary administrative option. A person’s welfare entitlement is converted into long-term care. Their housing is released for someone else to use, their possessions are disposed of, and eventually a transport takes their body away.

Governments can call this autonomy, and providers can call it care. Those who remain awake watch entire communities leave without technically dying.

### Dreamer Repositories

Immersed people still need their bodies kept alive. When a shrinking Bloom no longer has enough residents or industry to maintain itself, scattered clinics cannot continue caring for its dreamers. Regional authorities consolidate that care in industrial repositories: facilities organized around power, nutrient cycling, waste processing, neural support, medical robotics, and physical security.

Official names emphasize continuity, welfare, or autonomous residence. People outside usually call them dreamer cities, vaults, or mega-necropolises.

A large repository can hold as many people as a metropolis, stretched through kilometers of cradles. Few of them need streets or public spaces, and relatively few awake workers maintain the site. Maintenance rail, pumps, corpse recovery, proprietary interfaces, and accounting remain in operation even where civic life has disappeared. The visible community may be a small, exhausted workforce keeping the physical systems running for an immense population experiencing paradise.

For relatives, consolidation means losing the last practical connection to someone who is still alive. A family member enters a nearby clinic, moves to a regional center, and eventually has only a row and cradle address millions of kilometers away. Visits become difficult or impossible in practice. There is no funeral to mark the separation.

That unresolved grief accompanies disputes over what the dreamers are owed and who may decide for them. Some claims treat them as citizens with rights; others as dependants using scarce capacity or customers bound by contracts. Even a dreamer’s expressed refusal to leave immersion is contested: some argue that the refusal cannot be trusted.

As the Collapse worsens, repositories become too important to let fail and harder to repair. Their conditions vary sharply. Competent automation keeps some immaculate. Others survive through caretaker communities whose duties pass between generations, or through mutual aid. Some depend on proprietary neural protocols whose owners have vanished, while automated legal entities continue billing governments that no longer function.

Most of the horror is experienced outside the cradles. Inside any functioning one, a person may be having the best day of their life.

Repositories caught in the Rupture enter Elysium along with the infrastructure keeping their inhabitants alive. These immense populations are usually Aetherically quiet, for the reasons described in [[Wireheading]]. A rare cradle that is not quiet poses a different problem.

### Contraction

Across Sol, resource exhaustion makes overdue maintenance impossible to conceal. Habitats do not all fail together. Poorly provisioned Blooms are abandoned, become ghost markets, or are militarized, while better-supported systems continue operating.

[[Aeronautics Unlimited]] prolongs expansion beyond what most powers can manage through ruthless logistics. [[Ewan Hart]] tries to preserve agricultural autonomy wherever it can. The [[Pirate Coalition]], uplift polities, and insurgent networks survive in the gaps left by shrinking formal systems.

The [[Pan-Solar Consortium]] displays more enforcement capacity than ever during this period, even as its actual authority declines. It can still impose sanctions, block passage, and mediate between powers that depend on markets. Those powers still have reasons to obey it. But arbitration and enforcement cannot provide the resources and maintainable infrastructure that the market system increasingly lacks.

Warfare changes as ships become better at managing the heat that would reveal them. Improved thermal materials, tighter circulation stacks, and tactics that delay heat emissions let a ship remain concealed for longer. Concealment still has a limit: retained heat must eventually be dumped.

The warning of the [[Cold Wake Panic]] becomes military doctrine. Running silent means accepting a future need to release heat, and enough forces now manage that obligation competently to change how encounters unfold. Detecting the other vessel first begins to matter more than many formal distinctions between a scout, a decoy, and a munition.

### The Escape Project

[[Quantum Dynamics]] and its allies argue that Sol has no viable strategic future except departure. Their faster-than-light program combines several kinds of expertise and power: cetacean cognition for understanding routes, Cognitum-conditioned firmware for nonlocal reasoning, Cryonix materials, and Dominion’s capacity to organize state action. Elite investors supply capital because they would rather risk cosmic exile than negotiate a diminished place within Sol.

By the 3010s, the FTL program has become less a common undertaking for the species than a final alliance of exhausted powers. Its participants no longer believe the solar system can be repaired politically.

The breakthrough that opens the way to Elysium is Sol’s last and greatest technological achievement. It comes from desperation: a civilization seeking escape after failing to live with what it has created.
