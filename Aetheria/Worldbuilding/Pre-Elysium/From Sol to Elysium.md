### From Sol to Elysium

Humanity reached Elysium while trying to escape a crisis in its home solar system. An experimental faster-than-light drive triggered a response that displaced humanity into a sealed spacetime domain. Elysium became its confinement, not the destination its explorers had intended.

For a thousand years after leaving Earth, human societies had fought over who controlled routes for extracting resources, who held authority over servers, and how workers were governed. Technologies that could alter identity made identity itself a matter of conflict. So did the question of which minds counted as persons. Every major bloc claimed to represent civilization; most sought control over the conditions everyone else would need to keep civilization alive. Settlement beyond Earth carried these conflicts into environments where survival was harder.

By the 31st century, resources were running out, social and technical systems were becoming unmanageably complex, and institutions were losing legitimacy. [[Wireheading]]—the capture of a mind's reward mechanisms—converged with abuse of uploaded minds, insurgency by uplifted beings, and governance through algorithms. Together, these pressures drove Sol into a worsening crisis. The backers of the experimental drive hoped technology could offer a way out of a political failure: if they could no longer stabilize Sol, perhaps they could leave it.

When activated, the drive interacted with structures older than human history whose reach was not confined to a single location. This triggered a defensive response from a civilization that had already decided it would not tolerate newcomers capable of faster-than-light travel. Humanity was displaced into Elysium, as was every other civilization that reached the same threshold.

Elysium had been built by frightened empires to contain future rivals they never intended to meet again. Humanity chose the name in hope and kept it through bitter inertia. It was an imposed afterlife rather than a paradise. The fleets, technology, and institutions that arrived there brought Sol's unresolved conflicts with them.
