# Crossbar Nights

On match nights in Ravel Bloom, people gather around a raised wooden cross to watch two runners decide who will blink first. Each runner must cross the middle, round the opposite end, and return to release a teammate. The middle is only wide enough for one. A good runner can make an opponent hesitate without ever touching them; a great one can yield, take the longer route underneath, and still arrive first. Spectators remember those recoveries for years. They also remember a boast made immediately before somebody lands sitting down.

Ravel is a yard settlement in the Ceres logistics region, in the early thirtieth century, under [[Aeronautics Unlimited]]. Its households live by ship repair, freight handling, fabrication, and the shops serving those trades. AU controls the docks and major maintenance spending; residents organize much of their own recreation. The local crossbar league rents its hall from the yard, which contributes lighting and prizes in exchange for its name above the score. Many supporters like the sponsor. They would still like their own team to beat the administration staff.

## The Race Through The Middle

The court stands on the inhabited inner surface of a rotating [[Bloom]], where apparent gravity holds runners to the floor. It turns with the neighbourhood; players never cross into a differently rotating structure. The hall occupies the broad civic interior made possible by [[Bloom Habitat Anatomy|Bloom construction]], well away from docking transfers and pressure machinery.

Four padded arms rise a little above head height over a sprung floor. Side rails stop short of the middle, leaving room to drop onto the handholds beneath it. Two teams of three start on adjacent arms, so their routes cross at right angles. Each runner goes out and back before tagging the next. At the junction, the first foot planted on the marked central square takes priority. The other runner must wait or swing beneath the crossing on handholds. Contact earns a time penalty; a fall stops both teams while the fallen runner is checked. Winning requires six clean crossings as well as speed.

The lower route gives the sport its peculiar beauty. Runners disappear beneath their opponents, travel hand over hand, and vault back onto the far arm. Strong shoulders help, but so does recognizing a lost approach early enough to keep moving. Children race on floor markings before they are allowed onto the raised court. Adults can stay with low practice frames, race against a clock, or become very knowledgeable people who never leave the benches.

Ravel's two largest followings belong to Red Steps and Lantern End, residential blocks whose teams recruit neighbours across workplaces. Red Steps spectators slap the benches twice before a crossing; Lantern End answers with one long stamping roll. Partners sometimes sit apart for the final. The repair shops sell replacement grips, supporters pay small subscriptions, and the crowded hall gives food stalls dependable trade. A season is worth keeping even for someone who never wins anything.

## The Late Step

Lantern End's runner Iven has learned to cut diagonally through that gap with a sideways vault, planting a foot on the central square before straightening. It is legal. It also postpones the moment when an opponent can tell whether Iven will arrive first. His supporters love the sudden unfolding of a body where there seemed to be empty air.

Red Steps captain Mara tried to beat that entry instead of dropping underneath. Their collision broke her wrist. She wants the return match played with the existing court: she has worked out how to read his takeoff and resents being made the reason everyone must slow down. Her substitute wants the approach rails extended so the sideways entry becomes physically impossible. He will be the one meeting Iven while Mara's wrist heals.

Iven has offered to abandon the move for the final. Other Lantern runners refuse to promise the same. They spent weeks learning it, and the championship cannot depend on one celebrity's courtesy. Meanwhile, supporters have begun booing early retreats to the lower route. That changes what a runner risks socially just before they must make a fast bodily decision.

## Unresolved Hooks

- The final is three nights away. Ravel's hall crew can extend the rails before then, but both teams must practise a changed approach or compete on the disputed court.
- Mara posts a demonstration of her counter-move. Her substitute tries it alone after practice and asks the hall attendant to keep the recording private.
- AU offers a larger inter-yard tournament using Ravel's rules. Lantern End wants the sideways vault in the demonstration; Red Steps has already promised its supporters a rematch.
