*"If the truth cannot travel, neither can justice."*

The [[Activist Network]] helps independent movements move evidence, witnesses, requests for help, and material aid across jurisdictions designed to keep them isolated. Its members share transport arrangements, archives, and ways to check whom and what they can trust. Each movement keeps its own politics, membership, and decisions about the people in its care. There is no central command: the Network cannot order a strike, decide who counts as a person, or force a sanctuary to take someone in.

An organizer with evidence may have nowhere safe to store it. A witness may reach a clinic but still lack legal standing. A labor cell may know a route out without knowing whether the habitat at the other end can support another household. Different struggles repeatedly needed the same practical help. The Network endured because shared procedures let these groups arrange transfers and negotiate responsibility without merging into one organization.

## Routes and Trust

Participating services, called nodes, check documents for authenticity and keep copies in several places. They match couriers to routes, arrange to receive witnesses, and connect emergency evacuations with clinics, lawyers, or sanctuaries. When a courier, archive, credential, or address is compromised, they warn their partners.

Each node controls only its part of the work, but its decisions can determine whether help gets through. A verification node can refuse to vouch for altered evidence. A dispatcher can close a route after it has been exposed. A clinic or sanctuary can refuse a transfer it cannot safely receive. These powers govern access to the Network's services; they give nodes no authority over an applicant's politics or status as a person.

The movements using those services retain control of their own work:

- The [[Free Upload Network]], which moves digital persons out of corporate ownership, may request backup copies and transport for the computing hardware that supports them while retaining custody of the people it moves.
- The [[Awakened Labor Front]], which organizes around the rights and resistance of engineered workers, may use Network couriers while keeping control of its strike strategy.
- [[Gene Commons]], which campaigns for shared genetic knowledge and access to therapies, has clinics that may accept referrals while refusing to work with partners whose methods endanger patients.

Two events show why these connections matter. During the [[Upload Vivisection Expose]], which exposed industrial torture of digital persons, testimony, memory fragments, and technical logs had to survive hostile institutions long enough to corroborate one another. Checking and preserving evidence across multiple locations helped meet that need.

The [[Pallas Species Strikes]] posed a different problem: workers needed to communicate across the categories employers used to separate them—products, uplifts, and ordinary labor. Neither event belonged to the Network. Both created practical problems that its routing methods were built to address.

## Work and Failure

Archivists check where records came from and maintain copies. Couriers carry records, people, medicine, and keys. Intake workers assess what someone needs without promising resources controlled by another group. Dispatchers weigh the risk of exposure, travel time, and the receiving site's capacity. Specialists take part through their own institutions or temporary agreements.

Workers receive only part of the information involved in an operation. This limits what any one person can reveal under pressure, but it also makes failures difficult to investigate. A courier may never learn why a route closed. An intake worker may hear traumatic testimony and never find out whether the witness arrived. Several people can each make a defensible decision within their own responsibilities and still produce a failed transfer, with no office able to explain the whole loss.

Experience can also become power. Long-serving verifiers and dispatchers may become gatekeepers whose doubts determine which claims travel. Their caution has consequences in both directions: delay can strand a witness, while haste can spread forged evidence or expose every receiving node. Infiltration gives workers reasons to keep secrets. Those same secrets make abuse harder to challenge.
