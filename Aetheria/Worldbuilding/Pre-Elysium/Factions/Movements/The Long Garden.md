# The Long Garden

At Triton, some uploaded residents remember their dead by growing places nobody remembers. They build a shared simulated garden, plant something for a lost friend, and invite others to change it. A daughter may return to find her father's carefully modeled pear tree surrounded by translucent reeds he would have considered tasteless. She can complain, help prune them, or come to like the sound they make.

The Long Garden takes shape among a small circle of digital residents and embodied neighbours at the Triton waystation complex in the first decade after the [[Triton Sanctuary Accords]] of 2994. Several founders belong to the [[Emancipated]]. They have preserved testimony about captivity; here they want somewhere to spend an afternoon without giving it. Residents support the garden through contributions from station maintenance, translation, and interface work, with occasional gifts from visiting crews. It occupies an ordinary shared simulation host.

## Keeping an Appointment

[[Mind Uploading]] lets people inhabit simulations, but their available computation affects how quickly they experience time and what they can perceive. Friends may accumulate very different amounts of lived experience between meetings. Someone returning after a suspension can find that another gardener has spent subjective years missing them.

The garden's seasons follow the station calendar. Its operators advance the landscape at that agreed pace regardless of how quickly a visitor thinks. Fast-running residents can design a whole hillside between visits; they cannot privately hurry its shared spring. People visit at different times, leaving spoken messages and proposed plantings. Occasional gatherings have long arrival windows. Slowing a participant's mind is never the price of entry.

This modest common calendar gives friends something they can still anticipate together. It also disappoints them. A blossom can open while somebody is unavailable. Gardeners usually preserve a recording and leave its next year's buds alone. Some keep private versions in perpetual flower. Others find those rooms unbearable.

The plants are designed scenery with simple growth rules. A memorial planting may begin with a remembered colour, a tune, or the shape of a tool. Nobody needs to supply a mind-state of the dead. Gardeners can translate a planting into sound or a simplified visual view, although each version loses something. The [[Cetacean Navigators]] already accommodate multiple sensory interfaces in station life; local gardeners borrow that practical habit. A beautiful acoustic grove earns admiration on its own terms, rather than as an inferior picture.

## What They Keep Sacred

For the circle's religious members, the dead remain guests of an unfinished creation. They believe making something genuinely new in a loved person's presence is a form of hospitality, even when that presence can only be hoped for. At a planting, each mourner describes something they wish the dead could encounter: a grandchild, a repaired friendship, an appalling new instrument. Then they leave room for another person's addition.

No apparition or reply establishes that belief. Secular gardeners attend because they want their memories to meet other people's futures. Some faithful members pray elsewhere and regard gardening as ordinary recreation. Participation says little about what anyone believes happens after death.

Children from neighbouring households visit through available station interfaces. They learn to ask before altering a memorial, describe a flower to someone who cannot see it, and admit when they have forgotten a story. Skilled gardeners are admired for making another person's addition look welcome. Famous designers still occasionally produce a magnificent clearing in which nobody else can fit anything.

The serious quarrel concerns a planting's future. One household wants an exact memorial kept intact; another feels that asking descendants to preserve every choice makes remembrance into unpaid curatorship. Some beds remain carefully conserved. Others pass between friends who openly redesign them. Families negotiate these intentions while planting, but affection changes, and the person who once welcomed alteration may later dread losing the last familiar view.

## Unresolved Hooks

- A gardener returning from suspension finds that friends have lovingly completed the grove they expected to spend years making together. They ask for part of it to be made unfinished again.
- Two diverged uploads remember the same biological mother. One wants to conserve her tree; the other wants room beneath it for a lover their mother never accepted.
- A celebrated acoustic designer loses access to the interface on which their work was composed. Their neighbours can preserve the piece or change it into something its maker can experience again.
