# Second Visit Association

The Second Visit Association helps people scattered along Triton's sanctuary routes see one another again. In the early 3010s, its members save for passages, prepare guest rooms, and arrange gatherings that can take months to assemble. Someone who once arrived frightened and carrying very little can return with a partner, a performance, or an excessively ambitious lunch. Being expected is part of the attraction.

The association grew among former sanctuary travellers and their hosts after the 2994 [[Triton Sanctuary Accords]]. Its strongest circles are in the Triton waystation complex and neighbouring Neptunian waystations. It includes bodily residents, uploaded people, crew families, and people born locally who have never needed to flee. Membership follows friendships and shared work; it claims no common ancestry and speaks for only part of the sanctuary population.

## Coming Back

Members call their gatherings visits even when most guests live nearby. A visit combines meals, games, performances, and news from people who cannot travel. Recorded contributions arrive ahead of time; replies leave afterward. Nobody expects distant relatives to share one convenient evening across the outer system.

The favourite performance is a paired account of an earlier meeting. Host and guest each tell it, and the audience discovers how much hospitality depended on mutual misunderstanding. People can decline to tell an escape story. A disastrous courtship or a misunderstood recipe supplies ample material. Children learn to introduce absent contributors, adapt a game to another person's senses, and ask before replaying a recording.

At stations of the [[Cetacean Navigators]], gatherings use the existing meeting points between wet-environment channels and dry galleries. Members pay for translation and accessible controls as readily as ingredients. Uploaded guests participate through locally supported interfaces or send recordings; an invitation does not make hosting capacity free or settle the questions surrounding [[Mind Uploading]]. A distant voice is welcome without being described as physically present.

Some members spend years saving for one journey. Others become dependable local hosts, musicians, technicians, or correspondents. An admired host remembers what someone enjoys now, rather than insisting on the person they were when rescued. Elders sometimes find that standard unreasonably demanding of their favourite anecdotes.

## A Constituency For Visits

Small dues, benefit performances, and shared passenger purchases sustain the circles. Hosting households contribute space when they can. Volunteers group journeys around available sailings, and residents who cannot travel still gain company and performances. The association persists because it gives scattered relationships recurring occasions to grow, including friendships formed after displacement.

Its political ambition is to make ordinary visiting affordable. Member residents and dock workers back Waystation Council delegates who favour usable guest space and modest passenger provision alongside freight. Circles can pool bookings or move a future gathering to another willing station, giving councils and carriers a reason to listen. They cannot order berths released or overrule rescue duties.

Local [[Emancipated]] participants value occasions where escaped uploads can be funny, tedious, or in love without continually serving as witnesses. Some also want the gatherings to carry testimony to new audiences. The association's friendships with [[Free Upload Network]] volunteers are more strained: rescue organisers can see another use for almost every contribution to a reunion. They also worry that public guest lists could expose protected relationships. Organisers therefore keep invitations private where guests require it; they do not advertise clandestine routes.

The central disagreement is between members who want a permanent gathering house on Triton and those who want the money kept available for dispersed visits. A house would support rehearsals, accessible equipment, and a dependable place to grow old among friends. Smaller stations fear becoming distant suppliers of dues to a capital of nostalgia. Rescue-first members would put much of either budget into helping the next arrivals. Others answer by describing the marriages and friendships that another postponement will cost. Families divide over this without necessarily leaving the association.

## Open Hooks

- A Triton circle can afford the lease on a gathering house if three smaller circles surrender their passage savings. One has already promised a reunion to an elderly host.
- An Emancipated performer wants to tour a comedy built from captivity memories. Their former companions disagree about whether recognisable shared experiences belong onstage.
- A rescue diversion strands a long-awaited gathering's guests at two different stations. Local organisers propose holding both gatherings and exchanging recordings, while passengers press for money back to finance another attempt.
