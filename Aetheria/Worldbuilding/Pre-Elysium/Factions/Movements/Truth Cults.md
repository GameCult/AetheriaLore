*"A glitch is what the lie looks like from the inside."*

The [[Truth Cults]] are revelationist anti-propaganda sects born from a civilization saturated with simulation, spin, and narrative optimization. They treat glitches, leaks, corrupted broadcasts, and testimonial fragments as sacred opportunities to pierce managed reality.

Some cells are disciplined investigative networks. Others are half-mystical communities built around the emotional shock of seeing through a lie. What unites them is the conviction that truth in late Sol survives first as contraband.

## Authored Elaboration: The Uncut Minute (2026-09-04)

The Uncut Minute is a verification rite used by some disciplined cells when they obtain conflicting versions of the same event. The cell rents a media-reclamation booth or assembles an illegal equivalent, places a public cut, an independently held copy, and testimony or machine telemetry on separate displays, then disables interpolation and automated summary for sixty seconds. Missing material stays black or silent. A locally hosted machine mind may participate as a witness rather than being treated as a playback appliance. The rite is called *uncut* because no one repairs the gaps during the minute, not because the surviving material is presumed pure.

After playback, participants speak in three rounds: what was seen, what was inferred, and what is owed. The first round admits only observable features. The second holds competing explanations open. The third decides material obligations such as duplicating the record, sheltering a contributor, paying another recovery fee, or leaving the claim unresolved. The booth's access receipt becomes part of the custody packet. Revelation arrives with an invoice, and the invoice is evidence of who could afford to look.

The practice depends on the infrastructure it distrusts. Cells need [[Lucent Media]] archives, rights-clearance and moderation workers willing to preserve discarded material, pirate relays, maintenance logs, machine indexers, and holders far enough apart that one seizure cannot erase every copy. Whistleblowers and counter-propaganda networks in turn depend on disciplined cells to retain provenance, distribute risk, and distinguish a usable contradiction from a compelling rumor. Costs fall unevenly: archive access consumes Buzz or money, absence from the feed erodes Clout, retrieval exposes worker credentials, and redundant custody consumes scarce storage and trust.

The rite breaks when charisma collapses inference into observation. [[Reality Architects]] can seed theatrical corruption, while [[Chaos Weavers]] can flood channels with useful static that also destroys source independence. Disciplined cells suspend revelation until custody lines can be compared; less disciplined sects let a vivid fragment become a speaking relic. Even at its best, the Uncut Minute does not prove who a copied voice belongs to or why two records disagree. Its numinous force is narrower: for one paid minute, people refuse the system's demand that every absence be smoothed into a marketable answer, and decide what they owe one another while the gap remains open.
