*"Tomorrow arrives on the ships we finance today."*

[[Horizon Ventures]] was one of the first firms to realize that launch capacity, fuel refining, debt contracts, and habitat provisioning were all parts of the same sovereign machine. It was founded in 2041 by plasma physicist Dr. Mara Varga and logistics economist Kenji Sato after their Budapest fusion work on compact helium-3 confinement made the first practical [[Soliton Drive]] prototypes credible to investors.

Horizon sold the [[Corporate Exodus]] as frontier opportunity while building the debt architecture that trapped entire generations of workers off-world. Its expansion pace made it indispensable, then brittle. When shipping insurers and lunar creditors closed in after the 2091 convoy failures, Horizon accepted merger with [[TerraCore Industries]], creating [[SolEx]] and burying most of its founding idealism under extraction accounting.

### Authored elaboration: The Mare Crisium Continuity Default

The 2091 failures became immediate at **Mare Crisium Receiving Cylinder**, a lunar transfer habitat whose repair shops, freight crews, and Ewan Hart food loop served three Horizon mining routes. Missed machinery and nutrient cargoes did not make the cylinder uninhabitable at once. They pushed its replacement filters, water-loop membranes, berth propellant, and payroll beyond the period covered by Horizon's insurers. The insurers suspended rescue and replacement coverage; lunar creditors then refused to finance another cargo until Horizon reduced the habitat's supported population and pledged later ore receipts.

Horizon called the resulting procedure a **continuity default**. Its audit sorted residents into continuity labor, financed transfer, and unsupported occupancy. Continuity labor kept air, water, power, docks, and cargo records operating. Financed transfer received one berth and a strict personal-mass allowance, with the fare added to the passenger's existing transport debt. Unsupported households could remain only by signing revised work and habitat contracts before the final departure manifest closed. No office ordered an eviction. The dock stopped accepting their credentials, the food ledger stopped recognizing their scrip, and their sleeping bay was scheduled to lose pressure when the cylinder contracted to its insured occupancy.

The exodus window lasted thirty-six hours because every departing body consumed berth propellant and every occupied compartment consumed filters the next convoy might not bring. The transfer gallery's inner pressure hatch had a worn lower dog: its panel reported sealed after the first closure stroke, but the seal seated only when a technician drove the handle through a second manual stroke. This defect turned the last manifest into a physical hazard. If the hatch opened late, the departure shuttle missed its insured launch slot; if the gallery crew trusted the panel, the cylinder could lose air while people queued between a closing dock and a de-funded home.

Residents kept one unsupported sleeping bay alive by pooling personal oxygen allotments, hand-copying the scrubber rotation, and taking unpaid seal watches after Horizon removed the bay from the maintenance schedule. Nine people used the extra day to reach an independent cislunar ferry without signing the revised contracts. The act did not save the cylinder or cancel anyone's debt. It proved that the habitat's residents could briefly make survival depend on one another instead of the ledger, and it taught Horizon's creditors to demand household-level air and maintenance audits in later defaults.

Mare Crisium preserved the asset and damaged the company. Horizon still needed the workers it had made transferable, the creditors inherited a habitat whose income depended on delayed ore, and the insurers now priced every fast-grown settlement as a coupled transport and life-support risk. TerraCore's attraction was not merely its mines. It brought the audit practice and closed-loop administration that could turn a convoy failure into an orderly contraction. Four years later, that bargain became [[SolEx]].
