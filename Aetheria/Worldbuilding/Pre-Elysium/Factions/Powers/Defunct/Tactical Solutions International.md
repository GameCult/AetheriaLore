*"Reassurance scales better than force."*

Before it became [[Framgång]], the company was [[Tactical Solutions International]], a middling security and resilience contractor that sold premiumized calm to executives and crowd-control packages to employers. Its founder Viktor Lindgren learned early that frightened customers would pay more for emotional vocabulary than for honest material guarantees.

The rebrand did not mark a moral conversion. It marked a better margin. Tactical Solutions discovered that spiritual language, biometric coaching, and aspirational lifestyle goods could do much of the political work that brute security once handled more visibly. Late [[Framgång]] remained a coercive apparatus. It simply learned to smile while billing for the privilege.

### Authored elaboration — the Continuity Assurance Rider

_Tangle pass, 2026-09-04._

After the 2091 convoy failures, [[Horizon Ventures]] hired Tactical Solutions at Transfer Wheel Six, an Earth–Luna L1 freight habitat whose maintenance crews were rationing filter mesh while Horizon negotiated with shipping insurers and lunar creditors. TSI attached a **Continuity Assurance Rider** to the security contract. Horizon received a temporary insurance discount and kept bonded resupply departures moving so long as TSI classified each shift as behaviorally stable enough for safe operation.

The rider made TSI useful to parties that did not trust one another. Horizon needed an outside finding to reassure creditors without admitting that delayed parts and debt discipline had made the habitat unsafe. Insurers needed someone on site to translate interviews, incident reports, wearable stress traces, and security footage into a priceable category. TSI needed worker participation and access to Horizon's operational records; a calm score built from empty rooms would not survive a claim review.

Its leverage sat in the records clause. TSI retained the raw session archive and scoring method while clients received summaries. If Horizon changed contractors, or if workers withdrew access to the archive, the affected shifts became **unverified continuity intervals** that insurers could reprice until a new review reconstructed them. Horizon therefore could not dismiss TSI without threatening the same coverage the contractor had helped secure. Workers could refuse interviews on paper, but refusal could suspend clearance for safety-critical shifts and weaken the bond supporting the next resupply convoy. Their data was not merely harvested; it was held where every route out passed through somebody else's risk model.

The first serious dispute began when maintenance delegates refused an overloaded docking rotation and Horizon asked TSI to classify the stoppage as collective panic rather than a labor action. That classification would preserve Horizon's covenant and let security remove the delegates as impaired personnel. It also risked TSI's standing with the insurers if the maintenance logs proved the crews had identified a real seal fault. The delegates' counter-leverage was mundane: duplicate work orders, meal rosters that established who was awake when, and private check-ins kept outside TSI's archive. Shared food and accurate records did not free the wheel. They gave frightened workers enough unowned truth to contest one diagnosis together.

The arrangement foreshadowed Framgång's mature alignment market. Useful breathing drills, peer support, and fatigue checks were bundled with categories that employers and insurers could act on; continued help required continued measurement; and contract renewal preserved the vendor's authority to interpret distress. What broke the model was not refusal alone but credible alternate evidence. Once a crew could prove that the dangerous condition was mechanical and contractual rather than personal, reassurance stopped scaling cleanly.
