[[Aeronautics Unlimited]] (AU) builds and operates shipyards, habitats, extraction sites, salvage projects, and the transport networks that supply them. It finances new places to live and work, brings in the equipment and supplies needed to occupy them, and turns unfinished construction into operating projects that can issue claims and collect revenue.

For people living in an AU settlement, the company also decides whether their home is worth further investment. Its administrators control repairs, docks, and outside supplies. Their judgment that a project should keep growing—or be treated as a loss—can determine whether residents face another production cycle or evacuation.

### Living and Working on a Ramp

AU calls a developing yard, habitat, route, or settlement a **ramp**. Each has a Ramp Administration: a temporary authority meant to take the project from an unfinished shell to reliable operation. It assigns berths and shifts, releases maintenance budgets, approves safety exceptions, coordinates contractors, and records claims against future production.

Residents may elect councils or organize their own work crews. Those bodies still need docks, replacement machinery, and outside freight to carry out their decisions. The Ramp Administration controls access to these, giving it leverage over local government even where residents formally govern themselves.

The administration's temporary role rarely has a clean endpoint. When a ramp succeeds, AU has reason to keep its operating rights. When one fails, administrators review which districts will receive repairs, which contracts can move elsewhere, and whether evacuation would cost less than another production cycle.

Workers also build up a stake in keeping the project open. **Claimshares** are labor claims tied to a particular project and recorded through its ramp systems. They affect access to resources, upgrades, and timekeeping points within that project. Their practical value depends on the ramp continuing to be recognized, supplied, and open.

A household that stays can preserve its access and accumulated standing, but must also live with deferred maintenance and the risks of another cycle. Senior engineers and coordinators can spread their interests across several projects or negotiate credentials they can use elsewhere. Many crews have no such options.

### Different Contracts, Shared Workplaces

AU brings baseline crews, uplifts, biodrones, independent engineers, and supplier personnel into the same pressure walls and maintenance schedules under different contracts. Those distinctions let management treat the same refusal differently depending on who refuses: labor indiscipline for one worker, product failure or adaptation trouble for another.

The [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Timeline/Events/Pallas Species Strikes|Pallas Species Strikes]] became a serious threat to management when workers began coordinating across those categories. They organized around technical chokepoints where management could not easily replace them. Separate contracts had not made their work independent of one another.

AU keeps projects, suppliers, and labor categories partly separate, bringing them together when coordination becomes profitable. This gives the company flexibility while leaving those below it exposed to failure. Successful expansion lets AU consolidate operating rights and revenue from claims. If a project fails, administrators can abandon it and describe the workers, contractors, and local councils left behind as independent claimants.

### How AU Built Its Position

AU formed from aerospace, orbital-fabrication, and habitat firms left outside the merger that created [[SolEx]]. Both groups inherited the transport economy made possible by the [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Technology/Soliton Drive|Soliton Drive]]. SolEx concentrated extraction under its administration and favored audited routes. AU sent construction shells, equipment, and repair capacity ahead of settled administration, accepting more failures in return for the chance to establish claims earlier.

[[Orbital Forge]] became a major independent supplier. Forge develops cavity-building and salvage methods through standards that other builders can adapt; AU provides capital, large yards, transport, and customers able to use those methods at scale. They need each other and disagree over what should remain under their control. AU wants stable designs that can be financed and insured. Forge benefits when local builders can change techniques without asking permission.

AU became a major deployer of [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Technology/Bloom|Blooms]], large rotating habitats manufactured from asteroid material. It combined Forge's open cavity standards with financing, logistics, and control of operations. Its attempt to keep construction open enough for rapid expansion while retaining profitable authority over deployment helped produce the [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Timeline/Events/Bloom Expansion Conflicts|Bloom Expansion Conflicts]].

The AU/Forge sphere also lost a narrow but valuable Ceres–Pallas thermal-fabrication corridor in the [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Timeline/Events/Cinderlace Licensing War|Cinderlace Licensing War]].

### From Construction Management to Government

The [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Timeline/Events/Ceres Control War|Ceres Control War]] established ramp administration as a form of political authority. AU prevailed because many Belt workers and builders preferred its looser project system to SolEx consolidation.

That support did not make ramps democratic. It gave AU a basis for claiming the right to govern: its system allowed rapid development and more local variation. Residents could value those freedoms while remaining dependent on AU's decisions about supplies, operating rights, and whether their settlement would continue.
