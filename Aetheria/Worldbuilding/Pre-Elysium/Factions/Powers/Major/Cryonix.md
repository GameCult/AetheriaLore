# Cryonix

[[Cryonix]] makes high-performance materials that help reactors, ships, computers, and neural systems operate within their heat and material limits. It also governs a compact set of industrial enclaves. Its customers pay for greater cooling capacity, less conspicuous emissions, and reliable operation under demanding conditions. Access depends on Cryonix's prices, production schedules, licenses, and standards.

The company’s workers experience that control differently. Process engineers, lattice workers, contamination crews, inspectors, maintenance specialists, and refractory laborers keep fabrication environments stable enough to produce these materials. They handle solvents, heat, rejected batches, and contamination controls. When strategic contracts take priority, workers face delays and interrupted access while protected customers retain reliable service.

### What Cryonix Sells

Cryonix has three connected businesses: superconducting materials, surfaces that reject heat or control its emissions, and licensed fabrication under clean conditions. Certification ties the businesses together. Buying a high-performance surface also means securing qualified manufacture, inspection, repair access, and records that insurers or procurement offices will accept.

Its superconducting materials allow systems to sustain strong magnetic fields and fit demanding equipment into smaller spaces. Its **extraordinary emitters** improve heat rejection, while **signature-managed surfaces** help shape the emissions through which a system can be detected. Neither removes the need to dispose of waste heat. [[Advanced Superconductors]] and [[Thermal Management]] describe the wider systems these materials serve.

Cryonix supplies an important part of those systems without owning the reactor, drive, computer, or implant around it. [[Rossum & Douglas]] supplies much of the circulation, storage, switching, and failure certification that insurers require for the stages before heat reaches a premium surface. Better surface materials cannot replace that work.

Customers include NiteLife grids, elite clinics, upload sites, military fleets, and compute operators. They may disagree about government while all wanting compact, quiet systems that survive demanding use. Cryonix can attach political conditions to access without governing their wider territories: a narrow materials contract can determine which fabrication slot, license, or recognized standard a client can obtain.

### How a Supplier Acquired Territory

Founded in 2055, Cryonix initially supplied advanced superconducting materials for [[Fusion]] systems and the [[Soliton Drive]]. Reactor and propulsion firms owned the machines. Cryonix sold coils, lattice materials, and the precise fabrication needed to run those machines closer to their failure limits.

Demand grew because dense computation, compact power, neural infrastructure, and fast ships all produce heat. Cryonix controlled enough scarce materials and clean fabrication capacity to gain leverage, even though it never controlled every way of managing that heat.

During the 2241–2248 [[Cold Wake Panic]], insurers and corridor authorities began treating how a system emitted heat as a security question. Cryonix's signature-managed surfaces became strategic infrastructure rather than merely premium industrial equipment. Materials that could shape emissions gained value alongside those that could withstand demanding conditions.

In 2351, research by Sadaf Nouri and Tomas Vale at Ceres Polytechnic began the work that became the [[Cinderlace Cooperative]]. Its extraordinary emitters could be manufactured for practical use. They threatened to make premium heat rejection a Belt-governed industrial standard. The 2364–2368 [[Cinderlace Licensing War]] determined who could turn that research into production that financiers and insurers would support.

Cryonix absorbed Cinderlace's manufacturing arm and secured standards acceptable to insurers through PSC panels. The settlement also gave it control of far-side lunar materials-clinic districts, the Tycho thermal works, and a narrow Ceres–Pallas fabrication corridor previously contested within the [[Aeronautics Unlimited|AU]] and [[Orbital Forge]] sphere. Cryonix now governed territory containing the industrial processes its contracts depended on.

### Enclaves, Workers, and Alternatives

Cryonix's territory consists of the far-side lunar materials-clinic arc, the Tycho works and related fabrication enclaves, and the Ceres–Pallas corridor's weave yards, refractory plants, and emitter production. Its sovereignty centers on valuable processes rather than a large population or broad territory. Finch retains much of the neighboring embodiment and continuity economy; Aeronautics Unlimited and Orbital Forge remain dominant across most of the Belt.

Within Cryonix, status determines how much protection someone receives from failure and delay. Senior engineers and protected clients get redundant cooling, clean processing time, rapid maintenance, and room to absorb expensive failures. Contract and line workers bear more exposure and waiting to keep that service reliable. The company allocates its safest conditions according to how much institutional value it places on the people and contracts involved.

Cryonix's control has limits. Orbital Forge yards, displaced Cinderlace engineers, and gray markets continue to supply alternative emitters and improvised thermal stacks. Cryonix commands a premium segment recognized by insurers, but it cannot prevent others from building thermal systems outside it.

By 3025, Cryonix materials were a necessary layer of the Mars assembly described in [[FTL Trigger]]. Cryonix did not design the field geometry, power the grid, or cause the shunt.
