The [[Pirate Coalition]] is a loose network of crews and hidden bases that moves people and goods outside official control in late Sol. Its members include raiders, smugglers, mutinous crews, liberated uploads, shipminds, saboteurs, refugee ferrymen, and black-market carriers. They operate where the laws governing travel and transport fail, or where people find those laws intolerable.

There is no central command or shared political program. A crew carrying fugitives, strike funds, or banned medicine may rely on the same routes and contacts as a salvage syndicate or a predatory raider band. Some members keep people alive beyond corporate jurisdiction. Others claim to defend autonomy while treating isolated settlements, passengers, and weaker crews as goods to exploit. Moving secretly requires contacts; it does not require agreement about what freedom means or who deserves it.

### Living and Working Outside Official Control

The Coalition depends on maintenance crews, brokers, pilots, caregivers, loaders, fabricators, and people who keep air and heat moving through unofficial habitats. Their households face interrupted supplies, sudden moves, dangerous repairs, and irregular income. A captain may profit from a feud while dockside families face the retaliation before their next food shipment arrives.

Who controls a settlement makes a considerable difference. Some enclaves share maintenance and defense as disciplined commons. Others depend on the favor of captains, creditors, armed crews, or shipminds. No universal pirate law protects workers across these arrangements. Leaving can cost someone their housing, passage, accumulated trust, or access to the only repair yard willing to overlook a damaged identity record.

### Routes and Territory

Coalition bases are scattered rather than joined into a continuous territory. They include yards in the shadows of planetary rings, hidden docks, patched cavities in manufactured [[Bloom]] habitats, Trojan nests, falsified rescue stations, operations that attach themselves to convoys, and temporary storage habitats. A base may be abandoned once its traffic patterns become easy to recognize.

An introduction can help a visitor gain access, as can useful cargo or enough bargaining power. Hosts also consider what earlier visitors brought: payment, danger, or recovery teams following behind them. Keeping a route usable depends on these local judgments.

The networks carry contraband medicine, firmware, weapons, identity papers, fugitives, uploads, uplifted refugees, stolen goods, captured industrial components, illicit computing capacity, salvage, and intelligence about routes. They also transport cargo for institutions that publicly condemn piracy but want to deny involvement in a shipment. Tighter route controls after conflicts such as the [[Lagrange Broadcast War]] and [[Food Security Wars]] increased demand for ways around enforcement. That demand did not make every smuggler or convoy raider a Coalition member.

Local cells cooperate with independent networks, including the [[Corvid Collective]], [[Cephalopod Syndicate]], [[Free Upload Network]], and [[Ship Mutiny]] cultures. These relationships may supply infiltration, technical labor, refuge, or trusted contacts to receive people and cargo along a journey. The cooperating movements remain independent of pirate authority.

The Coalition also fought directly in the [[Ice Giant Navigation Wars]]. Its toll systems and raids helped undermine the agreements through which the [[Cetacean Navigators]] maintained trust between those using the routes. Pirates could keep passage open outside recognized governments while making it more dangerous for travelers who lacked the strength to fight or the resources to bargain.

### Reputation and Recourse

With little formal enforcement across the Coalition, crews partly depend on what others believe about their conduct. A witnessed rescue, reliable surrender terms, a fulfilled transport agreement, or restraint during capture can win access to docks and attract skilled crew members. Betrayal, abandoned passengers, false distress calls, and violence after surrender can cause others to close their routes to an offender.

These records are local, often disputed, and vulnerable to forgery. Reputation can limit what a crew gets away with, but the Coalition has no common court to establish what happened or enforce a remedy.

People harmed by pirates therefore have little assurance of recourse. A crew may return stolen medicine, honor evacuation terms, or spare a captured ship, yet leave the ship's owners, passengers, and workers with no recognized place to appeal its decisions. Concealment makes it harder to capture fugitives; it also makes it harder to hold extortionists and abusers accountable.

### Combat

Pirate cells choose methods according to what they can gain and what resources they have. They share no single fleet doctrine. When an intact ship or cargo is worth more than its wreckage, they may use ambush, spoofing, stolen credentials, intrusion into systems, boarding, pressure on a target's ability to manage heat, misdirection, or attacks that disrupt subsystems without destroying them.

They attack supply and transport operations, force ejection or surrender, withdraw from unfavorable fights, and salvage what remains. [[Combat Doctrine]] describes the equipment, defenses, and limits involved in these methods.

Together, these activities prevent official route law from controlling all movement through Sol. Escape and unauthorized transport remain possible, but those same routes are available to crews seeking people to rescue and crews seeking people to prey on.
