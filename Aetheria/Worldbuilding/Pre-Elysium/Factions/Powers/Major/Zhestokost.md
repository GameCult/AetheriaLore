[[Zhestokost]] is a Martian state organized for permanent military mobilization. It supplies residents with food, housing, repairs, and long-term planning, while command authorities decide where they work and live. Its claim to rule rests on its ability to keep settlements alive: the institution that coordinates oxygen, water, food, and armed response considers that competence a right to command.

### Living Under Command

For residents, the offer is credible. Rations arrive, housing is assigned, and repair crews attend damaged infrastructure. Agricultural and transport plans look beyond the next market shock. In a habitat where a missed shipment can kill people, these are substantial reasons to accept the state's authority.

The offices that provide this security also restrict people's choices. Assignment offices tie work, housing, schooling, leave, and permission for families to live together to a person's institutional standing. Two households can have the same entitlement on paper while one waits longer for a clinic appointment, lives farther from a reliable heating loop, or loses an appeal to keep relatives at the same posting. Senior officers, trusted technical staff, and the custodians who maintain uploaded minds receive better backup systems, medical care, and access to information than most industrial workers.

Residents use `Moneta`, the internal provision currency, for rations, clothing, housing charges, leave, and family needs. What a balance buys depends on the holder's assignment. It cannot pay for housing outside an authorized district or obtain supplies withheld from a unit under review. Residents can see the provision credited to them, but command still decides which goods and choices are available.

### How Contractors Became Rulers

Zhestokost grew out of the security businesses of the [[Corporate Exodus]]. Its founders protected convoys, broke strikes, staffed garrisons, and handled emergency logistics. This work taught them how dependent fragile settlements were on coordinated supplies and force—and how much power belonged to whoever provided both.

During the [[Terra Cimmeria Seizure]], security houses received temporary authority in response to a reactor failure, crop panic, and sabotage campaign. They took control of movement, rationing, and communications, then refused to return those powers to civilians. This gave their claim to command a territorial base. Later expansion brought garrisons, foundries, logistics offices, and ideological schools into the growing state.

Zhestokost treats ordinary politics as a dangerous interruption of campaign planning. It approaches scarcity, sabotage, labor disputes, and cultural differences as security problems. Negotiation is legitimate only within a command structure that can impose a decision. Officers, engineers, quartermasters, workers, and local commanders still disagree over priorities; the system reserves the authority to end those arguments for designated decision-makers.

### Who Runs the State

The **Eternal Council** consists of selected leaders and strategists preserved as uploaded minds. It maintains doctrine and long-range plans as biological administrations come and go. Its continuity depends on technicians, however: those who host the Council control its sensors, records, and operating time.

Other institutions turn campaign priorities into production, work assignments, and enforcement:

- **Arsenal commissions** coordinate foundry output, repair capacity, ammunition, and transport with military plans.
- **Ideological security offices** monitor organized dissent and investigate failures described as sabotage.
- **Training and assignment boards** direct education and adult labor toward military, engineering, maintenance, agriculture, and logistics needs.

These authorities overlap and obstruct one another. A quartermaster may protect reserve stock against a commander's demand for immediate deployment. A habitat administrator can conceal missed quotas long enough to preserve a crew.

Workers also find room to act. They trade favors, misuse official forms, joke about campaign language, and sometimes refuse orders despite the penalties. No command system can inspect every repair duct or private meal. Informal life continues within Zhestokost; the state protects its authority by making organized refusal costly.

### Industry and Armed Force

Zhestokost specializes in armor, heavy weapons, equipment for breaching defenses and conducting sieges, logistics built to withstand attack, and systems that can still be repaired after sustained damage. Its advantage comes from coordinating foundries, stockpiles, transport, and maintenance with military planning. No unique weapon supplies that advantage on its own.

Its [[Worldbuilding/Politics/Why Zhestokost’s Heavy-Weapons Specialization Works|heavy-weapons doctrine]] combines sustained fire with repairable platforms under the regulatory constraints described in [[Worldbuilding/Politics/Combat Doctrine|Combat Doctrine]]. Keeping equipment supplied and working is part of how it keeps force available.

### Frieden and the Right to Leave

Frieden offered deserters and dissidents a materially viable life outside militarized command. It was close enough to threaten Zhestokost by example: people could see a settlement meeting their needs without submitting to the state's authority.

Zhestokost destroyed it in the [[Frieden Colony Massacre]], describing the attack as an anti-insurgency operation. The settlement exposed the limit of the state's promise of dependable provision. That promise supported command while command appeared necessary to survival. Frieden challenged the necessity simply by remaining viable; it did not need to defeat Zhestokost in battle.
