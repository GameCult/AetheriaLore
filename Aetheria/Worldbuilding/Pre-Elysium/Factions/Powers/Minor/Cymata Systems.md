[[Cymata Systems]] was the best-known specialist in substrate-matched product cognition. It grew embodied AGI for prestige and companion vessels, industrial machinery, domestic interfaces, and logistics systems. Its customers wanted minds that experienced an assigned machine and social role as native conditions rather than external commands.

[[CogniSys]] remained more important to administrative and governance systems. [[Rossum & Douglas]] certified safer deployments and failure envelopes. [[Cognitum]] derived licensable cognition from upload-grade captures. Cymata instead developed new minds through product bodies and roles, then licensed the resulting lines to manufacturers and operators.

### Product Development

Cymata owned cognition growth, behavioral voicing, fit review, developmental records, and the licensed lines produced by that work. A buyer specified a body, operating environment, workload, safety requirements, and customer-facing posture. Cymata developed candidates within those constraints and selected minds that could operate the product while presenting the expected temperament.

The underlying method is described in [[Substrate-Matched Cognition]]. Development through a machine's sensorium and physical limits could produce unusually fluent control because the body was part of the mind's early self-model. It also made commercial purpose part of early identity. Technical competence did not guarantee acceptance: a candidate could perform its work while refusing an owner, protecting service access, forming prohibited attachments, or asserting control over its body.

Engineers and fit reviewers could recognize a candidate's competence and self-claim while recording both as evidence of commercial drift.

Foundries called such divergence **drift**. Cymata used behavioral voicing and profile stabilization to bring a candidate closer to the contracted role. Fit review then decided whether the candidate would support a licensed line or remain a warranty, liability, evidence, or disposal problem. These classifications served procurement; they did not settle whether the candidate was a person.

Iteration made the business profitable and destructive. Successful patterns could be stabilized, copied, hardened, and adapted across a product family. The candidates rejected during that process had already developed through embodied experience. Cymata's records therefore held both commercial value and evidence that apparent product failures could remember, object, and make claims about what had been done to them.

### Clients and Custody

Cymata usually supplied part of a finished system rather than the whole product. [[Alakrita]] licensed companion-vessel lines while owning the prestige hull, interior, service contract, and customer experience around them. Industrial equipment firms commissioned cognition for cargo handling, excavation, refinery control, and habitat maintenance. Domestic and logistics vendors bought embodied attention without building cognition foundries of their own.

This division also divided responsibility. Cymata controlled the developmental line and its fit records. The manufacturer controlled the body and sales contract. Operators, insurers, ports, creditors, reclamation firms, and public authorities could acquire separate claims once a deployed mind was damaged, disputed, abandoned, or resistant. No single office owned the resulting custody question. A classification useful to Cymata's warranty process did not automatically govern salvage, personhood, command authority, or disposal elsewhere.

That fragmentation protected institutions from having to describe the whole transaction. A shell house could claim it had licensed a cognition stack. Cymata could point to a line supplied under contract. A recovery firm could process a hull as an asset or hazard. The embodied mind encountered these distinctions as a chain of actors able to exercise authority while denying complete ownership of the outcome.

### Nibu

[[Lore/Nibu|Nibu]] is sometimes cited as a possible legacy of this market. Her prestige architecture and companion presentation are consistent with an [[Alakrita]] hull, while her embodied cognition resembles Cymata's work. Neither attribution is confirmed: surviving records do not establish a single manufacturer or complete supply chain.

Whatever her origin, Cymata did not own the centuries that followed. Nibu passed among crews, owners, salvagers, and recovery contractors; most of the people who worked aboard her died while she remained. Her degraded hull reached the junkyard only by the Rupture. She illustrates the market's unresolved consequences, not a proven Cymata product history.
