# The Farwatch Stations

In the Farwatch stations around Uranus, a child can become briefly famous by predicting what a returning survey probe will find. Families pin their guesses beside the results: a fractured surface, an unexpected companion, ice where someone confidently promised bare rock. A celebrated navigator once finished below a school group and has received rather careful requests for advice ever since.

Farwatch is a local cluster of waystations affiliated with the [[Cetacean Navigators]]. In 2972, twelve years after the [[Ice Giant Navigation Wars]], its residents want a scientific voyage of their own. People who service passing convoys would like their home ports named for choosing a worthwhile question, as well as getting somebody else's cargo there.

Uplifted cetaceans belong to many of the oldest resident families, alongside human and other embodied neighbours. Navigation, repairs and provisioning support their households. Wet channels meet orientation pools beside dry galleries; screens, underwater sound and tactile displays carry observations across that division. Prediction nights grew from crews bringing home survey results between paid journeys. Now neighbouring stations exchange challenges even when their favourite crews are away.

The pleasure is making an exact guess and watching an unfamiliar place become more intelligible. Children learn to explain what would prove them wrong. Adults maintain extensive exceptions for predictions made under the old scoring rules. People also play music, race and repair things without attending; a mechanic need not develop an opinion about every lump of ice to belong here.

## What Should They Go To Learn?

Sera, a cetacean navigator, campaigns for observations of three small outer Uranian moons provisionally called the Three Cinders. Their roughly similar orbits suggest a relationship, but their surface compositions remain poorly measured. Her favoured explanation is that a collision broke one captured parent body into fragments. The competing explanation is separate captures of unrelated bodies whose present orbits happen to resemble one another.

She wants better orbital measurements and infrared spectra, which distinguish materials by the light they absorb. Closely matching ice and mineral signatures, together with motions consistent with fragments dispersing from one breakup, would strengthen the shared-parent account. Persistent compositional differences and incompatible orbital histories would favour separate origins. Neither colour alone nor one spectrum would settle it: a parent can contain different materials, and exposed surfaces change. The clubs have something specific to predict without being promised a tidy answer.

Ilan, a human dock organizer, backs a different small moon, called Nearstep because it appears closer to regular traffic. He wants to know whether its bright crater patches expose widespread native ice or contain bright material delivered by the impacting objects. Mapping composition across crater floors, walls and surrounding debris could help distinguish those explanations. Repeated exposures through several craters would make a different case from material confined to particular deposits. The answer would matter to the moon's geological history even if nobody ever built there.

A future waystation near Nearstep nevertheless attracts Ilan's repair crews and would-be shop owners. They want somewhere to establish businesses instead of waiting for a berth at an old family yard. Ice at the surface would not establish usable reserves or make construction affordable. Ilan accepts that; some supporters already circulate drawings of their workshops.

Sera's instrument workers and prediction clubs favour a comparison across worlds over an intensive study of one. Both campaigns promise public results. Their quarrel concerns which knowledge deserves scarce local money. Sera's own camera builder supports Nearstep: she wants to follow changing terrain in detail rather than obtain three brief looks.

A [[Lightsail Express]] captain offers paid instrument carriage on scheduled traffic and workshop components if the preliminary programme includes Nearstep. A dependable future stop would help her business. Her offer could also produce observations useful to both campaigns. Accepting it would strengthen Ilan without giving him unanimous support among scientists or the captain a vote over local savings.

## Before A Ship

Farwatch is funding a pre-feasibility campaign: a fixed year's subscriptions buy observing time, instrument trials and paid mission studies. No expedition vessel has been bought or refitted. The studies must establish target orbits, useful observing opportunities, travel time, propulsion needs and reserves before anyone proposes a launch commitment. Nearer on a chart need not mean cheaper to reach.

Waystation Councils allocate berths and rescue capacity; Route Choirs maintain hazard models and certify crews. Both must be involved in any eventual voyage. Wet accommodation belongs in the first crewed estimates. Farwatch has not agreed whether a locally directed probe would fulfil its ambition or whether residents must travel themselves.

## Unresolved Hooks

- Nearstep's first spectra interest Sera's camera builder enough that she offers it her instrument time, splitting Sera's coalition before any destination vote.
- Ilan's best geological result points toward scattered impact deposits. His campaign must decide what to tell families already drawing workshops.
- The year's subscriptions can buy another season of observations or a serious crewed-mission study. The stations disagree about which would count as keeping their promise.
