# The Farwatch Stations

In the Farwatch stations around Uranus, a child can become briefly famous by predicting what a returning survey probe will find. Families pin their guesses beside the public results: a fractured surface, an unexpected companion, ice where someone confidently promised bare rock. Adults enter too. A celebrated navigator once finished below a school group and has been asked for navigational advice rather carefully ever since.

Farwatch is a local culture of several small waystations affiliated with the [[Cetacean Navigators]]. In 2972, twelve years after the [[Ice Giant Navigation Wars]], its residents want to send a ship beyond their regular freight routes to investigate three poorly observed icy bodies. They want their home ports named on a scientific achievement. People accustomed to guiding somebody else's cargo would like to choose where a ship goes and what questions it asks.

Most households make their living through convoy servicing, navigation, repairs, or provisioning. Uplifted cetaceans form many of the oldest resident families, alongside human and other embodied neighbors. Their shared regional identity comes from living at these stations, not from having the same ancestry. Wet channels open onto orientation pools beside dry galleries; screens, underwater sound, and tactile displays carry the same observations. At public prediction nights, visitors can follow the argument from either side of the water.

Farwatch audiences admire an exact, risky prediction more than an impressive recital of facts already known. Children learn to say what observation would prove them wrong. This does not make everyone gracious about losing. Some families maintain elaborate accounts of victories achieved before the current scoring rules, and people who prefer music, racing, or quiet find the survey enthusiasts fairly exhausting. A dock mechanic can belong completely while never wanting to see another annotated lump of ice.

The expedition grows out of this pleasure. Probe results give neighboring stations something to argue about between arrivals, while a shared voyage could turn their rivalry into a lasting collaboration. Each station wants one of its questions included. Local savings and paid workshop time have bought instruments and begun refitting a used vessel. Nobody expects the expedition to pay for itself by selling discoveries; its supporters regard a public body of observations as something worth buying together.

The sharpest disagreement concerns destinations. Sera, a cetacean navigator whose household has worked these corridors for generations, supports three contrasting bodies selected to test competing accounts of their formation. She wants the expedition's observations released for anyone to examine. Her allies include instrument workers and prediction clubs whose questions would never win a freight contract.

Ilan, a human dock organizer, wants the same ship to investigate one nearer body as a possible site for a future waystation. His supporters include crowded households and young repair crews who want another place where they could establish businesses. He thinks Sera enjoys having an established home rather too much to recognize settlement research as discovery. She thinks he has chosen his destination before asking whether it would teach them anything.

A local [[Lightsail Express]] captain offers transport assistance and replacement components if the expedition includes a useful assessment of that nearer site. The offer is public and materially valuable. It also gives Ilan's coalition a powerful ally. Some of Sera's backers want to reject it; others want to accept the components and contest the destination on its merits. The captain needs dependable future stopping places and has little interest in being cast as either a patron of pure science or its murderer.

Travel still costs propellant, power, maintenance, and time. Water and life support for the wet crew add mass; sending only dry-bodied investigators would change who gets to make the voyage. Farwatch's councils control the berths and rescue capacity the project needs, while Route Choirs judge crew competence and hazards under existing Navigator arrangements. Scientific enthusiasm cannot create a rescue ship. A destination that cannot be reached with adequate reserves must wait, however beautifully someone has drawn it.

## Unresolved Hooks

- A new observation makes Ilan's nearer target scientifically compelling. Sera must persuade her own supporters to reconsider after campaigning against it.
- One station offers its expedition contribution to a smaller probe mission instead. Its instruments could answer a major question sooner, but the common ship would lose its best camera.
- A wet-crew refit exceeds the estimate. The expedition's leading dry-bodied investigator offers to give up a berth and work remotely; a sponsor insists that their presence is what makes the voyage worth backing.
