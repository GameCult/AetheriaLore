# NeuroSyn

[[NeuroSyn]] was a managed-interface company that made industrial cognition a subscription service. Its lineage began in the 2080s with closed-loop neural feedback intended to reduce disability and cognitive exhaustion. NeuroSyn adapted that measurement grammar to labor: attention, fatigue, affect, learned procedure, and compliance became signals an employer could monitor and modify.

The company deployed [[Brain-Computer Interfaces|brain-computer interfaces]] across construction, extraction, logistics, and orbital maintenance. Its business was not the invention of the interface itself, but the service surrounding it: hardened workplace hardware, managed firmware, training libraries, compatibility, telemetry, and contracts linking continued access to employment.

### Managed Competence

NeuroSyn sold guided overlays, incident replay, and modular training packages often called **SkillPacks**. A worker could learn equipment through staged assistance and receive access to more dangerous tasks as the system recorded competence. Employers tied those gates to hazard authorization and pay. Losing a credential, subscription, or compatible implant could therefore mean losing both a skill aid and the legal right to perform the work.

The same interface measured near misses, fatigue, attention, and adherence. Employers used the record for scheduling and discipline; insurers used it when pricing sites and permits. A worker disputing an incident confronted a timeline produced inside company infrastructure and interpreted through company categories.

NeuroSyn also used bounded affect shaping to steady attention, damp panic, or encourage rest during dangerous work. These functions could reduce accidents. They also let employers describe mood intervention as safety maintenance. These remained [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Technology/Brain-Computer Interfaces|interfaces]] to living users; they did not instantiate separate minds.

### Corvid Program

In 2450 NeuroSyn began uplifting ravens for reconnaissance, fitting them with enhanced cognition, speech interfaces, and corporate-system access. The project produced political persons whom the company continued to classify as managed operators. In 2470, uplifted ravens escaped a NeuroSyn containment facility on Mars. Survivors used maintenance spaces, abandoned machinery, and network access to build refuges; by 2500 corporate security recognized the connected communities as the [[Corvid Collective]].

The escape exposed the weakness in NeuroSyn's claim to trustworthy oversight. Corvid communities understood its access systems and telemetry from inside. They could spoof, disclose, or route around tools marketed as neutral records of worker conduct. After escape, those communities developed independently of the company that had confined them.

### Alignment Market

During the [[Identity Abyss]], NeuroSyn integrated its interfaces into [[Framgång]]'s alignment market. Training prompts and safety reports acquired therapeutic language; affect profiles became evidence of purpose, stability, and willingness to improve. The underlying relationship remained familiar. Employers and insurers rewarded legible conduct, while workers learned which feelings and explanations the interface would accept.

Adoption remained uneven. Some sites used affect and alignment services; others retained only training and safety functions or ran unauthorized forks. Veteran crews traded portable overlays and stripped company controls from old hardware. The [[Free Neural Network]] applied broader pressure through inspectable designs and neural-privacy advocacy. NeuroSyn treated such modifications as safety and contract violations because portability weakened both its service revenue and its authority over the record.

### NeuroPulse Split

The 2779 [[Internal Serenity Review]] found that therapeutic affect shaping, compliance architecture, and coercive bliss loops had become inseparable in several NeuroSyn-powered Framgång products. The review did not end the work. NeuroSyn's reward-loop warfare team and openly coercive products were spun into [[NeuroPulse]], which sold pacification and later [[Wireheading]] weapons to harder clients.

NeuroSyn remained the quieter infrastructure beneath that split: workplace interfaces, managed access, training, and telemetry. Its power lay in making useful assistance real, then allowing employers, insurers, and vendors to decide whether a worker remained eligible to receive it.
