# NeuroSyn

[[NeuroSyn]] sold subscription services that helped industrial workers learn equipment and perform dangerous tasks through [[Brain-Computer Interfaces|brain-computer interfaces]]. Workers in construction, extraction, logistics, and orbital maintenance relied on its assistance. Their employers could make continued access a condition of employment—and make employment a condition of continued access.

The company’s roots lay in 2080s closed-loop neural feedback, developed to reduce disability and cognitive exhaustion by measuring a user’s state and adjusting assistance in response. NeuroSyn applied those methods to labor. Attention, fatigue, emotional state, learned procedures, and compliance became things employers could monitor and modify.

NeuroSyn did not invent the interface. It supplied the services that kept workplace interfaces usable: hardened hardware, managed firmware, training libraries, compatibility support, telemetry, and access contracts.

### Learning, Permission, and the Work Record

A worker could learn equipment with guided overlays, replays of incidents, and modular training packages commonly called **SkillPacks**. Assistance came in stages. As the system recorded competence, it could grant access to more dangerous tasks. Employers linked those permissions to hazard authorization and pay.

This made access to assistance part of a worker’s right to do the job. Losing a credential, subscription, or compatible implant could remove both the practical aid and the legal authorization to perform the work.

The interface also recorded near misses, fatigue, attention, and adherence to procedures. Employers used those records to schedule and discipline workers; insurers used them to price sites and permits. When a worker disputed an incident, the timeline they had to challenge had been produced within company infrastructure, using the company’s categories to describe what happened.

NeuroSyn’s systems could also make limited adjustments to a user’s emotional state: steadying attention, damping panic, or encouraging rest during dangerous work. These functions could prevent accidents. They also allowed employers to classify intervention in a worker’s mood as safety maintenance. The systems remained [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Technology/Brain-Computer Interfaces|interfaces]] serving living users; they did not create separate minds.

### Corvid Program

In 2450, NeuroSyn began enhancing ravens’ cognition for reconnaissance. It gave them speech interfaces and access to corporate systems. The uplift produced political persons, but the company continued to classify them as managed operators.

Uplifted ravens escaped a NeuroSyn containment facility on Mars in 2470. Survivors built refuges using maintenance spaces, abandoned machinery, and network access. By 2500, corporate security recognized the connected communities as the [[Corvid Collective]]. Those communities developed independently after escaping the company’s custody.

The escape also challenged NeuroSyn’s claim that its oversight could be trusted. Corvid communities knew its access systems and telemetry from the inside. They could spoof the records, disclose them, or bypass the systems that produced them. Tools sold as neutral accounts of worker conduct were vulnerable to people who understood how the company made those accounts.

### Alignment Services and Worker Alternatives

During the [[Identity Abyss]], NeuroSyn integrated its interfaces into [[Framgång]]’s alignment market: services concerned with purpose, emotional regulation, conduct, and self-improvement. Training prompts and safety reports began using therapeutic language. Records of a worker’s emotional state became evidence of purpose, stability, and willingness to improve.

Employers and insurers rewarded conduct they could recognize and evaluate through these systems. Workers learned which feelings—and which explanations of those feelings—the interface would accept.

Use varied between workplaces. Some adopted affect shaping and alignment services. Others kept only training and safety functions, or ran unauthorized forks. Veteran crews exchanged portable overlays and removed company controls from old hardware. The [[Free Neural Network]] supported wider resistance through inspectable designs and advocacy for neural privacy.

NeuroSyn treated these modifications as violations of safety requirements and contracts. Portable tools weakened its subscription business, while worker-controlled systems reduced its authority over the record of what workers had done.

### The NeuroPulse Split

The 2779 [[Internal Serenity Review]] found that several NeuroSyn-powered Framgång products could no longer separate therapeutic changes to emotion from systems enforcing compliance and coercive bliss loops. The review did not stop the work. NeuroSyn’s reward-loop warfare team and openly coercive products were spun into [[NeuroPulse]], which sold pacification systems and later [[Wireheading]] weapons to harder clients.

NeuroSyn continued supplying workplace interfaces, managed access, training, and telemetry. Its assistance remained useful. Employers, insurers, and vendors retained the power to decide whether a worker was still eligible to receive it.
