# The Welcome Supper

At Reed Court, introducing a new lover to your friends often means eating at a former lover's table. The host makes something they like, the newcomer brings something to share, and the person connecting them usually washes up. People remember particularly good welcome suppers for years: the unexpected friendship, the excellent bread, the evening when two people discovered that they had separately been told they were the only one who understood a certain poem.

Reed Court is a small agricultural commune around the Hellas Basin on [[Territories#Mars|Mars]] in late Sol, around 3000. Its residents belong to the [[Aya Collective]] and grow staple crops in maintained, enclosed growing space. Housing and common kitchens sit beside the cultivation areas. The settlement depends on the water coordination and shared reserves developed through the [[Hellas Mutual Defense Congress]]. Pumps, seals, and supply arrangements sustain this farming life. Most residents' daily concerns are crops, households, and neighbors.

The supper grew popular among people who kept seeing one another after separating. They still shared kitchens, visited the same friends, and helped raise children who had no interest in losing half their familiar adults. Inviting a new partner home became a pleasing way to stop treating every encounter as an awkward accident. An old attachment could acquire another person without everyone having to pretend it had vanished.

There is no obligation to invite every former partner. Usually one who remains a close friend offers to host when a new relationship becomes part of ordinary social life. Some suppers mark the end of a long partnership and the start of another; others welcome an additional partner into a circle where several relationships continue. People who prefer exclusive couples also hold them. The meal itself says nothing reliable about who shares a bed.

Reed Court admires hosts who make a newcomer feel interesting. Asking about their own friends is better manners than explaining the beloved's habits to them. Children absorb the distinction at ordinary meals: let a guest finish a story before supplying the family version. Adults are less consistently teachable. A former partner's affectionate account of how someone takes their tea can occupy surprising amounts of an evening.

Many residents sincerely want to become good at this. A former lover may be the person who knows which jokes are welcome, who can bring separated households together easily, or who becomes a new partner's independent friend. Others keep their past relationships at a comfortable distance. They may attend common meals while declining a welcome supper, though an empty invitation can still become gossip. The custom persists because enough people enjoy the encounters it makes possible. Its prestige also makes reluctance harder to admit.

Recently, Adina has offered to attend the welcome supper for her former partner, Beren, and his new lover, Malek. She will bring the pepper bread Malek admired at a common meal. She does not want to host. During her years with Beren, preparing their social life was mostly her work; she enjoys their friendship much more now that she can go home before the dishes.

Beren wants to hold the meal in Adina's kitchen, where their oldest friends still gather. He thinks her offer to attend leaves their separation conspicuously unfinished. Malek has offered his own table. He wants Adina there, and wants the first evening to include something he chose. The friends have already been asked to keep the evening free. Beren must now send them an address.

## Unresolved Hooks

- Adina has started the bread. Beren must decide whether to accept her company on the terms she offered or postpone the supper he has been praising to everyone.
- Malek owns a small table. Choosing which friends receive the first invitation will shape whose company the couple keeps; borrowing Adina's folding extension would make room for them all.
