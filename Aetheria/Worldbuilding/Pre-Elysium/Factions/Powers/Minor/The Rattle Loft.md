# The Rattle Loft

At the Rattle Loft, a young raven can spend weeks learning to leave one beat empty. That is where another player must answer. Getting the answer right earns an excited shuffle along the listening perch; getting it almost right can keep three friends awake arguing after rehearsal.

The Loft is a music-making circle in a small Martian community of the [[Corvid Collective]], during the late [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/index|Pre-Elysium]] centuries. Its members live in a reclaimed, pressurized maintenance complex and earn supplies through equipment repair and network work. Above their workshop, they build instruments for raven bodies: metal tongues fixed to shallow boxes, flexed by pulling and releasing beak-sized loops. A player stands on a broad perch, draws a loop sideways, and lets it snap back. Different tongues ring, clack, or buzz. Voices enter between the strokes.

The community's founders brought repair skills and recordings from several nests. Their young have grown up hearing the resulting mixture as the sound of home. Now the musicians want something more: to hear a piece they made answered by players they have never met.

## Music Someone Else Can Learn

Their favorite pieces pass a phrase between two or three players. A rough buzz becomes a clear note elsewhere; a voice completes a rhythm the instruments leave unfinished. Listeners know the approaching answer and sometimes lean toward its player before it arrives. An evening can succeed because one difficult exchange finally works, even if the rest remains dreadful.

The Loft sends recordings and instrument drawings through the Collective's existing exchanges. Alongside them travel short sung instructions: where to brace a box, how a loose mounting changes its sound, which pause leaves room for a breath. These extend the Collective's use of poems as practical records. Learning usually begins beside another player, with a muted instrument and someone willing to repeat the same few notes.

Being asked to teach a part is a local distinction. So is receiving a recording in which a distant nest has changed it well. A family may know its youngest member's composition long before that raven can play the hardest passage. Friends lend their skill to let each other hear unfinished work aloud. Some residents only listen, or prefer recorded music they can interrupt without offending three relatives.

The instruments make a modest claim on an existing workshop. Offcuts supply many tongues and boxes, but tuning takes patient labor; good spring material and quiet recording time remain scarce. Like other [[Uplift|uplifted communities]], these people depend on environments and tools fitted to their bodies. Here, that practical knowledge also gives an amateur musician somewhere to begin. Repairs and paid work continue downstairs. The musicians buy their special materials from the surplus and take turns making the dull replacement parts that produce it.

## The Piece With The Low Answer

Two longtime friends, called Rusk and Pell in human gloss, have spent a year on a piece whose last answer comes from a deep wooden box. Pell built it; Rusk wrote the answering phrase. Their neighbors recognize the ending from its first three strokes.

Another nest has learned the piece, but its smaller metal instruments cannot produce that low sound. Its players send back a bright, rattling ending. Rusk loves how it lifts the phrase. Pell hears the loss of the long vibration around which they built the whole passage.

Pell wants their next season of spare workshop time to make a second large instrument for the distant players. Rusk wants to spend those evenings rehearsing new pieces with them through exchanged recordings. Either undertaking would be a substantial gift between communities. Pell also wants to become an instrument maker whose work travels; Rusk wants collaborators who can answer without waiting for a shipment. They still rehearse together, and neither has stopped enjoying the original ending.

Distance reaches even this small ambition: a file can travel along their network routes, while a resonant box needs cargo space, packing, and someone willing to carry it.

- The distant players offer to build a larger box themselves if Pell teaches them. Pell must decide whether teaching will satisfy the wish to send something made by their own beak.
- A young learner has written an opening that fits both endings, but wants Rusk and Pell to perform it together before sending it anywhere.
