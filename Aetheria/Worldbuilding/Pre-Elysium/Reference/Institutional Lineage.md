Institutional lineage traces how research, infrastructure, patents, trained personnel, and founding stories passed from one organization to another. The great powers of late Sol grew by taking over earlier work. Failed startups, captured laboratories, debt restructurings, strikebreaking, and research programs all contributed to their power. The people who began that work usually lost control of it.

These transfers help explain the [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Timeline/index|Timeline]]. They also explain why a technology's original purpose could differ so sharply from its later use: research intended to ease dangerous labor became a way to sell control over workers' attention, while tools for independent habitat builders became assets worth fighting over.

### Transport, Extraction, and Independent Habitats

[[Horizon Ventures]] supplied transport; [[TerraCore Industries]] held extraction contracts. Debt pressure drove their merger into [[SolEx]] in 2095. The new company retained Horizon's transport network and TerraCore's contracts, but little of either company's public language about opportunity.

Horizon had already made a founding legend out of Dr. Mara Varga. She died during the Tycho coil-failure inquiry in 2068, after refusing to approve cheaper containment housings, and never saw SolEx formed. Horizon celebrated her as an inspirational founder and martyr while adopting the same compromises she had warned against.

For independent habitat builders, control over construction tools and standards was a practical question: could they use the same standards across jurisdictions, or would each jurisdiction constrain how they built? Tamsin Ro insisted that cavity-construction standards remain portable across those boundaries. That stance made her a hero to independent builders.

[[Orbital Forge]] began as a cluster of contractors working within SolEx's expansion operations. Engineers and cavity planners fought to keep Bloom tooling at least partly open-licensed, and the cluster broke away. The dispute left Orbital Forge commercially indispensable and politically suspect for centuries. Ro's death in the Pallas sabotage campaign of 2147 strengthened both views of the organization: it could enable independent settlement, and it could be treated as a lasting security threat.

Later thermal-weave research at Ceres Polytechnic produced the [[Cinderlace Cooperative]]. For a brief period, its extraordinary emitters might have become a shared industrial resource governed by the Belt. The [[Cinderlace Licensing War]] closed that possibility.

[[Cryonix]] took over the cooperative's manufacturing arm and enough control over its standards to become a sovereign enclave megacorporation. The technology did not remain entirely under its control: Orbital Forge yards and clandestine channels continued to preserve and circulate leaked variants.

### From Assistive Interfaces to Control Over Workers

Imani Okafor wanted brain-computer interfaces to reduce disability and the mental exhaustion of dangerous work. Her Lagos team developed ways to measure the processes involved. Those measurements later allowed companies to turn attention, emotional states, and compliance into billable services. [[NeuroSyn]], [[CogniSys]], [[Lucent Media]], and eventually [[NeuroPulse]] all used that inheritance.

NeuroSyn first sold interfaces as respectable tools for productivity and wellness. It also developed a team devoted to reward-loop warfare: using the brain's reward responses as a means of coercion.

After the 2779 Internal Serenity Review, that team was spun out into NeuroPulse. Several jurisdictions had formally banned coercive bliss architecture—the systems used to control people through induced bliss—while [[Zhestokost]] quietly provided a protected market for it.

### Uploads, Bodies, and Manufactured Minds

The corporate immortality economy divided customers by what they could afford and what they wanted from continued life. [[EternaMind]] sold immortality to elites. [[Finch Cybernetics]] offered the premium professional class graceful embodiment: an uploaded mind integrated with a body it could inhabit and use.

Mireille Finch had spent decades arguing that embodiment mattered. She warned that uploads denied dignity in how they sensed and acted through a body would become a new slave class. Finch Cybernetics turned that argument into a reason to buy premium integration. Cheaper upload markets continued exploiting people alongside those luxury services, treating Finch's example as evidence that the two markets could coexist.

The [[Cognitum]] arose where those commercial markets overlapped with secretly funded military cognition research. EternaMind, Finch Cybernetics, and the Cognitum each claimed a morally distinct purpose, though their server farms revealed how closely their operations were related.

[[Cymata Systems]] served a neighboring market. Its customers wanted artificial minds developed for particular bodies, rather than continuity for people who had been uploaded. Cymata sold AGI lines matched to their physical substrate to shell houses such as [[Alakrita]] and to industrial firms. The aim was a machine whose mind felt native to its assigned body.

The distinction affected how those minds came into existence and what could happen to them. Cognitum turned existing persons into firmware. Cymata raised new candidates within developmental worlds shaped around the products they would inhabit. Reclassification then determined which of those selves survived the contract.

### The Formation of Sol Dominion

Between 2728 and 2740, [[CogniSys]], [[GeneSys]], [[Quantum Dynamics]], and the managerial shell of [[SolEx]] were incorporated into [[Sol Dominion]].

The participants presented the merger as a stability pact. It was a coordinated response to scarcity and insurgency, together with fear that uncontrolled uploads, uplifts, and rogue AGI were making the system impossible to govern. Bringing these organizations together was their answer to those pressures.

### Navigation and the Escape Attempt

The cetacean navigator Choir-of-Tides helped make the final generation of long-range routing possible. Their work with Saeed al-Harith joined cetacean navigation methods to Quantum Dynamics mathematics in 2978, immediately before the FTL research program.

Choir-of-Tides came to be remembered less as an individual than as a school of shared navigational doctrine. That technical inheritance also carried political weight. Their work became inseparable from the dispute over who would be allowed to steer humanity's escape attempt in [[FTL Trigger]].

### The Inheritance by 3025

On the eve of the shunt into Elysium, every major bloc controlled some genuine part of the infrastructure that civilization needed. Each could therefore plausibly claim to be defending civilization.

Those overlapping claims helped keep late Sol unstable. The factions did useful work, promoted ideologies, and held assets acquired through theft as well as development. Understanding what each had inherited explains both why people depended on them and why no single faction's account of its own legitimacy was enough.
