# Last Witness Bonds

Late-Sol contract law learned to distinguish habitation from occupancy. A failing habitat could contain thousands of sleeping, dependent, or unrecognized minds and still count as vacant for a particular claim; one qualifying person could keep it occupied after ordinary life had ended.

A **Last Witness Bond** is a creditor covenant used to preserve continuous possession of a distressed [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Technology/Bloom|Bloom]], yard, relay, repository, or other inhabited asset during evacuation, seizure, or service default. Participating insurers, ports, clearing houses, and rival claimants agree not to treat the asset as abandoned while a named, legally recognized person remains inside its controlled boundary, answers notices, and possesses limited authority to isolate hazards and admit relief. The witness does not own the place. Their continued exposure keeps somebody else's ownership legible.

The bond grew during the contraction of the [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Timeline/Existential Collapse|Existential Collapse]], when empty projects became salvage and remote operators became cheap to counterfeit. A local person supplied three things telemetry could not: a body that could observe the asset, an agent who could accept notice, and someone the surrounding order could punish for a false statement. The [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Factions/Powers/Major/Pan-Solar Consortium|Pan-Solar Consortium]] never made the form universal. It became powerful because enough institutions independently recognized it when deciding insurance, clearing access, berth priority, receivership, and whether a later seizure would be saleable.

## The Two-Call Presumption

Terms vary, but the common Belt form uses a two-call presumption. During a declared drawdown, two institutions with separate interests issue unpredictable presence challenges inside a short claim interval. The witness must answer from physically separated control points, report the local safety state, and perform one bounded action that a remote signature cannot complete. Missing both calls creates a **presence lapse** until an accepted physical inspection restores the record.

A lapse does not make every claim vanish. It removes the presumption of continuous possession on which other contracts were priced. An insurer may convert an occupied loss into an excluded abandoned loss; a lender may accelerate the habitat debt; utilities and repair suppliers may demand cash terms; a receiver may freeze stores; and a rival may lodge a salvage or protective-custody claim. No office needs authority over the whole cascade. Each office only stops extending the privilege it had conditioned on a live witness.

The test is cruelest during the fault it is meant to clarify. Fire doors, severed data routes, failing frame-transfer machinery, medical incapacity, or a habitat-wide evacuation can prevent a truthful witness from reaching the second point. Saboteurs therefore attack clocks and communications rather than pressure shells. A rescue crew may be able to save the remaining person only by creating the lapse that transfers the home, workplace, or repository to the creditor waiting outside.

## Jurisdictional Ballast

Bond schedules promise the Last Witness hazard pay, survival reserves, a relief deadline, and priority passage after an accepted replacement arrives. In distressed portfolios, those promises are usually attached to something the witness cannot abandon cheaply: family housing at the receiving habitat, accumulated wages, clinic access, transport debt, or recognition of a dependent's residency. The witness remains formally free to leave. Departure merely converts every promised cure into a default at once.

This produces the inverse of an eviction. A firm clears an asset of everyone it no longer wants to feed, then keeps one low-status worker inside because an occupied ruin is worth more than an abandoned one. The exodus ship leaves on schedule even when the replacement does not arrive. Minimal power, air, water, and communications continue because the witness must survive, but comfort, ordinary staffing, and redundant escape capacity become disputed expenses.

Eligibility follows administrative personhood rather than need or competence. A jurisdiction may refuse to accept an uplift as a witness while using their labor to keep the habitat alive. Another may recognize the same [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Technology/Uplift|uplifted person]] and thereby make them eligible for the bond. Recognition can therefore open a route out of property status and a new route into compulsory exposure. Creditors call this equal capacity to contract. Workers call the eligible person **jurisdictional ballast**.

Last Witnesses are not powerless. Walking away can destroy more creditor value than their lifetime wages, and accurate local testimony can implicate a lender in concealed maintenance debt. Firms answer that leverage by dividing the consequences among dependants, suppliers, and other residents who need the claim to remain intact. The witness is made responsible for everyone who will suffer if they refuse to preserve the asset harming them.

## Second Chairs

Small dock and caretaker circles sometimes keep a **second chair**: one person with clean enough standing, compatible body support, passage, and current hazard credentials to replace a stranded witness. A valid substitution requires both people to complete a co-present handoff before the outgoing witness crosses the boundary. Because the bond claims to require evidence rather than detention, a creditor that rejects an eligible substitute risks invalidating its own form.

Second-chair circles pool suits, travel credit, food, accommodation hardware, and the private knowledge of which claims desks will accept which bodies. They do not save the asset or erase its debt. They let one person leave before isolation becomes injury, at the cost of placing another person under the clock. Each handoff exposes the replacement's records and can consume the clean standing that made them eligible. The practice works quietly because it distributes a burden the contract was designed to concentrate.

## Open Pressures

- If a Last Witness is reclassified as property or a nonperson during the bond, does the asset become vacant while they are still alive inside it?
- When rescue creates a presence lapse, who caused the default: the crew that removed the witness or the creditor that failed to provide the promised replacement?
- Does a dreamer repository hold millions of occupants, or none at all, when no awake rights-bearing person can answer the second call?
