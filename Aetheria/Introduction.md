Aetheria is a setting in which capitalism has escaped every restraint that claimed to civilize it. In the later history of Sol, and then in Elysium, corporations reorganize everyday life around their power. A person's citizenship depends on their contract status. Control of infrastructure gives its owners leverage over those who need it. Even consciousness can be priced, licensed, copied, or denied service.

These powers work together. Markets and labor systems shape how people make a living; institutions and military doctrine shape how power is exercised and defended. Media and spiritual language shape how people understand that order. The danger comes from how these arrangements reinforce one another, as much as from spectacular violence or advanced technology.

Every faction has been shaped by the pressures of the world it came from. Every promise of liberation creates new dependencies. Breakthroughs in transport, computation, biology, and cognition change who can rule and who can resist. They also change which kinds of beings can claim recognition as persons. Understanding a technology therefore means following what it lets people do, who controls it, and how it changes their choices.
