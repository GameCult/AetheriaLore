# Persistent Universe and Reset Loop

> **Status: long-horizon design north star.** The persistent world is Aetheria's intended final form, not a claim about current release scope, cadence, or implementation status. Smaller designs may prove pieces of it without inheriting the whole machine.

## Canon Substrate

[[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Concepts/Temporal Nonlinearity|Temporal Nonlinearity]] owns the setting claim. Humanity experiences one Sol history and, after the Rupture, one human branch at a time. The Aetheric Field surrounds that lived history with unrealized possibilities that can interfere with it, monitors whether the branch can continue, and resets it when human extinction becomes unavoidable. The inhabitants did not consent to become a managed continuity problem.

That substrate does not canonize a season length, visible extinction meter, reset warning, or retained currency, equipment, reputation, and territory. Those remain product decisions. The design commitment is narrower and harsher: the player population receives one shared history at a time, that history ends when humanity can no longer continue, and the next iteration tests what the community learned.

## Social Experiment

The loop asks how people learn to cooperate when nearly every local incentive rewards competition, acceleration, defection, or the transfer of risk to somebody less powerful. Information alone cannot solve that problem. Players may know that a technology is dangerous and still deploy it because rivals will, because the present emergency is real, or because individual restraint carries private cost while its benefit remains collective.

The intended difficulty is civilizational. Early iterations may last only weeks. Extending a branch into months should require dozens of failures in which players first learn individual disaster mechanisms, then discover how successful local interventions create shortages, legitimacy crises, arms races, or dependencies elsewhere. The eventual problem is not defeating one apocalypse. It is building a civilization that does not continuously manufacture new ones.

The campaign moves through three recurring pressures:

1. **Expansion:** players explore, establish institutions, open routes, build markets, and fight local wars.
2. **Dependency:** specialization, consolidation, production, and faction leverage make survival more capable and more brittle. Routes, labor, maintenance, and supplies become commitments rather than conveniences.
3. **Triage:** terminal pressure exposes what the civilization built. Hoarding, coordination, arms production, evacuation, rescue, research, abandonment, and betrayal compete for the same dwindling capacity.

The arc gives economic play a terminal question: not merely who accumulated the most, but what their arrangements could preserve when ordinary growth stopped solving problems. Ratfolk institutions may demonstrate that long-lived cultures can transmit caution, redundancy, and revocable emergency authority. They are neither a species-wide answer key nor proof that safety doctrine cannot itself become domination; see [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Species/Ratfolk|Ratfolk]].

## Failure As Progression

The primary persistent progression lives in human and community knowledge rather than permanent statistical advantage. Players remember. Their new characters do not automatically inherit that memory, and institutions inside the branch must be built, persuaded, documented, or contested again. A previous iteration can teach the community what to watch without granting its next characters unearned evidence or authority.

Useful account-level carryover may include access to historical records, comparative tools, or ways to inspect prior failures. Material power, reputation, and territorial control should not accumulate until extinction becomes a disguised grind. The community succeeds because it learned how to organize, not because iteration forty-seven awards a civilization-stability bonus.

Repeated failure must not make earlier participation disposable. Each iteration should remain available as a dead civilization with its duration, institutions, conflicts, discoveries, unresolved stories, surviving evidence, and contested explanations. Players can mourn a branch, argue about it, and carry its lesson forward without pretending that the people inside it knew they were tutorial material.

## Procedural Postmortem

Failure should be legible in retrospect and reported through material consequence rather than moral scoring. A postmortem should show which systems held, which collapsed, who profited, who abandoned whom, what records survived, and which shortages became fatal. Causal confidence and disagreement must remain visible. The game does not need to call anyone monstrous. It can show the evacuation queue closing.

Opaque extinction is merely a server failure wearing a philosophical hat. Players should usually be able to reconstruct how individually defensible actions joined into catastrophe, including cases where they knowingly accepted collective risk for private or factional advantage.

## Recurrence And Aetheric Inertia

History is unique; situations recur. Familiar institutions, crises, and people may return under different political and material conditions, while favorite arcs can be interrupted for many iterations by extinction. Reaching a previously unseen stage of an old life may become evidence that the community has entered uncharted history.

Player memory must remain useful without becoming a deterministic walkthrough. Repeated patterns can mutate, and the Aetheric Field permits limited interference among unrealized possibilities. Déjà vu, recurrent symbols, impossible correlations, and cognitive attractors that appear to gain weight across iterations are candidate expressions of that pressure. They are not ordinary character memory and should never excuse a quest from establishing how a person knows what they claim.

At the longest horizon, the experiment can ask whether repeated player culture is carving stable structure into the field: not merely whether the players remember the universe, but whether the universe has begun to remember them.

## Open Decisions

1. What evidence establishes that extinction is unavoidable, and how much warning can players reliably receive?
2. Which account-level historical tools persist without granting characters unearned knowledge?
3. How does the archive preserve consequence without turning extinction into a reward screen?
4. How can dangerous-technology governance reward bounded experimentation, dissent, and revocable safeguards rather than converging on a permanent prohibition authority?
