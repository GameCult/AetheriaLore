## Stories Across The Setting

Aetheria makes room for very different genres while keeping track of how events happen and what they change:

- **Pre-Elysium hard science fiction and black magic:** heat and habitat engineering determine what can stay alive; cognition markets and labor control shape whose abilities can be bought and directed. Dangerous research leads toward the [[Worldbuilding/Pre-Elysium/Timeline/FTL Trigger|FTL Trigger]].
- **Noir and institutional mystery:** investigators face custody disputes, insurers and ports controlling access, contradictory records, and media pressure. Crimes can arise from decisions spread across several respectable offices, each holding only part of the authority.
- **Post-Elysium mecha and cultivation drama:** pilots of the [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Factions/Daedal Houses|Daedal Houses]] learn to operate ship-scale vessels called Figures as extensions of their own bodies. [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Factions/Wavecrafters|Wavecrafters]] preserve the knowledge needed to operate their technology through practiced forms, guild discipline, and guarded teaching lineages.
- **First contact:** species such as the [[Worldbuilding/Post-Elysium/Species/Ratfolk|Ratfolk]] have diverse communities, accessibility needs, and political disputes of their own. An outsider asking for a single representative gives them reasons for distrust.
- **Corporate, domestic, or workplace stories:** decisions about food, cooling, credentials, medicine, repair, transport, and shelter shape people's lives. Nobody needs to fire a weapon for those decisions to become political.

## Where Stories Begin

Aetheria's conflicts often begin with a practical decision that cannot wait for agreement:

- A new technology works, but people have not agreed whether it should exist.
- One transaction recognizes someone as a person; the next treats them as property.
- A transformed body gains abilities, but fewer people are permitted to maintain it.
- Two credible records contradict each other. A port, crew, or court still has to decide what to do.
- The kindest available action keeps part of a harmful institution operating.
- Rescuers need temporary authority, then find reasons to keep it after the rescue.
- A sanctuary must decide how to keep welcoming people without using up the resources and strength of those already inside.
- Maintenance workers can see a system failing while its official dashboard still reports that it works.

People also work to make other choices possible. They provide food, cooling, truthful records, disability access, refuge, repair, and transport. Sharing authority matters too: those receiving help need enough say to refuse what others propose to do to save them.

Writers can take responsibility for a cast, a place, and the events they depict without having to control the entire universe. Their stories must respect the setting's shared constraints. [[Narrative Themes]] explores its recurring tensions; the [[Stories/How Branching Stories Are Made|writing process]] and [repository](https://github.com/GameCult/AetheriaLore) are open to inspection and contribution.
